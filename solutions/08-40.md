**Central idea.** In $(u,v)$ coordinates the likelihood factors into a conditional piece $g(v \mid u)$ whose maximum doesn't involve $\mu_X - \mu_Y$ (so it cancels) and a marginal piece $h(u)$ giving exactly the one-sample $t$ LRT on the differences.

**Solution.** (a) Put $U = X - Y$, $V = X + Y$ (constant Jacobian). Then $f_{X,Y}(x,y) = g(v \mid u)h(u)$ with $h$ the $n(\mu_X-\mu_Y, \sigma_W^2)$ marginal of $U$ and $g$ the normal conditional of $V \mid U$. The full likelihood is $\prod_i g(v_i \mid u_i)\prod_i h(u_i)$. The parameters of $g$ (conditional regression coefficients and variance) are unrestricted both under $H_0$ ($E[U] = 0$) and under $H_1$, so $\sup \prod g$ is identical in numerator and denominator and cancels. Hence $\lambda = \sup_{E[U]=0,\sigma_W^2}\prod h(u_i)/\sup_{E[U],\sigma_W^2}\prod h(u_i)$, the ordinary one-sample normal LRT for $H_0: E[U] = 0$ versus $H_1: E[U] \ne 0$ based on $U_i = X_i - Y_i$.
(b) By 8.38(b), that LRT rejects for large $|T_W| = |\bar W|/\sqrt{S_W^2/n}$ — precisely the (two-sided) paired-sample $t$ test of 8.39.
