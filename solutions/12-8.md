**Central idea.** The bivariate normal marginal (12.2.19) has five parameters but the structural model has six — one degree of freedom is unidentified; exhibit it by trading $\beta$ against the variance components while holding the five moments fixed.

**Solution.** (a) The marginal is determined by the five quantities $\xi$, $\alpha+\beta\xi$, $\sigma^2_\delta+\sigma^2_\xi$, $\sigma^2_\epsilon+\beta^2\sigma^2_\xi$, $\beta\sigma^2_\xi$. Take $\theta_1=(\alpha,\beta,\xi,\sigma^2_\delta,\sigma^2_\epsilon,\sigma^2_\xi)=(0,1,0,2,15,4)$: means $(0,0)$, $\mathrm{Var}\,X=6$, $\mathrm{Var}\,Y=19$, $\mathrm{Cov}=4$. Take $\theta_2=(0,2,0,4,11,2)$: means $(0,0+2\cdot0)=(0,0)$, $\mathrm{Var}\,X=4+2=6$, $\mathrm{Var}\,Y=11+4\cdot2=19$, $\mathrm{Cov}=2\cdot2=4$. Both give the identical bivariate normal, but $\theta_1\ne\theta_2$.

(b) The system (12.2.20) has five equations in six unknowns. E.g., with $\bar x,\bar y$ arbitrary and $S_{xx}/n=6$, $S_{yy}/n=19$, $S_{xy}/n=4$: one solution is $\hat\beta=1$, $\hat\alpha=\bar y-\bar x$, $\hat\sigma^2_\xi=4$, $\hat\sigma^2_\delta=2$, $\hat\sigma^2_\epsilon=15$; another is $\hat\beta=2$, $\hat\alpha=\bar y-2\bar x$, $\hat\sigma^2_\xi=2$, $\hat\sigma^2_\delta=4$, $\hat\sigma^2_\epsilon=11$. Both satisfy all five equations, so the MLE is not unique.
