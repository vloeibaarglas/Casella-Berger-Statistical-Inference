**Central idea.** Clear denominators to get a cubic polynomial identity in $w$; the four coefficients give four linear equations for $A, B, C, D$. The decomposition is engineered so the divergent log terms cancel ($A\sigma^2 = C\tau^2$), leaving arctan terms that assemble into the Cauchy$(0, \sigma+\tau)$ density.

**Solution.** (a) Multiplying through by $(w^2+\sigma^2)((z-w)^2+\tau^2)$ gives the polynomial identity
$$\sigma^2\tau^2 = \sigma^2(Aw+B)\big((z-w)^2+\tau^2\big) - \tau^2(Cw+D)(w^2+\sigma^2).$$
Equating coefficients of $w^3, w^2, w^1, w^0$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\sigma^2 A - \tau^2 C &= 0, \\
\sigma^2(B - 2Az) - \tau^2 D &= 0, \\
A(z^2+\tau^2-\sigma^2) - 2Bz &= 0, \\
B(z^2+\tau^2) - \tau^2 D &= \tau^2.
\end{aligned}$$
With $u = z^2+\tau^2-\sigma^2$ and $\Delta = u^2 + 4\sigma^2 z^2 = \big(z^2+(\sigma+\tau)^2\big)\big(z^2+(\sigma-\tau)^2\big)$, the solution is
$$A = \frac{2\tau^2 z}{\Delta}, \quad B = \frac{\tau^2 u}{\Delta}, \quad C = \frac{2\sigma^2 z}{\Delta}, \quad D = \frac{\sigma^2(u - 4z^2)}{\Delta} = \frac{\sigma^2(\tau^2-\sigma^2-3z^2)}{\Delta}.$$
(b) The convolution is $f_W(z) = \frac{1}{\pi^2\sigma\tau}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{dw}{[1+(w/\sigma)^2][1+((z-w)/\tau)^2]}$. Termwise antiderivatives:
$$\int \frac{Aw\,dw}{1+(w/\sigma)^2} = \tfrac{A\sigma^2}{2}\log\!\left(1+\tfrac{w^2}{\sigma^2}\right), \quad \int \frac{B\,dw}{1+(w/\sigma)^2} = B\sigma\arctan\!\left(\tfrac{w}{\sigma}\right),$$
$$\int \frac{Cw\,dw}{1+((z-w)/\tau)^2} = -C\tau z\arctan\!\left(\tfrac{z-w}{\tau}\right) + \tfrac{C\tau^2}{2}\log\!\left(1+\tfrac{(z-w)^2}{\tau^2}\right), \quad \int \frac{D\,dw}{1+((z-w)/\tau)^2} = -D\tau\arctan\!\left(\tfrac{z-w}{\tau}\right).$$
The two log terms are each logarithmically divergent, but their difference converges because $A\sigma^2 = C\tau^2$ (from $\sigma^2A = \tau^2C$), so the $\log|w|$ pieces cancel. Evaluating from $-R$ to $R$ and letting $R \to \infty$ (using $\arctan(t) \to \pm\pi/2$), the integral equals $\pi(B\sigma - C\tau z - D\tau)$. Substituting the constants,
$$B\sigma - C\tau z - D\tau = \frac{\sigma\tau(\sigma+\tau)}{z^2+(\sigma+\tau)^2},$$
hence $f_W(z) = \frac{1}{\pi^2\sigma\tau}\cdot\pi(B\sigma - C\tau z - D\tau) = \frac{\sigma+\tau}{\pi\big(z^2+(\sigma+\tau)^2\big)}$, the Cauchy$(0,\sigma+\tau)$ pdf — verifying (5.2.5): the sum of independent Cauchy$(0,\sigma)$ and Cauchy$(0,\tau)$ is Cauchy$(0,\sigma+\tau)$.
