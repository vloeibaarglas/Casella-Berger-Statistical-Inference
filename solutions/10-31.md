**Central idea.** Under $H_0$ the pooled variance estimate gives a standard normal pivot whose square is $\chi^2_1$; the Pearson $T^*$ collapses algebraically to the same $T$, while $T^{**}$ uses the unpooled (Wald) variance.

**Solution.** (a) Under $H_0$ ($p_1=p_2=p$), $\hat p_1-\hat p_2$ is asymptotically $n(0,p(1-p)(1/n_1+1/n_2))$ by independence and the CLT, and $\hat p\xrightarrow{p}p$; Slutsky gives $\frac{\hat p_1-\hat p_2}{\sqrt{(1/n_1+1/n_2)\hat p(1-\hat p)}}\xrightarrow{d}n(0,1)$, whose square is $T\xrightarrow{d}\chi^2_1$.
(b) $S_1-\frac{n_1S}{n} = \frac{n_2S_1-n_1S_2}{n} = \frac{n_1n_2}{n}(\hat p_1-\hat p_2)$, and similarly for the other cells. Summing $\frac{(\text{obs}-\text{exp})^2}{\text{exp}}$:
$$T^*= \left[\frac{n_1n_2}{n}(\hat p_1-\hat p_2)\right]^2\cdot n\left(\frac1{n_1}+\frac1{n_2}\right)\left(\frac1S+\frac1F\right) = \frac{(\hat p_1-\hat p_2)^2}{\left(\frac1{n_1}+\frac1{n_2}\right)\hat p(1-\hat p)} = T,$$
using $S/n=\hat p$, $F/n=1-\hat p$. Hence $T^*\xrightarrow{d}\chi^2_1$.
(c) Same CLT, but the denominator $\xrightarrow{p}\sqrt{p(1-p)(1/n_1+1/n_2)}$ without pooling: $T^{**}\xrightarrow{d}n(0,1)$, $(T^{**})^2\xrightarrow{d}\chi^2_1$. $(T^{**})^2\ne T^*$ because the denominators differ (unpooled $\hat p_1(1-\hat p_1)/n_1+\hat p_2(1-\hat p_2)/n_2$ vs.\ pooled $(1/n_1+1/n_2)\hat p(1-\hat p)$).
(d) $T$ ($=T^*$) is the score test: preferable under $H_0$ (pooled variance is more stable; locally most powerful). $T^{**}$ is the Wald test: preferable when a signed/directional test or a confidence interval for $p_1-p_2$ is wanted, or when the $H_0$ variance assumption is doubted.
(e) $\hat p_1=34/40=0.85$, $\hat p_2=19/35=0.543$, $\hat p=53/75=0.707$: $T = \frac{(0.307)^2}{(1/40+1/35)(0.707)(0.293)} = 8.50$. Since $\chi^2_{1,0.95}=3.84$, reject $H_0$ ($p$-value $0.0036$): carbolic acid is significantly associated with lower mortality.
