**Central idea.** Chernoff bounds give $C\rho^n$ for fixed thresholds. At threshold zero the prefactor is 1; for arbitrary thresholds it can be absorbed into $c^n$ for all sufficiently large $n$, but not necessarily for every $n$.

**Solution.** (a) For $t>0$, Markov gives $P(S_n>a)\le e^{-ta}M_X(t)^n$. For $t<0$, the map $s\mapsto e^{ts}$ is decreasing, so $P(S_n\le a)\le e^{-ta}M_X(t)^n$.

(b) If $EX<0$, then $M_X(t)=1+tEX+o(t)<1$ for some small positive $t$. Put $\rho=M_X(t)\in(0,1)$ and $C=e^{-ta}$. Then $P(S_n>a)\le C\rho^n$. For any $\rho<c<1$, $C\rho^n\le c^n$ once $n$ is sufficiently large. If $a\ge0$, $C\le1$ and $c=\rho$ works for every $n$. The unrestricted all-$n$ statement in (b) needs qualification: taking $X_i\equiv-1$ and $a=-2$ gives $P(S_1>a)=1$, incompatible with $c<1$. No adjustment of finitely many $n$ can repair a probability equal to 1. Similarly, if $EX>0$, a small negative $t$ gives $P(S_n\le a)\le C\rho^n$, and an eventual bound $c^n$; for $a\le0$ the prefactor is at most 1 and the bound holds for every $n$.

(c) Apply (b) at threshold zero to $Y_i=X_i-\mu-\varepsilon$, whose mean is $-\varepsilon$. This gives $P(\bar X_n-\mu>\varepsilon)\le c_+^n$ for every $n$.

(d) Apply the same argument to $-X_i+\mu-\varepsilon$ to get $P(\bar X_n-\mu<-\varepsilon)\le c_-^n$. With $c=\max(c_+,c_-)<1$, the union bound gives $P(|\bar X_n-\mu|>\varepsilon)\le2c^n$. Summability and Borel–Cantelli, followed by a countable sequence of positive $\varepsilon$ tending to zero, give the SLLN under the stated mgf assumption.
