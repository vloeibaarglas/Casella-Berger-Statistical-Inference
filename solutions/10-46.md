**Central idea.** Simulate coverage ($n=15$, nominal $0.9$): median-with-bootstrap-SE intervals hold their level across distributions; Huber-with-bootstrap-variance intervals undercover slightly, like the book's plug-in versions.

**Solution.** Intervals: (a) $M_n\pm z_{0.05}\,\mathrm{SE}^*_{\text{boot}}(M_n)$; (b) $\hat\theta_M\pm z_{0.05}\sqrt{V^*_{\text{boot}}/n}$ ($B=200$, 1500 simulations):

| Underlying pdf | (a) median + bootstrap SE | (b) Huber + bootstrap var |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | 0.891 | 0.875 |
| $t_5$ | 0.893 | 0.871 |
| Logistic | 0.897 | 0.880 |
| Double exponential | 0.923 | 0.869 |

(a) The median intervals are robust: coverage stays near $0.90$ for all four distributions (best for the double exponential, $0.923$, where the median is the MLE). (b) The Huber bootstrap-variance intervals undercover slightly ($0.87$–$0.88$) across the board — the bootstrap variance is automatic and avoids density estimation, but inherits the $z$-cutoff optimism seen in Table 10.4.8. Neither is perfect; the median interval is the more robust of the two.
