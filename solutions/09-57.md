**Central idea.** Prediction adds the future observation variance. Tolerance requires a separate population-content parameter $p$, not $p=1-\alpha$.

**Solution.** (a) A future observation is independent of the sample, and $X_{n+1}-\bar X\sim N(0,\sigma^2(1+1/n))$. Thus the prediction interval is $\bar X\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sigma\sqrt{1+1/n}$, with upper-tail quantile notation.
(b) The displayed statement cannot hold for arbitrary $p$ because its width does not depend on $p$. A valid conservative replacement is
$$\bar X\pm\sigma\left[z_{(1-p)/2}+\frac{z_{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt n}\right].$$
The fixed interval $[\mu-\sigma z_{(1-p)/2},\mu+\sigma z_{(1-p)/2}]$ contains population probability $p$. On the event $|\bar X-\mu|\le\sigma z_{\alpha/2}/\sqrt n$, which has probability $1-\alpha$, the random interval contains that fixed interval. Hence with confidence at least $1-\alpha$ it contains at least proportion $p$ of the population. The earlier answer solved only the special case $p=1-\alpha$. The first-printing errata print $z_{p/2}$; with the statement's content convention $p$, the required cutoff is $z_{(1-p)/2}$.
(c) For unknown variance, $(X_{n+1}-\bar X)/(S\sqrt{1+1/n})\sim t_{n-1}$, giving $\bar X\pm t_{n-1,\alpha/2}S\sqrt{1+1/n}$.
