**Central idea.** The parameter space is truncated at $1/2$: MOM ignores the boundary ($\bar X$), while the MLE truncates ($\min(\bar X,1/2)$) and therefore dominates in MSE.
**Solution.** (a) $E_\theta X_1=\theta$, so the MOM estimator is $\tilde\theta=\bar X$. The unrestricted maximizer of the Bernoulli likelihood is $\bar X$; constrained to $[0,1/2]$, the MLE is $\hat\theta=\min(\bar X,1/2)$ (if $\bar X>1/2$ the likelihood is increasing on $[0,1/2]$).
(b) $\mathrm{MSE}(\tilde\theta)=\theta(1-\theta)/n$. Writing $D = (\bar X-1/2)_+ \ge 0$ so $\hat\theta = \bar X - D$,
$$\mathrm{MSE}(\hat\theta)=\mathrm{MSE}(\tilde\theta)-2E[(\bar X-\theta)D]+E[D^2]\le\mathrm{MSE}(\tilde\theta),$$
since on $\{D > 0\}$ (i.e. $\bar X > 1/2$), $2(\bar X-\theta)-D = \bar X-2\theta+1/2 \ge 1-2\theta \ge 0$ (as $\theta \le 1/2$), so $2E[(\bar X-\theta)D]-E[D^2] = E[(2(\bar X-\theta)-D)D] \ge 0$. Strict unless $P_\theta(\bar X > 1/2) = 0$. For $\theta < 1/2$ the correction vanishes as $n\to\infty$ ($P_\theta(\bar X>1/2)\to0$ exponentially), so both MSEs are $\theta(1-\theta)/n+o(1/n)$.
(c) The MLE is preferred: it has uniformly smaller MSE and always lies in the parameter space, whereas $\tilde\theta$ can exceed $1/2$.
