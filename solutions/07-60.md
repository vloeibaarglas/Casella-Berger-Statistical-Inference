**Central idea.** Use completeness of $S=\sum X_i\sim\mathrm{gamma}(k,\beta)$, $k=n\alpha$. Existence of the inverse moment requires $k>1$, and finite variance requires $k>2$.

**Solution.** With gamma scale $\beta$, $E(S^{-1})=1/[\beta(k-1)]$ for $k>1$, giving the unique unbiased function of the complete sufficient statistic
$$\delta(S)=\frac{k-1}{S}.$$
For $k>2$, $E(S^{-2})=1/[\beta^2(k-1)(k-2)]$, hence
$$\operatorname{Var}_\beta\delta=\frac{1}{\beta^2(k-2)}.$$
Lehmann–Scheffé gives the finite-variance UMVUE in this regime. For $1<k\le2$, $\delta$ is integrable and unbiased but has infinite variance; there is no finite-variance unbiased estimator, since Rao–Blackwellization would otherwise give a finite-variance unbiased function of $S$, contradicting completeness.

For $k\le1$ there is no integrable unbiased estimator. Indeed, if such an estimator existed, Rao–Blackwellization would yield an integrable $d(S)$ with, on writing $t=1/\beta$,
$$\int_0^\infty d(s)s^{k-1}e^{-ts}\,ds=\Gamma(k)t^{1-k}\quad(t>0).$$
The left side tends to zero as $t\to\infty$ by dominated convergence, using absolute integrability at any fixed $t_0>0$. The right side is constant and nonzero for $k=1$, and diverges for $k<1$. This is impossible. An unqualified claim of a finite-variance best unbiased estimator therefore needs the condition $n\alpha>2$.
