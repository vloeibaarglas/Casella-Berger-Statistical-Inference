**Central idea.** $X^\alpha \sim \text{exponential}(1)$ regardless of $\alpha$; taking logs turns the power $1/\alpha$ into a factor that cancels in the ratio.

**Solution.** For $X \sim f(\cdot\mid\alpha)$, $P(X^\alpha > t) = P(X > t^{1/\alpha}) = \int_{t^{1/\alpha}}^\infty \alpha x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x^\alpha}\,dx = e^{-t}$, so $Y_i = X_i^\alpha$ are iid $\text{exponential}(1)$, free of $\alpha$. Then $\log X_i = \frac{1}{\alpha}\log Y_i$, and
$$\frac{\log X_1}{\log X_2} = \frac{\log Y_1}{\log Y_2},$$
whose distribution does not depend on $\alpha$. Hence it is ancillary.
