**Central idea.** Bonferroni for (a); for (b) the independence of $\bar X$ and $S^2$ sharpens the bound to the exact product $(1-\alpha_1)(1-\alpha_2)$.

**Solution.** (a) Take $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = \alpha$ (e.g. $\alpha/2$ each). With $k = t_{n-1,\alpha_1/2}$, $a = \chi^2_{n-1,1-\alpha_2/2}$, $b = \chi^2_{n-1,\alpha_2/2}$ (upper-tail cutoffs), each set is $1-\alpha_i$, so $P(\text{both}) \ge 1-\alpha_1-\alpha_2 = 1-\alpha$ by Bonferroni.
(b) By independence of $\bar X$ and $S^2$,
$$P\left(|\bar X-\mu| \le \tfrac{k\sigma}{\sqrt n},\ a \le \tfrac{(n-1)S^2}{\sigma^2} \le b\right) = P(|Z| \le k)\,P(a \le \chi^2_{n-1} \le b) = (1-\alpha_1)(1-\alpha_2) \ge 1-\alpha,$$
with $k = z_{\alpha_1/2}$ and $a,b$ as in (a).
(c) (a) is a rectangle — simpler, but conservative (Bonferroni) with wider $t$-based $\mu$-slices. (b) has exact product coverage and narrower $z$-based $\mu$-slices, but the $\mu$-bounds involve the unknown $\sigma$, so the joint region is $\sigma$-dependent (trapezoidal), not a product of fixed intervals.
