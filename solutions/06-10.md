**Central idea.** The range $X_{(n)}-X_{(1)}$ has a $\theta$-free mean, so centering it gives a nonzero unbiased estimator of zero.

**Solution.** By Example 6.2.15, $T = (X_{(1)},X_{(n)})$ is minimal sufficient. For $X_i$ iid $\text{uniform}(\theta,\theta+1)$, $\mathrm{E}_\theta X_{(1)} = \theta + \frac{1}{n+1}$ and $\mathrm{E}_\theta X_{(n)} = \theta + \frac{n}{n+1}$, so $\mathrm{E}_\theta[X_{(n)}-X_{(1)}] = \frac{n-1}{n+1}$ for all $\theta$. Hence $g(X_{(1)},X_{(n)}) = X_{(n)}-X_{(1)}-\frac{n-1}{n+1}$ is not identically zero but satisfies $\mathrm{E}_\theta g = 0$ for all $\theta$: $T$ is not complete. (For $n = 1$ the witness is vacuous since $X_{(1)} = X_{(n)}$ a.s.; the argument needs $n \ge 2$.)
