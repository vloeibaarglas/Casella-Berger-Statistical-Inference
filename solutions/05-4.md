**Central idea.** Integrating out the random $P$ gives a joint pmf depending on $(x_1, \ldots, x_k)$ only through the sum $t$ — hence permutation-invariant — but the $X_i$ remain positively correlated through the shared $P$.

**Solution.** (a) Conditioning on $P = p$ and integrating over the uniform prior (density 1 on $(0,1)$):
$$P(X_1 = x_1, \ldots, X_k = x_k) = \int_0^1 \prod_{i=1}^{k} p^{x_i}(1-p)^{1-x_i} \, dp = \int_0^1 p^t(1-p)^{k-t} \, dp,$$
with $t = \sum x_i$. This is the Beta integral $B(t+1, k-t+1) = \frac{\Gamma(t+1)\Gamma(k-t+1)}{\Gamma(k+2)} = \frac{t!(k-t)!}{(k+1)!}$. The right side depends on $(x_1, \ldots, x_k)$ only through $t$, hence is unchanged by permuting the coordinates; the same holds for any $k$-subset. So the $X$s are exchangeable.
(b) Each marginal is $P(X_i = 1) = \int_0^1 p \, dp = 1/2$, so $\prod_{i=1}^{n} P(X_i = x_i) = 2^{-n}$. But the joint is $t!(n-t)!/(n+1)!$ with $t = \sum x_i$; e.g., for $n = 2$, $x_1 = x_2 = 1$: joint $= 1/3 \neq 1/4$. Hence not independent (they are positively correlated: a large $P$ makes all $X_i$ simultaneously likely to be 1).
