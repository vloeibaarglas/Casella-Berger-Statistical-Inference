**Central idea.** $\mathrm{E}_p[g(X)] = 0$ is a polynomial in $p$; it vanishes on an interval iff every coefficient vanishes — count the free coefficients against the three values of $g$.

**Solution.** Distribution 1: $\mathrm{E}_p[g(X)] = g(0)p + 3g(1)p + g(2)(1-4p) = g(2) + p\,[g(0)+3g(1)-4g(2)] \equiv 0$ on $(0,\frac14)$ gives $g(2) = 0$ and $g(0)+3g(1) = 0$, with nonzero solutions (e.g. $g(0) = 3$, $g(1) = -1$, $g(2) = 0$). Not complete. Distribution 2: $\mathrm{E}_p[g(X)] = g(2) + p\,[g(0)-g(2)] + p^2\,[g(1)-g(2)] \equiv 0$ on $(0,\frac12)$ forces $g(2) = 0$, $g(0) = g(2)$, $g(1) = g(2)$, i.e. $g \equiv 0$. Complete.
