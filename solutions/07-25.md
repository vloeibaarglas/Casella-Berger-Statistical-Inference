**Central idea.** The pairs $(X_i,\theta_i)$ are iid across $i$; marginalizing an iid sequence coordinate-wise preserves iid-ness, and the normal case is a convolution.
**Solution.** (a) $X_i=\theta_i+(X_i-\theta_i)$ with $\theta_i\sim\mathrm{n}(\mu,\tau^2)$ independent of $X_i-\theta_i\sim\mathrm{n}(0,\sigma^2)$, so $X_i\sim\mathrm{n}(\mu,\sigma^2+\tau^2)$ marginally; since the pairs $(X_i,\theta_i)$ are independent across $i$ with a common joint law, the marginals $X_i$ are iid.
(b) The vectors $(X_i,\theta_i)$ are iid across $i$ (independent pairs, each with joint density $f(x\mid\theta)\pi(\theta\mid\tau)$); hence their $X_i$-marginals, $m(x\mid\tau)=\int f(x\mid\theta)\pi(\theta\mid\tau)\,d\theta$, are iid.
