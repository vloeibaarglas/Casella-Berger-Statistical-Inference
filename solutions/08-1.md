**Central idea.** A large-sample $z$-test of $H_0: \theta = 1/2$: the observed $z \approx 3.8$ gives a two-sided $p$-value of about $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$, far below any conventional level.

**Solution.** Let $X \sim \text{binomial}(1000, \theta)$ count heads and test $H_0: \theta = 1/2$. Under $H_0$, $E X = 500$, $\text{sd}(X) = \sqrt{250} \approx 15.81$, so
$$z = \frac{560 - 500}{\sqrt{250}} \approx 3.79.$$
The (normal-approximated) two-sided $p$-value is $2\Phi(-3.79) \approx 0.00015$. No — it is not reasonable to assume fairness; the data reject $H_0$ at any conventional $\alpha$ (the deviation is nearly $4$ standard errors).
