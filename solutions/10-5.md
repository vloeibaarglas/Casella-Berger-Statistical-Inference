**Central idea.** $\bar X_n$ has a density bounded away from zero near $0$, so $E[1/\bar X_n^2]=\infty$; truncating away from $0$ bounds $T_n$, and for $\delta<|\mu|$ the bad region has vanishing probability since $\bar X_n \xrightarrow{p}\mu$.

**Solution.** Here $X_i$ are iid $n(\mu,\sigma^2)$, so $\bar X_n$ has a continuous density $f_n$ with $f_n(0)>0$.
(a) $E[T_n^2] = nE[1/\bar X_n^2] \ge n\min_{|x|\le\delta}f_n(x)\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}x^{-2}dx = \infty$ for any $\delta>0$. Hence $\mathrm{Var}(T_n)=\infty$.
(b) Deleting $(-\delta,\delta)$ forces $|\bar X_n|\ge\delta$ a.s., so $T_n^2 = n/\bar X_n^2 \le n/\delta^2$ is bounded; a bounded variable has finite variance.
(c) For fixed $\delta<|\mu|$, $\{|\bar X_n|<\delta\}\subseteq\{|\bar X_n-\mu|>|\mu|-\delta\}$, and $P(|\bar X_n-\theta|>|\mu|-\delta)\to 0$ since $\bar X_n\xrightarrow{p}\mu$. Thus $P(\bar X_n\in(-\delta,\delta))\to 0$: the region driving the infinite variance has vanishing probability. (For $\delta\ge|\mu|$ this fails — the claim is about a small neighborhood of $0$.)
