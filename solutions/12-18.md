**Central idea.** Grouped binomial likelihood with a quadratic logit; the LRT for $\gamma=0$ is $2(\ell_{\text{quad}}-\ell_{\text{lin}})\sim\chi^2_1$. The survival proportions rise then fall, which a linear logit cannot capture.

**Solution.** (a) With $n_j$ birds and $y^*_j$ survivors at age $x_j$, $\pi_j=e^{\alpha+\beta x_j+\gamma x_j^2}/(1+e^{\alpha+\beta x_j+\gamma x_j^2})$,
$$L(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\prod_j\pi_j^{y^*_j}(1-\pi_j)^{n_j-y^*_j}.$$
Since $f_j/(F_j(1-F_j))=1$ for the logistic link, the score equations are $\sum_j(y^*_j-n_j\pi_j)(1,x_j,x_j^2)'=0$, solved by Newton–Raphson (strictly concave log-likelihood).

(b) Let $(\tilde\alpha,\tilde\beta)$ be the MLEs under $\gamma=0$ and $(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta,\hat\gamma)$ the full MLEs. The LRT statistic $-2\log\lambda=2[\log L(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta,\hat\gamma)-\log L(\tilde\alpha,\tilde\beta,0)]$ is approximately $\chi^2_1$ under $H_0$; reject $H_0$ if it exceeds $\chi^2_{1,\alpha}$.

(c) Linear fit: $\tilde\alpha=0.480$, $\tilde\beta=0.0074$, $\log L=-457.57$. Quadratic fit: $\hat\alpha=-0.845$, $\hat\beta=0.837$, $\hat\gamma=-0.103$, $\log L=-445.89$ (Newton–Raphson, gradients $<10^{-12}$). The quadratic captures the hump: fitted survival rises to $\approx 0.70$ near age 4 then declines, matching the observed proportions $(.455,.597,.714,.669,.667,.609,.519,.300,.250)$.

(d) $-2\log\lambda=2(-445.89+457.57)=23.36\gg\chi^2_{1,.05}=3.84$ ($p\approx 10^{-6}$): reject $H_0$. The quadratic model is decisively better — survival first increases then decreases with age, which the linear logit (with its near-zero slope) cannot represent.
