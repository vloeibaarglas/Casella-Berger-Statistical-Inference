**Central idea.** Check the three defining properties directly; for (c), a set in the intersection inherits each property from both algebras.

**Solution.** (a) $S \in \mathcal{B}$; complements: $\emptyset^c = S$, $S^c = \emptyset$; countable unions of $\emptyset$s and $S$s are $\emptyset$ or $S$. (b) The power set: $S$ is a subset of itself; complements and arbitrary unions of subsets are subsets. (c) Let $\mathcal{B}_1, \mathcal{B}_2$ be sigma algebras and $\mathcal{B} = \mathcal{B}_1 \cap \mathcal{B}_2$. Then $S \in \mathcal{B}$; if $A \in \mathcal{B}$ then $A^c \in \mathcal{B}_1$ and $A^c \in \mathcal{B}_2$, so $A^c \in \mathcal{B}$; if $A_i \in \mathcal{B}$ for all $i$, then $\cup_i A_i$ is in each $\mathcal{B}_j$, hence in $\mathcal{B}$.
