**Central idea.** For Huber scores, average derivatives estimate the central probability; the expected score can be differentiated by moving the distribution while holding its argument fixed.

**Solution.** (a) $\psi'(u)=1\{|u|<k\}$ almost everywhere, so $A=E\psi'(\epsilon)=P(|\epsilon|<k)$. For symmetric errors,
$$B=E\psi(\epsilon)^2=\int_{-k}^k u^2f(u)\,du+2k^2\int_k^\infty f(u)\,du.$$
The asymptotic variance of $\sqrt n(\hat\theta_M-\theta)$ is $B/A^2$; the approximate variance of $\hat\theta_M$ itself is $B/(nA^2)$.
(b) The inspected textbook already gives the correct moving-distribution derivative. Write $X=\theta_0+\epsilon$ under $P_{\theta_0}$, and hold the argument parameter $\theta$ fixed:
$$\left.\frac{d}{d\theta_0}E_{\theta_0}\psi(X-\theta)\right|_{\theta_0=\theta}=\left.\frac{d}{d\theta_0}E\psi(\epsilon+\theta_0-\theta)\right|_{\theta_0=\theta}=E\psi'(\epsilon).$$
For Huber $\psi$, dominated convergence applies since $\psi$ is Lipschitz and the continuous error law assigns zero probability to its two kinks. Differentiating $E_\theta\psi(X-\theta_0)$ instead gives $-A$; moving both parameters gives zero.
