**Central idea.** (a) Continuous mapping for constant limits: $\{|h(X_n)-h(x)| > \varepsilon\} \subseteq \{|X_n - x| \geq \delta\}$. (b) The "sliding hump" $Y_n \to_p 0$ but not a.s.; freezing the hump at the left edge gives $Y_{m,1} = \mathbf{1}_{[0,2^{-m})} \to 0$ pointwise.

**Solution.** (a) (Theorem 5.5.4: if $X_n \to_p x$, constant, and $h$ is continuous at $x$, then $h(X_n) \to_p h(x)$.) Given $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $\delta > 0$ with $|x_n - x| < \delta \implies |h(x_n)-h(x)| < \varepsilon$. Then $P(|h(X_n)-h(x)| > \varepsilon) \leq P(|X_n - x| \geq \delta) \to 0$.
(b) (Example 5.5.8 is the "typewriter"/sliding-hump sequence: $Y_{m,k} = \mathbf{1}_{[(k-1)/2^m,\, k/2^m)}$ on $(0,1)$, $k = 1,\ldots,2^m$, $m = 1,2,\ldots$, so $Y_n \to_p 0$ but $Y_n(\omega) = 1$ infinitely often for every $\omega$.) Take the subsequence $Y_{m,1} = \mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2^m)}$: for each fixed $\omega > 0$, $Y_{m,1}(\omega) = 0$ for all large $m$, so $Y_{m,1} \to 0$ pointwise (hence a.s.).
