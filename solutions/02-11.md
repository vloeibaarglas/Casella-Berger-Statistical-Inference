**Central idea.** Integration by parts kills the boundary term for $EX^2$; $|X|$ folds the normal in half (half-normal).

**Solution.** (a) Directly,
$$EX^2=\int_{-\infty}^\infty x^2\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}dx=\left[-x\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}\right]_{-\infty}^\infty+\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}dx=0+1=1.$$
From Example 2.1.7, $f_Y(y)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt y}(f_X(\sqrt y)+f_X(-\sqrt y))=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi y}}e^{-y/2}$, $y>0$, so $EY=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_0^\infty\sqrt y\,e^{-y/2}dy$; with $t=y/2$ this equals $\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\Gamma(\tfrac32)=1$.
(b) For $y>0$, $F_Y(y)=P(-y\le X\le y)=2\Phi(y)-1$, so $f_Y(y)=2\phi(y)=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,e^{-y^2/2}$, $y>0$. Then $E|X|=\sqrt{2/\pi}\int_0^\infty ye^{-y^2/2}dy=\sqrt{2/\pi}\big[-e^{-y^2/2}\big]_0^\infty=\sqrt{2/\pi}$, and $\mathrm{Var}|X|=EX^2-(E|X|)^2=1-2/\pi$.
