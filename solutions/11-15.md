**Central idea.** $t^2_{\nu,\alpha/2} = F_{1,\nu,\alpha}$, and the quantiles of $kF_{k,\nu}$ increase with $k$; Scheffé pays for simultaneity over all contrasts with a larger critical value.

**Solution.** (a) Since $t^2_\nu \stackrel{d}{=} F_{1,\nu}$, $t^2_{\nu,\alpha/2} = F_{1,\nu,\alpha} = 1\cdot F_{1,\nu,\alpha}$. The distributions $kF_{k,\nu}$ are stochastically increasing in $k$, so their upper-$\alpha$ quantiles increase in $k$: for $k - 1 \geq 1$, $(k-1)F_{k-1,\nu,\alpha} \geq 1\cdot F_{1,\nu,\alpha} = t^2_{\nu,\alpha/2}$. Taking square roots gives the inequality.
(b) The Scheffé interval for a contrast $\sum a_i\theta_i$ has half-width $\sqrt{(k-1)F_{k-1,N-k,\alpha}}\,S_p\sqrt{\sum a_i^2/n_i}$, while the single-contrast $t$ interval (from (11.2.7)) has half-width $t_{N-k,\alpha/2}\,S_p\sqrt{\sum a_i^2/n_i}$. By (a) with $\nu = N-k$, the Scheffé multiplier is $\geq$ the $t$ multiplier, so Scheffé intervals are always at least as wide.
(c) The Scheffé test rejects $H_0: \sum a_i\theta_i = 0$ when $|T| > \sqrt{(k-1)F_{k-1,N-k,\alpha}}$, the $t$ test when $|T| > t_{N-k,\alpha/2}$, for the same statistic $T$. Since the Scheffé cutoff is larger, whenever Scheffé rejects the $t$ test also rejects — the Scheffé rejection region is smaller, so its power is $\leq$ the $t$ test's power at every alternative.
