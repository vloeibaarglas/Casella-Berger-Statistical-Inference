**Central idea.** Tower property: conditional unbiasedness $E[\hat\beta\mid\mathbf{X}] = \beta$ survives averaging over $\mathbf{X}$; but $\mathrm{Cov}(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta\mid\mathbf{X}) = -\sigma^2\bar X/S_{xx}$ does not average to the fixed-$X$ value — for normal $X$, $\bar X \perp S_{xx}$ and $E[1/S_{xx}] = 1/[\sigma_X^2(n-3)]$.

**Solution.** (a) Conditionally on $\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{x}$, $\hat\alpha$ and $\hat\beta$ are the usual least squares estimators, hence unbiased: $E[\hat\alpha\mid\mathbf{X}] = \alpha$ and $E[\hat\beta\mid\mathbf{X}] = \beta$ for every $\mathbf{x}$ with $S_{xx} > 0$. By the tower property, $E\hat\alpha = E[E[\hat\alpha\mid\mathbf{X}]] = \alpha$ and $E\hat\beta = E[E[\hat\beta\mid\mathbf{X}]] = \beta$ — no assumption on the distribution of $\mathbf{X}$ is needed.
(b) $\mathrm{Cov}(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta) = E[\mathrm{Cov}(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta\mid\mathbf{X})] + \mathrm{Cov}(E[\hat\alpha\mid\mathbf{X}],E[\hat\beta\mid\mathbf{X}])$. The second term is $\mathrm{Cov}(\alpha,\beta) = 0$; conditionally, $\mathrm{Cov}(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta\mid\mathbf{X}) = -\sigma^2\bar X/S_{xx}$ (11.30(c)). For iid normal $X_i$, $\bar X$ is independent of $S_{xx}$, so $E[\bar X/S_{xx}] = E\bar X\cdot E[1/S_{xx}] = \mu_X\cdot\frac{1}{\sigma_X^2(n-3)}$, using $S_{xx}/\sigma_X^2 \sim \chi^2_{n-1}$ and $E[1/\chi^2_{n-1}] = 1/(n-3)$ for $n > 3$. Hence the unconditional covariance is $-\dfrac{\sigma^2\mu_X}{\sigma_X^2\,(n-3)}$.
