**Central idea.** Each is a smooth function of sample means, so the delta method applies; for (a) the cross terms cancel beautifully, leaving the familiar OLS variance $\sigma^2/(nE X^2)$.

**Solution.** Write each estimator as $g(\bar U,\bar V)$ and use $\mathrm{Var}\approx \nabla g^T\Sigma\nabla g/n$ with $\Sigma$ the one-observation covariance.
(a) $T_a = \overline{XY}/\overline{X^2}$, $E[XY]=\beta(\mu^2+\tau^2)$, $E[X^2]=\mu^2+\tau^2$: approximate mean $\beta$. With $U=XY$, $V=X^2$: $\mathrm{Var}(V)=2\tau^2(2\mu^2+\tau^2)$, $\mathrm{Var}(U)=\beta^2\mathrm{Var}(V)+(\mu^2+\tau^2)\sigma^2$, $\mathrm{Cov}(U,V)=\beta\,\mathrm{Var}(V)$ (using independence of $X,\varepsilon$). At $(\beta(\mu^2+\tau^2),\mu^2+\tau^2)$, $\nabla g=(1,-\beta)/(\mu^2+\tau^2)$, and
$$\nabla g^T\Sigma\nabla g = \frac{\mathrm{Var}(U)-2\beta\,\mathrm{Cov}(U,V)+\beta^2\mathrm{Var}(V)}{(\mu^2+\tau^2)^2} = \frac{(\mu^2+\tau^2)\sigma^2}{(\mu^2+\tau^2)^2},$$
so $\mathrm{Var}(T_a)\approx \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n(\mu^2+\tau^2)}$.
(b) $T_b = \bar Y/\bar X$: approximate mean $\beta\mu/\mu=\beta$. With $U=Y$, $V=X$: $\mathrm{Var}(U)=\beta^2\tau^2+\sigma^2$, $\mathrm{Var}(V)=\tau^2$, $\mathrm{Cov}(U,V)=\beta\tau^2$; $\nabla g=(1,-\beta)/\mu$ gives $\nabla g^T\Sigma\nabla g = \sigma^2/\mu^2$, so $\mathrm{Var}(T_b)\approx \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n\mu^2}$.
(c) $T_c = \frac1n\sum Y_i/X_i$ is a sample mean. Formally $E[Y_i/X_i]=\beta+E[\varepsilon_i/X_i]=\beta$ by independence ($E\varepsilon_i=0$), and the ratio-formula $E[Y/X]\approx \beta$ (second-order terms cancel), $\mathrm{Var}(Y/X)\approx \sigma^2/\mu^2$, so $\mathrm{Var}(T_c)\approx \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n\mu^2}$. *Caveat:* this is formal — for normal $X$, $E|1/X|=\infty$, so $Y/X$ has Cauchy-like tails, infinite variance, and the CLT does not strictly apply; the delta-method value should be read as a large-sample heuristic. The ranking is nonetheless clear: (a), the OLS estimator, has the smallest approximate variance.
