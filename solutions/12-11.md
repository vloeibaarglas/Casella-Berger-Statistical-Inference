**Central idea.** Bilinearity of covariance plus the structural moments makes the two linear combinations uncorrelated; for bivariate normals, uncorrelated means independent, so the ordinary sample-correlation $t$-statistic is pivotal for every $\beta$.

**Solution.** (a) Expand by bilinearity: $\mathrm{Cov}(aY+bX,cY+dX)=ac\,\mathrm{Var}\,Y+ad\,\mathrm{Cov}(Y,X)+bc\,\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)+bd\,\mathrm{Var}\,X$.

(b) With $a=\beta\lambda$, $b=1$, $c=1$, $d=-\beta$: $\mathrm{Cov}=\beta\lambda\,\mathrm{Var}\,Y_i+(1-\lambda\beta^2)\mathrm{Cov}(X_i,Y_i)-\beta\,\mathrm{Var}\,X_i$. Using (12.2.19), $\mathrm{Var}\,Y_i=\sigma^2_\epsilon+\beta^2\sigma^2_\xi$, $\mathrm{Cov}(X_i,Y_i)=\beta\sigma^2_\xi$, $\mathrm{Var}\,X_i=\lambda\sigma^2_\epsilon+\sigma^2_\xi$:
$$\beta\lambda(\sigma^2_\epsilon+\beta^2\sigma^2_\xi)+(1-\lambda\beta^2)\beta\sigma^2_\xi-\beta(\lambda\sigma^2_\epsilon+\sigma^2_\xi)=0,$$
all terms cancelling.

(c) $(\beta\lambda Y_i+X_i,\,Y_i-\beta X_i)$ are linear combinations of independent normals, hence bivariate normal; zero correlation implies independence. For iid pairs with zero true correlation, the sample correlation $r_\lambda(\beta)$ satisfies $\sqrt{n-2}\,r_\lambda(\beta)/\sqrt{1-r^2_\lambda(\beta)}\sim t_{n-2}$ (Exercise 11.33), for every $\beta$ — in particular at the true $\beta$. Hence
$$P\!\left(\beta\in C_\lambda(\hat\beta)\right)=P\!\left(\frac{(n-2)r^2_\lambda(\beta)}{1-r^2_\lambda(\beta)}\le F_{1,n-2,\alpha}\right)=1-\alpha,$$
constant in $\beta$ (here $t_{n-2}^2\sim F_{1,n-2}$).
