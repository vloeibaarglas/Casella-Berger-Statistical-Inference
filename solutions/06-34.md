**Central idea.** The random-$n$ factor $p_n$ is $\theta$-free, so both likelihoods are proportional to $\theta^x(1-\theta)^{n-x}$.

**Solution.** In Exercise 6.12, $L(\theta\mid(n,x)) = p_n\binom{n}{x}\theta^x(1-\theta)^{n-x} \propto \theta^x(1-\theta)^{n-x}$ as a function of $\theta$. In a fixed-$n$ $\text{binomial}(n,\theta)$ experiment, $L(\theta\mid x) = \binom{n}{x}\theta^x(1-\theta)^{n-x} \propto \theta^x(1-\theta)^{n-x}$. The two likelihoods are proportional, so by the Formal Likelihood Principle the evidence about $\theta$ is identical: the randomness of $N$ is irrelevant to inference about $\theta$.
