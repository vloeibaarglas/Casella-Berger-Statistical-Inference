**Central idea.** Sequential sampling without replacement: the joint probability is the ratio of favorable ordered selections to all ordered selections (a hypergeometric ratio); the "innocence probability" is then maximized numerically over $N+M = 496$.

**Solution.** (a) Choose the 4 tested packets from the $N$ cocaine packets and the 2 sold packets from the $M$ non-cocaine packets, out of all ways to draw 4 then 2 from $N+M = 496$ packets: $\binom{N}{4}\binom{M}{2}\big/\left[\binom{496}{4}\binom{492}{2}\right]$. (b) With $M = 496-N$ the denominator is constant, so maximize $g(N) = \binom{N}{4}\binom{496-N}{2}$. Numerically $g$ peaks at $N = 331$ ($M = 165$), giving $0.02208 \approx 0.022$ (neighbors: $N=330$ gives $0.022081$, $N=332$ gives $0.022080$ — 331 is the unique maximizer).
