**Central idea.** At the boundary the score equation degenerates, so graft on the exact Clopper–Pearson endpoint $(1-p)^n=\alpha/2$; truncating to $[0,1]$ only removes impossible values.

**Solution.** (a) When $y=0$ ($\hat p=0$) the Wald/score formulas degenerate. Inverting the exact test (Section 9.2.3): the set of $p$ with $P_p(Y=0)=(1-p)^n\ge\alpha/2$ is $p\le 1-(\alpha/2)^{1/n}$ — the exact one-sided upper limit. Similarly $y=n$ gives $P_p(Y=n)=p^n\ge\alpha/2$, i.e. $p\ge(\alpha/2)^{1/n}$. These are the Clopper–Pearson boundary endpoints, the sensible exact replacements.
(b) $p\in[0,1]$ by definition; any interval extending outside $[0,1]$ contains impossible values. Intersecting with $[0,1]$ strictly shortens such intervals and cannot reduce coverage (no true $p$ lies outside $[0,1]$) — an improvement on both length and coverage, complementing (a)'s boundary fix.
