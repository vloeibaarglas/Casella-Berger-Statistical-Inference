**Central idea.** Symmetric $\rho$ has odd derivative $\psi$; odd $\times$ even integrates to zero, so the true $\theta$ is the zero of $E_\theta\psi(X-\theta)$ that (10.2.6) centers on.

**Solution.** From $\rho(-u)=\rho(u)$, differentiating gives $-\psi(-u)=\psi(u)$, i.e. $\psi$ is odd. With $u=x-\theta$ and $f$ symmetric ($f(-u)=f(u)$),
$$\int \psi(x-\theta)f(x-\theta)\,dx = \int \psi(u)f(u)\,du = 0,$$
since $\psi(u)f(u)$ is odd. Thus $\theta_0=\theta$ (the true value) satisfies $E_{\theta_0}\psi(X-\theta_0)=0$ (uniquely, as $\psi$ is increasing), and (10.2.6) gives $\sqrt n(\hat\theta_M-\theta)\xrightarrow{d} n\!\left(0, E_\theta\psi^2/(E_\theta\psi')^2\right)$: asymptotically normal with mean $\theta$.
