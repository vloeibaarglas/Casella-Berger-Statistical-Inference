**Central idea.** Compute the exact two-sided tail from the cdf; Chebychev's $1/k^2$ is universal but loose — the exact probabilities are much smaller and hit 0.

**Solution.** Uniform$(0,1)$: $\mu_X=1/2$, $\sigma_X=1/\sqrt{12}$, so $P(|X-\mu_X|\ge k\sigma_X)=P(|X-1/2|\ge k/\sqrt{12})=\max(0,1-k/\sqrt3)$ (0 for $k\ge\sqrt3$). Exponential$(\lambda)$ (mean $\lambda$): $\mu_X=\sigma_X=\lambda$, so $P(|X-\lambda|\ge k\lambda)=P(X\ge(1+k)\lambda)+P(X\le(1-k)\lambda)=e^{-(1+k)}+\max(0,1-e^{-(1-k)})$ (the second term is 0 for $k\ge1$). Versus Chebychev's $1/k^2$: e.g. at $k=2$: uniform $0$, exponential $e^{-3}\approx0.050$, bound $0.25$; at $k=1$: uniform $1-1/\sqrt3\approx0.423$, exponential $e^{-2}\approx0.135$, bound $1$. The bound is valid but very conservative.
