**Central idea.** Each risk is a parabola $(1-\eta)^2+\eta^2\mu^2$; the concentrated prior wins near the prior mean but loses in the tails, crossing at $|\mu|\approx1.54$.
**Solution.** Here $n=\sigma^2=1$. For $\pi=\mathrm{n}(0,1)$: $\eta=1/2$, $\delta^\pi=X/2$, $R(\mu,\delta^\pi)=\frac{1+\mu^2}{4}$. For $\pi=\mathrm{n}(0,10)$: $\eta=1/11$, $\delta^\pi=\frac{10}{11}X$, $R(\mu,\delta^\pi)=\frac{100+\mu^2}{121}$. Both graphs are upward parabolas with minima at $\mu=0$ (values $0.25$ and $\approx0.826$); they cross where $\frac{1+\mu^2}{4}=\frac{100+\mu^2}{121}$, i.e. $|\mu|=\sqrt{279/117}\approx1.544$. Comment: the concentrated $\mathrm{n}(0,1)$ prior shrinks harder toward $0$, giving much smaller risk when $\mu$ is near $0$ but larger risk when $|\mu|\gtrsim1.54$; the diffuse $\mathrm{n}(0,10)$ prior is more robust to prior misspecification. Stronger prior concentration helps exactly when the prior is "right".
