**Central idea.** For the Poisson, the score is $n(\bar X-\lambda)/\lambda$ and the expected information is $n/\lambda$; their ratio is exactly $(\bar X-\lambda)/\sqrt{\lambda/n}$ — i.e. this statistic *is* Wilks' $Q$ in (10.4.3).

**Solution.** $\log L(\lambda\mid x) = n\bar x\log\lambda-n\lambda-\sum\log(x_i!)$. Then $\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\log L = \frac{n\bar x}{\lambda}-n = \frac{n(\bar x-\lambda)}{\lambda}$ and $\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\lambda^2}\log L = -\frac{n\bar x}{\lambda^2}$, so $-E_\lambda[\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\lambda^2}\log L] = \frac{n\lambda}{\lambda^2} = \frac{n}{\lambda}$. Therefore
$$\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\log L(\lambda\mid\mathbf X)}{\sqrt{-E_\lambda\left(\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\lambda^2}\log L(\lambda\mid\mathbf X)\right)}} = \frac{n(\bar X-\lambda)/\lambda}{\sqrt{n/\lambda}} = \frac{\bar X-\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda/n}},$$
which is Wilks' statistic $Q$ of (10.4.3) — the score interval, asymptotically optimal (shortest) by Wilks (1938).
