**Central idea.** Independence across treatment groups kills all cross terms; only $\mathrm{Var}(\bar{Y}_{i\cdot}) = \sigma^2/n_i$ survives.

**Solution.** $\mathrm{Cov}(\sum_i a_i\bar{Y}_{i\cdot}, \sum_j b_j\bar{Y}_{j\cdot}) = \sum_{i,j} a_ib_j\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{Y}_{i\cdot},\bar{Y}_{j\cdot})$. By independence across $i$, $\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{Y}_{i\cdot},\bar{Y}_{j\cdot}) = 0$ for $i \neq j$ and $= \sigma^2/n_i$ for $i = j$. Hence the covariance is $\sigma^2\sum_i a_ib_i/n_i$, which is 0 iff $\sum a_ib_i/n_i = 0$.
