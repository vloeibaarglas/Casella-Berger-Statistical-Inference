**Central idea.** A pool is positive iff at least one member is positive; each pool costs $1$ test plus $k$ more with probability $1-(1-p)^k$; as $p\to0$ the per-person cost under (ii) tends to $1/k<1$.

**Solution.** (a) $1-(1-p)^k$ (one minus the probability all $k$ are negative).
(b) Per pool, the number of tests is $1$ with probability $(1-p)^k$ and $k+1$ otherwise: expected $1+k(1-(1-p)^k)$. With $m = N/k$ pools, $\mathrm{E}X = m[1+k(1-(1-p)^k)] = N\left[\frac1k+1-(1-p)^k\right]$.
(c) Plan (ii). As $p\to0$, $\mathrm{E}X/N = 1/k+1-(1-p)^k \to 1/k < 1 = \mathrm{E}X_{(i)}/N$: pooling uses about $1/k$ as many tests when positives are rare.
