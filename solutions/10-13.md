**Central idea.** Averages over all $n^n$ resamples inherit the empirical distribution's moments by symmetry; the only subtlety is the $n^n$ vs.\ $n^n-1$ divisor.

**Solution.** (a)(b) $\bar x = (2+4+9+12)/4 = 6.75$. Each resample mean $\bar x_i^*$ averages $n$ iid draws from the empirical distribution, so $E^*[\bar x^*]=\bar x$; averaging over all $4^4=256$ resamples gives $\bar{\bar x}^*= 6.75$. For the variance, $\mathrm{Var}^*(\bar X^*) = \hat\sigma^2/n$ with $\hat\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2 = 62.75/4 = 15.6875$, i.e. $3.921875$; with the sample-variance divisor $n^n-1=255$ this becomes $\frac{256}{255}\times 3.921875 = 3.937\approx 3.94$.
(c) The population version of the bootstrap variance is $\hat\sigma^2/n = \big[\frac1n\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2\big]/n$, exactly the usual estimate of $\mathrm{Var}(\bar X)$ when $\sigma^2$ is estimated with divisor $n$ (the MLE) rather than $n-1$.
(d) List the 35 distinct multisets of size 4 from $\{2,4,9,12\}$, each weighted by its multinomial multiplicity $\frac{4!}{m_1!m_2!m_3!m_4!}$ (e.g. $\{2,2,2,4\}$ has weight 4 and mean 2.5; $\{2,4,9,12\}$ has weight 24 and mean 6.75). Then $\bar{\bar x}^*= \frac{1}{256}\sum w_j\bar x_j^*= 6.75$ and $\mathrm{Var}^*= \frac{1}{255}\sum w_j(\bar x_j^*-\bar{\bar x}^*)^2 = 3.94$, using weights $w_j$ summing to 256.
(e) For general $X_1,\ldots,X_n$: $E^*[X_1^*]=\bar X$, so $E^*[\bar X^*]=\bar X$ (part (b)); $\mathrm{Var}^*(\bar X^*) = \frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Var}^*(X_1^*) = \frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2$ (part (c)).
