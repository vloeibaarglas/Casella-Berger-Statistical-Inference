**Central idea.** $\hat\theta=\bar Y/\bar X$; Example 5.5.27's delta-method variance plus the CLT gives a Wald interval with plug-in moments.

**Solution.** Estimate $\theta$ by $\hat\theta=\bar Y/\bar X$. From Example 5.5.27 (delta method for a ratio),
$$\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)\approx \frac{1}{n}\left[\frac{\sigma_Y^2}{\mu_X^2}+\frac{\mu_Y^2\sigma_X^2}{\mu_X^4}-2\frac{\mu_Y\rho\sigma_X\sigma_Y}{\mu_X^3}\right] = \frac{\sigma_Y^2+\theta^2\sigma_X^2-2\theta\rho\sigma_X\sigma_Y}{n\mu_X^2},$$
and by the multivariate CLT/delta method, $(\hat\theta-\theta)/\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta)}\xrightarrow{d}n(0,1)$. Substituting sample moments gives the approximate $1-\alpha$ interval
$$\hat\theta \pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{S_Y^2+\hat\theta^2S_X^2-2\hat\theta\,rS_XS_Y}{n\bar X^2}},$$
with $S_X^2,S_Y^2$ the sample variances and $r$ the sample correlation. (This is the Wald/delta-method analogue of Fieller's exact set; unlike Fieller's it is always a bounded interval, at the cost of the normal approximation.)
