**Central idea.** $E[\text{length}] = 2z_{\alpha/2}\sigma/\sqrt n$ versus $2t_{n-1,\alpha/2}E[S]/\sqrt n$; the $t$ quantile exceeds $z$ by more than $E[S]$ falls short of $\sigma$.

**Solution.** (a) $E[L] = 2z_{\alpha/2}\sigma/\sqrt n$ (nonrandom). (b) $E[L] = 2t_{n-1,\alpha/2}E[S]/\sqrt n$ with $E[S] = \sigma\sqrt{2/(n-1)}\,\Gamma(n/2)/\Gamma((n-1)/2)$. The ratio (b)/(a) is $\frac{t_{n-1,\alpha/2}}{z_{\alpha/2}}\cdot\frac{E[S]}{\sigma} > 1$, decreasing to $1$ as $n\to\infty$ (e.g. $5.17, 1.95, 1.33, 1.12, 1.03$ for $n = 2,3,5,10,30$ at $\alpha=.05$): not knowing $\sigma^2$ always costs expected length.
