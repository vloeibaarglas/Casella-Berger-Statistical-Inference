**Central idea.** $\{Z=0\}=\{X_2=1\}$ is the same observation, but the two likelihoods differ by a Jacobian factor — the same null-event equality, but different observed statistics and limiting neighborhoods.
**Solution.** (a) $L(\theta\mid x_2)=\theta^{-1}e^{-x_2/\theta}$ is maximized at $\hat\theta=x_2$.
(b) With $x_2=1+zx_1$, $|\partial(x_1,x_2)/\partial(x_1,z)|=x_1$, so $f_{X_1,Z}(x_1,z)=\theta^{-2}x_1\exp\{-(x_1(1+z)+1)/\theta\}$ on $x_1>0$, $1+zx_1>0$. For $z\ge 0$, $L(\theta\mid z)=\int_0^\infty\theta^{-2}x_1e^{-(x_1(1+z)+1)/\theta}dx_1=e^{-1/\theta}/(1+z)^2$.
For $z<0$ let $L=-1/z$ and $a=1+z$. The support requires $0<x_1<L$, and the remaining likelihood is
$$f_Z(z\mid\theta)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{e^{-1/\theta}}{a^2}\left[1-\left(1+\frac{aL}{\theta}\right)e^{-aL/\theta}\right],&z<0,\ z\ne-1,\\[5pt]\displaystyle\frac{e^{-1/\theta}}{2\theta^2},&z=-1.\end{cases}$$
This follows by integrating $x_1e^{-a x_1/\theta}$ on the finite interval and completes the negative-support case, including the continuous limit at $z=-1$.
(c) At $X_2=1$ (i.e. $Z=0$): (a) gives $L\propto\theta^{-1}e^{-1/\theta}$, $\hat\theta=1$; (b) gives $L\propto e^{-1/\theta}$, which is strictly increasing in $\theta$ — no finite MLE (supremum at $\theta=\infty$). The equality of these probability-zero events does not make the observed statistics informationally equivalent; their likelihoods and limiting neighborhoods differ, illustrating the Borel phenomenon.
(d) The posteriors are $\pi(\theta\mid x_2=1)\propto\pi(\theta)\theta^{-1}e^{-1/\theta}$ versus $\pi(\theta\mid z=0)\propto\pi(\theta)e^{-1/\theta}$ — different posteriors from conditioning on different observed statistics at corresponding null-event values.
