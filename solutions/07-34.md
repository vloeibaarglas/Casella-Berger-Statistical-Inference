**Central idea.** The flip group forces $\delta(x)+\delta(n-x)=1$; among $\hat p_B=(x+\alpha)/(n+\alpha+\beta)$ this singles out $\alpha=\beta$, and the minimax choice is the constant-risk prior of Exercise 7.33.
**Solution.** (a) Reading the problem as a single observation $X\sim\mathrm{binomial}(n,p)$ — the reading parts (b)–(c) require, since the Bayes class there is Example 7.2.14's, indexed by $n$ — Example 6.4.1's group (the flip $x\leftrightarrow n-x$, $p\leftrightarrow 1-p$) gives the equivariant class $\{\delta:\delta(x)=1-\delta(n-x),\ x=0,\dots,n\}$ by (6.4.1).
(b) $\hat p_B(x)=(x+\alpha)/(n+\alpha+\beta)$ satisfies $\hat p_B(x)=1-\hat p_B(n-x)$ iff $(x+\alpha)=(x+\beta)$, i.e. iff $\alpha=\beta$. The equivariant Bayes estimators are $\delta_\alpha(x)=(x+\alpha)/(n+2\alpha)$, $\alpha>0$.
(c) *"Smallest MSE" is read as minimax, since $\mathrm{MSE}(\delta_\alpha)$ depends on $p$.* $\mathrm{MSE}(\delta_\alpha,p)=\frac{np(1-p)+\alpha^2(1-2p)^2}{(n+2\alpha)^2}$, whose maximum over $p\in[0,1]$ is $\frac{\max(n/4,\alpha^2)}{(n+2\alpha)^2}$ (the numerator is $\alpha^2+(n-4\alpha^2)p(1-p)$, maximized at $p(1-p)=1/4$ or $0$). This is minimized over $\alpha>0$ at $\alpha=\sqrt{n}/2=\sqrt{n/4}$ (decreasing for $\alpha<\sqrt{n}/2$, increasing after), giving the constant (minimax) MSE $\frac{n/4}{(n+\sqrt{n})^2}$ — the estimator of Exercise 7.33.
