**Central idea.** The joint density splits into $e^{n\mu}I(\mu < x_{(1)})$ times a $\mu$-free factor; completeness follows by differentiating the zero-expectation integral, and $S^2$ is location-invariant hence ancillary.

**Solution.** (a) $\prod_{i=1}^n e^{-(x_i-\mu)}I(x_i > \mu) = e^{n\mu}I(\mu < x_{(1)})\,e^{-\sum x_i}$: $X_{(1)}$ sufficient by factorization. Moreover $X_{(1)} = \mu + Z$ with $Z\sim\text{exponential}(n)$ ($P(Z > t) = e^{-nt}$). If $\mathrm{E}_\mu[g(X_{(1)})] = 0$ for all $\mu$, then $\int_\mu^\infty g(u)ne^{-n(u-\mu)}\,du = 0$, i.e. $H(\mu) = \int_\mu^\infty g(u)e^{-nu}\,du \equiv 0$; differentiating, $H'(\mu) = -g(\mu)e^{-n\mu} = 0$ a.e., so $g = 0$ a.e.: $X_{(1)}$ is complete.
(b) $S^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum (X_i-\bar{X})^2$ depends on the $X_i$ only through $X_i-\bar{X} = Z_i-\bar{Z}$, free of $\mu$: ancillary. By Basu's Theorem (complete sufficient $X_{(1)}$, ancillary $S^2$), they are independent.
