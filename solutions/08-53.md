**Central idea.** With $z = \sqrt{n}\bar x/\sigma$, the Bayes factor is $(1+n\tau^2/\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\exp\left(\frac{z^2}{2}\frac{n\tau^2/\sigma^2}{1+n\tau^2/\sigma^2}\right)$; at fixed $z$ the $(1+n)^{-1/2}$ penalty drives the posterior to $1$ as $n \to \infty$ — Lindley's paradox.

**Solution.** (a) $P(\theta = 0) + P(\theta \ne 0) = \frac12 + \frac12 \int n(0,\tau^2) = \frac12 + \frac12 = 1$: proper.
(b) With prior odds $1$, $P(\theta = 0 \mid \mathbf x) = (1+B)^{-1}$ where $B = \frac{\int f(\mathbf x \mid \theta)\,\frac12 n(\theta;0,\tau^2)d\theta}{\frac12 f(\mathbf x \mid 0)}$. Since $\sum(x_i-\theta)^2 - \sum x_i^2 = n(\bar x-\theta)^2 - n\bar x^2$, completing the square gives
$$B = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+n\tau^2/\sigma^2}}\exp\left(\frac{z^2}{2}\,\frac{n\tau^2/\sigma^2}{1+n\tau^2/\sigma^2}\right), \qquad z = \frac{\sqrt{n}\,\bar x}{\sigma},$$
so $P(\theta = 0 \mid \mathbf x) = \left[1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+n\tau^2/\sigma^2}}\exp\left(\frac{z^2}{2}\frac{n\tau^2/\sigma^2}{1+n\tau^2/\sigma^2}\right)\right]^{-1}$.
(c) Two-sided $z$-test: $p = 2(1-\Phi(|z|)) = 2\Phi(-|z|)$.
(d) With $\sigma^2 = \tau^2 = 1$: $P(\theta=0\mid\mathbf x) = \left[1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+n}}e^{\frac{z^2}{2}\frac{n}{n+1}}\right]^{-1}$, $z = \sqrt{n}\bar x$. i. For $n = 9$:

![p-value versus posterior probability $P(\theta=0\mid\mathbf{x})$ as functions of $\bar x$, $n=9$.]({{ '/plots/ex853_posterior.png' | relative_url }})

the posterior lies above the p-value for moderately large $\bar x$ (e.g. at $\bar x = 0.65$, $p \approx .05$ but posterior $\approx .35$). ii. At $\bar x = Z_{\alpha/2}/\sqrt{n}$ ($z = 1.96$, $p \equiv .05$): $B = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+n}}\exp\left(\frac{1.96^2}{2}\frac{n}{n+1}\right) \sim \frac{e^{1.92}}{\sqrt{n}} \to 0$, so $P(\theta = 0 \mid \mathbf x) = (1+B)^{-1} \to 1$ as $n \to \infty$: with p-value fixed at $.05$, the posterior increasingly favors $H_0$ — Lindley's paradox.
