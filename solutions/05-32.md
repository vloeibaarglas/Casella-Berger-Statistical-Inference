**Central idea.** Continuous mapping: $\sqrt{\cdot}$ is continuous on $(0,\infty)$ (and at $0$ from the right), $x \mapsto a/x$ is continuous at $a \neq 0$; then $S_n^2 \to_p \sigma^2$ (WLLN) gives $\sigma/S_n \to_p 1$.

**Solution.** (a) $Y_i = \sqrt{X_i} \to_p \sqrt{a}$: $\sqrt{\cdot}$ is continuous at every $a \geq 0$ (from the right at $0$), and $X_i > 0$ a.s., so this is the continuous mapping theorem (Theorem 5.5.4). For $Y_i' = a/X_i$: if $a \neq 0$, $x \mapsto a/x$ is continuous at $a$, so $Y_i' \to_p a/a = 1$; if $a = 0$, then $Y_i' \equiv 0 \to_p 0$. Either way $Y_i'$ converges in probability.
(b) By the WLLN applied to $(X_i-\mu)^2$, $S_n^2 \to_p \sigma^2$ (Example 5.5.18; assume $\sigma > 0$). By (a) with the square root, $S_n = \sqrt{S_n^2} \to_p \sigma$, and applying the reciprocal form again, $\sigma/S_n \to_p \sigma/\sigma = 1$.
