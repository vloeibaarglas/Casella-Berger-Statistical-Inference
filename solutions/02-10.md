**Central idea.** Couple the discrete variable to a uniform through its generalized inverse; then $F_X(X)\ge U$ pointwise.

**Solution.** Let $U\sim U(0,1)$ and $Q(u)=\inf\{x:F_X(x)\ge u\}$. Then $Q(U)$ has the law of $X$, and right-continuity gives $F_X(Q(u))\ge u$ for $0<u<1$. Hence
$$P(F_X(X)\le y)=P(F_X(Q(U))\le y)\le P(U\le y)=y.$$
For strictness, take an atom $x_0$ of mass $p>0$ and set $a=F_X(x_0-)$, $b=F_X(x_0)=a+p$. For $a<y<b$, $F_X(X)\le y$ implies $X<x_0$, so $P(F_X(X)\le y)\le a<y$. Taking complements proves (a).
The earlier supremum proof was invalid: for a Bernoulli variable with $P(X=0)=1/2$ and $y=3/4$, $\sup\{x:F_X(x)\le y\}=1$ but $F_X(1)=1>y$.
