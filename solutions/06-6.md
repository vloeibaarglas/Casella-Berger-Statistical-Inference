**Central idea.** The gamma density is a two-parameter exponential family with natural statistics $x$ and $\log x$.

**Solution.** With $f(x\mid\alpha,\beta) = x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x/\beta}/(\Gamma(\alpha)\beta^\alpha)$,
$$\prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i\mid\alpha,\beta) = \frac{\big(\prod_{i=1}^n x_i\big)^{\alpha-1} e^{-\sum x_i/\beta}}{[\Gamma(\alpha)]^n\beta^{n\alpha}} = g\!\left(\textstyle\prod X_i,\sum X_i \mid \alpha,\beta\right)\cdot 1.$$
Hence $\big(\prod_{i=1}^n X_i,\ \sum_{i=1}^n X_i\big)$ — equivalently $\big(\sum_{i=1}^n \log X_i,\ \sum_{i=1}^n X_i\big)$ — is a two-dimensional sufficient statistic for $(\alpha,\beta)$ (this is Theorem 6.2.10 with $t_1(x) = \log x$, $t_2(x) = x$).
