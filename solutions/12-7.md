**Central idea.** (a) The exponent is quadratic in $\xi_i$; completing the square reduces the integral to a normal normalizing constant times the minimized exponent, which collapses to the squared residual $(y_i-(\alpha+\beta x_i))^2$. (b) An improper prior produces a formal expression, not a genuine conditional distribution — and in a genuine structural model $E[Y\mid X]\ne\alpha+\beta X$.

**Solution.** (a) Write the exponent as $-\tfrac12[A\xi_i^2-2B\xi_i+C]$ with $A=1/\sigma^2_\delta+\beta^2/\sigma^2_\epsilon$, $B=x_i/\sigma^2_\delta+\beta(y_i-\alpha)/\sigma^2_\epsilon$, $C=x_i^2/\sigma^2_\delta+(y_i-\alpha)^2/\sigma^2_\epsilon$. Completing the square, the integral equals $\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_\delta\sigma_\epsilon}\sqrt{2\pi/A}\,\exp(B^2/(2A)-C/2)$. Since $\sqrt{2\pi/A}=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_\delta\sigma_\epsilon/\sqrt{\beta^2\sigma^2_\delta+\sigma^2_\epsilon}$, the prefactor is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{\beta^2\sigma^2_\delta+\sigma^2_\epsilon}}$. Moreover $C-B^2/A=\min_{\xi_i}\big[(x_i-\xi_i)^2/\sigma^2_\delta+(y_i-\alpha-\beta\xi_i)^2/\sigma^2_\epsilon\big]=(y_i-(\alpha+\beta x_i))^2/(\beta^2\sigma^2_\delta+\sigma^2_\epsilon)$ (the profile calculation of Section 12.2.3 with $\lambda=\sigma^2_\delta/\sigma^2_\epsilon$), giving the stated exponential.

(b) The calculation is purely formal: $\mathrm{uniform}(-\infty,\infty)$ is not a probability distribution, so the integrated "marginal" is not a legitimate marginal and certainly not the conditional distribution of $Y_i$ given $X_i$. In a genuine structural model with a proper $\xi_i$ distribution, $(X_i,Y_i)$ is bivariate normal (12.2.19) and $E[Y_i\mid X_i]=\alpha+\beta\xi+\frac{\beta\sigma^2_\xi}{\sigma^2_\delta+\sigma^2_\xi}(X_i-\xi)$ — the regression of $Y$ on $X$ is attenuated (slope $\beta\sigma^2_\xi/(\sigma^2_\delta+\sigma^2_\xi)\ne\beta$), not $\alpha+\beta X_i$. The formal integral also treats the random $X_i$ as a fixed design point. So nothing here justifies OLS; the resemblance to a normal linear-regression pdf is an artifact of the improper prior.
