**Central idea.** $R(C) - R(C') = E[I(s\ge K)(2bcs - P(|X-\mu|\le cs\mid s))] \ge (2bcK-1)P(s\ge K)$: when $s$ is large the length penalty dominates any possible coverage gain, so deleting the interval there *reduces* risk — the loss is at fault, not the $t$-interval.

**Solution.** $R(C) - R(C') = b\cdot2cE[S I(S\ge K)] - P(|X-\mu|\le cS,\ S\ge K) = E[I(S\ge K)(2bcS - P(|X-\mu|\le cS\mid S))]$. Since $P(|X-\mu|\le cS\mid S) \le 1$ and $S \ge K$ on the event,
$$R(C) - R(C') \ge (2bcK - 1)P(S \ge K) \ge 0,$$
with strict inequality for every $(\mu,\sigma)$ (as $P(S\ge K) > 0$) whenever $K > 1/(2bc)$, e.g. $K = 1/(bc)$. Thus the "do exactly the wrong thing" rule $C'$ dominates $C$ — an indictment of the loss function, which over-penalizes length when $s$ is large.
