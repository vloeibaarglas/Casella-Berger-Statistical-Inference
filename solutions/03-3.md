**Central idea.** "Wait exactly four seconds" means seconds 5, 6, 7 are the first three consecutive car-free seconds; this pins down disjoint blocks of seconds, and independence does the rest.

**Solution.** Let $p = P(\text{car in a given second})$. Waiting exactly 4 seconds means the checks at times $0,1,2,3$ all fail (a car somewhere in the next three seconds) but the check at time 4 succeeds. Given no car in seconds $\{5,6,7\}$: the time-3 check fails iff there is a car in second 4 (which then also blocks the time-1 and time-2 checks), and the time-0 check fails iff there is a car in $\{1,2,3\}$. These events involve the disjoint second-sets $\{4\}$, $\{1,2,3\}$, $\{5,6,7\}$, hence are independent, and
$$P(\text{wait exactly 4 s}) = p\cdot\big(1-(1-p)^3\big)\cdot(1-p)^3.$$
