**Central idea.** With $z_i = x_i^2$ this is regression through the origin; least squares, MLE, and UMVUE coincide at $\sum z_iY_i/\sum z_i^2$, attaining the Cramér–Rao bound.

**Solution.** Put $z_i = x_i^2$; then $Y_i = \theta z_i + \varepsilon_i$.
(a) Minimize $\sum_i(y_i-\theta z_i)^2$: $-2\sum_i z_i(y_i-\theta z_i) = 0$ gives $\hat\theta_{LS} = \dfrac{\sum_i z_iy_i}{\sum_i z_i^2} = \dfrac{\sum_i x_i^2y_i}{\sum_i x_i^4}$.
(b) The log likelihood is $-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_i(y_i-\theta z_i)^2$ plus a constant; maximizing over $\theta$ is the same minimization, so $\hat\theta_{MLE} = \dfrac{\sum_i x_i^2Y_i}{\sum_i x_i^4}$.
(c) $\hat\theta = \sum_i z_iY_i/\sum_i z_i^2$ is unbiased ($E\hat\theta = \theta\sum_iz_i^2/\sum_iz_i^2 = \theta$) with $\mathrm{Var}\,\hat\theta = \sigma^2/\sum_i z_i^2$. The score gives Fisher information $I(\theta) = \sum_i z_i^2/\sigma^2$, so the Cramér–Rao bound is $\sigma^2/\sum_i z_i^2 = \mathrm{Var}\,\hat\theta$: $\hat\theta$ attains the bound, hence is the best (uniformly minimum variance) unbiased estimator. Equivalently, $T = \sum_i z_iY_i$ is complete sufficient for $\theta$, and $\hat\theta = T/\sum_i z_i^2$ is unbiased — UMVUE by Lehmann–Scheffé.
