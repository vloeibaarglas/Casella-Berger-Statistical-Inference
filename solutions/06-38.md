**Central idea.** The inverse of any group element, composed with the element via (ii), must be the identity function — and (ii) puts that composition back in $G$.

**Solution.** Definition 6.4.2: $G$ is a group of transformations of $\mathcal{X}$ if (i) every $g\in G$ has an inverse $g'\in G$ with $g'(g(x)) = x$ for all $x$, and (ii) for $g,g'\in G$ there is $g''\in G$ with $g'(g(x)) = g''(x)$ for all $x$; (iii) asserts the identity $e(x) = x$ belongs to $G$. Let $G$ be nonempty and satisfy (i)–(ii). Pick $g\in G$ and let $g'$ be its inverse from (i): $g'(g(x)) = x$ for all $x$. Apply (ii) to this pair $(g,g')$: there is $g''\in G$ with $g'(g(x)) = g''(x)$ for all $x$. Hence $g''(x) = x$ for all $x$, i.e. $g'' = e$, so $e\in G$: (iii) holds.
