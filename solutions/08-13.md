**Central idea.** $\phi_1$ has size $.05$; matching it forces $C = 2-\sqrt{0.1} \approx 1.684$. The two closed-form power functions both start at $.05$ but cross at $\theta \approx .184$ — neither test dominates — so the "better" test for (d) is the UMP test of 8.33 ($n = 2$: reject iff $\max X_i \ge 1$ or $\min X_i \ge 1-\sqrt{.05}$).

**Solution.** (a) $\phi_1$ has size $P_0(X_1 > .95) = .05$. Under $H_0$, $S = X_1+X_2$ has the triangular density $f(s) = s$ on $(0,1)$, $2-s$ on $(1,2)$. For $C \in (1,2)$, $P_0(S > C) = (2-C)^2/2 = .05$ gives $C = 2-\sqrt{0.1} \approx 1.6838$.
(b) $\gamma_1(\theta) = P_\theta(X_1 > .95) = \min(1, \theta+.05)$ for $\theta \ge 0$ (i.e. $\theta+.05$ on $[0,.95]$, then $1$). For $\phi_2$, write $X_i = \theta+U_i$, $U_i \sim$ iid $U(0,1)$; $\gamma_2(\theta) = P(U_1+U_2 > C-2\theta)$. With $t = C-2\theta$:
$$\gamma_2(\theta) = \begin{cases} \frac{(2-t)^2}{2} = \frac{(2-C+2\theta)^2}{2}, & 0 \le \theta \le \frac{C-1}{2} \approx .342, \\ 1-\frac{t^2}{2} = 1-\frac{(C-2\theta)^2}{2}, & .342 \le \theta \le C/2 \approx .842, \\ 1, & \theta \ge .842.\end{cases}$$
Both start at $.05$ when $\theta = 0$; $\gamma_1$ initially lies above $\gamma_2$ (e.g. $\gamma_1(.05) = .1 > .0866 \approx \gamma_2(.05)$), but the curves cross at $\theta \approx .184$, after which $\gamma_2$ dominates: $\gamma_2$ reaches $1$ at $\theta \approx .842$ while $\gamma_1$ reaches $1$ only at $\theta = .95$.

(c) Disprove. "More powerful" requires $\gamma_2(\theta) \ge \gamma_1(\theta)$ for all $\theta > 0$, but at $\theta = .05$, $\gamma_1 = .1 > .0866 \approx \gamma_2$.
(d) The UMP test of 8.33 does the job. For $n = 2$, $\alpha = .05$ it rejects iff $Y_2 \ge 1$ or $Y_1 \ge k$ with $k = 1-\sqrt{.05} \approx .776$ (size $P_0(Y_1 \ge k) = (1-k)^2 = .05$, since $Y_2 < 1$ a.s. under $H_0$). Because it is UMP (proved in 8.33), its power $\gamma^*(\theta) = 1.05-(1-\theta)^2$ on $[0,k]$, $= 1$ for $\theta \ge k$, dominates $\gamma_2$ at every $\theta > 0$ — strictly on $(0,.842)$, e.g. $\gamma^*(.05) = .1475 > .0866 \approx \gamma_2(.05)$. (Note $\phi_1$ itself does not work: $\gamma_2$ overtakes $\gamma_1$ past $\theta \approx .184$.)
