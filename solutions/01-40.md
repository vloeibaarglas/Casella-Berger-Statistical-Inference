**Central idea.** Theorem 1.3.9: independence is preserved under complementation; write the target intersection as a difference (or use DeMorgan) and apply independence of $A$ and $B$.

**Solution.** Theorem 1.3.9 states that if $A$ and $B$ are independent, so are the pairs in (a) $A, B^c$ (proved in text), (b) $A^c, B$, (c) $A^c, B^c$. (b) $P(A^c \cap B) = P(B) - P(A \cap B) = P(B) - P(A)P(B) = P(B)(1-P(A)) = P(B)P(A^c)$. (c) $P(A^c \cap B^c) = 1 - P(A \cup B) = 1 - P(A) - P(B) + P(A \cap B) = (1-P(A))(1-P(B)) = P(A^c)P(B^c)$.
