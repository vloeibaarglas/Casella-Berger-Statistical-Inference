**Central idea.** Inversion $Y=1/X$ and the reciprocal parametrization $\theta\mapsto1/\theta$ both reverse order, turning "increasing" into "decreasing".

**Solution.** (a) For $y>0$, $F_Y(y|\theta)=P(X\ge1/y)=1-F_X((1/y)-|\theta)$. Positive support does not imply continuity; the left limit makes the argument valid for atoms as well. For $y\le0$, $F_Y(y|\theta)=0$. If $F_X(\cdot|\theta)$ is stochastically increasing, $\theta_1>\theta_2\Rightarrow F_X((1/y)-|\theta_1)\le F_X((1/y)-|\theta_2)\Rightarrow F_Y(y|\theta_1)\ge F_Y(y|\theta_2)$: stochastically decreasing. (b) $\theta_1>\theta_2>0\Rightarrow1/\theta_1<1/\theta_2\Rightarrow F_X(x|1/\theta_1)\ge F_X(x|1/\theta_2)$: $F_X(x|1/\theta)$ is stochastically decreasing in $\theta$.
