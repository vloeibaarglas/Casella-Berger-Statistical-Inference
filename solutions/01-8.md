**Central idea.** Ring areas are differences of squares; higher scores live in smaller (inner) rings.

**Solution.** The board has radius $r$ with rings spaced $r/5$; $i$ points are scored in the ring between radii $(5-i)r/5$ and $(6-i)r/5$.
(a) $$P(\text{scoring } i) = \frac{\pi\big(\frac{(6-i)r}{5}\big)^2 - \pi\big(\frac{(5-i)r}{5}\big)^2}{\pi r^2} = \frac{(6-i)^2-(5-i)^2}{25} = \frac{11-2i}{25}, \quad i=1,\dots,5,$$ independent of $r$.
(b) $\frac{11-2i}{25}$ is strictly decreasing in $i$ (values $9/25, 7/25, 5/25, 3/25, 1/25$): the bullseye is smallest.
(c) Each value is $\geq 0$ and $\sum_{i=1}^5 \frac{11-2i}{25} = \frac{9+7+5+3+1}{25} = 1$; with the sample space $\{1,\dots,5\}$ this satisfies the three axioms (Theorem 1.2.6).
