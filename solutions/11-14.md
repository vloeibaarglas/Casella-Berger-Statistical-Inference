**Central idea.** Without the $\sum a_i = 0$ restriction, Cauchy–Schwarz gives the unconstrained maximum $\sum c_iv_i^2$; with $v_i = \bar{Y}_{i\cdot}-\theta_i$, $c_i = n_i$ this is $\chi^2_k$, gaining one degree of freedom over the contrast case.

**Solution.** First, by Cauchy–Schwarz, $(\sum a_iv_i)^2 = (\sum \frac{a_i}{\sqrt{c_i}}\cdot v_i\sqrt{c_i})^2 \leq (\sum a_i^2/c_i)(\sum c_iv_i^2)$, with equality at $a_i \propto c_iv_i$. Hence $\max_{\mathbf{a}}(\sum a_iv_i)^2/(\sum a_i^2/c_i) = \sum c_iv_i^2$.
Now let $T_{\mathbf{a}} = \sum a_i(\bar{Y}_{i\cdot}-\theta_i)/\sqrt{S_p^2\sum a_i^2/n_i}$. Adapting Theorem 11.2.10: the simultaneous intervals hold iff $\max_{\mathbf{a}} T_{\mathbf{a}}^2 \leq \mathbf{M}^2$. With $v_i = \bar{Y}_{i\cdot}-\theta_i$, $c_i = n_i$, the identity gives $\max_{\mathbf{a}} T_{\mathbf{a}}^2 = \sum n_i(\bar{Y}_{i\cdot}-\theta_i)^2/S_p^2$. Since $\sqrt{n_i}(\bar{Y}_{i\cdot}-\theta_i)/\sigma$ are iid $n(0,1)$, the numerator$/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_k$, independent of $S_p^2$ (by 11.6(b)), so $\max_{\mathbf{a}}T_{\mathbf{a}}^2 \sim kF_{k,N-k}$. Taking $\mathbf{M}^2 = kF_{k,N-k,\alpha}$ gives simultaneous coverage $1-\alpha$ for all linear combinations.
