**Central idea.** By (11.3.44) (Exercise 11.40(b)), simultaneous coverage over all $x_0$ is the event that a $\chi^2_2/\chi^2_{n-2}$ ratio — i.e. an $F_{2,n-2}$ variable — falls below $2F_{2,n-2,\alpha}$.

**Solution.** The intervals (11.3.42) are, for every $x_0$,
$$\hat\alpha + \hat\beta x_0 \pm \sqrt{2F_{2,n-2,\alpha}}\cdot S\sqrt{\frac{1}{n} + \frac{(x_0-\bar x)^2}{S_{xx}}}.$$
All $x_0$ are covered simultaneously iff $\max_{x_0}\frac{(\hat\alpha+\hat\beta x_0-\alpha-\beta x_0)^2}{S^2\big(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{(x_0-\bar x)^2}{S_{xx}}\big)} \le 2F_{2,n-2,\alpha}$. By (11.3.44), verified in 11.40(b), the maximum equals $\dfrac{n(\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y})^2 + S_{xx}(\hat\beta-\beta)^2}{S^2}$, where $\mu_{\bar Y} = \alpha + \beta\bar x$. Now $n(\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y})^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_1$ and $S_{xx}(\hat\beta-\beta)^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_1$ independently ($\bar Y \perp \hat\beta$), so the numerator$/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_2$, independent of $\mathrm{SSE}/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_{n-2}$. Hence
$$\frac{n(\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y})^2 + S_{xx}(\hat\beta-\beta)^2}{S^2} = 2\cdot\frac{\chi^2_2/2}{\chi^2_{n-2}/(n-2)} \sim 2F_{2,n-2},$$
and $P\big(2F_{2,n-2} \le 2F_{2,n-2,\alpha}\big) = 1-\alpha$: the simultaneous coverage probability is exactly $1-\alpha$. ✓
