**Central idea.** $\{X_n + Y_n \leq c\} \subseteq \{X_n \leq c - M\} \cup \{Y_n \leq M\}$: make $Y_n \leq M$ negligible by hypothesis, then send $M \to \infty$ through continuity points of $F_X$ to kill $P(X_n \leq c-M)$.

**Solution.** For any fixed $M$,
$$P(X_n + Y_n \leq c) \leq P(X_n \leq c - M) + P(Y_n \leq M).$$
Let $M_k \to \infty$ with each $c - M_k$ a continuity point of $F_X$ (possible since discontinuities are countable). Then $\limsup_n P(X_n + Y_n \leq c) \leq F_X(c - M_k) + 0$ for each $k$; letting $k \to \infty$, $F_X(c-M_k) \to 0$. Hence $P(X_n + Y_n \leq c) \to 0$, i.e., $P(X_n + Y_n > c) \to 1$. (Intuition: $Y_n \to_p \infty$ drags the sum to $+\infty$ regardless of $X_n$'s limit.)
