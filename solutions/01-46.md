**Central idea.** $X_3 \in \{0,1,2\}$; count directly: choose the triple-occupied cells, choose their balls (multinomial), and place the rest avoiding new triples.

**Solution.** Total outcomes: $7^7 = 823543$.
- $X_3 = 2$: choose 2 cells $\binom{7}{2}$; assign balls $3+3+1$: $\binom{7}{3}\binom{4}{3}\binom{1}{1} = 140$; the single ball goes to one of the other 5 cells. Count $= 21\cdot140\cdot5 = 14700$; $P = 14700/7^7 \approx 0.0178$.
- $X_3 = 1$: choose the cell ($7$), its 3 balls ($\binom{7}{3}$); distribute the remaining 4 balls into the other 6 cells with no cell getting exactly 3: $6^4 - 6\binom{4}{3}\cdot5 = 1296-120 = 1176$. Count $= 7\cdot35\cdot1176 = 288120$; $P = 288120/7^7 \approx 0.3499$.
- $X_3 = 0$: the rest, $P = 1 - 288120/7^7 - 14700/7^7 = 520723/7^7 \approx 0.6323$.
(Three triple-cells would need 9 balls, so $x \geq 3$ is impossible.)
