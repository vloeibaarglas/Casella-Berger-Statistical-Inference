**Central idea.** Bounded design values bound the average squared design, but do not ensure that it converges. For the plug-in score, use a deterministic neighborhood rather than treating an estimated threshold as independent of the data.

**Solution.** (a)(i) If $x_i=1$, $q_n=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i^2=1$. (ii) If $|x_i|\le1$, $0\le q_n\le1$, but boundedness alone does not imply convergence or a positive limit. For example, successive blocks of zeros and ones, each longer than all previous blocks by a factor tending to infinity, have subsequences with $q_n\to0$ and $q_n\to1$. Retain $q_n\to q>0$ as an additional assumption for the asymptotic-normality result.
(b) Suppose $\hat\beta_L\to_p\beta$, errors are independent with median zero and continuous at zero, and $|x_i|\le1$. Put $S_i(t)=x_i\operatorname{sign}(Y_i-tx_i)$. The average at $t=\beta$ has mean zero and variance at most $1/n$, hence converges to zero in probability. For any fixed $\delta>0$, on $|\hat\beta_L-\beta|\le\delta$,
$$\frac1n\sum_i|S_i(\hat\beta_L)-S_i(\beta)|\le\frac2n\sum_i1\{|\epsilon_i|\le\delta\}.$$
For $\eta>0$, Markov's inequality gives a bound $P(|\hat\beta_L-\beta|>\delta)+2P(|\epsilon|\le\delta)/\eta$ for the probability that the discrepancy exceeds $\eta$. First let $n\to\infty$, then $\delta\downarrow0$. This proves the assertion without a false independence assumption about $\hat\beta_L$ and $\epsilon_i$.
