**Central idea.** Dropping the unbiasedness constraint $a_1+a_2=1$ strictly lowers the minimum MSE; truncating at $0$ helps since $\theta>0$; the family is $\frac1\theta g(x/\theta)$, i.e. scale.
**Solution.** *Note:* the textbook cites Exercise 7.50 here.
(a) $E_\theta(\theta-a_1\bar X-a_2cS)^2/\theta^2=a_1^2/n+a_2^2v+(1-a_1-a_2)^2$ with $v=c^2-1=\mathrm{Var}(cS)/\theta^2$ (using $\bar X\perp cS$; note $\mathrm{Var}(cS)=c^2\mathrm{Var}(S)=\theta^2(c^2-1)$ since $E_\theta S=\theta/c$). FOCs: $a_1/n=a_2v=1-a_1-a_2=:d$, so $a_1^*=nd^*$, $a_2^*=d^*/v$ with $d^*=1/(1+n+1/v)$; $T^*=a_1^*\bar X+a_2^*cS$.
(b) Exercise 7.50(b) minimizes over the unbiased subclass $\{a_1+a_2=1\}\subsetneq\mathcal{T}$; since $a_1^*+a_2^*=(n+1/v)/(n+1+1/v)<1$, $T^*$ is not in that subclass, and minimizing over the strictly larger $\mathcal{T}$ gives a strictly smaller minimum MSE.
(c) For $\theta>0$: if $T^*\ge0$, $T^{*+}=T^*$; if $T^*<0$, $(\theta-0)^2<(\theta-T^*)^2$. Hence $E_\theta(\theta-T^{*+})^2<E_\theta(\theta-T^*)^2$.
(d) Scale parameter: $f(x\mid\theta)=\frac{1}{\theta\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(x-\theta)^2/(2\theta^2)}=\frac1\theta g(x/\theta)$ with $g(u)=\phi(u-1)$, the scale-family form (not $f(x-\theta)$).
