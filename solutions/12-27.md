**Central idea.** Use the true-parameter weighted sign score for the CLT and differentiate its expectation. Convex LAD objective expansion replaces differentiation of the discontinuous empirical sign score.

**Solution.** Write $Y_i=\beta x_i+\epsilon_i$, $S_i(t)=x_i\operatorname{sign}(Y_i-tx_i)$, and assume independent identically distributed errors with continuous density at zero, median zero, $f(0)>0$, bounded fixed design, and $q_n=n^{-1}\sum x_i^2\to q>0$.
(a) At the true parameter,
$$Z_n=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_i S_i(\beta)=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_i x_i\operatorname{sign}(\epsilon_i)\Rightarrow N(0,q).$$
Each summand has mean zero and variance $x_i^2$; bounded design implies Lindeberg's condition. The printed plug-in assertion is false: for $x_i=1$ and odd $n$, $\hat\beta_L$ is the sample median and the plug-in sign sum is zero almost surely.
(b) $E_\beta S_i(t)=x_i[1-2F((t-\beta)x_i)]$, so
$$\left.\frac1n\sum_i\frac d{dt}E_\beta S_i(t)\right|_{t=\beta}=-2f(0)q_n,$$
with a minus sign.
To obtain the limiting law rigorously, for fixed $h$ expand the LAD objective $Q_n(h)=\sum_i(|\epsilon_i-hx_i/\sqrt n|-|\epsilon_i|)$ using
$$|e-v|-|e|=-v\operatorname{sign}(e)+2\int_0^v[1\{e\le s\}-1\{e\le0\}]\,ds.$$
Its linear term is $-hZ_n$. Continuity of $f$ at zero makes the expectation of the integral terms $f(0)q_nh^2+o(1)$; independence and bounded design make their centered sum $o_p(1)$. Convexity upgrades this to convergence on compact $h$ sets and yields the argmin expansion
$$\sqrt n(\hat\beta_L-\beta)=\frac{Z_n}{2f(0)q_n}+o_p(1)\Rightarrow N\left(0,\frac1{4f(0)^2q}\right).$$
This justifies the final law. The empirical sign score has derivative zero between its jumps, so an ordinary sample-score Taylor derivative is not a valid denominator.
