**Central idea.** Truncation just renormalizes by $P(X>0)$; moments of $X_T$ are moments of $X$ divided by $P(X>0)$.

**Solution.** (a) $P(X_T=x) = \dfrac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x/x!}{1-e^{-\lambda}}$, $x=1,2,\ldots$. $EX_T = \dfrac{\lambda}{1-e^{-\lambda}}$; $E[X_T^2] = \dfrac{\lambda+\lambda^2}{1-e^{-\lambda}}$, so $\text{Var}\,X_T = \dfrac{\lambda+\lambda^2}{1-e^{-\lambda}}-\dfrac{\lambda^2}{(1-e^{-\lambda})^2} = \dfrac{\lambda}{1-e^{-\lambda}}\left(1+\lambda-\dfrac{\lambda}{1-e^{-\lambda}}\right)$.
(b) $P(X_T=x) = \dfrac{\binom{r+x-1}{x}p^r(1-p)^x}{1-p^r}$, $x=1,2,\ldots$. $EX_T = \dfrac{r(1-p)/p}{1-p^r}$; with $E[X^2] = \dfrac{r(1-p)}{p^2}+\dfrac{r^2(1-p)^2}{p^2}$,
$$\text{Var}\,X_T = \frac{r(1-p)/p^2+r^2(1-p)^2/p^2}{1-p^r}-\frac{r^2(1-p)^2/p^2}{(1-p^r)^2}.$$
