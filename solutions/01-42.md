**Central idea.** Use indicators to count membership pointwise, then integrate. This works for continuous as well as discrete sample spaces.

**Solution.** Let $N(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^n1_{A_i}(\omega)$ and $E_k=\{N=k\}$.
(a) The $E_1,\ldots,E_n$ partition the union, so $P(\bigcup_i A_i)=\sum_{k=1}^nP(E_k)$.
(b) On $E_1$, exactly one indicator is one: $1_{E_1}\sum_i1_{A_i}=1_{E_1}$. The notation about the probability of a fixed point should be read as a counting explanation, not a new probability operation.
(c) On $E_k$, exactly $\binom kj$ of the $j$-fold intersection indicators are one. Consequently $P_j=E\binom Nj=\sum_{k=j}^n\binom kjP(E_k)$.
(d) $\sum_{j=1}^k(-1)^{j+1}\binom kj=1-(1-1)^k=1$ for $k\ge1$.
(e) Substitute (c), interchange the finite sums, and use (d): $\sum_{j=1}^n(-1)^{j+1}P_j=\sum_{k=1}^nP(E_k)=P(\bigcup_i A_i)$. The reference in (e) needs (d) as well.
