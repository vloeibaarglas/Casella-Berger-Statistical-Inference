**Central idea.** The inversion method (5.6.7) locates $Y$ by sequential search through the cumulative probabilities; finding $Y = i$ costs exactly $i+1$ comparisons, so the count is $Y+1$ a.s.

**Solution.** In (5.6.7), $U \sim$ uniform$(0,1)$ is compared successively against $p_0$, $p_0+p_1$, $\ldots$ until the interval containing $U$ is found. If the outcome is $Y = i$ (i.e., $y_i = i$), exactly $i+1$ comparisons were performed. Hence the number of comparisons $C$ satisfies $C = Y+1$ a.s., and $E[C] = E[Y+1]$ (equivalently, by Exercise 2.14's tail-sum formula, $E[Y+1] = \sum_{k \geq 0}P(Y \geq k)$).
