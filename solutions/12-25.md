**Central idea.** With two observations sharing an $x$, the LAD objective splits into $|y_1-v|+|y_2-v|$ (minimized by any $v$ between $y_1$ and $y_2$) plus the third residual (minimized at zero by passing through $(x_3,y_3)$) — a whole interval of minimizers.

**Solution.** (a) For a line $y=a+bx$ put $v=a+bx_1$. The LAD objective is $|y_1-v|+|y_2-v|+|y_3-(a+bx_3)|\ge|y_1-y_2|+0=|y_1-y_2|$, with equality iff $v$ lies between $y_1$ and $y_2$ and the line passes through $(x_3,y_3)$. Any line not through $(x_3,y_3)$ has objective $>|y_1-y_2|$. Hence the LAD lines are exactly the lines through $(x_3,y_3)$ whose value at $x_1$ lies between $y_1$ and $y_2$ — not unique.

(b) Least squares: $\bar x=130$, $\bar y=14.733$, $S_{xx}=54$, $S_{xy}=28.5$, so $\hat b=0.528$, $\hat a=\bar y-\hat b\bar x=-53.88$. LAD: lines through $(136,17.9)$ with $y(127)=17.9-9b\in[11.9,14.4]$, i.e. slope $b\in[3.5/9,\,6/9]=[0.389,0.667]$ and intercept $a=17.9-136b\in[-72.77,-34.99]$. The LS line ($b=0.528$) lies inside the LAD range.
