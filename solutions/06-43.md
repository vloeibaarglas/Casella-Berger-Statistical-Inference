**Central idea.** $S^2$ is translation-invariant and scales by $c^2$, matching $\sigma^2\mapsto\sigma^2$ under $\mathcal{G}_2$ and $\sigma^2\mapsto c^2\sigma^2$ under $\mathcal{G}_1,\mathcal{G}_3$; the ratio $\bar{X}/S$ is scale-invariant but not translation-invariant, so a nonconstant $\phi$ survives only $\mathcal{G}_3$.

**Solution.** The induced maps on $\sigma^2$ are: $\mathcal{G}_1$: $\sigma^2\mapsto c^2\sigma^2$; $\mathcal{G}_2$: $\sigma^2\mapsto\sigma^2$; $\mathcal{G}_3$: $\sigma^2\mapsto c^2\sigma^2$.
(a) $S^2(\mathbf{x}+a\mathbf{1}) = S^2(\mathbf{x})$ and $S^2(c\mathbf{x}+a\mathbf{1}) = c^2S^2(\mathbf{x})$. Hence $kS^2(g_a(\mathbf{x})) = kS^2(\mathbf{x})$ (invariant under $\mathcal{G}_2$, equivariance with the identity parameter map), and $kS^2(g_{a,c}(\mathbf{x})) = c^2kS^2(\mathbf{x})$, $kS^2(g_c(\mathbf{x})) = c^2kS^2(\mathbf{x})$ (equivariant under $\mathcal{G}_1,\mathcal{G}_3$).
(b) Under $\mathcal{G}_3$: $\bar{X}(c\mathbf{x})/S(c\mathbf{x}) = \bar{X}/S$ and $S^2(c\mathbf{x}) = c^2S^2$, so $W(c\mathbf{x}) = \phi(\bar{X}/S)c^2S^2 = c^2W(\mathbf{x})$: equivariant. Under $\mathcal{G}_2$ (identity on $\sigma^2$), equivariance needs $W(\mathbf{x}+a\mathbf{1}) = W(\mathbf{x})$, i.e. $\phi((\bar{X}+a)/S) = \phi(\bar{X}/S)$ for all $a$, forcing $\phi$ constant on the range of $(\bar{X}+a)/S$. Under $\mathcal{G}_1$, equivariance needs $W(c\mathbf{x}+a\mathbf{1}) = c^2W(\mathbf{x})$, i.e. $\phi((c\bar{X}+a)/(cS)) = \phi(\bar{X}/S)$ for all $a$ and $c > 0$, again forcing $\phi$ constant. If $\phi\equiv k$, $W = kS^2$ is equivariant under all three groups by (a).
