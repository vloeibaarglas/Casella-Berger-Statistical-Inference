**Central idea.** $(X-\mu)/S \sim t_v$ replaces $Z$; the FOC $f_{t_v}(c) = bM$ is the $t$ analogue of $2b\sigma = 2\phi(c)$, and $t_v \to$ normal as $v\to\infty$.

**Solution.** (a) $R = \frac{b}{\sigma}2cE[S] - P_\mu(|X-\mu| \le cS) = 2bcM - P(|T| \le c)$ with $T = \frac{(X-\mu)/\sigma}{S/\sigma} \sim t_v$; $P(|T|\le c) = 2P(T\le c)-1$.
(b) $\frac{dR}{dc} = 2bM - 2f_{t_v}(c) = 0$. With $f_{t_v}(c) = \frac{\Gamma((v+1)/2)}{\sqrt{v\pi}\,\Gamma(v/2)}(1+c^2/v)^{-(v+1)/2}$ and $M = \sqrt{2/v}\,\Gamma((v+1)/2)/\Gamma(v/2)$, $f_{t_v}(c) = bM$ simplifies to $b = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(\frac{v}{v+c^2})^{(v+1)/2}$.
(c) As $v\to\infty$: $(\frac{v}{v+c^2})^{(v+1)/2} = (1+c^2/v)^{-(v+1)/2} \to e^{-c^2/2}$, $M \to 1$, $T \xrightarrow{d} Z$. The FOC becomes $b = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-c^2/2}$, i.e. $2b = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-c^2/2}$ — exactly 9.53's FOC with $b\sigma$ replaced by $b$, as the loss here scales length by $b/\sigma$ instead of $b$.
