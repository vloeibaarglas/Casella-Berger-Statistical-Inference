**Central idea.** (a) $\sqrt{n}$-consistency implies consistency: $P(|Y_n-\mu| > \varepsilon) = P(|\sqrt{n}(Y_n-\mu)| > \varepsilon\sqrt{n}) \to 0$ by tightness. (b) The delta method: factor $\sqrt{n}(g(Y_n)-g(\theta)) = \frac{g(Y_n)-g(\theta)}{Y_n-\theta}\cdot\sqrt{n}(Y_n-\theta)$ and apply Slutsky to the (constant) $\times$ (asymptotically normal) product.

**Solution.** (a) $P(|Y_n-\mu| > \varepsilon) = P(|\sqrt{n}(Y_n-\mu)| > \varepsilon\sqrt{n})$. Given $M > 0$, for large $n$, $\varepsilon\sqrt{n} > M$, so $\limsup_n P(|Y_n-\mu| > \varepsilon) \leq P(|Z| > M)$ with $Z \sim n(0,\sigma^2)$; letting $M \to \infty$ gives $0$. Hence $Y_n \to_p \mu$.
(b) (Theorem 5.5.24 is the delta method.) Write, for $Y_n \neq \theta$,
$$\sqrt{n}\big(g(Y_n)-g(\theta)\big) = \frac{g(Y_n)-g(\theta)}{Y_n-\theta}\cdot\sqrt{n}(Y_n-\theta)$$
(defining the ratio as $g'(\theta)$ when $Y_n = \theta$). By (a), $Y_n \to_p \theta$, and differentiability of $g$ at $\theta$ gives $\frac{g(Y_n)-g(\theta)}{Y_n-\theta} \to_p g'(\theta)$ (given $\eta > 0$, the ratio is within $\eta$ of $g'(\theta)$ whenever $0 < |Y_n-\theta| < \delta$, an event of probability $\to 1$). Slutsky's theorem (5.5.17) then yields $\sqrt{n}(g(Y_n)-g(\theta)) \to_d g'(\theta)\cdot n(0,\sigma^2) = n(0,\sigma^2[g'(\theta)]^2)$.
