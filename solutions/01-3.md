**Central idea.** Membership in a union (intersection) is the logical "or" ("and") of membership statements, which is commutative and associative; complements flip "or" to "and".

**Solution.** (a) $x \in A \cup B \iff$ ($x \in A$ or $x \in B$) $\iff$ ($x \in B$ or $x \in A$) $\iff x \in B \cup A$. Same with "and" for $\cap$. (b) $x \in A \cup (B \cup C) \iff$ ($x \in A$ or ($x \in B$ or $x \in C$)) $\iff$ (($x \in A$ or $x \in B$) or $x \in C$) $\iff x \in (A \cup B) \cup C$, using associativity of "or". Same with "and". (c) $x \in (A \cup B)^c \iff$ not ($x \in A$ or $x \in B$) $\iff$ ($x \notin A$ and $x \notin B$) $\iff x \in A^c \cap B^c$. The second law is dual.
