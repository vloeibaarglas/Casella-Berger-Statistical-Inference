**Central idea.** The coordinate differences are iid $n(0,2)$, so the distance is $\sqrt{2}$ times a Rayleigh$(1)$ variable.

**Solution.** With $(X_1,Y_1),(X_2,Y_2)$ iid, $X_1-X_2$ and $Y_1-Y_2$ are iid $n(0,2)$. The distance $D = \sqrt{(X_1-X_2)^2+(Y_1-Y_2)^2} = \sqrt{2}\sqrt{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}$ with $Z_i$ iid $n(0,1)$; $\sqrt{Z_1^2+Z_2^2}$ is Rayleigh$(1)$, so $f_D(d) = \frac{d}{2}e^{-d^2/4}$, $d>0$ (Rayleigh with scale $\sqrt{2}$; mean $\sqrt{\pi}$).
