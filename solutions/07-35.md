**Central idea.** $d_P$ is the posterior mean under a flat prior; shifting all observations by $c$ shifts it by $c$, and for the normal/uniform the integrals collapse to $\bar X$ and the midrange.
**Solution.** (a) With $u=t-c$, $\int t\prod f(x_i+c-t)\,dt=\int(u+c)\prod f(x_i-u)\,du$, so $d_P(\mathbf{x}+c)=d_P(\mathbf{x})+c$: location-equivariant (the "invariance" of Example 7.3.6).
(b) $\prod\phi(x_i-t)\propto\exp(-\frac{n}{2}(t-\bar x)^2)$; hence $d_P=\int t\,e^{-n(t-\bar x)^2/2}dt/\int e^{-n(t-\bar x)^2/2}dt=\bar x$.
(c) $\prod_{i}\mathbf{1}_{[-1/2,1/2]}(x_i-t)=\mathbf{1}_{[x_{(n)}-1/2,\ x_{(1)}+1/2]}(t)$; thus $d_P$ is the midpoint of $[x_{(n)}-\frac12,x_{(1)}+\frac12]$, i.e. $\frac12(X_{(1)}+X_{(n)})$.
