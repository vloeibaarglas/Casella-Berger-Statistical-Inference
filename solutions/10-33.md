**Central idea.** The log-likelihood Taylor expansion multiplies the squared estimation error by observed information.

**Solution.** **Correction to the displayed statement:** use $(\theta_0-\hat\theta)\sqrt{-\ell_n''(\hat\theta)}$, not division by that square root.
Let $J_n(\hat\theta)=-\ell_n''(\hat\theta)$ be total observed information and $I_1(\theta_0)$ information per observation. Under the usual interior, identifiability, consistency, smoothness and information-convergence conditions,
$$\sqrt n(\hat\theta-\theta_0)\Rightarrow N(0,I_1(\theta_0)^{-1}),\qquad J_n(\hat\theta)/n\to_p I_1(\theta_0).$$
Thus
$$\sqrt{J_n(\hat\theta)}(\hat\theta-\theta_0)=\sqrt{J_n(\hat\theta)/n}\,\sqrt n(\hat\theta-\theta_0)\Rightarrow N(0,1).$$
Expanding about the likelihood maximizer, whose score is zero, gives
$$-2\log\Lambda_n=2[\ell_n(\hat\theta)-\ell_n(\theta_0)]=J_n(\hat\theta)(\hat\theta-\theta_0)^2+o_p(1)\Rightarrow\chi^2_1.$$
The original division is $O_p(n^{-1})$, so it converges to zero. The multiplication correction is essential.
