**Central idea.** Each nuisance-coordinate minimization is a convex quadratic. The squared weighted distance fails the triangle inequality; its square root is a metric.

**Solution.** (a) For $\lambda>0$, $g_i(\xi)=(x_i-\xi)^2+\lambda(y_i-\alpha-\beta\xi)^2$ has second derivative $2(1+\lambda\beta^2)>0$. Its unique minimum solves $\xi(1+\lambda\beta^2)=x_i+\lambda\beta(y_i-\alpha)$, giving the displayed $\xi_i^*$.
(b) Extend the two-point expression to $D_\lambda((x,y),(u,v))=(x-u)^2+\lambda(y-v)^2$. It satisfies zero self-distance, positivity for distinct points, and symmetry, but is not a metric: for $A=(0,0)$, $C=(1,0)$, $B=(2,0)$ and $\lambda=1$, $D(A,B)=4>1+1$.
The correct metric is $d_\lambda(A,B)=\sqrt{(x-u)^2+\lambda(y-v)^2}$, the Euclidean norm after scaling the second coordinate by $\sqrt\lambda$. The expression in the exercise is its restriction when the second point lies on the fitted line. The earlier claim that $D(A,A)=\lambda(y-\alpha-\beta x)^2$ was false: that expression compares $A$ to the different projected point $(x,\alpha+\beta x)$, not to itself.
