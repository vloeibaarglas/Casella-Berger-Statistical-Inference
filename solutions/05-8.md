**Central idea.** (a) is algebra: double-centering the pairwise differences. For (b)–(c), center ($Y_i = X_i - \theta_1$) and expand moments of $\sum Y_i^2 - n\bar{Y}^2$, using independence to kill every term with an unmatched $Y_i$.

**Solution.** (a) $\sum_{i,j}(X_i - X_j)^2 = \sum_{i,j}\big[(X_i-\bar{X}) - (X_j-\bar{X})\big]^2 = 2n\sum_i (X_i-\bar{X})^2 - 2\big(\sum_i(X_i-\bar{X})\big)^2 = 2n(n-1)S^2$, since $\sum_i(X_i - \bar{X}) = 0$. Dividing gives the result.
(b) Put $Y_i = X_i - \theta_1$ (so $EY_i = 0$); then $S^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}(A - B)$ with $A = \sum Y_i^2$, $B = n\bar{Y}^2 = (\sum Y_i)^2/n$. Now $E[A^2] = n\theta_4 + n(n-1)\theta_2^2$, $E[AB] = \theta_4 + (n-1)\theta_2^2$ (the cross terms $E[(\sum_iY_i^2)Y_jY_k]$, $j \neq k$, vanish since $EY_i = 0$), and $E[B^2] = \frac{1}{n^2}E[(\sum Y_i)^4] = \frac{1}{n^2}\big(n\theta_4 + 3n(n-1)\theta_2^2\big) = \frac{\theta_4}{n} + \frac{3(n-1)}{n}\theta_2^2$. Hence
$$E[(A-B)^2] = \frac{(n-1)^2}{n}\theta_4 + \frac{(n-1)(n^2-2n+3)}{n}\theta_2^2,$$
and subtracting $(E[A-B])^2 = (n-1)^2\theta_2^2$ gives $\mathrm{Var}(A-B) = \frac{n-1}{n}\big[(n-1)\theta_4 - (n-3)\theta_2^2\big]$. Dividing by $(n-1)^2$:
$$\mathrm{Var}\, S^2 = \frac{1}{n}\left(\theta_4 - \frac{n-3}{n-1}\,\theta_2^2\right).$$
(c) $\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{X}, S^2) = E[\bar{Y}S^2]$ (since $E\bar{Y} = 0$). Now $E\big[\bar{Y}\sum_j Y_j^2\big] = \frac{1}{n}E\big[(\sum_iY_i)(\sum_jY_j^2)\big] = \frac{1}{n}\cdot n\theta_3 = \theta_3$ (only $i = j$ terms survive), and $E[n\bar{Y}^3] = n\cdot \theta_3/n^2 = \theta_3/n$ (since $E[(\sum Y_i)^3] = n\theta_3$). Thus
$$\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{X}, S^2) = \frac{1}{n-1}\left(\theta_3 - \frac{\theta_3}{n}\right) = \frac{\theta_3}{n}.$$
So $\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{X}, S^2) = 0$ iff $\theta_3 = E(X_1-\theta_1)^3 = 0$, e.g., whenever the population is symmetric about its mean (symmetry is sufficient, not necessary).
