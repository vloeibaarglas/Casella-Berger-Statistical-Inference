**Central idea.** The Fundamental Theorem of Counting: group the first $k$ tasks into one super-task, then apply the $k=2$ case.

**Solution.** Theorem 1.2.14: if a job consists of $k$ tasks with $n_i$ ways to do task $i$, there are $n_1 \cdots n_k$ ways total. The text proves $k = 2$. Assume the result for $k$ tasks. For $k+1$ tasks, view the first $k$ tasks as a single composite task, doable in $n_1 \cdots n_k$ ways by the induction hypothesis; pairing each with the $n_{k+1}$ ways for task $k+1$ gives $(n_1 \cdots n_k) \cdot n_{k+1}$ by the $k=2$ case.
