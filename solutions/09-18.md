**Central idea.** Most-probable ordering can create disconnected Sterne confidence sets. The displayed modified acceptance regions invert to connected intervals at the intentionally low nominal coefficient $0.442$; $44.2\%$ is not undercoverage relative to that nominal level.

**Solution.** (a) $P_p(X=0) = (1-p)^3$, $P_p(X=1) = 3p(1-p)^2$, $P_p(X=2) = 3p^2(1-p)$, $P_p(X=3) = p^3$. $P_p(X=1)$ is maximized at $p = 1/3$ (value $4/9$); $P_p(X=2)$ at $p = 2/3$ (value $4/9$).
(b) At $p = 1/3+\epsilon$: $P(X=0) = (2/3-\epsilon)^3 = 8/27 - \frac43\epsilon + O(\epsilon^2)$ while $P(X=2) = 3(1/3+\epsilon)^2(2/3-\epsilon) = 6/27 + \epsilon + O(\epsilon^2)$. The difference is $2/27 - \frac73\epsilon + O(\epsilon^2) > 0$ for small $\epsilon$: just right of $1/3$, the far-tail point $0$ outranks the central point $2$ under most-probable ordering.
(c) Inverting, $C(x) = \{p : x \in A(p)\}$: $C(0) = [0,.305)$, $C(1) = (.238,.634)$, $C(2) = (.362,.762)$, $C(3) = (.695,1]$ — all intervals. But $\inf_p P_p(p \in C(X)) = .442$: e.g. as $p \downarrow .305$ only $x=1$ covers, giving $3(.305)(.695)^2 = .442$; at $p = .238$, $(.762)^3 = .442$. This modified construction achieves the intended approximately $44.2\%$ coefficient and connected intervals. The printed three-decimal cutpoints are rounded: for example $3(0.305)(0.695)^2=0.441951375$ and $0.762^3=0.442450728$. Exact cutpoints solve the corresponding equations at $0.442$; the defect being illustrated is disconnectedness of the original Sterne sets, not coverage below the stated nominal level.
