**Central idea.** $\sin^2$ has four monotone branches on $(0,2\pi)$, each contributing the identical Jacobian $1/(2\sqrt{y(1-y)})$; the cdf route differentiates $F_Y(y)=2\arcsin(\sqrt y)/\pi$.

**Solution.** In Example 2.1.2, $X\sim\text{uniform}(0,2\pi)$ and $Y=\sin^2 X$. Partition $(0,2\pi)$ (up to the null set of endpoints) into $A_1=(0,\pi/2)$, $A_2=(\pi/2,\pi)$, $A_3=(\pi,3\pi/2)$, $A_4=(3\pi/2,2\pi)$, on each of which $\sin^2$ is monotone with range $\mathcal Y=(0,1)$. The inverses are $\arcsin\sqrt y$, $\pi-\arcsin\sqrt y$, $\pi+\arcsin\sqrt y$, $2\pi-\arcsin\sqrt y$, each with $|dx/dy| = 1/(2\sqrt{y(1-y)})$. By Theorem 2.1.8,
$$f_Y(y) = \sum_{i=1}^4 \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{y(1-y)}} = \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{y(1-y)}},\qquad 0<y<1,$$
the arcsine law. From (2.1.6), with $x_1=\arcsin\sqrt y$ and $x_2=\pi-\arcsin\sqrt y$ the two roots of $\sin^2x=y$ in $(0,\pi)$,
$$F_Y(y) = 2\frac{x_1}{2\pi}+2\frac{\pi-x_2}{2\pi} = \frac{2\arcsin\sqrt y}{\pi},\quad 0<y<1,$$
($0$ for $y\le0$, $1$ for $y\ge1$), and differentiating gives $f_Y(y)=\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-y}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt y}=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{y(1-y)}}$, the same.
