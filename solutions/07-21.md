**Central idea.** Averaging the individual ratios $Y_i/x_i$ is unbiased but inefficient: Cauchy–Schwarz again shows its variance dominates the MLE's.
**Solution.** (a) $E\big[\frac1n\sum\frac{Y_i}{x_i}\big]=\frac1n\sum\big(\beta+\frac{E\varepsilon_i}{x_i}\big)=\beta$ (for $x_i\ne 0$).
(b) $\mathrm{Var}\big(\frac1n\sum Y_i/x_i\big)=\frac{\sigma^2}{n^2}\sum x_i^{-2}$. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $(\sum x_i^{-2})(\sum x_i^2)\ge n^2$, so $\frac{\sigma^2}{n^2}\sum x_i^{-2}\ge\sigma^2/\sum x_i^2=\mathrm{Var}(\hat\beta_{\mathrm{MLE}})$. Hence the MLE of Exercise 7.19 dominates both this estimator and that of Exercise 7.20 (the latter two have no uniform ordering between themselves).
