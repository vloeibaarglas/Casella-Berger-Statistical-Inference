**Central idea.** Same Bernoulli likelihood with $\Phi$ in place of the logistic cdf; the score equations are nonlinear and solved by Newton–Raphson (the log-likelihood is strictly concave). The probit and logistic links are nearly indistinguishable on data.

**Solution.** (a) $L(\alpha,\beta\mid\mathbf y)=\prod_{i=1}^n\Phi(\alpha+\beta x_i)^{y_i}(1-\Phi(\alpha+\beta x_i))^{1-y_i}$. With $\phi=\Phi'$,
$$\frac{\partial\log L}{\partial\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\Phi_i)\frac{\phi_i}{\Phi_i(1-\Phi_i)},\qquad \frac{\partial\log L}{\partial\beta}=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i(y_i-\Phi_i)\frac{\phi_i}{\Phi_i(1-\Phi_i)},$$
where $\Phi_i=\Phi(\alpha+\beta x_i)$. Setting these to zero gives nonlinear equations solved numerically (Newton–Raphson/IRLS); strict concavity guarantees a unique solution when one exists.

(b) For the Challenger data (Table 12.3.1), Newton–Raphson gives $\hat\alpha=9.20$, $\hat\beta=-0.141$ (compare logistic $\hat\alpha=15.81$, $\hat\beta=-0.243$). The fits are virtually identical: e.g. fitted failure probabilities at 53/63/70/81°F are logistic $(.949,.623,.231,.020)$ vs probit $(.958,.626,.253,.013)$, and both give $P(\text{fail}\mid 31^\circ\text{F})\approx 1$. The probit coefficients are roughly the logistic ones divided by $1.6$–$1.8$ (the usual scaling between the links); the logistic link has slightly heavier tails, but the data cannot distinguish the two models.
