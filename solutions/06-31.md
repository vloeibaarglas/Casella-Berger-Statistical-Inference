**Central idea.** Basu's Theorem turns "complete sufficient + ancillary" into independence for free: (a) gives $\bar{X}\perp S^2$; (b) kills the covariance term in the swindle; (c) makes the ratio $X_{(i)}/\sum X_i$ ancillary, hence independent of the complete sufficient sum.

**Solution.** (a)(i) $\bar{X}\sim n(\mu,\sigma^2/n)$ is sufficient (factorization) and complete (full exponential family, open parameter space) for $\mu$. $S^2$ depends only on $X_i-\bar{X}$, free of $\mu$: ancillary. By Basu, $\bar{X}\perp S^2$.
(ii) The joint law is $\bar{X}\sim n(\mu,\sigma^2/n)\perp (n-1)S^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$ for every $\sigma^2 > 0$; "knowing" $\sigma^2$ changes nothing about this factorization, so independence holds whether or not $\sigma^2$ is known.
(b)(i) With $\sigma^2$ known, $\bar{X}$ is complete sufficient for $\mu$ and $M-\bar{X}$ is ancillary for $\mu$ (location family), so $\mathrm{Cov}(M-\bar{X},\bar{X}) = 0$ by Basu. Hence $\mathrm{Var}(M) = \mathrm{Var}((M-\bar{X})+\bar{X}) = \mathrm{Var}(M-\bar{X}) + \mathrm{Var}(\bar{X})$.
(ii) Estimating $\mathrm{Var}(M)$ by the sample variance of $N$ simulated medians has variance $\approx 2[\mathrm{Var}(M)]^2/(N-1)$ (the $\chi^2$-type variance of a sample variance); estimating $\mathrm{Var}(M-\bar{X})$ analogously has variance $\approx 2[\mathrm{Var}(M-\bar{X})]^2/(N-1)$. Since $\mathrm{Var}(M-\bar{X}) = \mathrm{Var}(M)-\sigma^2/n < \mathrm{Var}(M)$, the swindle estimator $\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(M-\bar{X}) + \sigma^2/n$ has strictly smaller Monte Carlo error.
(c)(i) $\mathrm{E}[X^k] = \mathrm{E}[(X/Y)^kY^k] = \mathrm{E}[(X/Y)^k]\,\mathrm{E}[Y^k]$ by independence; dividing gives $\mathrm{E}[(X/Y)^k] = \mathrm{E}[X^k]/\mathrm{E}[Y^k]$ (moments assumed finite, $Y \ne 0$).
(ii) Put $Y = \sum X_i$ (complete sufficient for $\beta$, $\alpha$ known) and $W_i = X_{(i)}/Y$. The vector $(X_1/Y,\ldots,X_n/Y)$ is scale-invariant, hence ancillary for $\beta$; $W_i$, a function of it, is ancillary too, so $W_i \perp Y$ by Basu. Then $\mathrm{E}[X_{(i)}\mid Y] = \mathrm{E}[W_iY\mid Y] = Y\,\mathrm{E}[W_i] = Y\,\mathrm{E}[X_{(i)}/Y]$, and by (i) with $X = X_{(i)}$, $\mathrm{E}[X_{(i)}/Y] = \mathrm{E}[X_{(i)}]/\mathrm{E}[Y]$. Hence $\mathrm{E}[X_{(i)}\mid\sum X_i] = \sum X_i\cdot\mathrm{E}[X_{(i)}]/\mathrm{E}[\sum X_i]$.
