**Central idea.** The inverted gamma is conjugate in both cases; the posterior is unimodal, so the HPD region is $\{\pi(\cdot\mid x) \ge k\}$ — an interval with equal posterior density at the endpoints.

**Solution.** (a) ($\text{exponential}(\lambda)$ = mean $\lambda$.) $L(\lambda) \propto \lambda^{-n}e^{-\sum x_i/\lambda}$; posterior $\pi(\lambda\mid x) \propto \lambda^{-(a+n)-1}\exp(-(1/b + \sum x_i)/\lambda)$, i.e. $IG(a+n,\ (1/b+\sum x_i)^{-1})$. The HPD set $\{\lambda : \pi(\lambda\mid x) \ge k\}$ is the interval $[\lambda_L,\lambda_U]$ solving $\pi(\lambda_L\mid x) = \pi(\lambda_U\mid x)$ and $\int_{\lambda_L}^{\lambda_U}\pi(\lambda\mid x)\,d\lambda = 1-\alpha$ (numerically).
(b) $L(\sigma^2) \propto (\sigma^2)^{-(n-1)/2}\exp(-(n-1)s^2/(2\sigma^2))$; posterior $IG(a + (n-1)/2,\ [1/b + (n-1)s^2/2]^{-1})$. Same HPD construction as (a).
(c) As $a\to0$, $b\to\infty$, $\pi(\sigma^2) \to (\sigma^2)^{-1}$ (Jeffreys) and the posterior tends to $IG((n-1)/2,\ 2/((n-1)s^2))$; the limiting HPD set is $\{\sigma^2 : \pi(\sigma^2\mid x) \ge k\}$ for this limiting posterior, i.e. the interval $[\sigma^2_L,\sigma^2_U]$ with equal posterior density at the endpoints and posterior probability $1-\alpha$.
