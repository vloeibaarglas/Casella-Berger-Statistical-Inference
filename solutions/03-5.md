**Central idea.** Under "equally effective", $X \sim$ Binomial$(100,0.8)$; the $p$-value is the upper tail $P(X \ge 85)$.

**Solution.** Under the null, $P(X \ge 85) = \sum_{k=85}^{100}\binom{100}{k}(0.8)^k(0.2)^{100-k} \approx 0.1285$ (normal approximation: $\mu = 80$, $\sigma = 4$, $P \approx 1-\Phi(1.125) \approx 0.130$). Since $0.1285 > 0.05$, 85 successes is well within the range of chance under the null — there is no convincing evidence that the new drug is superior.
