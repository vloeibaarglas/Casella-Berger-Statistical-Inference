**Central idea.** A linear image of independent $n(0,1)$s is bivariate normal with moments read off the coefficients; the coefficient matrix is a square root of the covariance matrix, and square roots are unique only up to an orthogonal factor — infinitely many.

**Solution.** (a) Direct from $\mathrm{E}Z_i = 0$, $\mathrm{Var}\,Z_i = 1$, independence: e.g. $\mathrm{Var}\,X = a_X^2+b_X^2$, $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y) = a_Xa_Y+b_Xb_Y$.
(b) $\mathrm{Var}\,X = \frac{1+\rho}{2}\sigma_X^2+\frac{1-\rho}{2}\sigma_X^2 = \sigma_X^2$; similarly $\mathrm{Var}\,Y = \sigma_Y^2$; $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y) = \frac{1+\rho}{2}\sigma_X\sigma_Y-\frac{1-\rho}{2}\sigma_X\sigma_Y = \rho\sigma_X\sigma_Y$, so $\rho_{XY} = \rho$; means are $c_X,c_Y$.
(c) $M_{X,Y}(s,t) = e^{c_Xs+c_Yt}\mathrm{E}[e^{(a_Xs+a_Yt)Z_1+(b_Xs+b_Yt)Z_2}] = \exp(\mu_Xs+\mu_Yt+\tfrac12(\sigma_X^2s^2+2\rho\sigma_X\sigma_Yst+\sigma_Y^2t^2))$, the bivariate normal mgf.
(d) Another solution: the Cholesky choice $a_X = \sigma_X$, $b_X = 0$, $a_Y = \rho\sigma_Y$, $b_Y = \sigma_Y\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$ (check: $a_X^2+b_X^2 = \sigma_X^2$, $a_Y^2+b_Y^2 = \sigma_Y^2$, $a_Xa_Y+b_Xb_Y = \rho\sigma_X\sigma_Y$). In general the coefficient matrix is $\Sigma^{1/2}Q$ for any $2\times2$ orthogonal $Q$ — infinitely (a circle's worth of) solutions.
