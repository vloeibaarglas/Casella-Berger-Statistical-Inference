**Central idea.** $\hat\beta=\bar X/\alpha$; $I(\beta)=n\alpha/\beta^2$, so the Wald statistic is $\sqrt{n\alpha}(\hat\beta-\beta_0)/\hat\beta$, with $S$ substituted for the model-based se in (c).

**Solution.** (a) $l(\beta)=-n\alpha\log\beta-\sum x_i/\beta+c$; $l'(\beta)=-n\alpha/\beta+\sum x_i/\beta^2=0$ gives $\hat\beta=\bar X/\alpha$.
(b) $l''(\beta)=n\alpha/\beta^2-2\sum x_i/\beta^3$, so $I(\beta)=-E[l'']/n=\alpha/\beta^2$ and $\mathrm{se}(\hat\beta)=\hat\beta/\sqrt{n\alpha}$. The Wald statistic (MLE in both numerator and denominator) is
$$W = \frac{\hat\beta-\beta_0}{\hat\beta/\sqrt{n\alpha}} = \sqrt{n\alpha}\,\frac{\hat\beta-\beta_0}{\hat\beta}.$$
(c) $\mathrm{Var}(\hat\beta)=\beta^2/(n\alpha)=\mathrm{Var}(X)/(n\alpha^2)$, estimated by $S^2/(n\alpha^2)$; hence $\mathrm{se}=S/(\alpha\sqrt n)$ and
$$W = \frac{\hat\beta-\beta_0}{S/(\alpha\sqrt n)} = \alpha\sqrt n\,\frac{\hat\beta-\beta_0}{S}.$$
