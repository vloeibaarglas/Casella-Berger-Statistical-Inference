**Central idea.** The oscillatory perturbation $\frac12\sin(2\pi x)$ is large on the density scale but its mgf contribution picks up the factor $e^{-2\pi^2}\approx2.7\times10^{-9}$ — invisible numerically, present analytically.

**Solution.** In Miscellanea 2.6.3, $f_1$ is the $n(0,1)$ pdf and $f_2(x)=f_1(x)[1+\frac12\sin(2\pi x)]$, with $K_1(t)=t^2/2$ and $K_2(t)=K_1(t)+\log\big(1+\frac12e^{-2\pi^2}\sin(2\pi t)\big)$.
(a) The densities differ visibly: $f_2$ oscillates around $f_1$ with relative amplitude up to $50\%$ and period 1:

![pdfs f1 and f2]({{ '/plots/ex237_pdfs.png' | relative_url }})

(b) The cgfs are indistinguishable to the eye — $\max_t|K_2(t)-K_1(t)|\le\log\big(1+\tfrac12e^{-2\pi^2}\big)\approx1.34\times10^{-9}$:

![cgfs K1 and K2]({{ '/plots/ex237_cgfs.png' | relative_url }})

(c) $M_1(t)=e^{t^2/2}$. Since $\int e^{tx}\phi(x)\sin(2\pi x)dx=\mathrm{Im}\,e^{(t+2\pi i)^2/2}=e^{t^2/2}e^{-2\pi^2}\sin(2\pi t)$,
$$M_2(t)=e^{t^2/2}\left(1+\tfrac12e^{-2\pi^2}\sin(2\pi t)\right).$$
Analytically different, numerically identical (relative difference $\le1.34\times10^{-9}$).
(d) Same construction as Example 2.3.10: $f_2=f_1\cdot(1+\text{oscillation})$ with the oscillation tuned so a transform cannot see it. In 2.3.10 the sine is in $\log x$, making all polynomial moments exactly equal; here the sine is in $x$, making the mgfs exponentially close but not equal. Both show that moments/mgfs can fail — exactly or numerically — to distinguish visibly different densities.
