**Central idea.** For odd $n$, $\{M_n\le t\}=\{\sum Y_i\ge (n+1)/2\}$ turns the median into a binomial tail; the threshold is a difference quotient converging to $-2af(\mu)$.

**Solution.** (a) With $Y_i = I(X_i\le \mu+a/\sqrt n)\sim$ Bernoulli$(p_n)$, $p_n = F(\mu+a/\sqrt n)$, and $n$ odd, $\{M_n\le\mu+a/\sqrt n\} = \{\sum_i Y_i\ge (n+1)/2\}$. Standardizing gives the displayed identity.
(b) $p_n = F(\mu+a/\sqrt n)\to F(\mu)=1/2$ by continuity. Write the threshold as
$$\frac{\frac{(n+1)/2-np_n}{\sqrt n}}{\sqrt{p_n(1-p_n)}},\qquad \frac{(n+1)/2-np_n}{\sqrt n} = -\sqrt n\left[F\!\left(\mu+\frac{a}{\sqrt n}\right)-F(\mu)\right]+\frac{1}{2\sqrt n}\to -aF'(\mu) = -af(\mu),$$
the limit form of the derivative. Since $\sqrt{p_n(1-p_n)}\to 1/2$, the threshold $\to -2af(\mu)$.
(c) $P(Z\ge -2af(\mu)) = P(Z\le 2af(\mu)) = \Phi(2af(\mu)) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{a}{1/[2f(\mu)]}\right)$, which is exactly the cdf of $n\!\left(0,\frac{1}{[2f(\mu)]^2}\right)$ evaluated at $a$. Convergence of cdfs at all $a$ gives $\sqrt n(M_n-\mu)\xrightarrow{d} n(0,1/[2f(\mu)]^2)$. (Because $p_n$ varies with $n$, the $Y_i$ form a triangular array, so the ordinary CLT does not apply directly — a Lindeberg-type argument is needed for full rigor.)
