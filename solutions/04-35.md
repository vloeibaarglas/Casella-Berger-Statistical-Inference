**Central idea.** Both are the variance decomposition $\mathrm{Var} = \mathrm{E}[\mathrm{Var}(\cdot|\cdot)]+\mathrm{Var}(\mathrm{E}[\cdot|\cdot])$, regrouped to isolate the non-hierarchical variance plus the hierarchy's inflation.

**Solution.** (a) $\mathrm{Var}\,X = \mathrm{E}[nP(1-P)]+\mathrm{Var}(nP) = n(\mathrm{E}P-\mathrm{E}P^2)+n^2\mathrm{Var}\,P$. Since $\mathrm{E}P^2 = (\mathrm{E}P)^2+\mathrm{Var}\,P$, this is $n\mathrm{E}P(1-\mathrm{E}P)-n\mathrm{Var}\,P+n^2\mathrm{Var}\,P = n\mathrm{E}P(1-\mathrm{E}P)+n(n-1)\mathrm{Var}\,P$.
(b) $\mathrm{Var}\,Y = \mathrm{E}[\Lambda]+\mathrm{Var}(\Lambda) = \alpha\beta+\alpha\beta^2$. With $\mu = \alpha\beta$, $\alpha\beta^2 = \mu^2/\alpha$, so $\mathrm{Var}\,Y = \mu+\mu^2/\alpha$. The "extra-Poisson" variation is $\mathrm{Var}\,\Lambda = \mu^2/\alpha$ — the variance injected by the random $\Lambda$.
