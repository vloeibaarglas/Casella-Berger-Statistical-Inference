**Central idea.** The posterior is proportional to the normal likelihood times $e^{-|\theta|/a}$; as $a \to \infty$ the prior flattens to (improper) uniform and the posterior tends to $n(\bar x, \sigma^2/n)$, whose tail is $1$ minus the one-sided p-value.

**Solution.** (a) By sufficiency,
$$P(\theta > K \mid \mathbf x, a) = \frac{\int_K^\infty \exp\left(-\frac{n}{2\sigma^2}(\bar x-\theta)^2 - \frac{|\theta|}{a}\right)d\theta}{\int_{-\infty}^\infty \exp\left(-\frac{n}{2\sigma^2}(\bar x-\theta)^2 - \frac{|\theta|}{a}\right)d\theta}.$$
(b) As $a \to \infty$, $e^{-|\theta|/a} \to 1$ pointwise and dominated convergence (dominated by the integrable normal kernel) gives
$$\lim_{a\to\infty} P(\theta > K \mid \mathbf x, a) = \frac{\int_K^\infty \exp(-\frac{n}{2\sigma^2}(\bar x-\theta)^2)d\theta}{\int_{-\infty}^\infty \exp(-\frac{n}{2\sigma^2}(\bar x-\theta)^2)d\theta} = 1 - \Phi\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}(K-\bar x)}{\sigma}\right).$$
(c) The classical one-sided p-value (observed $\bar x$) is $p = \sup_{\theta \le 0} P_\theta(\bar X \ge \bar x) = 1 - \Phi(\sqrt{n}\bar x/\sigma)$. With $K = 0$, the limiting posterior is $P(\theta > 0 \mid \mathbf x) \to \Phi(\sqrt{n}\bar x/\sigma) = 1 - p$: in the one-sided problem the (flat-prior) posterior probability of $H_1$ is exactly one minus the p-value — no conflict.
