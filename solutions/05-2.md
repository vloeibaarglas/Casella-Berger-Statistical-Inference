**Central idea.** For $N = n$ we need $X_1$ to be the largest of $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ but not of $X_1, \ldots, X_{n+1}$ — i.e., $X_1$ ranks exactly second among the first $n+1$ observations, whose ranks are all equally likely.

**Solution.** (a) Let $N = \min\{n \geq 1 : X_{n+1} > X_1\}$ (with $N = 1$ if $X_2 > X_1$). Then
$$\{N = n\} = \{X_2 < X_1, \ldots, X_n < X_1, X_{n+1} > X_1\},$$
i.e., among $X_1, \ldots, X_{n+1}$, the largest is $X_{n+1}$ and the second largest is $X_1$. Since the $X_i$ are iid continuous, all $(n+1)!$ orderings of $X_1, \ldots, X_{n+1}$ are equally likely, and $(n-1)!$ of them have $X_{n+1}$ largest and $X_1$ second largest. Hence
$$P(N = n) = \frac{(n-1)!}{(n+1)!} = \frac{1}{n(n+1)}, \qquad n = 1, 2, \ldots$$
(This sums to 1 since $\sum_{n \geq 1} [1/n - 1/(n+1)] = 1$, so $X_1$ is exceeded eventually with probability 1.)
(b) $E[N] = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \cdot \frac{1}{n(n+1)} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n+1} = \infty$, the harmonic series. The waiting time is almost surely finite but has infinite mean — a few enormous droughts dominate the average.
