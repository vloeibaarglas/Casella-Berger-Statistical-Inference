**Central idea.** The textbook proof is algebraic in $\phi$, $f_0$, $f_1$ — replacing integrals by sums changes nothing, and attainability removes the boundary randomization.

**Solution.** Let $f_0,f_1$ be the pmfs under $H_0,H_1$ and let $\phi^*$ be the NP test: $\phi^*(x)=1$ if $f_1(x)>kf_0(x)$, $0$ if $f_1(x)<kf_0(x)$ (attainability means $P_{0}(f_1=kf_0)=0$ or the boundary can be assigned without randomization while keeping size $\alpha$). Let $\phi$ be any test with $E_0\phi \le \alpha = E_0\phi^*$. Then
$$E_1\phi^*-E_1\phi = \sum_x \bigl(\phi^*(x)-\phi(x)\bigr)\bigl(f_1(x)-kf_0(x)\bigr) + k\bigl(E_0\phi^*-E_0\phi\bigr).$$
In the sum, each summand is $\ge 0$: where $f_1>kf_0$, $\phi^*=1\ge\phi$; where $f_1<kf_0$, $\phi^*=0\le\phi$; where equal, the factor vanishes. The last term is $\ge 0$ since $k\ge0$ and $E_0\phi^*-E_0\phi\ge0$. Hence $E_1\phi^*\ge E_1\phi$: $\phi^*$ is most powerful. The argument is identical to the continuous case with $\sum$ in place of $\int$.
