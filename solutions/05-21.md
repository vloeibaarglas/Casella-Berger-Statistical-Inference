**Central idea.** The max exceeds the median unless *all* observations fall below it; each observation is below the median with probability $1/2$, independently.

**Solution.** Let $m$ be the population median, $P(X_i \leq m) = 1/2$. Then $P(\max(X_1, X_2) > m) = 1 - P(X_1 \leq m, X_2 \leq m) = 1 - (1/2)^2 = 3/4$. In general, $P(\max_{1\leq i\leq n} X_i > m) = 1 - 2^{-n}$.
