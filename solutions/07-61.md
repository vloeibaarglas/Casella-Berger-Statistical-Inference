**Central idea.** The $\chi^2_\nu$ density of $\nu S^2/\sigma^2$ contributes $-\frac{\nu}{2}(s^2/\sigma^2)$ from the exponential and $+\frac{\nu}{2}\log(s^2/\sigma^2)$ from the Jacobian/power — exactly Stein's loss up to affine transformation.
**Solution.** For the stated $\nu>0$, $f(s^2\mid\sigma^2)=\frac{\nu}{\sigma^2}f_{\chi^2_\nu}(\nu s^2/\sigma^2)$, so
$$\log L=-\frac{\nu}{2}\frac{s^2}{\sigma^2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\log\frac{s^2}{\sigma^2}+K_3,$$
i.e. $K_1=-\nu/2$, $K_2=-\nu/2$, $K_3$ collecting terms free of $\sigma^2$. Hence $-\log L(\sigma^2\mid s^2)=\frac{\nu}{2}\big[\frac{s^2}{\sigma^2}-\log\frac{s^2}{\sigma^2}-1\big]+\text{const}=\frac{\nu}{2}L(\sigma^2,s^2)+\text{const}$, where $L(\sigma^2,a)=\frac{a}{\sigma^2}-1-\log\frac{a}{\sigma^2}$ is Stein's loss of Example 7.3.27 (with $a=s^2$): maximizing the normal likelihood in $\sigma^2$ is equivalent to minimizing Stein's loss.
