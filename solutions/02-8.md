**Central idea.** Check monotonicity, right-continuity, and the 0/1 limits; the generalized inverse $F_X^{-1}(y)=\inf\{x:F_X(x)\ge y\}$ inverts flat stretches to their left endpoint and jumps to the jump location.

**Solution.** Each function is nondecreasing, right-continuous, with limits 0 at $-\infty$ and 1 at $+\infty$, hence a cdf.
(a) Continuous and strictly increasing on $[0,\infty)$: $F_X^{-1}(y)=-\log(1-y)$, $0<y<1$.
(b) Flat at $1/2$ on $[0,1)$: $F_X^{-1}(y)=\log(2y)$ for $0<y\le\frac12$ (solving $e^x/2=y$), and $F_X^{-1}(y)=1-\log(2(1-y))$ for $\frac12<y<1$ (solving $1-e^{1-x}/2=y$); at $y=\frac12$, $\inf\{x:F_X(x)\ge\frac12\}=0$.
(c) Jump of size $1/2$ at 0 ($P(X=0)=1/2$), yet right-continuous. $F_X^{-1}(y)=\log(4y)$ for $0<y\le\frac14$; $=0$ for $\frac14<y\le\frac34$ (every $y$ in the jump gap inverts to the jump point 0); $=-\log(4(1-y))$ for $\frac34<y<1$.
