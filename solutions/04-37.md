**Central idea.** (a) *As stated for general $n$ this is incorrect*: with $C=(1+P)/2$ the mixing variable, beta-binomial$(n,\alpha,\beta)$ would require $\mathrm{E}[C^r] = (\alpha)^{\overline{r}}/(\alpha+\beta)^{\overline{r}}$ for $r=1,\dots,n$, but $C \ge 1/2$ a.s. while a beta$(\alpha,\beta)$ charges $(0,1/2)$ — and concretely, for $n=3$, $P\sim$ uniform$(0,1)$, the first two moments force $(\alpha,\beta)=(6,2)$ yet $\mathrm{E}[C^3]=15/32 \ne 7/15$. (The claim is true for $n\le2$, where only two moments must match.) (b) The mean and variance are still elementary via iterated expectation.

**Audit addition.** The authors' first-printing correction asks for a *mixture* of beta-binomials. Introduce discrimination indicators $D_i\mid P\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(P)$ and independent guesses $G_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$, and put $X_i=D_i+(1-D_i)G_i$. Conditional on $J=\sum G_i=j$, the remaining $n-j$ trials mix over $P\sim\mathrm{Beta}(a,b)$, so
$$\sum X_i\mid J=j\ \stackrel d=\ j+\mathrm{BetaBinomial}(n-j,a,b),\qquad J\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,1/2).$$
This gives the intended mixture of shifted beta-binomial laws and an explicit marginal. The counterexample below correctly disproves a single beta-binomial law. For a fixed finite $n$, differing mixing supports alone do not prove that; the moment counterexample does.

**Solution.** (a) *Erratum.* Let $C = (1+P)/2$, so $X_i|C$ are iid Bernoulli$(C)$ and $\mathrm{E}[(\Sigma X_i)_{\underline{r}}] = (n)_{\underline{r}}\mathrm{E}[C^r]$. If $\Sigma X_i$ were beta-binomial$(n,\alpha,\beta)$, we would need $\mathrm{E}[C^r] = (\alpha)^{\overline{r}}/(\alpha+\beta)^{\overline{r}}$ for $r=1,\dots,n$. Take $a=b=1$ ($P\sim$ uniform$(0,1)$) and $n=3$: $\mathrm{E}[C]=3/4$, $\mathrm{E}[C^2]=7/12$ force $\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)=3/4$ and $\alpha(\alpha+1)/((\alpha+\beta)(\alpha+\beta+1))=7/12$, i.e. $(\alpha,\beta)=(6,2)$ — but then beta$(6,2)$ predicts $\mathrm{E}[C^3]=6\cdot7\cdot8/(8\cdot9\cdot10)=7/15$, whereas $\mathrm{E}[C^3]=\int_0^1((1+p)/2)^3dp=15/32$. Contradiction, so no beta-binomial fits. (For $n\le2$ the claim holds, since only the first two moments must match a beta.)
(b) With $\mu = \mathrm{E}[C] = (2a+b)/(2(a+b))$: $\mathrm{E}[\Sigma X_i] = n\mu = \frac{n(2a+b)}{2(a+b)}$. $\mathrm{Var}(\Sigma X_i) = n\mu(1-\mu)+n(n-1)\mathrm{Var}(C)$ with $\mathrm{Var}(C) = \mathrm{Var}(P)/4 = \frac{ab}{4(a+b)^2(a+b+1)}$.
