**Central idea.** Under (8.3.13), $R(\theta) = c(\theta-\theta_0)^2\beta(\theta)$ on $H_0$ and $b(\theta_0-\theta)(1-\beta(\theta))$ on $H_1$, where $\beta(\theta) = \Phi(-z_\alpha-(\theta-\theta_0))$; larger $\alpha$ shifts risk from $H_1$ to $H_0$.

**Solution.** With $d = \theta-\theta_0$ and $\beta(\theta) = P_\theta(X < \theta_0-z_\alpha) = \Phi(-z_\alpha-d)$: for $\theta \ge \theta_0$, $R(\theta) = c\,d^2\beta(\theta)$; for $\theta < \theta_0$, $R(\theta) = b(-d)(1-\beta(\theta))$.
(a) $b = c = 1$:

![Risk functions for $\alpha=.1,.3,.5$ with $b=c=1$.]({{ '/plots/ex855_risk_a.png' | relative_url }})

(b) $b = 3$, $c = 1$:

![Risk functions for $\alpha=.1,.3,.5$ with $b=3,c=1$.]({{ '/plots/ex855_risk_b.png' | relative_url }})

In both, larger $\alpha$ (easier rejection) lowers risk on $H_1$ ($\theta < \theta_0$) at the cost of higher risk on $H_0$; with $b = 3$ the $H_1$ risks are tripled, favoring larger $\alpha$. (c) The powers $\beta(\theta) = \Phi(-z_\alpha-d)$:

![Power functions of the three tests.]({{ '/plots/ex855_power.png' | relative_url }})

Risk is power scaled by the loss: on $H_0$, $R = c\,d^2\beta$ (grows quadratically in $d$ times power); on $H_1$, $R = b|d|(1-\beta)$ (grows linearly in $|d|$ times Type II probability). The crossing of the risk curves reflects the $\alpha$ tradeoff seen in the powers.
