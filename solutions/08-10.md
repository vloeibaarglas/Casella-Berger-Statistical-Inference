**Central idea.** The gamma posterior turns $P(\lambda \le \lambda_0 \mid \mathbf x)$ into a chi-squared tail probability after the scale change $\text{gamma}(a,b) =_d \frac b2\chi^2_{2a}$.

**Solution.** With the C&B gamma$(\alpha,\beta)$ (mean $\alpha\beta$), the posterior is $\lambda \mid \mathbf x \sim \text{gamma}(\alpha + \sum x_i,\ \beta/(1+n\beta))$.
(a)
$$P(H_0 \mid \mathbf x) = P(\lambda \le \lambda_0 \mid \mathbf x) = F_{\text{gamma}}\!\left(\lambda_0;\ \alpha+\textstyle\sum x_i,\ \tfrac{\beta}{1+n\beta}\right), \qquad P(H_1 \mid \mathbf x) = 1 - P(H_0 \mid \mathbf x),$$
where $F_{\text{gamma}}$ is the cdf. The Bayes test (0–1 loss) decides $H_1$ iff $P(H_1 \mid \mathbf x) > 1/2$.
(b) $\text{gamma}(5/2, 2) = \chi^2_5$, and the posterior is $\text{gamma}(5/2+\sum x_i,\ 2/(1+2n))$. Since $\text{gamma}(a,b) =_d \frac b2\,\chi^2_{2a}$,
$$\lambda \mid \mathbf x \ =_d\ \frac{1}{1+2n}\,\chi^2_{\,5+2\sum x_i},$$
so $P(H_0 \mid \mathbf x) = P\bigl(\chi^2_{5+2\sum x_i} \le (1+2n)\lambda_0\bigr)$, with integer df $5+2\sum x_i$. Look up $(1+2n)\lambda_0$ in a $\chi^2_{5+2\sum x_i}$ table: decide $H_1$ iff the upper-tail probability exceeds $1/2$ (equivalently, iff $(1+2n)\lambda_0$ is below the median of that chi-squared distribution).
