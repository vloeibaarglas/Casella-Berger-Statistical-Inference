**Central idea.** Wilks' $Q$ for $p$ is $\sqrt n\,[r(1-p)-p\bar X]/\sqrt{r(1-p)}$; for the mean $\mu=r(1-p)/p$ the pivot $(\bar X-\mu)/\sqrt{\mu(\mu+r)/(nr)}$ inverts to a quadratic, with $\pm 1/(2n)$ continuity correction.

**Solution.** (a) $\log L(p\mid x) = nr\log p+\sum x_i\log(1-p)+c$ ($r$ known). $\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\log L = \frac{nr}{p}-\frac{n\bar x}{1-p} = \frac{n[r(1-p)-p\bar x]}{p(1-p)}$, $-E_p[\frac{\partial^2}{\partial p^2}\log L] = \frac{nr}{p^2(1-p)}$. Hence Wilks' statistic is
$$Q = \frac{\sqrt n\,[r(1-p)-p\bar X]}{\sqrt{r(1-p)}}\;\dot\sim\;n(0,1),$$
and $\{p:|Q|\le z_{\alpha/2}\}$ is the interval — i.e. $n[r-p(r+\bar X)]^2\le z_{\alpha/2}^2r(1-p)$, a quadratic in $p$ whose roots are the endpoints.
(b) $E\bar X=\mu=r(1-p)/p$, $\mathrm{Var}(\bar X)=r(1-p)/(np^2)=\mu(\mu+r)/(nr)$, so $\frac{\bar X-\mu}{\sqrt{\mu(\mu+r)/(nr)}}\xrightarrow{d}n(0,1)$. Inverting $|\bar X-\mu|/\sqrt{\mu(\mu+r)/(nr)}\le z_{\alpha/2}$ gives the quadratic $\mu^2(1-\frac{z^2}{nr})-\mu(2\bar X+\frac{z^2}{n})+\bar X^2\le 0$; its roots are the endpoints. Continuity correction ($\bar X$ takes values $k/n$): the upper endpoint is the larger root with $\bar X$ replaced by $\bar X+1/(2n)$, the lower endpoint the smaller root with $\bar X-1/(2n)$ (as in Example 10.4.6).
(c) Aphid data $155,104,66,50,36,40,30,35,42$ ($n=9$, $\bar x=62$, $S^2=1735.75$): MOM $\hat r=\bar x^2/(S^2-\bar x)=2.30$. The 90% NB interval for the mean is $(45.2,97.8)$ (with continuity correction $(45.2,97.9)$). The Poisson-based 90% intervals are $(57.7,66.3)$ (Wald), $(57.8,66.5)$ (score), $(57.8,66.4)$ (LRT) — much narrower, because the Poisson model forces $\mathrm{Var}=\mathrm{mean}$ while the data are wildly overdispersed ($S^2=1736\gg 62$); the NB interval honestly reflects the extra-Poisson variation.
