**Central idea.** Normalize $(S^2)^{p/2}$ using its chi-square moment. When $\mu$ is unknown, the complete sufficient statistic is the **pair** $(\bar X,S^2)$, not $S^2$ alone.

**Solution.** For $n\ge2$, $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$, so
$$E_{\mu,\sigma^2}[(S^2)^{p/2}]=\sigma^p\frac{2^{p/2}\Gamma((n-1+p)/2)}{(n-1)^{p/2}\Gamma((n-1)/2)}.$$
Thus
$$\delta=\frac{(n-1)^{p/2}\Gamma((n-1)/2)}{2^{p/2}\Gamma((n-1+p)/2)}(S^2)^{p/2}$$
is unbiased for $\sigma^p$ and has finite variance for $p>0$. The normal family is a full two-parameter exponential family with natural statistic $(\sum X_i,\sum X_i^2)$ and natural parameter space $\{(\eta_1,\eta_2):\eta_1\in\mathbb R,\eta_2<0\}$, which is open. Therefore the equivalent pair $(\bar X,S^2)$ is complete sufficient. Since $\delta$ is a function of this pair, Lehmann–Scheffé proves it is the UMVUE. The estimator is correct, but $S^2$ alone is not sufficient for the family with unknown mean.
