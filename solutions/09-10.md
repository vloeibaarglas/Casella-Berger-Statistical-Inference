**Central idea.** This is the change-of-variables formula: the Jacobian $|\partial Q/\partial t|$ absorbs the $\theta$-dependence, leaving the density of $Q$ equal to $g$.

**Solution.** (a) (9.2.11) is $f(t\mid\theta) = g(Q(t,\theta))\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Q(t,\theta)\right|$. By Theorem 2.1.5, for $q = Q(t,\theta)$ monotone in $t$, $f_Q(q\mid\theta) = f_T(t\mid\theta)/|\partial q/\partial t| = g(q)$ on the range of $Q$ — independent of $\theta$. Hence $Q(T,\theta)$ is pivotal.
(b) $\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F_\theta(t)\right| = f(t\mid\theta)$, so (9.2.11) holds with $g \equiv 1$. Then $Q(T,\theta) = F_\theta(T) \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)$, a pivot.
