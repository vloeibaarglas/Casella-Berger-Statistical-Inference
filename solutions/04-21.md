**Central idea.** The $\chi^2_2$ law for $R^2$ makes $R$ Rayleigh, and the polar Jacobian $r$ converts $re^{-r^2/2}/(2\pi)$ into the iid $n(0,1)$ density — this is the Box–Muller construction.

**Solution.** $R^2 \sim \chi^2_2$ is exponential with mean $2$, so $f_R(r) = re^{-r^2/2}$, $r>0$, and $f_{R,\Theta}(r,\theta) = \frac{1}{2\pi}re^{-r^2/2}$. The polar transformation has Jacobian $r$, so $f_{X,Y}(x,y) = \frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-(x^2+y^2)/2}$: $X$ and $Y$ are independent $n(0,1)$.
