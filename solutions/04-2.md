**Central idea.** Expectation is a sum/integral, so linearity and order-preservation pass straight through; (c) and (d) reduce to (a) and (b).

**Solution.** (a) $\mathrm{E}(ag_1+bg_2+c) = \sum\sum (ag_1+bg_2+c)f = a\sum\sum g_1f + b\sum\sum g_2f + c\sum\sum f$ (integrals in the continuous case), giving the result since $\sum\sum f = 1$. (b) Every term $g_1(x,y)f(x,y) \ge 0$, so the sum/integral is $\ge 0$. (c) Apply (b) to $g_1-g_2 \ge 0$ and use (a): $\mathrm{E}(g_1)-\mathrm{E}(g_2) = \mathrm{E}(g_1-g_2) \ge 0$. (d) From $g_1 \ge a$ and (c), $\mathrm{E}(g_1) \ge \mathrm{E}(a) = a$; from $g_1 \le b$, $\mathrm{E}(g_1) \le b$.
