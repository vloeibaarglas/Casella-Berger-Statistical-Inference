**Central idea.** A weighted version of the Lemma 5.3.2 sum-of-squares identity splits off one independent $\chi^2_1$ piece at a time by induction.

**Solution.** $\bar{U}_i = \bar{Y}_{i\cdot} - \theta_i \sim n(0,\sigma^2/n_i)$ independently, and $(\bar{Y}_{i\cdot}-\bar{\bar{Y}}) - (\theta_i-\bar\theta) = \bar{U}_i - \bar{\bar{U}}$. Let $N_m = \sum_{i=1}^m n_i$, $W_m = \sum_{i=1}^m n_i\bar{U}_i/N_m$. The weighted identity
$$\sum_{i=1}^m n_i(\bar{U}_i - W_m)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{m-1} n_i(\bar{U}_i - W_{m-1})^2 + \frac{N_{m-1}n_m}{N_m}(\bar{U}_m - W_{m-1})^2$$
holds by algebra (write $\bar{U}_i - W_m = (\bar{U}_i - W_{m-1}) + (W_{m-1}-W_m)$; the cross term vanishes since $\sum_{i<m}n_i(\bar{U}_i-W_{m-1}) = 0$). Now $\bar{U}_m - W_{m-1} \sim n(0, \sigma^2/n_m + \sigma^2/N_{m-1}) = n(0, \sigma^2 N_m/(n_mN_{m-1}))$, independent of $\bar{U}_1,\dots,\bar{U}_{m-1}$, so $\frac{N_{m-1}n_m}{N_m}(\bar{U}_m-W_{m-1})^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_1$ independently of the first sum. Induction from $m=2$ (where the identity gives $\frac{n_1n_2}{n_1+n_2}(\bar{U}_1-\bar{U}_2)^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_1$) yields $\sum_{i=1}^k n_i(\bar{U}_i-\bar{\bar{U}})^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_{k-1}$.
