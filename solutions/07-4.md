**Central idea.** The unrestricted maximizer is $\bar x$; if it violates the constraint $\theta\ge 0$, the constrained maximum occurs at the boundary because the likelihood is monotone on either side of $\bar x$.
**Solution.** In Example 7.2.8 the log-likelihood (normal with known variance, or any location family with log-concave likelihood) is strictly increasing for $\theta<\bar x$ and strictly decreasing for $\theta>\bar x$, with unique unconstrained maximizer $\bar x$. If $\bar x\ge 0$, the constraint $\theta\ge 0$ is inactive and $\hat\theta=\bar x$. If $\bar x<0$, then on the feasible set $\theta\ge 0>\bar x$ the log-likelihood is strictly decreasing, so the maximum is at the boundary $\hat\theta=0$. Hence $\hat\theta=\max(\bar x,0)$, as asserted.
