**Central idea.** Same argument as part (a) with the monotonicity reversed: when $F_T(t\mid\theta)$ increases in $\theta$, small cdf values correspond to small $\theta$.

**Solution.** Assume $F_T(t\mid\theta)$ is increasing in $\theta$ for each $t$, and put $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = \alpha$. By the probability integral transform, $\{t : \alpha_1 \le F_T(t\mid\theta_0) \le 1-\alpha_2\}$ is a $1-\alpha$ acceptance region. Define $\theta_L(t)$, $\theta_U(t)$ by $F_T(t\mid\theta_U) = 1-\alpha_2$, $F_T(t\mid\theta_L) = \alpha_1$ (unique since $1-\alpha_2 > \alpha_1$ and $F_T$ is continuous and strictly monotone in $\theta$ where it matters). Increasingness gives $F_T(t\mid\theta) < \alpha_1 \iff \theta < \theta_L(t)$ and $F_T(t\mid\theta) > 1-\alpha_2 \iff \theta > \theta_U(t)$, whence $\{\theta : \alpha_1 \le F_T(t\mid\theta) \le 1-\alpha_2\} = [\theta_L(t),\theta_U(t)]$, a $1-\alpha$ interval.
