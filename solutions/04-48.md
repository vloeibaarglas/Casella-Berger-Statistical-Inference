**Central idea.** In $x$, the exponent is quadratic — complete the square to read off the normal conditional; for (b) solve $\nabla\log f = 0$ to find three critical points, two of which are local maxima at $(4\pm\sqrt{15},\,4\mp\sqrt{15})$.

**Solution.** (a) As a function of $x$, the exponent is $-\frac12[(Ay^2+1)x^2-2(By+C)x+(y^2-2Dy)]$; completing the square gives $\exp(-\frac{Ay^2+1}{2}(x-\frac{By+C}{Ay^2+1})^2)$: $X|Y=y \sim n\left(\frac{By+C}{Ay^2+1},\frac{1}{Ay^2+1}\right)$. Similarly $Y|X=x \sim n\left(\frac{Bx+D}{Ax^2+1},\frac{1}{Ax^2+1}\right)$.
(b) With $A=1,B=0,C=D=8$: $\nabla\log f = 0$ gives $x(y^2+1) = 8$, $y(x^2+1) = 8$. Solutions: $x=y\approx1.83$ (unique positive root of $x^3+x=8$), and $xy=1$, $x+1/x=8$, i.e. $(4+\sqrt{15},4-\sqrt{15})$ and $(4-\sqrt{15},4+\sqrt{15})$. The Hessian of $\log f$ is $\begin{pmatrix}-(y^2+1) & -2xy\\ -2xy & -(x^2+1)\end{pmatrix}$: at $(4+\sqrt{15},4-\sqrt{15})$ it is $\begin{pmatrix}-1.02&-2\\-2&-63\end{pmatrix}$ (determinant $>0$, trace $<0$: local max), likewise by symmetry at $(4-\sqrt{15},4+\sqrt{15})$, while $(1.83,1.83)$ is a saddle (determinant $<0$). Two local maxima: the joint distribution is bimodal.
