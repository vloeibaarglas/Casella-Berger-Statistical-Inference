**Central idea.** Each $m$-test is just the one-sided $z$-test on $m$ observations; its power $1 - \Phi(z_\alpha - \sqrt{m}(\theta-\theta_0)/\sigma)$ is increasing in both $\theta$ and $m$.

**Solution.** For fixed $m$, $\bar X_m \sim n(\theta, \sigma^2/m)$, so $P_\theta(\text{reject}) = 1 - \Phi\left(z_\alpha - \frac{\sqrt{m}(\theta-\theta_0)}{\sigma}\right)$, increasing in $\theta$ and equal to $\alpha$ at $\theta = \theta_0$: size $\alpha$. For $\theta > \theta_0$ it exceeds $\alpha$: unbiased. The power curves ($\alpha = .05$, $\sigma = 1$, versus $\theta - \theta_0$) rise more steeply for larger $m$: the full-sample ($m = 4$) test is uniformly most powerful among the four, the $m = 1$ test weakest — all are unbiased size-$\alpha$ tests.
