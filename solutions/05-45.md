**Central idea.** Each component survives 150h with probability $p = P(X > 150)$ (Erlang/Poisson sum for integer $a$, numerical otherwise); the number surviving is Binomial$(c,p)$, and we need its upper tail at $0.75c$.

**Solution.** (C&B's gamma$(a,b)$ uses $b$ as the *scale*: $E[X] = ab$.) Let $p = P(X > 150)$; the number of survivors is Binomial$(c,p)$.
(a) $a = 4$ integer: $p = P(\text{Gamma}(4,5) > 150) = \sum_{j=0}^{3}e^{-30}\frac{30^j}{j!} = 4981e^{-30} \approx 4.66\times10^{-10}$. Then $P(\text{Bin}(300,p) \geq 225) \approx 0$ (about $10^{-2100}$ — numerically zero).
(b) $a = 20$ integer: $p = \sum_{j=0}^{19}e^{-30}\frac{30^j}{j!} = P(\text{Poisson}(30) \leq 19) \approx 0.0219$. Then $P(\text{Bin}(100,p) \geq 75) \approx 4.4\times10^{-102} \approx 0$.
(c) $a = 20.7$: no closed form; numerical integration gives $p = P(\text{Gamma}(20.7,5) > 150) \approx 0.0307$. Then $P(\text{Bin}(100,p) \geq 75) \approx 4.0\times10^{-91} \approx 0$.
In all three cases the required probability is essentially zero: with these gamma lifetimes, getting 75% of components past 150 hours is virtually impossible (the mean lifetimes are $20$, $100$, $103.5$ hours respectively, far below 150).
