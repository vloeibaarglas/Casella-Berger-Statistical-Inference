**Central idea.** The figure gives height $Y=d\tan X$, a strictly increasing transformation; its tail is too heavy for a finite mean.

**Solution.** The triangle has adjacent side $d$ and angle $X$ at the origin, so $Y=d\tan X$, strictly increasing on $(0,\pi/2)$ with range $(0,\infty)$. With $x=\arctan(y/d)$, $dx/dy=d/(d^2+y^2)$, and $f_X=2/\pi$,
$$f_Y(y)=\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{d}{d^2+y^2},\qquad y>0.$$
$EY=\int_0^\infty y\frac{2d}{\pi(d^2+y^2)}dy=+\infty$ (the integrand is $\sim\frac{2d}{\pi y}$, logarithmically divergent), so the mean does not exist as a finite number.
