**Central idea.** The "plus four" interval $\tilde p\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\tilde p(1-\tilde p)/(n+4)}$ mimics the score interval's shrinkage; exact coverage/length calculations show it tracks the score interval closely and dominates Wald.

**Solution.** Exact coverage and expected length (95% nominal):

| $n=20$, $p$ | 0.1 | 0.3 | 0.5 | 0.7 | 0.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| coverage: score | 0.957 | 0.975 | 0.959 | 0.975 | 0.957 |
| coverage: Agresti–Coull | 0.957 | 0.975 | 0.959 | 0.975 | 0.957 |
| coverage: Wald | 0.876 | 0.947 | 0.959 | 0.947 | 0.876 |
| length: score | 0.263 | 0.364 | 0.393 | 0.364 | 0.263 |
| length: AC | 0.292 | 0.370 | 0.393 | 0.370 | 0.292 |

(At $n=40$ the pattern is the same: AC coverage 0.944–0.962 vs.\ score 0.943–0.962, Wald dipping to 0.914.) Yes — the Agresti–Coull interval is a reasonable alternative: its coverage is virtually identical to the score interval's (never below nominal here, unlike Wald's dip to 0.876), its length is within a few percent of the score length, and the formula is no harder than Wald's.
