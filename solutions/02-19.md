**Central idea.** $E(X-a)^2=EX^2-2aEX+a^2$ is a quadratic in $a$; differentiating under the expectation is justified by a finite second moment.

**Solution.** Assume $EX^2<\infty$ (hence $E|X|<\infty$). Then $h(a)=E(X-a)^2=\int(x-a)^2\,dF_X(x)$ may be differentiated under the integral by dominated convergence, since $\frac{d}{da}(x-a)^2=-2(x-a)$ is dominated near any $a_0$ by the integrable function $2|x|+2(|a_0|+1)$:
$$h'(a)=E[-2(X-a)]=-2EX+2a,$$
so $h'(a)=0\iff a=EX$. Moreover $h''(a)=2>0$, so $a=EX$ is the unique global minimizer. Needed: $X$ has finite second moment (so $EX$ exists and $h$ is finite); no continuity of $F_X$ and no density $f_X$ are required.
