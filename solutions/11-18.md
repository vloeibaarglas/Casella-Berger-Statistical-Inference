**Central idea.** Conditioning on the event that suggested the cutoff ($Y > b$) can only inflate the rejection probability, since $\{Y > a\} \subset \{Y > b\}$.

**Solution.** (a) Since $a > b$, $\{Y > a\} \subset \{Y > b\}$, so $P(Y > a \mid Y > b) = P(Y > a)/P(Y > b)$. With $P(Y > b) < 1$, dividing by it strictly increases: $P(Y > a \mid Y > b) > P(Y > a)$.
(b) Suppose the data suggest testing at a cutoff $a$ slightly below the observed statistic — the hypothesis is entertained only because the data already exceeded some threshold $b < a$ (the "suggestion" event). The nominal size $P(Y > a)$ is computed unconditionally, but the actual rejection probability of this data-suggested procedure is $P(Y > a \mid Y > b) > P(Y > a)$: data snooping inflates the true Type I error above the advertised level.
