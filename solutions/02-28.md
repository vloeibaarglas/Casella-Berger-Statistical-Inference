**Central idea.** Odd central moments vanish under symmetry; $\alpha_4$ is scale-free, so the normal's 3 is the benchmark.

**Solution.** (a) $\mu_3=E[(X-a)^3]=\int_{-\infty}^\infty u^3f(a+u)\,du=0$, since $u^3$ is odd and $f(a+u)$ is even in $u$ (assuming $\mu_3$ exists). Hence $\alpha_3=0$.
(b) For Exp$(1)$: $EX=1$, $\mu_2=1$, $\mu_3=E[(X-1)^3]=E[X^3]-3E[X^2]+3EX-1=6-6+3-1=2$. So $\alpha_3=2>0$, right-skewed.
(c) Normal: $\mu_4=3$, $\mu_2=1$, so $\alpha_4=3$ (the benchmark). Uniform$(-1,1)$: $\mu_2=1/3$, $\mu_4=\frac12\int_{-1}^1x^4dx=\frac15$, so $\alpha_4=\frac{1/5}{1/9}=\frac95=1.8<3$ (flatter top, lighter tails than normal). Laplace: $\mu_2=2$, $\mu_4=24$, so $\alpha_4=24/4=6>3$ (sharper peak, heavier tails than normal).
