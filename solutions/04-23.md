**Central idea.** In Example 4.3.3, $X \sim \text{beta}(\alpha,\beta)$, $Y \sim \text{beta}(\alpha+\beta,\gamma)$ are independent; both transformations lead (after a change of variable) to the same $V$-integral, which is a beta integral giving $U \sim \text{beta}(\alpha,\beta+\gamma)$.

**Solution.** (a) $x = u/v$, $y = v$, $|J| = 1/v$ on $0<u<v<1$. $f_{U,V}(u,v) = f_X(u/v)f_Y(v)/v$; integrating $v$ from $u$ to $1$:
$$f_U(u) = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(\gamma)}u^{\alpha-1}\int_u^1 (v-u)^{\beta-1}(1-v)^{\gamma-1}\,dv.$$
With $w = (v-u)/(1-u)$ the integral is $(1-u)^{\beta+\gamma-1}B(\beta,\gamma)$, so $f_U(u) = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta+\gamma)}u^{\alpha-1}(1-u)^{\beta+\gamma-1}$: $U \sim \text{beta}(\alpha,\beta+\gamma)$.
(b) $x = \sqrt{uv}$, $y = \sqrt{u/v}$, $|J| = 1/(2v)$ on $0<u<1$, $u<v<1/u$. Substituting $t = \sqrt{u/v}$ (so $dt/t = -dv/(2v)$) turns $\int_u^{1/u} f_X(\sqrt{uv})f_Y(\sqrt{u/v})\frac{dv}{2v}$ into $\int_u^1 f_X(u/t)f_Y(t)\frac{dt}{t}$ — exactly the integral in (a). Hence $U \sim \text{beta}(\alpha,\beta+\gamma)$ again.
