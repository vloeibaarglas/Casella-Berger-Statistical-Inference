**Central idea.** A one-sided Poisson $p$-value: $P(X \le 10 \mid \lambda = 15) \approx 0.12$, not significant at conventional levels.

**Solution.** Test $H_0: \lambda \ge 15$ versus $H_1: \lambda < 15$ with $X = 10$ observed. The $p$-value is the smallest $\alpha$ at which $H_0$ rejects, i.e.
$$p = \sup_{\lambda \ge 15} P_\lambda(X \le 10) = P_{\lambda=15}(X \le 10) = \sum_{k=0}^{10} e^{-15}\frac{15^k}{k!} \approx 0.119.$$
Since $p \approx 0.12 > 0.05$, the drop from 15 to 10 is within ordinary Poisson fluctuation; the claim of a drop is not justified at the usual significance levels.
