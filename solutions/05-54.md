**Central idea.** Under Poisson, $E[S^2] = \bar{x} \approx 142$, but the observed $S^2 \approx 6371$ is astronomically larger — the simulated Poisson $S^2$ distribution never gets near it. A gamma–Poisson (negative binomial) mixture adds the extra variance; method-of-moments gives $r \approx 3.2$, and the reported simulation tails support several possible shapes, including $r=5$ under a conventional two-sided tail check.

**Solution.** Here $n = 13$, $\bar{x} = 141.54$, $S^2_{\text{obs}} = 6370.6$.
(a) Simulate 20,000 samples of size 13 from Poisson$(141.54)$ and compute $S^2$ each time: *none* reached $6370.6$ (estimated $P(S^2 \geq S^2_{\text{obs}}) \approx 0$). The Poisson assumption is untenable — the data are wildly overdispersed ($S^2/\bar{x} \approx 45$).
(b) With $\mu = \bar{x}$ and $\mathrm{Var} = \mu + \mu^2/r$, matching $S^2_{\text{obs}}$ gives $\hat{r} = \bar{x}^2/(S^2_{\text{obs}}-\bar{x}) \approx 3.22$. Simulating $S^2$ under NB$(r, p)$ with mean $\bar{x}$ (via the gamma–Poisson mixture): $P(S^2 \geq S^2_{\text{obs}}) \approx 0.90, 0.70, 0.41, 0.13, 0.002, 0.000$ for $r = 1, 2, 3.2, 5, 10, 20$. A consistency cutoff requires specifying a test. For a two-sided equal-tail check at level $0.05$, all displayed shapes $r=1,2,3.2,5$ have tail probabilities between $0.025$ and $0.975$ and are not excluded; $r=10$ and $20$ are excluded by the displayed lower tail values. The earlier $r\lesssim4$ cutoff was not justified by the simulation results. (Smaller $r$ = more clumping = more overdispersion.)
