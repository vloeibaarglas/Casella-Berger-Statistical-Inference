**Central idea.** Standardizing ($X_j = \sigma_j Z_j + \mu_j$) turns each linear form into an affine function of a linear form in iid $n(0,1)$ variables; covariances are shift-invariant and scale predictably, and independence is preserved under affine maps.

**Solution.** (a) $\sum_j a_{ij}X_j = \sum_j (a_{ij}\sigma_j)Z_j + \sum_j a_{ij}\mu_j$: a linear form in the $Z$'s plus a constant (similarly for the $b$'s). Constants don't affect covariance or independence, and rescaling coefficients $a_{ij} \to a_{ij}\sigma_j$ is absorbed into the "arbitrary constants" of the special case. Hence zero covariance of the $X$-forms gives zero covariance of the corresponding $Z$-forms, which are then independent by the special case, and adding back constants preserves independence.
(b) By bilinearity and independence of the $X_j$,
$$\mathrm{Cov}\left(\sum_j a_{ij}X_j, \sum_r b_{rj}X_r\right) = \sum_{j,r} a_{ij}b_{rj}\,\mathrm{Cov}(X_j, X_r) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}b_{ij}\,\sigma_j^2,$$
since $\mathrm{Cov}(X_j, X_r) = 0$ for $j \neq r$ and $= \sigma_j^2$ for $j = r$.
