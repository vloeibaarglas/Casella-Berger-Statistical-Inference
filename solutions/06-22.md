**Central idea.** The likelihood depends on the data only through $\sum \log X_i$; $\sum X_i$ does not determine $\sum \log X_i$, and $\sum \log X_i$ is the natural statistic of a full exponential family.

**Solution.** (a) No. The joint pdf is $\theta^n\exp\big((\theta-1)\sum\log x_i\big)$, so by Theorem 6.2.13 the ratio is $\theta$-free iff $\sum\log x_i = \sum\log y_i$. But $\sum x_i = \sum y_i$ does not imply $\sum\log x_i = \sum\log y_i$ (e.g. $n = 2$, $\mathbf{x} = (0.5,0.5)$, $\mathbf{y} = (0.25,0.75)$), so $\sum X_i$ is not sufficient.
(b) $T = \sum_{i=1}^n \log X_i$ (equivalently $\prod X_i$) is sufficient by factorization, and $\log f(x\mid\theta) = (\theta-1)\log x + \log\theta$ is a one-parameter exponential family with natural parameter $\theta-1 \in (-1,\infty)$, an open set: complete by Theorem 6.2.25.
