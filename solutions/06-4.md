**Central idea.** Multiply the $n$ exponential-family densities: the $\theta$-dependent part collects into sums of the $t_i(X_j)$, and the rest factors off as $h(\mathbf{x})$.

**Solution.** The joint pdf/pmf is
$$\begin{aligned}
\prod_{j=1}^n f(x_j\mid\theta) &= \prod_{j=1}^n h(x_j)\,c(\theta)\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^k w_i(\theta)t_i(x_j)\right) \\
&= \underbrace{[c(\theta)]^n \exp\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^k w_i(\theta)\sum_{j=1}^n t_i(x_j)\right)}_{g(T(\mathbf{x})\mid\theta)}\ \underbrace{\prod_{j=1}^n h(x_j)}_{h(\mathbf{x})}.
\end{aligned}$$
This is the factorization $f(\mathbf{x}\mid\theta) = g(T(\mathbf{x})\mid\theta)h(\mathbf{x})$, so $T(\mathbf{X})$ is sufficient by the Factorization Theorem.
