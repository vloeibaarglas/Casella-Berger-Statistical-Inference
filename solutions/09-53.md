**Central idea.** The risk trades deterministic length $2bc\sigma$ against coverage $P(|Z|\le c)$; the FOC $2b\sigma = 2\phi(c)$ either has no positive solution (point estimator wins) or recovers the $z$-interval.

**Solution.** (a) $R = bE[\text{Length}] - P_\mu(\mu\in C(X)) = 2bc\sigma - P(|X-\mu|/\sigma \le c) = b(2c\sigma) - P(-c\le Z\le c)$.
(b) $\frac{d}{dc}P(-c\le Z\le c) = 2\phi(c) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-c^2/2}$; increasing in $c\ge0$ since $\phi$ decreases.
(c) At $c=0$ the derivative is $2b\sigma - 2/\sqrt{2\pi} > 0$ and increasing, so $R$ is strictly increasing: minimum at $c=0$.
(d) $2b\sigma = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-c^2/2} \iff c^2 = -2\log(b\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$. With $b$ such that $c = z_{\alpha/2}$, $C(x) = [x \pm z_{\alpha/2}\sigma]$ is the usual $1-\alpha$ interval.
