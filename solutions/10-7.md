**Central idea.** Consistency is the continuous mapping theorem (Theorem 10.1.6); efficiency is the delta method, whose asymptotic variance is exactly the Cramér–Rao bound for $\tau(\theta)$.

**Solution.** Consistency: $\hat\theta\xrightarrow{p}\theta$ and $\tau$ continuous imply $\tau(\hat\theta)\xrightarrow{p}\tau(\theta)$ (this is Theorem 10.1.6). For efficiency, the proof of Theorem 10.1.12 gives $\sqrt n(\hat\theta-\theta)\xrightarrow{d} n(0,1/I(\theta))$. By the delta method (Theorem 5.5.24),
$$\sqrt n(\tau(\hat\theta)-\tau(\theta)) \xrightarrow{d} n\!\left(0,\frac{[\tau'(\theta)]^2}{I(\theta)}\right),$$
and $[\tau'(\theta)]^2/I(\theta)$ is precisely the Cramér–Rao lower bound for estimators of $\tau(\theta)$ (Theorem 7.3.9 applied to $\tau$). Hence $\tau(\hat\theta)$ attains the bound: it is asymptotically efficient. *Remark:* the efficiency step uses the delta method, which needs $\tau$ differentiable at $\theta$ (with $\tau'(\theta)\ne 0$ for the stated normal limit); continuity alone gives only the consistency part.
