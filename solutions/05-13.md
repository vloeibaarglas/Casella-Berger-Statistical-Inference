**Central idea.** $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_{n-1}$, and $E\sqrt{\chi^2_{n-1}} = \sqrt{2}\,\Gamma(n/2)/\Gamma((n-1)/2)$; choose $c$ to cancel this factor.

**Solution.** Take $g(S^2) = cS$. With $W = (n-1)S^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_{\,n-1}$,
$$E[S] = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n-1}}E[\sqrt{W}] = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n-1}}\int_0^\infty \sqrt{w}\,\frac{w^{(n-1)/2-1}e^{-w/2}}{2^{(n-1)/2}\Gamma((n-1)/2)}\,dw = \sigma\sqrt{\frac{2}{n-1}}\,\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma((n-1)/2)}.$$
Thus $g(S^2) = cS$ with
$$c = \sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\frac{\Gamma((n-1)/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)}$$
satisfies $Eg(S^2) = \sigma$: an unbiased estimator of $\sigma$ (necessarily a slight inflation of $S$, since $ES < \sigma$ by 5.11).
