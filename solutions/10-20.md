**Central idea.** The breakdown value is the largest contamination fraction the estimator survives: one bad point breaks the mean, half the sample breaks the median, and $\alpha n$ bad points break the $\alpha$-trimmed mean.

**Solution.** (a) Driving a single observation ($1/n$ of the sample) to $\infty$ sends $\bar X_n\to\infty$; hence no positive contamination fraction is safe and $b=0$.
(b) If fewer than half the observations are driven to $\infty$, the median remains among the uncontaminated values (bounded); if more than half are driven to $\infty$, the median follows them to $\infty$. Hence $b=1/2$.
(c) Deleting the $\alpha n$ extremes: driving at most $\alpha n$ observations to $\infty$ leaves $\bar X_n^\alpha$ computed from uncontaminated values (bounded), while driving more than $\alpha n$ to $\infty$ contaminates the retained middle block, sending $\bar X_n^\alpha\to\infty$. Thus $b=\alpha$, and $0<\alpha<1/2$ gives $0<b<1/2$.
