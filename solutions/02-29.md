**Central idea.** Factorial moments absorb the combinatorial coefficients: $x(x-1)\binom{n}{x}=n(n-1)\binom{n-2}{x-2}$. *Note on (c): as printed it is incorrect — with the leading factor $a(y+a)$ the "probabilities" do not sum to 1 (e.g. for $n=2,a=b=1$ they sum to $14/3$). This is a typesetting erratum: the intended factor is $\frac{a}{y+a}$, which sums to 1 and equals $\binom{n}{y}\frac{B(y+a,n-y+b)}{B(a,b)}$.*

**Solution.** (a) Binomial$(n,p)$: $E[X(X-1)]=\sum_x x(x-1)\binom{n}{x}p^x(1-p)^{n-x}=n(n-1)p^2\sum_y\binom{n-2}{y}p^y(1-p)^{n-2-y}=n(n-1)p^2$. Poisson$(\lambda)$: $E[X(X-1)]=\sum_{x\ge2}\frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{(x-2)!}=\lambda^2$.
(b) $\mathrm{Var}X=E[X(X-1)]+EX-(EX)^2$: binomial gives $n(n-1)p^2+np-n^2p^2=np(1-p)$; Poisson gives $\lambda^2+\lambda-\lambda^2=\lambda$.
(c, corrected) In beta form the pmf is $\binom{n}{y}\frac{B(y+a,n-y+b)}{B(a,b)}=\binom{n}{y}\frac{(a)_y(b)_{\,n-y}}{(a+b)_n}$, with $(a)_y$ the rising factorial. Then, with $(Y)_r=Y(Y-1)\cdots(Y-r+1)$,
$$E[(Y)_r]=\sum_y\frac{y!}{(y-r)!}\binom{n}{y}\frac{(a)_y(b)_{n-y}}{(a+b)_n}=\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}\frac{(a)_r}{(a+b)_n}\sum_z\binom{n-r}{z}(a+r)_z(b)_{\,n-r-z},$$
using $(a)_y=(a)_r(a+r)_{y-r}$ and $z=y-r$. By Chu–Vandermonde the sum is $(a+b+r)_{\,n-r}$, and $\frac{(a+b+r)_{n-r}}{(a+b)_n}=\frac{1}{(a+b)_r}$, so $E[(Y)_r]=\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}\frac{(a)_r}{(a+b)_r}$. Hence $EY=n\frac{a}{a+b}$ and $E[Y(Y-1)]=n(n-1)\frac{a(a+1)}{(a+b)(a+b+1)}$, giving
$$\mathrm{Var}Y=n(n-1)\frac{a(a+1)}{(a+b)(a+b+1)}+n\frac{a}{a+b}-n^2\frac{a^2}{(a+b)^2}=\frac{nab\,(n+a+b)}{(a+b)^2(a+b+1)},$$
the usual beta-binomial variance (overdispersed relative to binomial$(n,\frac{a}{a+b})$ by the factor $\frac{n+a+b}{a+b+1}$).
