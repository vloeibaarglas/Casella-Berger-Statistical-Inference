**Central idea.** Differentiate $\int f(x|\theta)\,dx=1$ under the integral sign: the first derivative gives (3.4.4); the second gives (3.4.5) after using (3.4.4) to cancel the squared terms.

**Solution.** (a) $0=\frac{d}{d\theta}\int h(x)c(\theta)e^{\sum w_i(\theta)t_i(x)}dx=\int f(x|\theta)\left[\frac{c'(\theta)}{c(\theta)}+\sum_i w_i'(\theta)t_i(x)\right]dx$ (regularity allows differentiating under the integral), i.e. $\sum_i w_i'(\theta)E_\theta t_i(X)=-\frac{d}{d\theta}\log c(\theta)$, which is (3.4.4). (b) Differentiating again:
$$0=E_\theta\!\left[\left(\frac{c'}{c}+\sum_i w_i't_i\right)^2\right]+\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2}\log c(\theta)+\sum_i w_i''(\theta)E_\theta t_i(X),$$
using $\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2}\log c=\frac{c''}{c}-(\frac{c'}{c})^2$. Expanding the square and substituting $\sum_i w_i'E_\theta t_i=-c'/c$ from (a), the $(c'/c)^2$ terms cancel, leaving $\sum_i w_i''E_\theta t_i+\sum_{i,j}w_i'w_j'\text{Cov}_\theta(t_i,t_j)=-\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2}\log c(\theta)$, which is (3.4.5).
