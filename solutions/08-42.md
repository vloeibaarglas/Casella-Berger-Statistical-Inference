**Central idea.** Satterthwaite: match the first two moments of $S_X^2/n + S_Y^2/m$ to $c\chi^2_\nu$; then $T'$ is (normal numerator) over (root of an independent approximate-$\chi^2$/df).

**Solution.** (a) $S_X^2/n + S_Y^2/m = \frac{\sigma_X^2}{n}\frac{\chi^2_{n-1}}{n-1} + \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{m}\frac{\chi^2_{m-1}}{m-1}$ (independent). Approximating by $c\chi^2_\nu$ and matching mean and variance: $c\nu = \frac{\sigma_X^2}{n}+\frac{\sigma_Y^2}{m}$, $2c^2\nu = \frac{2\sigma_X^4}{n^2(n-1)}+\frac{2\sigma_Y^4}{m^2(m-1)}$, giving $\nu = \frac{(\sigma_X^2/n+\sigma_Y^2/m)^2}{\sigma_X^4/(n^2(n-1))+\sigma_Y^4/(m^2(m-1))}$, $c = (\sigma_X^2/n+\sigma_Y^2/m)/\nu$. Dividing by $\sigma_X^2/n+\sigma_Y^2/m$ gives the $\chi^2_\nu/\nu$ approximation; substituting $S_X^2, S_Y^2$ for $\sigma_X^2, \sigma_Y^2$ gives $\hat\nu$.
(b) Under $H_0$, $\bar X - \bar Y \sim n(0, \sigma_X^2/n+\sigma_Y^2/m)$ independent of $(S_X^2, S_Y^2)$, so $T' = \frac{n(0,1)}{\sqrt{(S_X^2/n+S_Y^2/m)/(\sigma_X^2/n+\sigma_Y^2/m)}} \approx \frac{n(0,1)}{\sqrt{\chi^2_{\hat\nu}/\hat\nu}} \sim t_{\hat\nu}$.
(c) $T' = \frac{-11.48}{\sqrt{591.4/14 + 176.0/9}} = \frac{-11.48}{\sqrt{61.80}} = -1.46$, $\hat\nu = \frac{(61.80)^2}{591.4^2/(14^2\cdot13) + 176.0^2/(9^2\cdot8)} \approx 20.6$; two-sided $p \approx .159$. Same conclusion: fail to reject.
(d) $F = S_X^2/S_Y^2 = 591.4/176.0 = 3.36$ on $(13,8)$ df; the Example 5.4.1 two-sided $p \approx .092$. Not significant at $.05$: no strong evidence the variances differ (supporting the pooled test in 8.41).
