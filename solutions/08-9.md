**Central idea.** The LRT statistic never exceeds 1, and writing out "$\lambda \le 1$" for these two parametrizations is exactly AM $\ge$ GM and GM $\ge$ HM.

**Solution.** (a) $L(\boldsymbol\lambda) = (\prod\lambda_i)\exp(-\sum\lambda_i y_i)$. Under $H_0$ ($\lambda_i \equiv \lambda$): $\hat\lambda = 1/\bar y$, $\sup L = \bar y^{-n}e^{-n}$. Unconstrained: $\hat\lambda_i = 1/y_i$, $\sup L = (\prod y_i)^{-1}e^{-n}$. Hence
$$\lambda(\mathbf y) = \frac{(\bar Y)^{-n}}{(\prod_i Y_i)^{-1}} \le 1,$$
so $(\bar Y)^{-n} \le (\prod_i Y_i)^{-1}$, i.e. $\prod_i Y_i \le \bar Y^{\,n}$, the AM–GM inequality $\bar Y \ge (\prod_i Y_i)^{1/n}$.
(b) With $X_i = 1/Y_i$, $f_{X_i}(x\mid\lambda_i) = \lambda_i e^{-\lambda_i/x}/x^2$. Under $H_0$: $\ell(\lambda) = n\log\lambda - \lambda\sum(1/x_i) - 2\sum\log x_i$, maximized at $\hat\lambda = n/\sum(1/x_i)$ (the harmonic mean), giving $\sup L = [n/\sum(1/x_i)]^n e^{-n}(\prod x_i)^{-2}$. Unconstrained: $\hat\lambda_i = x_i$, $\sup L = (\prod x_i)^{-1}e^{-n}$. Thus
$$\lambda(\mathbf x) = \frac{[n/\sum_i(1/X_i)]^n}{\prod_i X_i} \le 1,$$
i.e. $[n/\sum_i(1/X_i)]^n \le \prod_i X_i$, the GM–HM inequality $(\prod_i X_i)^{1/n} \ge n/\sum_i(1/X_i)$.
