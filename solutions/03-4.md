**Central idea.** With replacement the trials are iid Bernoulli$(1/n)$ (geometric waiting time); without replacement the correct key is equally likely to sit in any position.

**Solution.** (a) Each trial succeeds independently with probability $1/n$, so the number of trials $N \sim$ geometric$(1/n)$ and $EN = n$. (b) Without replacement, the correct key is equally likely to be the 1st, 2nd, $\ldots$, $n$th key tried, i.e. uniform on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, so $EN = (n+1)/2$.
