**Central idea.** This is a two-component mixture of independent pairs; the joint-minus-product-of-marginals factors as $a(1-a)(f_1-f_2)(g_1-g_2)$, and the covariance is the mixture covariance $a(1-a)(\mu_1-\mu_2)(\xi_1-\xi_2)$ — so equal component means ($\mu_1=\mu_2$) with $f_1\ne f_2$ gives dependence with zero correlation.

**Solution.** (a) Integrating out $y$ (resp. $x$) kills the $g$ (resp. $f$) factors since they integrate to $1$: $f_X(x) = af_1(x)+(1-a)f_2(x)$, $f_Y(y) = ag_1(y)+(1-a)g_2(y)$.
(b) $f_{X,Y}-f_Xf_Y = af_1g_1+(1-a)f_2g_2-[af_1+(1-a)f_2][ag_1+(1-a)g_2] = a(1-a)(f_1-f_2)(g_1-g_2)$. With $0<a<1$, independence holds iff $(f_1-f_2)(g_1-g_2) = 0$ a.e.
(c) $\mathrm{E}[XY] = a\mu_1\xi_1+(1-a)\mu_2\xi_2$, $\mathrm{E}X = a\mu_1+(1-a)\mu_2$, $\mathrm{E}Y = a\xi_1+(1-a)\xi_2$; subtracting gives $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y) = a(1-a)(\mu_1-\mu_2)(\xi_1-\xi_2)$. For dependent but uncorrelated variables: take $\mu_1=\mu_2$ (so Cov $=0$) but $f_1\ne f_2$ and $g_1\ne g_2$ with $\xi_1\ne\xi_2$ (so $(f_1-f_2)(g_1-g_2)\ne0$, dependent).
(d) Independent: $f_1=f_2$ (e.g. both binomial$(5,\tfrac12)$). Correlated: $f_1=$ binomial$(5,\tfrac14)$, $f_2=$ binomial$(5,\tfrac34)$, $g_1=$ binomial$(5,\tfrac14)$, $g_2=$ binomial$(5,\tfrac34)$ (means differ on both sides). Uncorrelated but dependent: $f_1=$ binomial$(2,\tfrac12)$, $f_2=$ binomial$(4,\tfrac14)$ (both mean $1$, different pmfs), $g_1=$ binomial$(4,\tfrac14)$, $g_2=$ binomial$(4,\tfrac34)$ (means $1\ne3$).
