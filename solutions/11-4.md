**Central idea.** Exercise 10.17(d) gives $\sqrt{n}(r-\rho) \to n(0,(1-\rho^2)^2)$; the Delta Method with $g(r) = \mathrm{artanh}(r)$ has $g'(\rho) = 1/(1-\rho^2)$, exactly canceling the asymptotic variance. The $1/(n-3)$ refinement is a first-order expansion match.

**Solution.** (a) Let $g(r) = \frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+r}{1-r} = \mathrm{artanh}(r)$, so $g'(r) = 1/(1-r^2)$. From 10.17(d), $\sqrt{n}(r-\rho) \xrightarrow{d} n(0,(1-\rho^2)^2)$. By the Delta Method, $\sqrt{n}(g(r)-g(\rho)) \xrightarrow{d} n(0, (1-\rho^2)^2 \cdot [1/(1-\rho^2)]^2) = n(0,1)$, i.e. $g(r) - g(\rho) \approx n(0, 1/n)$.
(b) The mean $\rho/(2(n-1)) \approx 0$ for small $\rho$. For the variance: $\frac{1}{n-3} - \frac{1}{n-1} = \frac{2}{(n-3)(n-1)} \approx \frac{2}{(n-1)^2}$ for moderate $n$, while $\frac{4-\rho^2}{2(n-1)^2} \approx \frac{2}{(n-1)^2}$ for small $\rho$. Hence the variance $\approx 1/(n-1) + 2/(n-1)^2 \approx 1/(n-3)$.
