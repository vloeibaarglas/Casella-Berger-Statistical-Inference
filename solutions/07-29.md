**Central idea.** Independence of the Poisson and multinomial pieces gives the joint pmf; the $\tau$ score blends the $X$ and $Y$ counts, and the EM plugs $E[X_1]=m\tau_1^{(r)}$ into the complete-data MLE formulas.
**Solution.** (a) By independence, the joint pmf is the product of the $n$ Poisson pmfs and the multinomial pmf $m!\prod_i\tau_i^{x_i}/x_i!$. (As typeset, the $m!$ appears inside the $\prod_{i=1}^n$; the intended reading — consistent with "$(X_1,\dots,X_n)\sim\mathrm{multinomial}(m;\tau)$" — has a single factor $m!$ outside the product, since $\prod_i[m!\tau_i^{x_i}/x_i!]$ would carry $(m!)^n$ and would not be a valid pmf. The MLEs are unaffected, as $m$ is known.)
(b) $\log L=\sum_i[-m\beta\tau_i+y_i\log(m\beta\tau_i)]+\sum_i x_i\log\tau_i+C$ with $\sum\tau_i=1$. With Lagrange multiplier (or $\phi_i=\beta\tau_i$), the joint MLEs are $\hat\beta=\sum y_i/m$ and $\hat\tau_j=(x_j+y_j)/(m+\sum y_i)$.
(c) E-step: $E[X_1\mid\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}_{-1},\theta^{(r)}]=m\hat\tau_1^{(r)}$. The M-step plugs the expected sufficient statistics ($E[X_1]=m\hat\tau_1^{(r)}$, $E[\sum X_i]=m\hat\tau_1^{(r)}+\sum_{i\ge2}x_i$) into the complete-data MLEs from (b), giving the stated sequence (for $j=1$, $x_1$ is replaced by $m\hat\tau_1^{(r)}$).
(d) Full data ($m=55{,}860$, $\sum y_i=47$): $\hat\beta=47/55{,}860\approx 8.414\times10^{-4}$, $\hat\tau_j=(x_j+y_j)/55{,}907$ (e.g. $\hat\tau_1=3543/55{,}907\approx 0.06337$). With $x_1$ missing, iterating the EM sequence converges to $\hat\beta\approx 8.414\times10^{-4}$, $\hat\tau_1\approx 0.06337$ — essentially the full-data MLEs, since the EM reconstructs $x_1$.
