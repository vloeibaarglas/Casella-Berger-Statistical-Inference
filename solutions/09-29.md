**Central idea.** The posterior is $\text{beta}(a+x,\ b+n-x)$; the beta–$F$ transformation puts it in Clopper–Pearson form with prior pseudo-counts.

**Solution.** (a) With $x = \sum x_i$, $p\mid x \sim \text{beta}(a+x,\ b+n-x)$; e.g. equal-tail $[B^{-1}(\alpha/2),\ B^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)]$.
(b) Via $\frac{Y}{1-Y}\cdot\frac{v}{u} \sim F_{2u,2v}$ for $Y \sim \text{beta}(u,v)$:
$$\frac{1}{1 + \frac{b+n-x}{a+x}F_{2(b+n-x),2(a+x),1-\alpha/2}} \le p \le \frac{\frac{a+x}{b+n-x}F_{2(a+x),2(b+n-x),\alpha/2}}{1 + \frac{a+x}{b+n-x}F_{2(a+x),2(b+n-x),\alpha/2}}.$$
This is algebraically the Clopper–Pearson $F$-form with the data counts augmented by prior pseudo-counts ($a$ successes, $b$ failures): it shrinks the interval toward $a/(a+b)$ relative to 9.21, with the difference vanishing as $n$ grows.
