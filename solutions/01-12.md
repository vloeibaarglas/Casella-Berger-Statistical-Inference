**Central idea.** (a) Pad a finite disjoint collection with empty sets. (b) Write the infinite union as a finite union plus a "tail", and let continuity kill the tail since the tails decrease to $\emptyset$.

**Solution.** (a) If $A_1, \dots, A_n$ are disjoint, set $A_{n+1} = A_{n+2} = \cdots = \emptyset$; countable additivity gives $P(\cup_{i=1}^n A_i) = \sum_{i=1}^n P(A_i) + \sum_{i>n} P(\emptyset) = \sum_{i=1}^n P(A_i)$.
(b) Let $\{A_i\}$ be disjoint and put $S_n = \cup_{i=1}^n A_i$, $S = \cup_{i=1}^\infty A_i$, $T_n = S \setminus S_n = \cup_{i=n+1}^\infty A_i$. Then $T_n \downarrow \emptyset$, and by finite additivity $P(S) = P(S_n) + P(T_n) = \sum_{i=1}^n P(A_i) + P(T_n)$. By continuity, $P(T_n) \to 0$, so $P(S) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty P(A_i)$.
