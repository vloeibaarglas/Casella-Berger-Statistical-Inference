**Central idea.** $E X^2=\sigma^2$ gives unbiasedness immediately; the MLE follows from maximizing in $\sigma$; moments can match $E|X|$ instead of $EX^2$.
**Solution.** (a) $E_\sigma[X^2]=\mathrm{Var}(X)=\sigma^2$, so $\delta(X)=X^2$ is unbiased for $\sigma^2$.
(b) $L(\sigma\mid x)=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\exp(-x^2/(2\sigma^2))$; $\partial\log L/\partial\sigma=-\sigma^{-1}+x^2\sigma^{-3}=0$ gives $\hat\sigma=|x|$, a maximum. So the MLE of $\sigma$ is $|X|$.
(c) The usual MOM equation $E_\sigma X=\bar x$ is useless ($E_\sigma X\equiv 0$). Instead match a nonzero moment, e.g. $E_\sigma|X|=\sigma\sqrt{2/\pi}$ (half-normal mean), giving the MOM estimator $\tilde\sigma=\sqrt{\pi/2}\,|X|$; equivalently match $E_\sigma X^2=\sigma^2$ to get $\tilde\sigma=|X|$.
