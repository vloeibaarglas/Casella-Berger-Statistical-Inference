**Central idea.** If the three probabilities are equal they must each be $1/3$; this forces $u + w = 1$ and $uw = 1/3$, whose discriminant is negative.

**Solution.** By independence, $p_0 = (1-u)(1-w)$, $p_1 = u(1-w) + w(1-u)$, $p_2 = uw$. If $p_0 = p_1 = p_2 = p$, then $3p = 1$ so $p = 1/3$. From $p_2 = 1/3$, $uw = 1/3$; from $p_0 = 1/3$, $1 - u - w + uw = 1/3$, so $u + w = 2/3 + uw = 1$. Thus $u, w$ are roots of $t^2 - t + 1/3 = 0$, with discriminant $1 - 4/3 = -1/3 < 0$. No real (hence no legitimate) $u, w$ exist.
