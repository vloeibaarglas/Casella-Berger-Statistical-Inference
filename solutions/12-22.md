**Central idea.** Write the intercept as a linear combination $a=\sum c_iY_i$; under variance contamination its variance inflates by the same factor $(1-\delta)\sigma^2+\delta\tau^2$ as the slope's — small perturbations hurt it only mildly.

**Solution.** With $d_i=(x_i-\bar x)/\sum_j(x_j-\bar x)^2$, $b=\sum d_iY_i$ and $a=\bar y-b\bar x=\sum_i c_iY_i$ where $c_i=1/n-\bar x d_i$. Under $\mathrm{Var}(\epsilon_i)=(1-\delta)\sigma^2+\delta\tau^2$ for all $i$,
$$\mathrm{Var}(a)=\sum_i c_i^2\big[(1-\delta)\sigma^2+\delta\tau^2\big]=\big[(1-\delta)\sigma^2+\delta\tau^2\big]\sum_i c_i^2.$$
Since $\sum_i d_i=0$ and $\sum_i d_i^2=1/\sum_j(x_j-\bar x)^2$,
$$\sum_i c_i^2=\frac1n-2\bar x\frac1n\sum_i d_i+\bar x^2\sum_i d_i^2=\frac1n+\frac{\bar x^2}{\sum_j(x_j-\bar x)^2}.$$
Hence $\mathrm{Var}(a)=[(1-\delta)\sigma^2+\delta\tau^2]\big(1/n+\bar x^2/\sum_j(x_j-\bar x)^2\big)$: exactly the uncontaminated variance with $\sigma^2$ replaced by the mixture variance. The variance inflation factor is $1+\delta(\tau^2/\sigma^2-1)$. For fixed finite $\tau^2$ it tends to one as $\delta\to0$, but it is unbounded over contaminant scales even at arbitrarily small fixed $\delta>0$. This calculation does not establish robustness to unrestricted contamination.
