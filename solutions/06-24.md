**Central idea.** With only two parameter values, $\mathrm{E}_\lambda[g(X)] = 0$ is just two linear constraints on infinitely many values $g(x)$ — plenty of room for a nonzero solution.

**Solution.** $P_0$ is degenerate at $0$, so $\mathrm{E}_0[g(X)] = g(0) = 0$. Also $\mathrm{E}_1[g(X)] = e^{-1}\sum_{x\ge 1} g(x)/x! = 0$ is one linear constraint on $\{g(x)\}_{x\ge 1}$, satisfied by e.g. $g(1) = 1$, $g(2) = -2$, $g(x) = 0$ otherwise (since $1/1! - 2/2! = 0$). This nonzero $g$ has $\mathrm{E}_\lambda g(X) = 0$ for $\lambda = 0,1$: $\mathcal{P}$ is not complete. (With $\lambda > 0$ continuous, Exercise 6.18 shows the Poisson family is complete — the range matters.)
