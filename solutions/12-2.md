**Central idea.** Setting $f'(b)=0$ clears denominators to a quadratic in $b$; the range of $f$ is found by asking for which constants $c$ the equation $f(b)=c$ has a real root, and the smaller root $c_-$ is attained exactly at the "$+$" critical point.

**Solution.** Assume $S_{xy}\ne 0$ (otherwise $f$ is minimized trivially at $b=0$). Then
$$f'(b)=0\iff \frac{(-2S_{xy}+2bS_{xx})(1+b^2)-2b(S_{yy}-2bS_{xy}+b^2S_{xx})}{(1+b^2)^2}=0,$$
and the numerator simplifies to $-2S_{xy}+2b(S_{xx}-S_{yy})+2b^2S_{xy}=0$, i.e.
$$b^2S_{xy}+b(S_{xx}-S_{yy})-S_{xy}=0,$$
whose roots are the stated values. For the range, $f(b)=c$ is $b^2(S_{xx}-c)-2bS_{xy}+(S_{yy}-c)=0$, which has a real root iff its discriminant is nonnegative: $S_{xy}^2-(S_{xx}-c)(S_{yy}-c)\ge 0$, i.e. $c^2-c(S_{xx}+S_{yy})+S_{xx}S_{yy}-S_{xy}^2\le 0$. Hence $f$ ranges over $[c_-,c_+]$ with $c_\pm=\tfrac12\big[(S_{xx}+S_{yy})\pm\sqrt{(S_{xx}-S_{yy})^2+4S_{xy}^2}\big]$. The minimum $c_-$ is attained where the quadratic in $b$ has a double root, $b=S_{xy}/(S_{xx}-c_-)=2S_{xy}/(S_{xx}-S_{yy}+\sqrt{D})$ with $D=(S_{xx}-S_{yy})^2+4S_{xy}^2$; rationalizing gives $b=(\sqrt{D}-(S_{xx}-S_{yy}))/(2S_{xy})$, the "$+$" root.
