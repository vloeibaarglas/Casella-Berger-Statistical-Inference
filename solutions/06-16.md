**Central idea.** The multinomial is an exponential family, but the four cell probabilities trace a one-dimensional curve (not an open set) in the natural parameter space; the ratio criterion then merges the two symmetric cells.

**Solution.** (a) The pmf is $\frac{n!}{x_1!x_2!x_3!x_4!}\exp\big(x_1\log(\tfrac12+\tfrac\theta4) + (x_2+x_3)\log(\tfrac{1-\theta}{4}) + x_4\log(\tfrac\theta4)\big)$, an exponential family with natural statistics $(X_1,X_2+X_3,X_4)$. The natural parameters $\eta_1 = \log(\frac12+\frac\theta4)$, $\eta_2 = \log(\frac{1-\theta}{4})$, $\eta_3 = \log(\frac\theta4)$ trace a one-dimensional curve in $\Re^3$ as $\theta \in (0,1)$ varies (e.g. $\eta_2-\eta_3 = \log((1-\theta)/\theta)$ ranges over $\Re$ while nonlinearly tied to $\eta_1$), containing no open set: curved.
(b) By Theorem 6.2.10 (or factorization), $(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)$ — and hence $(X_1,X_2+X_3,X_4)$ — is sufficient.
(c) By Theorem 6.2.13, with $d_1 = x_1-y_1$, $d_{23} = (x_2+x_3)-(y_2+y_3)$, $d_4 = x_4-y_4$,
$$\log\frac{p(\mathbf{x}\mid\theta)}{p(\mathbf{y}\mid\theta)} = \text{const} + d_1\log(2+\theta) + d_{23}\log(1-\theta) + d_4\log\theta,$$
constant in $\theta \in (0,1)$ iff $d_1 = d_{23} = d_4 = 0$ (differentiate: $d_1/(2+\theta) - d_{23}/(1-\theta) + d_4/\theta \equiv 0$ forces all $d$'s to be $0$). Thus $(X_1,X_2+X_3,X_4)$ is minimal sufficient; since $X_1+X_2+X_3+X_4 = n$, this is equivalent to the two-dimensional $(X_1,X_4)$.
