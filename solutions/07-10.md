**Central idea.** The density $f(x\mid\alpha,\beta)=\alpha x^{\alpha-1}/\beta^\alpha\mathbf{1}_{[0\le x\le\beta]}$ is a threshold family: $\beta$ is estimated by the largest observation and $\alpha$ from the log-spacings below it.
**Solution.** The pdf is $f(x\mid\alpha,\beta)=\alpha x^{\alpha-1}\beta^{-\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{[0\le x\le\beta]}$.
(a) $L(\alpha,\beta\mid\mathbf{x})=\alpha^n(\prod x_i)^{\alpha-1}\beta^{-n\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{[\beta\ge x_{(n)}]}$ factors through $(\prod X_i,X_{(n)})$, a two-dimensional sufficient statistic.
(b) For fixed $\alpha$, $L$ is decreasing in $\beta$ on $\beta\ge x_{(n)}$, so $\hat\beta=X_{(n)}$. Then $\log L=n\log\alpha+(\alpha-1)\sum\log x_i-n\alpha\log\hat\beta$ gives $\hat\alpha=n/(n\log\hat\beta-\sum\log x_i)=-n/\sum\log(x_i/\hat\beta)$.
(c) Here $x_{(n)}=25.0$, so $\hat\beta=25.0$, and $\hat\alpha=14/(14\log 25.0-43.953)\approx 12.59$.
