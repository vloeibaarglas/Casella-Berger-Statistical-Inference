**Central idea.** $S^2$ inherits the mean's fragility (one outlier inflates it without bound); the MAD inherits the median's: it needs a majority of bad points to break.

**Solution.** (a) Send $X_1\to\infty$: then $\bar X\to\infty$ and $(X_1-\bar X)^2\to\infty$, so $S^2\to\infty$ from a single bad observation; $b=0$.
(b) If fewer than half the $X_i$ are contaminated, $m$ stays bounded and a majority of the $|X_i-m|$ stay bounded, so their median stays bounded. If more than half are driven to $\infty$, then $m\to\infty$ and a majority of $|X_i-m|\to\infty$, so MAD$\to\infty$. Hence $b=1/2$.
