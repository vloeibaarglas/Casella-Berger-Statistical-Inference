**Central idea.** Each $Y_i$ is Bernoulli; iid $X_i$ give iid $Y_i$, so the sum is binomial — but the success probability is $P(X_1 > \mu)$, which is *not* $1/2$ in general (mean $\neq$ median).

**Solution.** The $Y_i$ are iid (functions of iid $X_i$) with $P(Y_i = 1) = P(X_i > \mu) =: p$ and $P(Y_i = 0) = 1 - p$. Therefore
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} Y_i \sim \text{Binomial}(n, p), \qquad p = P(X_1 > \mu) = 1 - F_X(\mu).$$
In particular $p$ need not equal $1/2$: $EX_1 = \mu$ says nothing about the median. (If the distribution is symmetric about $\mu$, then $p = 1/2$ by continuity.)
