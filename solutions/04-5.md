**Central idea.** Integrate the joint density over the described region, choosing the order that makes the inner limits simple.

**Solution.** (a) $\int_0^1\int_{\sqrt{y}}^1 (x+y)\,dx\,dy = \int_0^1\left(\frac12+\frac{y}{2}-y^{3/2}\right)dy = \frac12+\frac14-\frac25 = \frac{7}{20}$.
(b) For $0 < x < 1$, $y$ runs from $x^2$ to $x$: $\int_0^1\int_{x^2}^{x} 2x\,dy\,dx = \int_0^1 2x(x-x^2)\,dx = 2\left(\frac13-\frac14\right) = \frac16$.
