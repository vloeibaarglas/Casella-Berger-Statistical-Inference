**Central idea.** Fold the two sides of a non-monotone (or piecewise) transformation together; in (c) the two pieces have different inverses but the same range $(0,1)$.

**Solution.** (a) For $y>0$, $F_Y(y)=P(|X|\le y^{1/3})=P(-y^{1/3}\le X\le y^{1/3})$, so
$$f_Y(y) = 2\cdot\tfrac12e^{-y^{1/3}}\cdot\tfrac13y^{-2/3} = \tfrac13y^{-2/3}e^{-y^{1/3}},\quad y>0.$$
With $t=y^{1/3}$ ($dy=3t^2dt$), $\int_0^\infty f_Y\,dy=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}dt=1$.
(b) For $0<y<1$, $F_Y(y)=P(X\le-\sqrt{1-y})+P(X\ge\sqrt{1-y})$, so with $t=\sqrt{1-y}$,
$$f_Y(y) = \frac{f_X(-t)+f_X(t)}{2t} = \frac{\frac38(1-t)^2+\frac38(1+t)^2}{2t} = \frac{3(2-y)}{8\sqrt{1-y}},\quad 0<y<1.$$
With $u=1-y$, $\int_0^1 f_Y\,dy=\frac38\int_0^1\frac{1+u}{\sqrt u}\,du=\frac38\big(2+\tfrac23\big)=1$.
(c) On $(-1,0]$, $g_1(x)=1-x^2$ increases from 0 to 1 with inverse $-\sqrt{1-y}$; on $(0,1)$, $g_2(x)=1-x$ decreases from 1 to 0 with inverse $1-y$. For $0<y<1$,
$$f_Y(y) = f_X(-\sqrt{1-y})\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-y}} + f_X(1-y)\cdot1 = \frac{3}{16}\frac{(1-\sqrt{1-y})^2}{\sqrt{1-y}}+\frac38(2-y)^2.$$
With $t=\sqrt{1-y}$ ($dy=-2t\,dt$), the first term integrates to $\frac38\int_0^1(1-t)^2dt=\frac18$ and the second to $\frac38\int_0^1(2-y)^2dy=\frac78$, totaling 1.
