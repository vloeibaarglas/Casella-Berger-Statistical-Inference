**Central idea.** The normal pivot is exact for every $\theta>0$. Inverting it requires considering all observed $\bar x$, including negative values, because a normal sample mean can be negative.

**Solution.** Let $z=z_{\alpha/2}>0$ denote the upper-tail standard normal cutoff. Then
$$Q=\frac{\sqrt n(\bar X-\theta)}{\sqrt\theta}\sim N(0,1),\qquad C(\bar x)=\{\theta>0:n(\bar x-\theta)^2\le z^2\theta\}.$$
This set has exact coverage $1-\alpha$. Put $D=4n\bar x+z^2$. When $D\ge0$ the quadratic roots are
$$r_\pm=\bar x+\frac{z^2}{2n}\pm\frac{z}{2n}\sqrt D.$$
For $D>0$, $C=[r_-,r_+]\cap(0,\infty)$; this includes negative $\bar x$ with $\bar x>-z^2/(4n)$. At $\bar x=0$, it is $(0,z^2/n]$. At $D=0$, it is the singleton $\{z^2/(4n)\}$. At $D<0$, it is empty. Empty realizations do not invalidate the exact coverage identity, which follows directly from the pivot. The earlier positive-$\bar x$ formula was correct but omitted possible observations.
