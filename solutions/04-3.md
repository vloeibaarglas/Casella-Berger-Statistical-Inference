**Central idea.** Count sample points: with two fair dice the 36 outcomes are equally likely, and $X$ depends only on the first die while $Y$ depends only on the second.

**Solution.** In Example 4.1.5, $S$ is the 36-point toss-two-dice space, $X=0$ if the first die shows at most 2 ($X=1$ otherwise), and $Y=0$ if the second die is odd ($Y=1$ if even). By Definition 4.1.1, $f(x,y) = P(X=x,Y=y)$ is the fraction of sample points mapping to $(x,y)$. $P(X=0) = 12/36 = 1/3$, $P(X=1) = 2/3$, $P(Y=0) = P(Y=1) = 1/2$, and the dice are independent, so $f(0,0) = f(0,1) = (1/3)(1/2) = 1/6$ and $f(1,0) = f(1,1) = (2/3)(1/2) = 1/3$, with $f(x,y)=0$ elsewhere — exactly the pmf stated in the example.
