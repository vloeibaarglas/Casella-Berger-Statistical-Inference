**Central idea.** (a) is a nonregular family handled by differentiating the zero-expectation integral; (b), (c), (e) are full exponential families with open natural parameter spaces; (d) is a one-parameter exponential family in disguise ($t(x) = e^{-x}$).

**Solution.** (a) Joint $= 2^n(\prod x_i)\theta^{-2n}I(x_{(n)} < \theta)$: $X_{(n)}$ sufficient. Its pdf is $2nt^{2n-1}/\theta^{2n}$ on $(0,\theta)$. If $\mathrm{E}_\theta[g(X_{(n)})] = \theta^{-2n}\int_0^\theta g(t)2nt^{2n-1}\,dt = 0$ for all $\theta > 0$, then $H(\theta) = \int_0^\theta g(t)t^{2n-1}\,dt \equiv 0$, so $H'(\theta) = g(\theta)\theta^{2n-1} = 0$ a.e.: $g = 0$ a.e. $X_{(n)}$ is complete sufficient.
(b) Joint $= \theta^n\exp(-(1+\theta)\sum\log(1+x_i))$: $T = \sum\log(1+X_i)$ sufficient. Since $\log f = -\theta\log(1+x) + \log\theta - \log(1+x)$, this is a full exponential family with natural parameter $-\theta \in (-\infty,0)$, open: complete by Theorem 6.2.25.
(c) Joint $= (\log\theta)^n\theta^{\sum x_i}/(\theta-1)^n$: $\sum X_i$ sufficient; $\log f = (\log\theta)x + \log(\log\theta/(\theta-1))$, natural parameter $\log\theta \in (0,\infty)$, open: complete by Theorem 6.2.25.
(d) Joint $= \exp(n\theta - \sum x_i - e^{\theta}\sum e^{-x_i})$: $T = \sum e^{-X_i}$ sufficient, and $\log f = -e^{\theta}e^{-x} + \theta - x$ is a one-parameter exponential family in $t(x) = e^{-x}$ with natural parameter $-e^{\theta} \in (-\infty,0)$, open: complete by Theorem 6.2.25. (Indeed the ratio criterion gives minimal sufficiency of $\sum e^{-X_i}$ directly.)
(e) Each $X_i \sim \text{binomial}(2,\theta)$, so $T = \sum X_i \sim \text{binomial}(2n,\theta)$ is sufficient. If $\mathrm{E}_\theta[g(T)] = \sum_{k=0}^{2n} g(k)\binom{2n}{k}\theta^k(1-\theta)^{2n-k} \equiv 0$ on $[0,1]$, dividing by $(1-\theta)^{2n}$ gives a degree-$2n$ polynomial in $\theta/(1-\theta)$ vanishing on $[0,\infty)$, so all $g(k)\binom{2n}{k} = 0$: $g \equiv 0$. $\sum X_i$ is complete sufficient.
