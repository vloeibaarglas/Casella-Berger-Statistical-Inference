**Central idea.** Both are Markov's inequality: $P(|X|\ge b)=P(X^2\ge b^2)\le EX^2/b^2$ and $P(|X|\ge b)\le E|X|/b$; plug in $EX=1$, $EX^2=2$ for Exp$(1)$.

**Solution.** $P(|X|\ge b)=P(X^2\ge b^2)\le EX^2/b^2$ and $P(|X|\ge b)\le E|X|/b$, both by Markov's inequality. For $f(x)=e^{-x}$ ($x>0$): $E|X|=1$, $EX^2=2$, so the bounds are $2/b^2$ and $1/b$. At $b=3$: $2/9\approx0.222<1/3\approx0.333$ — the $EX^2/b^2$ bound is better. At $b=\sqrt2$: $2/2=1>1/\sqrt2\approx0.707$ — the $E|X|/b$ bound is better.
