**Central idea.** The posterior is $\text{gamma}(a+\sum x_i,\ \text{rate }n+1/b)$, so $T$ is exactly $\chi^2$; the confidence interval's endpoints, rescaled into $T$-units, drift to $+\infty$ relative to $T$'s mean because of the factor $(nb+1)/(nb) > 1$.

**Solution.** (a) $\lambda\mid x \sim \text{gamma}(a+\sum x_i,\ [n+1/b]^{-1})$ (shape–scale, Example 9.2.16), so $2(n+1/b)\lambda = \frac{2(nb+1)}{b}\lambda \sim \chi^2_{2(a+\sum x_i)}$.
(b) $M(t) = E\exp(t(\chi^2_\nu-\nu)/\sqrt{2\nu}) = (1-2t/\sqrt{2\nu})^{-\nu/2}e^{-t\nu/\sqrt{2\nu}}$; $\log M(t) = -\frac{\nu}{2}\log(1-2t/\sqrt{2\nu}) - t\sqrt{\nu/2} = t^2/2 + o(1) \to t^2/2$.
(c) (9.2.21) is $P(\frac{1}{2n}\chi^2_{2\sum x,1-\alpha/2} \le \lambda \le \frac{1}{2n}\chi^2_{2(\sum x+1),\alpha/2})$ under the posterior. In $T$-units: $P(c\,\chi^2_{2\sum x,1-\alpha/2} \le T \le c\,\chi^2_{2(\sum x+1),\alpha/2})$ with $c = \frac{nb+1}{nb} = 1 + \frac{1}{nb} > 1$. Standardizing, the lower cutoff is
$$\frac{c\,\chi^2_{2\sum x,1-\alpha/2} - 2(a+\sum x)}{\sqrt{4(a+\sum x)}} = \frac{\sqrt{\sum x}}{nb} + O(1) \to \infty,$$
since $\chi^2_{2\sum x,1-\alpha/2} = 2\sum x + O(\sqrt{\sum x})$. Hence the credible probability $\to 0$. (The argument uses only $c > 1$, so it holds for every $b > 0$ — including $b = 1/n$, contrary to the text's "unless $b = 1/n$" aside, which appears to be an overstatement; numerically the probability decays to $0$ for $b=1/n$ as well.)
