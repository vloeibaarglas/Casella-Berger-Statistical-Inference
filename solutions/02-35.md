**Central idea.** $f_1$ is the law of $e^Z$, $Z\sim n(0,1)$, so moments are normal mgf values; the substitution $y=\log x-r$ turns the perturbation integral into an odd function over $\mathbb R$.

**Solution.** (a) $f_1(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}x}e^{-(\log x)^2/2}$ ($0\le x<\infty$) is the pdf of $X_1=e^Z$ with $Z\sim n(0,1)$. Hence $EX_1^r=E[e^{rZ}]=M_Z(r)=e^{r^2/2}<\infty$ for all $r\ge0$.
(b) With $y=\log x-r$ ($x=e^{y+r}$, $dx/x=dy$):
$$x^r f_1(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{r(y+r)}e^{-(y+r)^2/2}dy=\frac{e^{r^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-y^2/2}dy,$$
and, $r$ being an integer, $\sin(2\pi\log x)=\sin(2\pi(y+r))=\sin(2\pi y)$. Thus
$$\int_0^\infty x^r f_1(x)\sin(2\pi\log x)\,dx=e^{r^2/2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-y^2/2}\sin(2\pi y)\,dy=0,$$
the integrand being odd. Since $f_2(x)=f_1(x)[1+\sin(2\pi\log x)]$, $EX_2^r=EX_1^r+0=EX_1^r$ for all $r$.
