**Central idea.** Differentiate $\int f(x\mid\theta)\,dx=1$ twice and rewrite $\partial^2f/\partial\theta^2$ via $\partial^2\log f/\partial\theta^2$.
**Solution.** Lemma 7.3.11 (the Bartlett identities): under regularity permitting differentiation under the integral, $0=\frac{d}{d\theta}\int f=\int\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}=\int\frac{\partial\log f}{\partial\theta}f$, i.e. $E_\theta\big[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log f(X\mid\theta)\big]=0$. Differentiating again, $0=\int\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial\theta^2}\,dx$; since $\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta^2}\log f=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial\theta^2}-\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log f\big)^2$, taking expectations gives $E_\theta\big[\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta^2}\log f(X\mid\theta)\big]=-E_\theta\big[\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log f(X\mid\theta)\big)^2\big]$.
