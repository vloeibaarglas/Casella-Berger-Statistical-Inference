**Central idea.** With $x_{24}$ observed and $y_{24}$ missing, the E-step imputes $E[Y_{24}\mid x_{24}]$ and $E[Y_{24}^2\mid x_{24}]$ from the conditional normal; the M-step is the complete-data MLE (sample moments) with imputed sufficient statistics. In (d) the roles reverse.

**Solution.** Let $(x_i,y_i)$, $i = 1,\dots,23$ be complete, and write $\boldsymbol{\theta} = (\mu_X,\mu_Y,\sigma_X^2,\sigma_Y^2,\rho)$.
(a) Observed data: $\prod_{i=1}^{23}f(x_i,y_i;\boldsymbol{\theta})\cdot f_X(x_{24};\boldsymbol{\theta})$, so
$$\ell_{obs}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \sum_{i=1}^{23}\log f(x_i,y_i;\boldsymbol{\theta}) + \log f_X(x_{24};\boldsymbol{\theta}),$$
$f$ the bivariate normal density, $f_X$ its $n(\mu_X,\sigma_X^2)$ marginal. The expected complete-data log likelihood: $Q(\boldsymbol{\theta}\mid\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}) = \sum_{i=1}^{23}\log f(x_i,y_i;\boldsymbol{\theta}) + E_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}}[\log f(x_{24},Y_{24};\boldsymbol{\theta})\mid x_{24}]$.
(b) E-step: compute, under $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}$, with $Y_{24}\mid x_{24} \sim n(\mu_Y^{(t)} + \rho^{(t)}\frac{\sigma_Y^{(t)}}{\sigma_X^{(t)}}(x_{24}-\mu_X^{(t)}),\, \sigma_Y^{2(t)}(1-\rho^{2(t)}))$:
$$\hat y_{24} = E[Y_{24}\mid x_{24}], \qquad \widehat{y_{24}^2} = \mathrm{Var}(Y_{24}\mid x_{24}) + \hat y_{24}^2.$$
M-step: with $S_x = \sum_{1}^{24}x_i$, $S_{xx} = \sum_{1}^{24}x_i^2$ (known), $S_y = \sum_{1}^{23}y_i + \hat y_{24}$, $S_{yy} = \sum_{1}^{23}y_i^2 + \widehat{y_{24}^2}$, $S_{xy} = \sum_{1}^{23}x_iy_i + x_{24}\hat y_{24}$:
$$\mu_X = S_x/24,\ \mu_Y = S_y/24,\ \sigma_X^2 = S_{xx}/24 - \mu_X^2,\ \sigma_Y^2 = S_{yy}/24 - \mu_Y^2,\ \rho = \frac{S_{xy}/24 - \mu_X\mu_Y}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y}.$$
(c) Under Example 12.4.2's model — the regression of $CO_2$ ($y$) on $O_2$ ($x$) — the 24th animal has $x_{24} = 16.3$ observed but $y_{24}$ missing, so it contributes no information about the regression: the MLEs are the 23-animal least squares, $y = 18.675-0.894x$ with $\hat\sigma^2 = .0503$ (the book's note in (d) confirms only (d) needs the bivariate-normal formulation). As a bivariate-normal alternative, EM from the 23 complete observations (MLEs: $\hat\mu_X = 18.33$, $\hat\mu_Y = 2.29$, $\hat\sigma_X^2 = 0.83$, $\hat\sigma_Y^2 = 0.72$, $\hat\rho = -0.964$) converges to

| $\mu_X$ | $\mu_Y$ | $\sigma_X^2$ | $\sigma_Y^2$ | $\rho$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18.24 | 2.37 | 0.962 | 0.819 | $-0.969$ |

with $E[Y_{24}\mid x_{24}=16.3] = 4.10$ at the MLE (the missing $CO_2$ is imputed high, consistent with the strong negative correlation). Convergence was verified from two different starting values to the same fixed point; at the MLE, $\ell_{obs} = -31.85$.
(d) Now $y_{24} = 4.2$ is observed and $x_{24}$ missing. E-step: $X_{24}\mid y_{24} \sim n(\mu_X^{(t)} + \rho^{(t)}\frac{\sigma_X^{(t)}}{\sigma_Y^{(t)}}(y_{24}-\mu_Y^{(t)}),\, \sigma_X^{2(t)}(1-\rho^{2(t)}))$; impute $\hat x_{24} = E[X_{24}\mid y_{24}]$, $\widehat{x_{24}^2} = \mathrm{Var}(X_{24}\mid y_{24}) + \hat x_{24}^2$. M-step: sample moments with $S_x = \sum_{1}^{23}x_i + \hat x_{24}$, $S_{xx} = \sum_{1}^{23}x_i^2 + \widehat{x_{24}^2}$, $S_{xy} = \sum_{1}^{23}x_iy_i + \hat x_{24}y_{24}$ ($S_y$, $S_{yy}$ known). EM converges to

| $\mu_X$ | $\mu_Y$ | $\sigma_X^2$ | $\sigma_Y^2$ | $\rho$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18.24 | 2.37 | 0.957 | 0.831 | $-0.969$ |

with $E[X_{24}\mid y_{24}=4.2] = 16.34$ at the MLE — close to the 16.3 supposed in (a)–(c), as expected.
