**Central idea.** The Neyman–Pearson ratio $e^{y-\sqrt y}/(2\sqrt y)$ is U-shaped in $\sqrt y$ (minimum $1/2$ at $y=1$), so the MP rejection region is two-sided, $\{Y < .0765\} \cup \{Y > 3.638\}$; for simple vs.\ simple this is automatically UMP.

**Solution.** $P_\theta(Y \le y) = P(X \le y^{1/\theta}) = 1-e^{-y^{1/\theta}}$, so $f_Y(y\mid\theta) = \frac1\theta y^{1/\theta-1}e^{-y^{1/\theta}}$, $y>0$. Under $H_0$, $Y \sim$ exponential$(1)$; under $H_1$, $f_Y(y\mid2) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt y}e^{-\sqrt y}$. The likelihood ratio
$$r(y) = \frac{f_Y(y\mid2)}{f_Y(y\mid1)} = \frac{e^{\,y-\sqrt y}}{2\sqrt y}, \qquad \frac{d}{dt}\log r(t^2) = \frac{(2t+1)(t-1)}{t},\ t=\sqrt y,$$
decreases on $0<t<1$ and increases on $t>1$, with minimum $r(1)=1/2$. By Neyman–Pearson the MP test rejects for $r(Y) > k$, i.e. $\{Y<a\}\cup\{Y>b\}$ with $r(a)=r(b)=k$. Size $.10$ under $Y\sim$ exponential$(1)$: $(1-e^{-a})+e^{-b}=.10$ gives (numerically) $k \approx 1.479$, $a \approx 0.0765$, $b \approx 3.638$. Since $H_0,H_1$ are simple, this MP test is UMP. Type II error: under $H_1$, $P(Y\le y)=1-e^{-\sqrt y}$, so
$$P_{H_1}(\text{accept}) = P_{H_1}(a \le Y \le b) = \bigl(1-e^{-\sqrt b}\bigr)-\bigl(1-e^{-\sqrt a}\bigr) \approx 0.610.$$
