**Central idea.** This is complete separation: the unconstrained binomial MLEs sit on the boundary $\pi(0)=1$, which the logistic curve can only approach asymptotically.

**Solution.** (a) Unrestricted, the likelihood is $\pi(0)^{10}(1-\pi(0))^0\cdot\pi(1)^5(1-\pi(1))^5$, maximized at the binomial MLEs $\hat\pi(0)=10/10=1$, $\hat\pi(1)=5/10=0.5$, with maximum value $(1/2)^{10}$.

(b) Under (12.3.2), $\pi(0)=e^\alpha/(1+e^\alpha)<1$ for every finite $\alpha$, so the value $(1/2)^{10}$ is unattainable at finite $(\alpha,\beta)$. But with $\alpha=-\beta\to\infty$: $\pi(0)=e^\alpha/(1+e^\alpha)\to 1$ and $\pi(1)=e^{\alpha+\beta}/(1+e^{\alpha+\beta})=1/2$ exactly, so the likelihood tends to $(1/2)^{10}$, the unrestricted maximum. Hence no finite maximizer exists — the MLE is "at infinity."
