**Central idea.** Minimal sufficiency makes $T_2$ a function of $T_1$, so Rao–Blackwellizing twice collapses to once — and the conditional variance decomposition shows the second Rao–Blackwellization can only remove variance.

**Solution.** (a) Since $T_2$ is minimal sufficient and $T_1$ is sufficient, $T_2 = r(T_1)$ for some $r$, so $\sigma(T_2)\subset\sigma(T_1)$. By the tower property, $E(U_1\mid T_2) = E(E(U\mid T_1)\mid T_2) = E(U\mid T_2) = U_2$.
(b) By Theorem 4.4.7, $\mathrm{Var}(U_1) = \mathrm{Var}(E(U_1\mid T_2)) + \mathrm{E}[\mathrm{Var}(U_1\mid T_2)] = \mathrm{Var}(U_2) + \mathrm{E}[\mathrm{Var}(U_1\mid T_2)] \ge \mathrm{Var}(U_2)$, since the second term is nonnegative. Thus Rao–Blackwellizing with the minimal sufficient statistic gives the smallest variance.
