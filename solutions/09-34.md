**Central idea.** Known $\sigma$: deterministic length gives $n \ge (8z_{.025})^2$; unknown $\sigma$: the random length condition becomes a chi-squared probability.

**Solution.** (a) Length $= 2z_{.025}\sigma/\sqrt n \le \sigma/4 \iff n \ge (8 \times 1.96)^2 = 245.86$, so $n = 246$.
(b) Length $= 2t_{n-1,.025}s/\sqrt n \le \sigma/4 \iff (n-1)s^2/\sigma^2 \le (n-1)n/(64t_{n-1,.025}^2)$. Since $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_{\,n-1}$, need $P(\chi^2_{n-1} \le (n-1)n/(64t_{n-1,.025}^2)) \ge .90$, giving $n = 276$ ($n=275$ gives $.8956 < .90$).
