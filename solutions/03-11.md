**Central idea.** Write the hypergeometric pmf as $\binom{K}{x}$ times a ratio of falling factorials; each fixed-length product tends to 1 while the powers tend to $p^x(1-p)^{K-x}$; the Poisson step is the ordinary binomial-to-Poisson limit (or Lemma 2.3.14 applied directly).

**Solution.** Write $(a)_b = a(a-1)\cdots(a-b+1)$. Then
$$P(X=x\mid N,M,K) = \binom{K}{x}\frac{(M)_x\,(N-M)_{K-x}}{(N)_K},$$
since $\binom{M}{x}\binom{N-M}{K-x}\big/\binom{N}{K} = \binom{K}{x}(M)_x(N-M)_{K-x}/(N)_K$.
(a) $\frac{(M)_x(N-M)_{K-x}}{(N)_K} = \frac{M^x(N-M)^{K-x}}{N^K}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i<x}(1-i/M)\prod_{j<K-x}(1-j/(N-M))}{\prod_{l<K}(1-l/N)} \to p^x(1-p)^{K-x}$, because $M/N\to p$ and each product has a fixed number of factors, each tending to 1. (b) By (a), for $K\to\infty$ the hypergeometric tends to Binomial$(K,M/N)$; with $M/N\to0$ and $K(M/N)\to\lambda$, the Poisson limit theorem gives $\binom{K}{x}(M/N)^x(1-M/N)^{K-x}\to e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x/x!$. (c) Directly: for fixed $x$, $\binom{K}{x} = \frac{K^x}{x!}(1+o(1))$ and $\frac{(M)_x}{(N)_x} = \frac{M^x}{N^x}(1+o(1))$, while, with $(N)_K = (N)_x(N-x)_{K-x}$,
$$\frac{(N-M)_{K-x}}{(N-x)_{K-x}} = \prod_{j=0}^{K-x-1}\left(1-\frac{M-x}{\,N-x-j\,}\right) \to e^{-\lambda}$$
by Lemma 2.3.14 (the $K-x$ terms each tend to 0 and sum to $\frac{(K-x)(M-x)}{N-x}\to\lambda$). Multiplying gives $\frac{K^x}{x!}\frac{M^x}{N^x}e^{-\lambda}\to\frac{\lambda^x}{x!}e^{-\lambda}$.
