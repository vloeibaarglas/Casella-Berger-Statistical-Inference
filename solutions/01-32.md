**Central idea.** Being hired at trial $i$ implies being the best among the first $i$; by symmetry, $P(\text{best of } i \text{ is best of } N) = i/N$.

**Solution.** Let $R_i$ = {candidate $i$ is the best among the first $i$}; hiring at trial $i$ implies $R_i$. $P(R_i) = 1/i$ (all $i$ positions equally likely to hold the max of the first $i$). Also $P(R_i \cap \{\text{candidate } i \text{ is best overall}\}) = P(\text{candidate } i \text{ is best overall}) = 1/N$ (a global best is automatically a record). Hence
$$P(\text{best hired} \mid \text{hired at } i) = \frac{1/N}{1/i} = \frac{i}{N}.$$
