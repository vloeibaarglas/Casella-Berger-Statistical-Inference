**Central idea.** The LRT statistic is monotone in $t = \sqrt n(\bar x-\theta_0)/s$; one- and two-sided inversions give the one- and two-sided $t$-intervals, and both LRTs are unbiased tests.

**Solution.** (a) The LRT of $H_0:\theta=\theta_0$ vs $H_1:\theta<\theta_0$ rejects for small $t = \sqrt n(\bar x-\theta_0)/s$: $-2\log\lambda = n\log(1+t^2/(n-1))$, reject iff $t \le -t_{n-1,\alpha}$. $A(\theta_0) = \{\theta_0 \le \bar x + t_{n-1,\alpha}s/\sqrt n\}$; inversion is exact.
(b) For $H_1:\theta\ne\theta_0$, reject iff $|t| > t_{n-1,\alpha/2}$; $A(\theta_0) = \{|\bar x-\theta_0| \le t_{n-1,\alpha/2}s/\sqrt n\}$, inverting gives (9.2.14).
(c) The one-sided LRT is UMP, hence unbiased; inverting gives the unbiased bound as in 9.47. The two-sided $t$-test is unbiased (Example 8.3.20/Exercise 8.38); by 9.46 the inverted interval is unbiased.
