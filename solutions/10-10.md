**Central idea.** $\tau(p)=p(1-p)$ has $\tau'(p)=1-2p$; away from $1/2$ the delta method gives efficiency, but at $p=1/2$ the linear term vanishes and the second-order (chi-squared) term takes over at rate $n$.

**Solution.** (a) For $p\ne 1/2$, $\tau'(p)=1-2p\ne 0$, so by the delta method $\sqrt n(\hat p(1-\hat p)-p(1-p))\xrightarrow{d} n(0,(1-2p)^2p(1-p))$. The Cramér–Rao bound for $\tau(p)$ is $[\tau'(p)]^2/(nI(p)) = (1-2p)^2p(1-p)/n$; the MLE attains it, hence is asymptotically efficient.
(b) At $p=1/2$, $\tau'(1/2)=0$, $\tau''(1/2)=-2$. Theorem 5.5.26 with $\sigma^2=p(1-p)=1/4$ gives $n(\hat p(1-\hat p)-1/4)\xrightarrow{d} \frac{(1/4)(-2)}{2}\chi^2_1 = -\tfrac14\chi^2_1$.
(c) With $T\sim$ Binomial$(n,p)$ and $U = \hat p(1-\hat p) = T/n-T^2/n^2$, binomial moments give the exact
$$\mathrm{Var}[\hat p(1-\hat p)] = \frac{p(1-p)(n-1)\big[n(1-2p)^2+6p(1-p)-1\big]}{n^3},$$
in particular $\frac{n-1}{8n^3}$ at $p=1/2$. Yes — the reason is now clear: the first-order delta-method variance $(1-2p)^2p(1-p)/n$ is the $O(1/n)$ term, which vanishes at $p=1/2$; the exact formula shows the surviving terms are $O(1/n^2)$, a second-order effect the linear approximation cannot see.
