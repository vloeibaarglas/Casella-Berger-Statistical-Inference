**Central idea.** Embed two experiments with proportional likelihoods in a mixture experiment, collapse the two data points with a sufficient statistic, and let the Sufficiency and Conditionality Principles force the evidences equal.

**Solution.** Let $E_1,E_2$ have data $\mathbf{x}^*_1,\mathbf{x}^*_2$ with $L_1(\theta\mid\mathbf{x}^*_1) \propto L_2(\theta\mid\mathbf{x}^*_2)$. Form the mixture experiment $E^*$ (perform $E_j$ with probability $1/2$) and the statistic $T$ collapsing $(1,\mathbf{x}^*_1)$ and $(2,\mathbf{x}^*_2)$ to one value (identity elsewhere); $T$ is sufficient in $E^*$ (Exercise 6.33(a)). By the Formal Sufficiency Principle, $\mathrm{Ev}(E^*,(1,\mathbf{x}^*_1)) = \mathrm{Ev}(E^*,(2,\mathbf{x}^*_2))$. By the Conditionality Principle, $\mathrm{Ev}(E^*,(j,\mathbf{x}^*_j)) = \mathrm{Ev}(E_j,\mathbf{x}^*_j)$. Hence $\mathrm{Ev}(E_1,\mathbf{x}^*_1) = \mathrm{Ev}(E_2,\mathbf{x}^*_2)$: the evidence depends on $(E,\mathbf{x})$ only through the likelihood function up to proportionality.
