**Central idea.** The same physical measurement is $y = \frac95x+32$; converting the Fahrenheit estimate back to Celsius reproduces $T(x)$ — but the two rules are different functions, so formal invariance fails.

**Solution.** (a) The same data in Fahrenheit is $y = \frac95x+32$. Then
$$\tfrac59(T^*(y)-32) = \tfrac59\big(\tfrac12y+\tfrac12(212)-32\big) = \tfrac59\big(\tfrac12y+74\big).$$
Substituting $y = \frac95x+32$: $\frac12\big(\frac95x+32\big)+74 = \frac{9}{10}x+90$, and $\frac59\big(\frac{9}{10}x+90\big) = \frac12x+50 = T(x)$. The converted estimate agrees with $T(x)$: measurement equivariance holds.
(b) $T(x) = .5x+50$ while $T^*(x) = .5x+106$: $T(x)\ne T^*(x)$. The estimation rule changes with the scale, violating formal invariance, so the estimators are not equivariant in the sense of the Equivariance Principle.
