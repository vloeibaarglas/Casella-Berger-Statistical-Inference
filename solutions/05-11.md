**Central idea.** Jensen's inequality for the strictly concave square root: $E\sqrt{S^2} \leq \sqrt{ES^2}$, with strict inequality unless $S^2$ is a.s. constant — and a non-degenerate sample forces $S^2$ to take both the value $0$ and positive values with positive probability.

**Solution.** By Jensen, $ES = E\sqrt{S^2} \leq \sqrt{E[S^2]} = \sqrt{\sigma^2} = \sigma$, since $\sqrt{\cdot}$ is concave. For strict concavity, equality holds iff $S^2$ is a.s. constant. Suppose $\sigma^2 > 0$ and, to get a contradiction, $S^2 = c$ a.s. Then $c = ES^2 = \sigma^2 > 0$. But $E[S^2 \mid X_1] = \sigma^2$ a.s. is a quadratic equation in $X_1$ (expanding $S^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}(\sum X_i^2 - n\bar{X}^2)$ and conditioning, the $X_1^2$ coefficient is $1/n \neq 0$), so $X_1$ takes at most two values a.s. With $X_1$ non-degenerate two-valued, $S^2 = 0$ on the all-equal event (positive probability) and $S^2 > 0$ on $\{X_1 \neq X_2\}$ (positive probability) — not a.s. constant, a contradiction. Hence $S^2$ is non-degenerate and $ES < \sigma$: the sample standard deviation is always biased downward.
