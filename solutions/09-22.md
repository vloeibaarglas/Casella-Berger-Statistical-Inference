**Central idea.** For the negative binomial (counting failures), tail probabilities are *decreasing* in $p$, so the Clopper–Pearson equations are reversed relative to the binomial: $L$ solves $P_L(X \le x) = \alpha/2$ and $U$ solves $P_U(X \ge x) = \alpha/2$.

**Solution.** With $X$ = failures before $r$ successes: $P_p(X \le x) = P_p(\text{Bin}(x+r,p) \ge r) = P(\text{beta}(r,x+1) \le p)$ and $P_p(X \ge x) = P_p(\text{Bin}(x+r-1,p) \le r-1) = P(\text{beta}(r,x) \ge p)$. Since $P_p(X\le x)$ increases in $p$, $L$ solves $P_L(X\le x)=\alpha/2$, i.e. $L$ is the $\alpha/2$ quantile of $\text{beta}(r,x+1)$: $\frac{L}{1-L}\cdot\frac{x+1}{r} = F_{2r,2(x+1),1-\alpha/2} = 1/F_{2(x+1),2r,\alpha/2}$, giving the stated lower bound. Since $P_p(X\ge x)$ decreases in $p$, $U$ solves $P_U(X\ge x)=\alpha/2$, i.e. $U$ is the $1-\alpha/2$ quantile of $\text{beta}(r,x)$: $\frac{U}{1-U}\cdot\frac{x}{r} = F_{2r,2x,\alpha/2}$, giving the stated upper bound. (If $x=0$, $L=0$.) Numerically ($r=3,x=7,\alpha=.1$): direct solution gives $[.087264,.549642]$, matching the formulas.
