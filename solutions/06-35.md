**Central idea.** Stopping after the first failure gives four possible histories; each has the same $\theta$-dependence as a fixed-$n$ binomial outcome with the same success count.

**Solution.** The four sample points (stopping at the first failure) are $(0)$, $(1,0)$, $(1,1,0)$, $(1,1,1)$, with likelihoods $1-p$, $p(1-p)$, $p^2(1-p)$, $p^3$. These are proportional (in $p$) to fixed-sample-size binomial likelihoods: $\text{binomial}(1,p)$ with $0$ successes ($\propto 1-p$), $\text{binomial}(2,p)$ with $1$ success ($\propto p(1-p)$), $\text{binomial}(3,p)$ with $2$ successes ($\propto p^2(1-p)$), $\text{binomial}(3,p)$ with $3$ successes ($\propto p^3$). By the Formal Likelihood Principle, each yields the same evidence about $p$ as its fixed-$n$ counterpart: the data-dependent stopping rule does not affect inference about $p$.
