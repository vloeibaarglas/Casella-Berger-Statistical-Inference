**Central idea.** Both sides vanish at $p=1$ (for $x<n$) and have the same derivative: differentiating the binomial cdf telescopes to a single term matching the integral's derivative. *Note: as stated the identity needs $x<n$ — for $x=n$ the left side is 1 while the improper integral on the right diverges for $p<1$.*

**Solution.** Let $L(p)=\sum_{k=0}^x\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ and $R(p)=(n-x)\binom{n}{x}\int_0^{1-p}t^{n-x-1}(1-t)^xdt$, for $0\le x<n$. Differentiating under the integral,
$$R'(p)=-(n-x)\binom{n}{x}(1-p)^{n-x-1}p^x.$$
For $L$, using $k\binom{n}{k}=n\binom{n-1}{k-1}$ and $(n-k)\binom{n}{k}=n\binom{n-1}{k}$,
\begin{align*}
L'(p)&=\sum_{k=0}^x\binom{n}{k}\big[kp^{k-1}(1-p)^{n-k}-(n-k)p^k(1-p)^{n-k-1}\big]\\
&=n\sum_{j=0}^{x-1}\binom{n-1}{j}p^j(1-p)^{n-1-j}-n\sum_{j=0}^{x}\binom{n-1}{j}p^j(1-p)^{n-1-j}\\
&=-n\binom{n-1}{x}p^x(1-p)^{n-1-x}=-(n-x)\binom{n}{x}p^x(1-p)^{n-x-1}=R'(p),
\end{align*}
the two sums telescoping (with $j=k-1$ in the first). Since $L(1)=P(\mathrm{Bin}(n,1)\le x)=0=R(1)$ for $x<n$, $L\equiv R$. (For $x=n$ and $p<1$, the integral has a $t^{-1}$ singularity at zero, so the displayed right side is an undefined $0\cdot\infty$, not zero. The identity requires integer $0\le x\le n-1$.)
