**Central idea.** (a) The substitution $u = x/v-x/\lambda$ turns the $v$-integral into $\Gamma(1-r)$ times the gamma kernel. (b) $v = \lambda w$ gives a beta$(r,1-r)$ integral. (c) A gamma$(r,\lambda)$ density with $r>1$ increases near $0$ ($\frac{d}{dx}\log f = (r-1)/x-1/\lambda > 0$), but any exponential mixture has $\frac{d}{dx}\log h(x) = h'(x)/h(x) < 0$ since $h'(x) = -\int v^{-2}e^{-x/v}q(v)\,dv < 0$.

**Solution.** (a) For $0<r<1$ (the case $r=1$ is the degenerate point mass at $v=\lambda$), substitute $u = x/v-x/\lambda$ ($dv = -v^2du/x$): the integrand becomes $\frac{1}{\Gamma(r)\Gamma(1-r)}x^{r-1}\lambda^{-r}e^{-x/\lambda}u^{-r}e^{-u}$, and $\int_0^\infty u^{-r}e^{-u}du = \Gamma(1-r)$, leaving $\frac{x^{r-1}e^{-x/\lambda}}{\Gamma(r)\lambda^r} = f(x)$.
(b) With $v = \lambda w$: $\int_0^\lambda p_\lambda(v)dv = \frac{1}{\Gamma(r)\Gamma(1-r)}\int_0^1 w^{r-1}(1-w)^{-r}dw = \frac{B(r,1-r)}{\Gamma(r)\Gamma(1-r)} = 1$ (convergent for $r<1$).
(c) If $f(x) = h(x) := \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x/v}}{v}q_\lambda(v)dv$, then $h'(x) = -\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x/v}}{v^2}q_\lambda(v)dv < 0$, so $\frac{d}{dx}\log h(x) < 0$ for all $x>0$. But for gamma$(r,\lambda)$ with $r>1$, $\frac{d}{dx}\log f(x) = \frac{r-1}{x}-\frac{1}{\lambda} > 0$ for $0 < x < \lambda(r-1)$ — contradiction.
