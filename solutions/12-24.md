**Central idea.** $|y_i-\beta x_i|=|x_i|\cdot|t_i-\beta|$, so the LAD problem is a *weighted median* of the ratios $t_i=y_i/x_i$ with weights $|x_i|$; the $k^*$ conditions are exactly the weighted-median balance conditions.

**Solution.** Terms with $x_i=0$ are constant in $\beta$ and can be dropped; assume $x_i\ne 0$. Then
$$\sum_i|y_i-\beta x_i|=\sum_i|x_i|\,|t_i-\beta|\equiv L(\beta),\qquad t_i=y_i/x_i,$$
a convex piecewise-linear function. Its subgradient at $\beta$ is $\sum_i|x_{(i)}|s_i$ with $s_i=+1$ if $t_{(i)}<\beta$, $-1$ if $t_{(i)}>\beta$, and $s_i\in[-1,1]$ if $t_{(i)}=\beta$. At $\beta=t_{(k^*+1)}$ this equals
$$\sum_{i\le k^*}|x_{(i)}|-\sum_{i>k^*+1}|x_{(i)}|+|x_{(k^*+1)}|u,\quad u\in[-1,1].$$
With $W=\sum_i|x_{(i)}|$ and $W_L(k)=\sum_{i\le k}|x_{(i)}|$, the two conditions on $k^*$ are $W_L(k^*)\le W/2$ and $W_L(k^*+1)>W/2$; choosing $u=(W-2W_L(k^*))/|x_{(k^*+1)}|-1\in[-1,1]$ makes the subgradient contain $0$. Hence $t_{(k^*+1)}$ minimizes $L$ — it is the weighted median of the $t_i$ with weights $|x_i|$.
