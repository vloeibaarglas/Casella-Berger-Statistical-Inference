**Central idea.** Write $X = (U/p)/(V/q)$ with $U \sim \chi^2_p \perp V \sim \chi^2_q$; moments come from $E[V^{-k}]$ of a chi-square, and (d) is the classic $U/(U+V) \sim \text{Beta}$ fact in disguise.

**Solution.** Write $X = \frac{U/p}{V/q}$, $U \sim \chi^2_p \perp V \sim \chi^2_q$.
(a) Transforming $(U, V) \to (X, V)$ ($u = pxv/q$, Jacobian $pv/q$) and integrating out $v$:
$$f_X(x) = \frac{\Gamma((p+q)/2)}{\Gamma(p/2)\Gamma(q/2)}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{p/2} x^{p/2-1}\left(1 + \frac{p}{q}x\right)^{-(p+q)/2}, \quad x > 0.$$
(b) $E[X] = E[U/p]\,E[q/V] = 1 \cdot q \cdot E[V^{-1}]$. For $V \sim \chi^2_q$, $E[V^{-k}] = 1/[(q-2)(q-4)\cdots(q-2k)]$ ($q > 2k$), so $E[X] = \frac{q}{q-2}$ ($q > 2$). Similarly $E[X^2] = E[(U/p)^2]E[(q/V)^2] = \frac{p+2}{p}\cdot\frac{q^2}{(q-2)(q-4)}$, giving
$$\mathrm{Var}\, X = \frac{2q^2(p+q-2)}{p(q-2)^2(q-4)}, \qquad q > 4.$$
(c) $1/X = (V/q)/(U/p)$ is exactly the $F$ construction with $p,q$ swapped: $1/X \sim F_{q,p}$.
(d) $\frac{(p/q)X}{1+(p/q)X} = \frac{U/V}{1+U/V} = \frac{U}{U+V} \sim \text{Beta}(p/2, q/2)$, since for independent $\chi^2$ variables the ratio $U/(U+V)$ is Beta (Example 4.5.4/Theorem 4.4.7).
