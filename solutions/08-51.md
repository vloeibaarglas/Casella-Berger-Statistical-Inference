**Central idea.** $p(\mathbf x) = \sup_{\Theta_0} P_\theta(W \ge W(\mathbf x))$; monotonicity of $c_\alpha$ in $\alpha$ turns "$W(\mathbf x) \ge c_\alpha$" into "$\alpha \ge p(\mathbf x)$".

**Solution.** Let $p(\mathbf x) = \sup_{\theta \in \Theta_0} P_\theta(W(\mathbf X) \ge W(\mathbf x))$ (Definition 8.3.9) and $A = \{\alpha: W(\mathbf x) \ge c_\alpha\}$ (levels rejecting on $\mathbf x$). Since $\sup_{\Theta_0}P_\theta(W \ge c_\alpha) = \alpha$, $c_\alpha$ is nonincreasing in $\alpha$. If $c_\alpha \le W(\mathbf x)$ then $\{W \ge W(\mathbf x)\} \subseteq \{W \ge c_\alpha\}$, so $p(\mathbf x) \le \alpha$; hence $p(\mathbf x) \le \inf A$. If $c_\alpha > W(\mathbf x)$ then $\{W \ge c_\alpha\} \subset \{W \ge W(\mathbf x)\}$, so $\alpha \le p(\mathbf x)$; hence $\sup\{\alpha: c_\alpha > W(\mathbf x)\} \le p(\mathbf x)$, and by monotonicity this sup equals $\inf A$. Thus $\inf A = p(\mathbf x)$: the p-value is the smallest level at which $H_0$ rejects.
