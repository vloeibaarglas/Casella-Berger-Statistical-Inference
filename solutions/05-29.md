**Central idea.** $P(\sum X_i > 100.4) = P(\bar{X} > 1.004)$; by the CLT, $\bar{X} \approx n(1, 0.05^2/100)$, reducing the problem to a standard normal tail.

**Solution.** Assume the 100 booklet weights are iid with mean $1$ and sd $0.05$. Then $P(\text{total} > 100.4) = P(\bar{X} > 1.004)$. By the Central Limit Theorem, $\bar{X} \;\dot\sim\; n(1, 0.05^2/100) = n(1, 0.005^2)$, so
$$P(\bar{X} > 1.004) \approx P\left(Z > \frac{1.004-1}{0.005}\right) = P(Z > 0.8) \approx 0.2119.$$
The approximation needs $n = 100$ large enough for the CLT and iid weights; no distributional assumption on individual weights is needed. (Exact value would require the weight distribution; with only mean/variance, CLT is the tool.)
