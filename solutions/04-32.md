**Central idea.** (a) The Poisson–gamma mixture integral is a gamma integral yielding a negative binomial pmf. (b) *As stated this is incorrect* — even the means differ ($p\alpha\beta$ versus $\alpha\beta$); the three-stage marginal is negative binomial with a different success probability, coinciding with (a) only if $p=1$.

**Solution.** (a) (Here $\text{gamma}(\alpha,\beta)$ uses the scale parameterization, mean $\alpha\beta$.)
$$P(Y=y) = \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^y}{y!}\frac{\lambda^{\alpha-1}e^{-\lambda/\beta}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\beta^\alpha}\,d\lambda = \frac{\Gamma(y+\alpha)}{y!\,\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\frac{1}{\beta+1}\right)^\alpha\left(\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}\right)^y,$$
i.e. negative binomial (failures before the $\alpha$th success) with success probability $1/(\beta+1)$ when $\alpha$ is an integer. $\mathrm{E}Y = \mathrm{E}\Lambda = \alpha\beta$; $\mathrm{Var}\,Y = \mathrm{E}\Lambda+\mathrm{Var}\Lambda = \alpha\beta+\alpha\beta^2$.
(b) *Erratum — the claim is false as stated.* By binomial thinning, $Y|\Lambda \sim \text{Poisson}(p\Lambda)$, so $\mathrm{E}Y = p\,\mathrm{E}\Lambda = p\alpha\beta \ne \alpha\beta$ for $p \ne 1$; the marginals cannot be the same. What is true: the three-stage marginal is again negative binomial,
$$P(Y=y) = \frac{\Gamma(y+\alpha)}{y!\,\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\frac{1}{1+\beta p}\right)^\alpha\left(\frac{\beta p}{1+\beta p}\right)^y,$$
i.e. $\text{NB}(\alpha, 1/(1+\beta p))$ in the failures parameterization — equal to (a)'s $\text{NB}(\alpha,1/(1+\beta))$ only when $p=1$.
