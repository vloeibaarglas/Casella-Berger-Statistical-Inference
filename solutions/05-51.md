**Central idea.** $E[U_i] = 1/2$, $\mathrm{Var}(U_i) = 1/12$: the sum has exactly mean 0 and variance 1, and 12 summands is enough for the CLT to bite — but the support stays $[-6,6]$ and the kurtosis is $2.9 \neq 3$.

**Solution.** (a) $E[X] = 12\cdot\frac12 - 6 = 0$, $\mathrm{Var}(X) = 12\cdot\frac{1}{12} = 1$; by the CLT, a sum of 12 iid variables is approximately normal, so $X \;\dot\sim\; n(0,1)$.
(b) $X$ is bounded: $|X| \leq 6$ always, whereas $P(|Z| > 6) > 0$ for $Z \sim n(0,1)$ — the tails are completely wrong (also the sum is exactly, not asymptotically, mean-0 variance-1, which is deceptive).
(c) By symmetry $E[X] = E[X^3] = 0$ and $\mathrm{Var}(X) = 1$, matching $n(0,1)$. With $Y_i = U_i - 1/2$ ($E[Y_i^2] = 1/12$, $E[Y_i^4] = 1/80$),
$$E[X^4] = E\Big[\Big(\sum Y_i\Big)^4\Big] = \sum_iE[Y_i^4] + 3\sum_{i\neq j}E[Y_i^2]E[Y_j^2] = 12\cdot\frac{1}{80} + 3\cdot12\cdot11\cdot\frac{1}{144} = 0.15 + 2.75 = \frac{29}{10} = 2.9,$$
versus $3$ for $n(0,1)$: slightly platykurtic (lighter tails), as expected from a bounded sum.
