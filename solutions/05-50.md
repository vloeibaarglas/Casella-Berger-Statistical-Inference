**Central idea.** In polar coordinates $(R,\Theta) = (\sqrt{-2\log U_2},\, 2\pi U_1)$, $R$ is Rayleigh and $\Theta$ uniform$(0,2\pi)$, independent; the polar-to-Cartesian Jacobian $r$ converts their joint density into the product of two standard normals.

**Solution.** Put $R = \sqrt{-2\log U_2}$, $\Theta = 2\pi U_1$. Then $R^2 = -2\log U_2 \sim \chi^2_2$ (by 5.47), i.e., $R$ has Rayleigh density $f_R(r) = re^{-r^2/2}$, $r > 0$, independent of $\Theta \sim \text{uniform}(0,2\pi)$. The map $(r,\theta) \mapsto (x_1,x_2) = (r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta)$ has Jacobian $r$, so
$$f_{X_1,X_2}(x_1,x_2) = f_{R,\Theta}(r,\theta)\cdot\frac{1}{r} = \frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-r^2/2} = \frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-(x_1^2+x_2^2)/2} = \phi(x_1)\phi(x_2),$$
with $r^2 = x_1^2+x_2^2$. Hence $X_1, X_2$ are independent $n(0,1)$.
