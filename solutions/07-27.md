**Central idea.** The complete-data log-likelihood separates in $(\beta,\tau)$; with $x_1$ missing the $\beta$ score involves only $i\ge 2$, giving the closed form directly.
**Solution.** (a) From (7.2.11), $\partial l/\partial\beta=-\sum\tau_i+\sum y_i/\beta=0$ and $\partial l/\partial\tau_j=-(\beta+1)+(x_j+y_j)/\tau_j=0$; solving jointly gives $\hat\beta=\sum y_i/\sum x_i$, $\hat\tau_j=(x_j+y_j)/(\hat\beta+1)$, i.e. (7.2.12).
(b) At a fixed point of (7.2.23), $\hat\beta=\sum y_i/(\hat\tau_1+\sum_{i\ge2}x_i)$ and $\hat\tau_j=(x_j+y_j)/(\hat\beta+1)$ ($j\ge2$), $\hat\tau_1=(\hat\beta\hat\tau_1+y_1)/(\hat\beta+1)$; these are exactly the incomplete-data likelihood equations (7.2.16).
(c) With $x_1$ missing, the incomplete-data log-likelihood drops the $x_1$ terms: $\partial l/\partial\tau_1=-\beta+y_1/\tau_1=0$ gives $\hat\tau_1=y_1/\hat\beta$; $\partial l/\partial\tau_j=-(\beta+1)+(x_j+y_j)/\tau_j=0$ gives $\hat\tau_j=(x_j+y_j)/(\hat\beta+1)$ ($j\ge2$); and $\partial l/\partial\beta=-\sum_{i\ge2}\tau_i+\sum_{i\ge2}y_i/\beta=0$ with $\sum_{i\ge2}\hat\tau_i=(\sum_{i\ge2}(x_i+y_i))/(\hat\beta+1)$ yields $\hat\beta=\sum_{i=2}^n y_i/\sum_{i=2}^n x_i$. This direct solution of (7.2.16) is unique, so the EM limit from part (b) — which satisfies (7.2.16) — coincides with these MLEs.
