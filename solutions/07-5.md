**Central idea.** The likelihood-ratio inequalities pin $k$ between consecutive values of $1/z$; solving $(1-p)^n=\prod_{i=1}^n(1-x_iz)$ for $\hat z$ and flooring $1/\hat z$ gives the integer MLE.
**Solution.** (a) In Example 7.2.9 the likelihood ratios give $L(k)/L(k-1)\ge 1$ iff $k\le 1/z$ (for the $z$ solving the likelihood equation), and the function $\prod_i(1-x_iz)$ is strictly decreasing in $z$, so there is a unique $\hat z$ with $(1-p)^n=\prod_i(1-x_i\hat z)$. The integer $k$ maximizing $L(k)$ is then the largest integer with $k\le 1/\hat z$, i.e. $\hat k=\lfloor 1/\hat z\rfloor$.
(b) Solve $(1/2)^4=(1-0\cdot z)(1-20z)(1-z)(1-19z)$, i.e. $(1-20z)(1-z)(1-19z)=1/16$. Numerically $\hat z\approx 0.0382$, so $1/\hat z\approx 26.20$ and $\hat k=\lfloor 26.20\rfloor=26$.
