**Central idea.** With $A=\sum X_i\sim\mathrm{gamma}(n,\theta)$, $B=\sum Y_i\sim\mathrm{gamma}(n,1/\theta)$, write $T=\theta\sqrt{G_2/G_1}$ and $U=(\theta A)(B/\theta)$ for iid $G_1,G_2\sim\mathrm{gamma}(n,1)$; inverse-gamma moments give everything.
**Solution.** In Exercise 6.37, $X_i\sim\mathrm{exponential}$(rate $\theta$), $Y_i\sim\mathrm{exponential}$(rate $1/\theta$), independent, so $A:=\sum X_i\sim\mathrm{gamma}(n,\theta)$, $B:=\sum Y_i\sim\mathrm{gamma}(n,1/\theta)$, independent.
(a) $\log L=-\theta A-B/\theta$; $\partial/\partial\theta=-A+B/\theta^2=0$ gives $\hat\theta=\sqrt{B/A}=T$. With $G_1=\theta A$, $G_2=B/\theta$ iid $\mathrm{gamma}(n,1)$: $U=AB=G_1G_2$ is free of $\theta$ (ancillary), and $T=\theta\sqrt{G_2/G_1}$. Since $E[G^{\pm1/2}]=\Gamma(n\pm1/2)/\Gamma(n)$, $E[T]=\theta\,\Gamma(n+1/2)\Gamma(n-1/2)/\Gamma(n)^2$; and $E[T^2]=\theta^2E[G_2]E[1/G_1]=\theta^2\cdot n\cdot\frac{1}{\,n-1\,}=\theta^2\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(n-1)/\Gamma(n)^2$.
(b) $E[1/A]=\theta/(n-1)$ (inverse-gamma mean), so $E[Z_1]=\theta$; $\mathrm{Var}(1/A)=\theta^2/[(n-1)^2(n-2)]$, so $\mathrm{Var}(Z_1)=\theta^2/(n-2)$. $E[Z_2]=\theta$, $\mathrm{Var}(Z_2)=\theta^2/n$.
(c) $Z_1\perp Z_2$; $\mathrm{Var}(aZ_1+(1-a)Z_2)=\theta^2\big[\frac{a^2}{n-2}+\frac{(1-a)^2}{n}\big]$, minimized at $a^*=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}$ with variance $\frac{\theta^2}{2(n-1)}$. The bias-corrected MLE $T_c=T\,\Gamma(n)^2/[\Gamma(n+1/2)\Gamma(n-1/2)]$ is unbiased with $\mathrm{Var}(T_c)=\theta^2\big[\frac{n}{n-1}c^2-1\big]$, $c=\Gamma(n)^2/[\Gamma(n+1/2)\Gamma(n-1/2)]<1$; at $n=3$, $\mathrm{Var}(T_c)\approx0.230\,\theta^2<0.25\,\theta^2=\mathrm{Var}(a^*Z_1+(1-a^*)Z_2)$, and $T_c$ stays ahead asymptotically: $\mathrm{Var}(T_c)=\theta^2/(2n)+3\theta^2/(8n^2)+o(1/n^2)$ versus $\theta^2/[2(n-1)]=\theta^2/(2n)+\theta^2/(2n^2)+o(1/n^2)$ (confirmed numerically at $n=4,5,6,10,50,200,2000$ as well).
