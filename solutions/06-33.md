**Central idea.** The constant $C$ is the likelihood proportionality factor; it absorbs the difference between the two collapsed points so the factorization goes through, and each principle then contributes one equality.

**Solution.** (a) In $E^*$, $f^*((j,\mathbf{x}_j)\mid\theta) = \frac12 f_j(\mathbf{x}_j\mid\theta)$. By hypothesis $f_1(\mathbf{x}^*_1\mid\theta) = c\,f_2(\mathbf{x}^*_2\mid\theta)$ for a $\theta$-free $c$; take $C = f_2(\mathbf{x}^*_2\mid\theta)/f_1(\mathbf{x}^*_1\mid\theta) = 1/c$, free of $\theta$. Recall $T(1,\mathbf{x}^*_1) = T(2,\mathbf{x}^*_2) = (1,\mathbf{x}^*_1)$. If $(j,\mathbf{x}_j) = (2,\mathbf{x}^*_2)$: $g(T\mid\theta)h = g((1,\mathbf{x}^*_1)\mid\theta)\,C = \frac12 f_1(\mathbf{x}^*_1\mid\theta)\,C = \frac12 f_2(\mathbf{x}^*_2\mid\theta) = f^*((2,\mathbf{x}^*_2)\mid\theta)$. If $(j,\mathbf{x}_j) = (1,\mathbf{x}^*_1)$: $g(T\mid\theta)h = \frac12 f_1(\mathbf{x}^*_1\mid\theta)\cdot 1 = f^*$. Otherwise $T(j,\mathbf{x}_j) = (j,\mathbf{x}_j)$, $h = 1$, and $g(T\mid\theta)h = f^*$. Thus $f^*= g(T\mid\theta)h$ with $h$ $\theta$-free: $T$ is sufficient by factorization.
(b) $T(1,\mathbf{x}^*_1) = T(2,\mathbf{x}^*_2)$, so the Formal Sufficiency Principle gives $\mathrm{Ev}(E^*,(1,\mathbf{x}^*_1)) = \mathrm{Ev}(E^*,(2,\mathbf{x}^*_2))$, i.e. (6.3.4). The Conditionality Principle ("only the performed experiment matters") gives $\mathrm{Ev}(E^*,(j,\mathbf{x}^*_j)) = \mathrm{Ev}(E_j,\mathbf{x}^*_j)$, i.e. (6.3.5). Together: $\mathrm{Ev}(E_1,\mathbf{x}^*_1) = \mathrm{Ev}(E_2,\mathbf{x}^*_2)$ whenever the likelihoods are proportional — the Formal Likelihood Principle.
(c) Apply the Formal Likelihood Principle to $E^*$ vs $E_j$ at $(j,\mathbf{x}_j)$: the $E^*$-likelihood $\frac12 f_j(\mathbf{x}_j\mid\theta)$ is proportional (in $\theta$) to the $E_j$-likelihood $f_j(\mathbf{x}_j\mid\theta)$, so $\mathrm{Ev}(E^*,(j,\mathbf{x}_j)) = \mathrm{Ev}(E_j,\mathbf{x}_j)$: the Conditionality Principle. If $T$ is sufficient and $T(\mathbf{x}) = T(\mathbf{y})$, factorization gives $L(\theta\mid\mathbf{x}) = g(T(\mathbf{x})\mid\theta)h(\mathbf{x}) \propto g(T(\mathbf{y})\mid\theta)h(\mathbf{y}) = L(\theta\mid\mathbf{y})$; the Formal Likelihood Principle then yields $\mathrm{Ev}(E,\mathbf{x}) = \mathrm{Ev}(E,\mathbf{y})$: the Formal Sufficiency Principle.
