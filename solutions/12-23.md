**Central idea.** $E\epsilon_i=\delta\mu$ is constant across $i$; the slope estimator differences the $x_i$'s out ($\sum d_i=0$), while the intercept absorbs the common bias.

**Solution.** Here $E\epsilon_i=(1-\delta)\cdot 0+\delta\cdot\mu=\delta\mu$ for every $i$.

(a) $b=\sum_i d_iY_i$ with $d_i=(x_i-\bar x)/\sum_j(x_j-\bar x)^2$, so $E b=\sum_i d_i(\alpha+\beta x_i+\delta\mu)=\beta+\delta\mu\sum_i d_i=\beta$ since $\sum_i d_i=0$ and $\sum_i d_ix_i=1$.

(b) $E a=E\bar y-\bar xEb=\alpha+\beta\bar x+\delta\mu-\beta\bar x=\alpha+\delta\mu$. Writing $\epsilon'_i=\epsilon_i-\delta\mu$ (so $E\epsilon'_i=0$), the model is $Y_i=(\alpha+\delta\mu)+\beta x_i+\epsilon'_i$ — the bias is absorbed into the intercept and $\beta$ estimation is unaffected.
