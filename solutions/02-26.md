**Central idea.** Shifting by $a$ reduces everything to the even case; the exponential's one-sided support cannot be mirrored.

**Solution.** (a) $n(a,1)$; uniform$(a-c,a+c)$ ($c>0$); $\frac12e^{-|x-a|}$ (Laplace centered at $a$).
(b) $\int_{-\infty}^a f(x)dx=\int_0^\infty f(a-u)du=\int_0^\infty f(a+u)du=\int_a^\infty f(x)dx=\frac12$, so $P(X\le a),P(X\ge a)\ge\frac12$: $a$ is a median.
(c) $EX-a=\int_{-\infty}^\infty(x-a)f(x)dx=\int_0^\infty u\,[f(a+u)-f(a-u)]\,du=0$ (the split is valid since $E|X|<\infty$).
(d) If symmetric about $a$, the support must mirror about $a$. For $a\ge0$: $f(a+(a+1))=e^{-(2a+1)}>0$ but $f(a-(a+1))=f(-1)=0$. For $a<0$: $f(a+(1-a))=e^{-1}>0$ but $f(a-(1-a))=f(2a-1)=0$ since $2a-1<0$. No $a$ works.
(e) Median $m=\log2\approx0.69<1=$ mean.
