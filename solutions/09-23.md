**Central idea.** The LRT statistic $2[Y\log(Y/(n\lambda_0)) - (Y - n\lambda_0)]$ is $\chi^2_1$ asymptotically; inverting it gives an interval nearly identical to the exact $\chi^2$-quantile interval of 9.2.15.

**Solution.** (a) With $Y \sim \text{Poisson}(n\lambda)$, $\lambda(Y;\lambda_0) = (Y/(n\lambda_0))^Y e^{-(Y-n\lambda_0)}$ and $-2\log\lambda = 2[Y\log(Y/(n\lambda_0)) - (Y-n\lambda_0)]$. The $1-\alpha$ LRT interval is $\{\lambda_0 : -2\log\lambda \le \chi^2_{1,\alpha}\}$, versus 9.2.15's exact $[\chi^2_{2Y,1-\alpha/2}/(2n),\ \chi^2_{2(Y+1),\alpha/2}/(2n)]$; both are centered near $Y/n$ and agree closely.
(b) $n=9$, $\sum x = 558$, $\bar x = 62$. LRT $90\%$ interval: $[57.78,\ 66.42]$; Example 9.2.15 interval: $[57.75,\ 66.49]$ — essentially identical.
