**Central idea.** With a continuous prior density, the branch $X=2M$ contributes a Jacobian factor $1/2$. Discrete prior masses use a different formula.

**Solution.** (a) Conditional on $M=m$, $X=m$ or $2m$, each with probability $1/2$. For a continuous prior density $\pi$ of the smaller amount, the two contributions to the density of $X$ at $x$ are $\pi(x)/2$ and $\pi(x/2)/4$. Thus
$$P(M=x\mid X=x)=\frac{2\pi(x)}{2\pi(x)+\pi(x/2)},\qquad P(M=x/2\mid X=x)=\frac{\pi(x/2)}{2\pi(x)+\pi(x/2)}.$$
These conditional probabilities describe the two possible branches of $M$ given $X=x$; they are not unconditional point probabilities.
(b) Therefore
$$E(Y\mid X=x)=\frac{4x\pi(x)+(x/2)\pi(x/2)}{2\pi(x)+\pi(x/2)}>x\iff\pi(x/2)<4\pi(x).$$
For the rate-$\lambda$ density $\pi(m)=\lambda e^{-\lambda m}$, switch iff $x<2\log4/\lambda$. The statement's factor $2$ and threshold $2\log2/\lambda$ omit the Jacobian for a continuous prior. If $\pi$ instead denotes *discrete masses*, those factor-$2$ formulas are appropriate wherever both amounts are in the discrete support. State which model is used.
(c) With a fixed smaller amount $m$, the other envelope is $2m$ when yours is $m$, and $m$ when yours is $2m$. Both envelopes have expectation $3m/2$ before observation, so switching unconditionally has no advantage.
