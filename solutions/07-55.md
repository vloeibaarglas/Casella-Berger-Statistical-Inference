**Central idea.** In each case the extreme order statistic is complete sufficient; Lehmann–Scheffé then blesses the (unique) unbiased function of it.
**Solution.** (a) $X_{(n)}$ is complete sufficient; $E_\theta[X_{(n)}^r]=n\theta^r/(n+r)$, so $\frac{n+r}{n}X_{(n)}^r$ is the UMVUE of $\theta^r$.
(b) For integer $r\ge1$, the explicit estimator is $X_{(1)}^r-(r/n)X_{(1)}^{r-1}$: if $M=\theta+Z$, $Z\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\text{rate }n)$, integration by parts gives $E[M^r]-(r/n)E[M^{r-1}]=\theta^r$. Also, $X_{(1)}$ is complete sufficient; writing $X_{(1)}=\theta+Z$, $Z\sim\mathrm{exponential}(n)$, $E_\theta[X_{(1)}^j]$ is a degree-$j$ polynomial in $\theta$ with leading coefficient $1$. Triangular inversion yields the unique degree-$r$ polynomial $p_r$ with $E_\theta[p_r(X_{(1)})]=\theta^r$ (e.g. $p_1(x)=x-1/n$, $p_2(x)=x^2-2x/n$), which by Lehmann–Scheffé is the UMVUE.
(c) Put $Y_i=e^{-X_i}$; then $Y_i\sim\mathrm{uniform}(e^{-b},\eta)$ iid with $\eta:=e^{-\theta}>e^{-b}$, and $Y_{(n)}=e^{-X_{(1)}}$ is complete sufficient for $\eta$. For $\varphi$ to satisfy $E_\eta[\varphi(Y_{(n)})]=(-\log\eta)^r=\theta^r$ for all $\eta>e^{-b}$,
$$\int_{e^{-b}}^\eta\varphi(y)(y-e^{-b})^{n-1}\,dy=\frac{(\eta-e^{-b})^n(-\log\eta)^r}{n}\qquad\forall\,\eta>e^{-b}.$$
Both sides vanish at $\eta=e^{-b}$; differentiating in $\eta$ gives
$$\varphi(\eta)=(-\log\eta)^r-\frac{r(\eta-e^{-b})}{n\eta}(-\log\eta)^{\,r-1}=(-\log\eta)^{\,r-1}\left[(-\log\eta)-\frac{r(\eta-e^{-b})}{n\eta}\right].$$
Hence, for integer $r\ge1$, the UMVUE of $\theta^r$ is the closed-form
$$\varphi(Y_{(n)})=X_{(1)}^{\,r-1}\left[X_{(1)}-\frac{r}{n}\left(1-e^{\,X_{(1)}-b}\right)\right],$$
a function of the complete sufficient $Y_{(n)}$, hence best unbiased by Lehmann–Scheffé. (Sanity check: as $b\to\infty$, $e^{X_{(1)}-b}\to0$ and this tends to $X_{(1)}^r-\frac{r}{n}X_{(1)}^{r-1}$, reproducing the untruncated-exponential UMVUEs $p_1(x)=x-1/n$, $p_2(x)=x^2-2x/n$ of part (b).)
