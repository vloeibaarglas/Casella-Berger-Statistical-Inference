**Central idea.** $T = \sum X_i \sim \text{Poisson}(n\lambda)$ is sufficient with MLR in $T$, so Karlin–Rubin gives the UMP test $\{T > c\}$. For (b), normal approximations at $\lambda = 1$ and $\lambda = 2$ give two equations in $n$ and $c$.

**Solution.** (a) For $\lambda_2 > \lambda_1$,
$$\frac{f_T(t \mid \lambda_2)}{f_T(t \mid \lambda_1)} = e^{-n(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)}\left(\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}\right)^t,$$
strictly increasing in $t$: MLR in $T$. By Karlin–Rubin, the UMP level-$\alpha$ test rejects for large $T$: $\phi(t) = 1$ if $t > c$, $0$ if $t < c$ (randomizing at $t = c$ if needed for exact size), where $P_{\lambda_0}(T > c) \le \alpha$ determines $c$.
(b) By CLT, $T \approx n(n\lambda, n\lambda)$. Size: $c = n + z_{.95}\sqrt{n} = n + 1.645\sqrt{n}$. Power at $\lambda = 2$: $P(T > c \mid \lambda = 2) \approx 1 - \Phi\left(\frac{c - 2n}{\sqrt{2n}}\right) = .9$, so $\frac{c-2n}{\sqrt{2n}} = -1.2816$. Hence $n + 1.645\sqrt{n} = 2n - 1.2816\sqrt{2n}$; with $t = \sqrt{n}$, $t = 1.645 + 1.2816\sqrt{2} \approx 3.457$, $n \approx 11.95$. Take $n = 12$ (then $c \approx 17.7$ and the power is $\approx .901$).
