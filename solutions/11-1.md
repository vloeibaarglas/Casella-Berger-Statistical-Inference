**Central idea.** The Delta Method: a one-term Taylor expansion linearizes $g$, so the variance scales by the squared derivative; choosing $g'$ proportional to $1/\sqrt{v}$ cancels the $\theta$-dependence.

**Solution.** (a) Expand $g$ about $\theta$: $g(Y) \approx g(\theta) + g'(\theta)(Y - \theta)$. Then $\mathrm{Var}(g(Y)) \approx [g'(\theta)]^2 \mathrm{Var}(Y) = [g'(\theta)]^2 v(\theta)$.
(b) By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, $\frac{d}{dy}g^*(y) = 1/\sqrt{v(y)}$, so $\frac{d}{d\theta}g^*(\theta) = 1/\sqrt{v(\theta)}$. Plugging into (a): $\mathrm{Var}(g^*(Y)) \approx [1/\sqrt{v(\theta)}]^2 v(\theta) = 1$, a constant independent of $\theta$.
