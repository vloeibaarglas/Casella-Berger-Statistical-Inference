**Central idea.** Parametric bootstrap: treat the fitted line and $\hat\sigma^2$ as truth, simulate new data from the assumed error law, refit, and use the spread of refits as the standard error. (Note: "Double Exponential" and "Laplace" are two names for the same distribution.)

**Solution.** (a) From Example 12.4.2, the LAD fit to Table 12.4.1 is $\tilde\alpha=18.59$, $\tilde\beta=-0.89$ (use the M-estimate fit analogously for the M-estimator standard error).
(b) Compute residuals $\hat e_i=y_i-(\tilde\alpha+\tilde\beta x_i)$ and $\hat\sigma^2=\frac1{n-2}\sum_i\hat e_i^2$ ($n=23$).
(c) For $b=1,\dots,B$: draw $e^{*(b)}_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}n(0,\hat\sigma^2)$, form $y^{*(b)}_i=\tilde\alpha+\tilde\beta x_i+e^{*(b)}_i$, and refit by LAD (resp. M-estimation) to get $(\tilde\alpha^{*(b)},\tilde\beta^{*(b)})$.
(d) With $B$ large (e.g. $B=1000$), the bootstrap standard errors are $\mathrm{se}(\tilde\alpha)=\mathrm{sd}\{\tilde\alpha^{*(b)}\}$, $\mathrm{se}(\tilde\beta)=\mathrm{sd}\{\tilde\beta^{*(b)}\}$.
(e) The Double Exponential distribution *is* the Laplace distribution (pdf $\frac1{2b}e^{-|e|/b}$); matching variances to $\hat\sigma^2$ requires scale $b=\hat\sigma/\sqrt2$. Repeat (c)–(d) drawing $e^{*(b)}_i$ from this Laplace law instead of normal. At fixed design and the same stipulated error variance $\hat\sigma^2$, least-squares coefficient covariance is exactly $\hat\sigma^2(X^TX)^{-1}$ under both error laws; heavier tails do not make those standard errors larger. For LAD, the asymptotic covariance is $(X^TX)^{-1}/[4f_\epsilon(0)^2]$: its scalar factor is $\pi\hat\sigma^2/2$ under normal errors and $\hat\sigma^2/2$ under a variance-matched Laplace law. Thus Laplace LAD standard errors are asymptotically $1/\sqrt\pi$ times the normal LAD standard errors. M-estimator comparisons depend on the chosen score and tuning. Numerical bootstrap results still require actually fitting the data and simulating the requested refits; the algorithm alone does not complete those numerical parts.
