**Central idea.** The signed exponential$(\lambda)$ is Laplace$(0,1/\lambda)$; $M(\lambda) = \sup_{v>0}\frac{\sqrt{2/\pi}\,e^{-v^2/2}}{\lambda e^{-\lambda v}} \propto e^{\lambda^2/2}/\lambda$, minimized at $\lambda = 1$.

**Solution.** By symmetry it suffices to dominate $|Y|$: target $f(v) = \sqrt{2/\pi}\,e^{-v^2/2}$ ($v > 0$), candidate $g(v) = \lambda e^{-\lambda v}$. $M(\lambda) = \sup_{v>0}\frac{f(v)}{g(v)} = \frac{1}{\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\sup_{v>0}e^{\lambda v - v^2/2} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\frac{e^{\lambda^2/2}}{\lambda}$ (max at $v = \lambda$). Minimizing: $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{e^{\lambda^2/2}}{\lambda} = \frac{e^{\lambda^2/2}(\lambda^2-1)}{\lambda^2} = 0$ gives $\lambda = 1$. The optimal algorithm uses exponential$(1)$ (i.e., the Laplace candidate of 5.62(b)), with $M = \sqrt{2e/\pi} \approx 1.315$.
