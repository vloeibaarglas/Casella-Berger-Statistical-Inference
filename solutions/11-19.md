**Central idea.** Write $S_i = X_1+\cdots+X_i$ and $T_i = S_i/S_{i+1}$; the $T_i$ are independent Beta variables, and $Y_i = (1-T_i)/T_i$ turns them into independent beta-prime (scaled $F$) ratios, all independent of the total $Y_n = S_n$.

**Solution.** (a) Put $S_i = \sum_{j=1}^{i}X_j$ and $\Lambda_i = \sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_j$, so $S_i \sim \mathrm{gamma}(\Lambda_i,1)$. For $i = 1,\dots,n-1$ let $T_i = S_i/S_{i+1}$. Since $S_i$ and $X_{i+1} = S_{i+1}-S_i$ are independent gammas, $T_i \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\Lambda_i,\lambda_{i+1})$ independent of $S_{i+1}$; working backward from $i = n-1$, the $T_i$ are mutually independent and independent of $S_n = Y_n \sim \mathrm{gamma}(\Lambda_n,1)$. Now $Y_i = X_{i+1}/S_i = (S_{i+1}-S_i)/S_i = (1-T_i)/T_i$. If $T \sim \mathrm{Beta}(a,b)$ then $(1-T)/T$ is beta-prime$(b,a)$, so the $Y_i$ are mutually independent with $Y_i \sim$ beta-prime$(\lambda_{i+1},\Lambda_i)$, i.e. $\frac{\Lambda_i}{\lambda_{i+1}}Y_i \sim F_{2\lambda_{i+1},\,2\Lambda_i}$, and all are independent of $Y_n \sim \mathrm{gamma}(\Lambda_n,1)$.
(b) These are independent sequential $F$ ratios: each new observation's contribution $X_{i+1}$, scaled by the accumulated sum $S_i$, is independent of everything before it. In ANOVA the same mechanism produces the independent $F$ statistics of sequential (Type I) sums of squares — each factor's extra sum of squares, scaled by what came before, tested independently of the earlier terms.
