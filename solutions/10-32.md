**Central idea.** Under $p_1=p_2$ the constrained MLEs are $(X_1+X_2)/(2m)$, and the Pearson sum collapses to $(X_1-X_2)^2/(X_1+X_2)$; the conditional version $p_1/(p_1+p_3)=p_2/(p_2+p_4)$ is genuinely different.

**Solution.** (a) Under $H_0:p_1=p_2$, maximizing the multinomial likelihood gives $\hat p_1=\hat p_2=(X_1+X_2)/(2m)$, so the expected counts are $(X_1+X_2)/2$ for cells 1, 2. Then
$$\sum_{i=1,2}\frac{(X_i-(X_1+X_2)/2)^2}{(X_1+X_2)/2} = 2\cdot\frac{[(X_1-X_2)/2]^2}{(X_1+X_2)/2} = \frac{(X_1-X_2)^2}{X_1+X_2},$$
(the remaining cells contribute nothing under the constraint as stated).
(b) The second hypothesis is $P(\text{change}\mid\text{initially agree}) = P(\text{change}\mid\text{initially disagree})$, i.e. $\frac{p_1}{p_1+p_3} = \frac{p_2}{p_2+p_4}$. This differs from $p_1=p_2$: e.g. $(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)=(0.1,0.1,0.7,0.1)$ satisfies $p_1=p_2$ but gives conditionals $0.1/0.8=0.125\ne 0.5=0.1/0.2$. It is a test of independence in the $2\times 2$ table, testable by the $\chi^2$ statistics of Exercise 10.31.
