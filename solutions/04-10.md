**Central idea.** Marginals come from summing rows/columns; dependence is one violated product $f(x,y) \ne f_X(x)f_Y(y)$, and independence with fixed marginals forces the product table.

**Solution.** Marginals: $f_X(1)=1/4$, $f_X(2)=1/2$, $f_X(3)=1/4$; $f_Y(2)=f_Y(3)=f_Y(4)=1/3$. (a) $f(1,3) = 1/6 \ne 1/12 = f_X(1)f_Y(3)$, so $X$ and $Y$ are dependent. (b) With $U,V$ independent and the same marginals, $f_{U,V}(u,v) = f_X(u)f_Y(v)$:

|     | $U$ 1 | $U$ 2 | $U$ 3 |
|-----|-------|-------|-------|
| $V=2$ | $\frac{1}{12}$ | $\frac{1}{6}$ | $\frac{1}{12}$ |
| $V=3$ | $\frac{1}{12}$ | $\frac{1}{6}$ | $\frac{1}{12}$ |
| $V=4$ | $\frac{1}{12}$ | $\frac{1}{6}$ | $\frac{1}{12}$ |
