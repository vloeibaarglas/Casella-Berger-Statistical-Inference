**Central idea.** $\mathrm{ARE}(M_n,\bar X)=\mathrm{Var}(\bar X)/\mathrm{Var}(M_n)=4\sigma^2f(0)^2$ for symmetric location families; heavy tails inflate $\sigma^2$ but not $1/f(0)^2$, favoring the median.

**Solution.** For symmetric $f$ with median $0$, $\mathrm{Var}(M_n)\approx 1/[4nf(0)^2]$, so $\mathrm{ARE}(M_n,\bar X) = 4\sigma^2f(0)^2$.
(a) Normal: $4\sigma^2/(2\pi\sigma^2)=2/\pi=0.637\approx .64$. Logistic ($f(0)=1/4$, $\sigma^2=\pi^2/3$): $4(\pi^2/3)(1/16)=\pi^2/12=0.823\approx .82$. Double exponential ($f(0)=1/2$, $\sigma^2=2$): $4\cdot 2\cdot 1/4 = 2$.
(b) Under $X=\sigma Z$, $\mathrm{Var}(\bar X)$ scales by $\sigma^2$ while $f_X(0)=f_Z(0)/\sigma$, so $4\sigma_X^2f_X(0)^2 = 4\sigma^2\sigma_Z^2\cdot f_Z(0)^2/\sigma^2$ — the $\sigma$ cancels.
(c) For $t_\nu$, $f(0)=\Gamma(\frac{\nu+1}{2})/(\sqrt{\nu\pi}\,\Gamma(\frac{\nu}{2}))$, $\sigma^2=\nu/(\nu-2)$:

| $\nu$ | 3 | 5 | 10 | 25 | 50 | $\infty$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ARE | 1.62 | 0.96 | 0.76 | 0.68 | 0.66 | 0.64 |

Heavier tails (small $\nu$) strongly favor the median (ARE $>1$ at $\nu=3$); as $\nu\to\infty$ the ARE decreases to the normal value $2/\pi$.
(d) Here $\sigma^2=(1-\delta)+\delta\sigma^2$ and $f(0)=[(1-\delta)+\delta/\sigma]/\sqrt{2\pi}$, so ARE $= \frac{2}{\pi}[(1-\delta)+\delta/\sigma]^2[(1-\delta)+\delta\sigma^2]$:

| $\delta\backslash\sigma$ | 1 | 3 | 5 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.01 | 0.64 | 0.68 | 0.78 | 1.24 |
| 0.05 | 0.64 | 0.83 | 1.29 | 3.46 |
| 0.10 | 0.64 | 1.00 | 1.83 | 5.75 |
| 0.20 | 0.64 | 1.24 | 2.61 | 8.90 |

More/wilder contamination (larger $\delta$, $\sigma$) dramatically favors the median — the mean's variance explodes while the median's barely moves.
