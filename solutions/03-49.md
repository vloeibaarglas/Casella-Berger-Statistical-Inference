**Central idea.** Stein's lemma is integration by parts against the pdf: arrange the integrand as $g$ times the derivative of (pdf $\times$ something), so the boundary term dies and $g'$ appears.

**Solution.** (a) $(x-\alpha\beta)x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x/\beta}=-\beta\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^\alpha e^{-x/\beta}\big]$. Hence $E[g(X)(X-\alpha\beta)]=\frac{-\beta}{\Gamma(\alpha)\beta^\alpha}\int_0^\infty g(x)\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^\alpha e^{-x/\beta}\big]dx$; integrating by parts gives $\frac{\beta}{\Gamma(\alpha)\beta^\alpha}\int_0^\infty g'(x)x^\alpha e^{-x/\beta}dx=\beta E[Xg'(X)]$ (boundary terms vanish under the hypotheses on $g$). (b) $\big[\beta x-(\alpha-1)(1-x)\big]x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1}=-x\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^\beta\big]$. Hence $E\left[g(X)\left(\beta-(\alpha-1)\frac{1-X}{X}\right)\right]=\frac{-1}{B(\alpha,\beta)}\int_0^1g(x)\frac{d}{dx}\big[x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^\beta\big]dx$; integrating by parts gives $\frac{1}{B(\alpha,\beta)}\int_0^1g'(x)(1-x)x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1}dx=E[(1-X)g'(X)]$.
