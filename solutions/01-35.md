**Central idea.** $P(A \mid B) = P(A \cap B)/P(B)$ inherits nonnegativity, normalization, and additivity from $P$, since intersecting with $B$ preserves disjointness.

**Solution.** Nonnegativity: $P(A \mid B) \geq 0$ as a ratio of nonnegative numbers. Normalization: $P(S \mid B) = P(S \cap B)/P(B) = P(B)/P(B) = 1$. Countable additivity: if $A_i$ are disjoint, so are $A_i \cap B$, hence $P(\cup_i A_i \mid B) = P(\cup_i(A_i \cap B))/P(B) = \sum_i P(A_i \cap B)/P(B) = \sum_i P(A_i \mid B)$.
