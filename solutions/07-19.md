**Central idea.** Through-the-origin regression: the likelihood depends on $(\beta,\sigma^2)$ only through $\sum(Y_i-\beta x_i)^2$, minimized at $\sum x_iY_i/\sum x_i^2$.
**Solution.** (a) $L\propto(\sigma^2)^{-n/2}\exp\{-\sum(Y_i-\beta x_i)^2/(2\sigma^2)\}$ and $\sum(Y_i-\beta x_i)^2=\sum Y_i^2-2\beta\sum x_iY_i+\beta^2\sum x_i^2$; hence $(\sum x_iY_i,\sum Y_i^2)$ is sufficient for $(\beta,\sigma^2)$.
(b) Minimizing $\sum(Y_i-\beta x_i)^2$ gives $\hat\beta=\sum x_iY_i/\sum x_i^2$. Since $E[\sum x_iY_i]=\beta\sum x_i^2$, $E\hat\beta=\beta$: unbiased.
(c) $\hat\beta$ is a linear combination of independent normals, so $\hat\beta\sim\mathrm{n}(\beta,\sigma^2/\sum x_i^2)$.
