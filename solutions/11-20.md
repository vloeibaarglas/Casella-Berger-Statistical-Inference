**Central idea.** Under $H_0$ the between-treatment sum of squares is $(k-1)\sigma^2$ in expectation (one degree lost estimating $\bar{\bar{Y}}$); writing $F = (\mathrm{MSB}/\sigma^2)/(\mathrm{MSW}/\sigma^2)$ as a ratio of independent scaled chi-squares is exactly the Example 5.3.5 construction.

**Solution.** (a) Under $H_0$, $\bar{Y}_{i\cdot} \sim n(\theta,\sigma^2/n_i)$ independently. $\sum n_i(\bar{Y}_{i\cdot}-\bar{\bar{Y}})^2 = \sum n_i(\bar{Y}_{i\cdot}-\theta)^2 - N(\bar{\bar{Y}}-\theta)^2$, so its expectation is $\sum n_i(\sigma^2/n_i) - N(\sigma^2/N) = k\sigma^2 - \sigma^2 = (k-1)\sigma^2$. Dividing by $k-1$ is unbiased for $\sigma^2$.
(b) Example 5.3.5 derives the $F$ distribution as the ratio of two independent scaled chi-squares, cf. (5.3.8). Under $H_0$, $\mathrm{MSB}/\sigma^2 = [\sum n_i(\bar{Y}_{i\cdot}-\bar{\bar{Y}})^2/\sigma^2]/(k-1) \sim \chi^2_{k-1}/(k-1)$ (by 11.20(a)/11.7) and $\mathrm{MSW}/\sigma^2 = S_p^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_{N-k}/(N-k)$, independent by 11.6(b). Hence $F = (\mathrm{MSB}/\sigma^2)/(\mathrm{MSW}/\sigma^2) \sim F_{k-1,N-k}$ under $H_0$ — the ANOVA $F$ test by the same variance-ratio method.
