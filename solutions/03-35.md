**Central idea.** Imposing $EX=\mu$ (or $\mu=\sigma^2$) cuts the full parameter space down to a curve; in natural parameters these constraints are straight rays.

**Solution.** (a) $\mu=\sigma^2=e^\theta$: $\eta_1=\mu/\sigma^2=1$, $\eta_2=-1/(2e^\theta)$: the vertical ray at $\eta_1=1$, $\eta_2<0$. Comparison with 3.34(a): both are vertical rays in the natural parameter space, but 3.34(a)'s sits at $\eta_1=n$ while this one sits at $\eta_1=1$ — the 3.34(a) family is n$(\lambda,\lambda/n)$ (variance $=$ mean$/n$, appropriate for a sample mean), whereas here variance $=$ mean (appropriate for a single Poisson count). Same shape, different location; the $n$ records the averaging. (b) $\alpha\beta=\mu$: with natural parameters $\eta_1=\alpha$, $\eta_2=-1/\beta$, the constraint is $\eta_2=-\eta_1/\mu$: the straight ray $\{(\eta_1,-\eta_1/\mu):\eta_1>0\}$ from the origin into the fourth quadrant, of slope $-1/\mu$. (c) The constraints $\alpha_i\beta_i=\mu$, $i=1,\ldots,n$, cut out the $n$-dimensional manifold $\{(\alpha_i,\beta_i)_{i=1}^n:\alpha_i>0,\ \beta_i>0,\ \alpha_i\beta_i=\mu\}$ — a product of $n$ hyperbolas — embedded in the $2n$-dimensional full gamma parameter space.
