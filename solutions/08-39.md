**Central idea.** Differencing reduces the bivariate problem to a one-sample $t$ problem for the $W_i$.

**Solution.** (a) Each $(X_i,Y_i)$ is bivariate normal, so $W_i = X_i - Y_i$ is normal with $E[W_i] = \mu_X - \mu_Y =: \mu_W$ and $\mathrm{Var}(W_i) = \sigma_X^2 + \sigma_Y^2 - 2\rho\sigma_X\sigma_Y =: \sigma_W^2$; the pairs being iid, the $W_i$ are iid.
(b) $H_0 \iff \mu_W = 0$. From (a), $\bar W$ and $S_W^2$ are the usual one-sample mean and variance of iid normals, independent, so under $\mu_W = 0$, $T_W = \bar W/\sqrt{S_W^2/n} \sim t_{n-1}$: the paired-sample $t$ test (reject for $|T_W| > t_{n-1,\alpha/2}$ at level $\alpha$).
