**Central idea.** $M = \sup_x \phi(x)/g(x)$: for Cauchy the ratio $\propto (1+x^2)e^{-x^2/2}$ peaks at $x = \pm 1$; for Laplace $\propto e^{|x|-x^2/2}$ also peaks at $|x| = 1$ but lower.

**Solution.** With $f = \phi$:
(a) $g(x) = \frac{1}{\pi(1+x^2)}$: $\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}(1+x^2)e^{-x^2/2}$, maximized at $x = \pm 1$ (derivative $\propto x(1-x^2)e^{-x^2/2}$). $M = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cdot 2e^{-1/2} = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{e}} \approx 1.520$.
(b) $g(x) = \frac12 e^{-|x|}$: $\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{|x|-x^2/2}$, maximized at $|x| = 1$. $M = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{1/2} = \sqrt{\frac{2e}{\pi}} \approx 1.315$.
(c) The double-exponential candidate has the smaller $M$ ($1.315 < 1.520$), i.e., acceptance probability $1/M \approx 0.76$ vs $0.66$ — about 15% fewer wasted draws. Recommend the double-exponential (Laplace) candidate: its tails match the normal's better than Cauchy's heavy tails do.
