**Central idea.** Write $\bar{Y}_{i\cdot} - \bar{\bar{Y}} = (\theta_i - \bar\theta) + (\text{mean-zero noise})$; the noise contributes $(k-1)\sigma^2$ (Exercise 11.20a) and the signal contributes $\sum n_i(\theta_i-\bar\theta)^2$.

**Solution.** $\mathrm{MSW} = S_p^2$, and $E[\mathrm{MSW}] = \sigma^2$ since each $S_i^2$ is unbiased. For MSB, write $\bar{Y}_{i\cdot} = \theta_i + e_i$, $\bar{\bar{Y}} = \bar\theta + \bar e$ with $\bar\theta = \sum n_i\theta_i/N$. Then $\mathrm{SSB} = \sum n_i[(\theta_i-\bar\theta) + (e_i-\bar e)]^2 = \sum n_i(\theta_i-\bar\theta)^2 + 2\sum n_i(\theta_i-\bar\theta)(e_i-\bar e) + \sum n_i(e_i-\bar e)^2$. The middle term has mean 0; the last has mean $(k-1)\sigma^2$ by 11.20(a). Hence
$$E[\mathrm{MSB}] = \sigma^2 + \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k n_i(\theta_i-\bar\theta)^2}{k-1}, \qquad E[\mathrm{MSW}] = \sigma^2.$$
Under $H_0$ both equal $\sigma^2$, which is why $F = \mathrm{MSB}/\mathrm{MSW}$ detects treatment effects.
