**Central idea.** Squeeze $\log n!$ between integrals; the gap between $\log n!$ and the averaged integral is a convergent series (the per-term error is $O(1/k^2)$), so the ratio tends to a constant.

**Solution.** Summing the hint's inequalities gives $L_n < \log n! < U_n$ with $L_n = \int_0^n \log x\,dx = n\log n - n$ and $U_n = \int_1^{n+1}\log x\,dx$. Write $\log k$ as the average $\tfrac12\big(\int_{k-1}^k + \int_k^{k+1}\big)\log x\,dx$ plus an error $\varepsilon_k$. By Taylor expansion of $\log x$ about $x = k$, $\varepsilon_k = O(1/k^2)$, hence $\sum_{k=1}^\infty \varepsilon_k$ converges. Therefore $\log n! - \tfrac12(L_n + U_n)$ converges to a finite limit $c$; since $\tfrac12(L_n+U_n) = (n+\tfrac12)\log n - n + \tfrac12 + o(1)$, exponentiating gives $n!/(n^{n+1/2}e^{-n}) \to e^{c+1/2}$, a constant.
