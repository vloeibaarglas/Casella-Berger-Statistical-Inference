**Central idea.** Chebyshev: $P(|\bar{X}-\mu| \geq k) \leq \frac{9/100}{k^2}$ (distribution-free but loose); CLT: $|\bar{X}-\mu|/\!0.3 \;\dot\sim\; |Z|$ (tight but approximate and asymptotic).

**Solution.** $\mathrm{Var}(\bar{X}) = 9/100 = 0.09$. Chebyshev: $P(|\bar{X}-\mu| < k) \geq 1 - 0.09/k^2 = 0.90$ gives $k = \sqrt{0.9} \approx 0.949$: $\bar{X}-\mu \in (-0.949,\, 0.949)$ with probability at least $.90$, for *any* population with variance 9 — but the bound is very conservative. CLT: $P(|\bar{X}-\mu| < k) \approx P(|Z| < k/0.3) = 0.90$ gives $k/0.3 = 1.6449$, i.e., $k \approx 0.493$: $\bar{X}-\mu \in (-0.493,\, 0.493)$ approximately. The CLT interval is about half as wide but is only approximate (relying on $n = 100$ being large enough and the unknown shape of the population); Chebyshev is rigorous and universal but pays for it in width.
