**Central idea.** Uniform angle suffices for the ratio; equality of the radial projections also requires independence of radius and angle.

**Solution.** (a) $X/Y=\cot\theta$ almost surely, and its cdf is $1/2+\arctan(t)/\pi$, so the ratio is standard Cauchy. No independence assumption on $R$ is needed here because $R>0$ cancels.
(b) Add the missing assumption $R\perp\theta$, as in the authors' seventh-printing errata. Then $2XY/\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}=R\sin2\theta$. Since $2\theta\bmod2\pi$ is uniform and independent of $R$, this has the same law as $R\cos\theta=X$.
Independence is necessary for the argument: take $R=1+a\cos\theta$, $0<a<1$. Then $E(R\cos\theta)=a/2$, but $E(R\sin2\theta)=0$, disproving the unqualified claim. For iid $N(0,\sigma^2)$ variables the radius and angle are independent, and the transformed variable is $N(0,\sigma^2)$.
