**Central idea.** $d_n$ equals $\bar X$ (resp. $a\bar X$) with probability tending to 1 when $\theta\ne 0$ (resp. $\theta=0$), because the $n^{-1/4}$ threshold separates the two regimes.

**Solution.** $d_n = \bar X$ if $|\bar X|\ge n^{-1/4}$, $a\bar X$ otherwise, with $X_i$ iid $n(\theta,1)$.
If $\theta\ne 0$: $P(d_n\ne\bar X) = P(|\bar X|<n^{-1/4}) \le P(|\bar X-\theta|>|\theta|/2)\to 0$ (eventually $n^{-1/4}<|\theta|/2$). Hence $\sqrt n(d_n-\theta) = \sqrt n(\bar X-\theta)+o_p(1)\xrightarrow{d} n(0,1)$.
If $\theta=0$: $P(d_n\ne a\bar X) = P(|\bar X|\ge n^{-1/4}) = P(|\sqrt n\bar X|\ge n^{1/4})\to 0$ since $\sqrt n\bar X = O_p(1)$. Hence $\sqrt n(d_n-0) = a\sqrt n\bar X\xrightarrow{d} n(0,a^2)$.
So $\sqrt n(d_n-\theta)\xrightarrow{d} n[0,v(\theta)]$ with $v(\theta)=1$ ($\theta\ne0$), $a^2$ ($\theta=0$); for $a<1$ this beats the Cramér–Rao bound at the single point $\theta=0$ — superefficiency on a Lebesgue-null set.
