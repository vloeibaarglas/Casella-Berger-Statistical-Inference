**Central idea.** The MLE solves the first-order condition $\lambda S_{xy}b^2+(S_{xx}-\lambda S_{yy})b-S_{xy}=0$; implicit differentiation of this identity gives the sign of $d\hat\beta/d\lambda$, and the limits interpolate between the two ordinary regressions.

**Solution.** $\hat\beta(\lambda)$ is the "$+$" root of $\lambda S_{xy}b^2+(S_{xx}-\lambda S_{yy})b-S_{xy}=0$ (the first-order condition for the concentrated likelihood (12.2.14)).

(a) Write $\sqrt{(S_{xx}-\lambda S_{yy})^2+4\lambda S_{xy}^2}=S_{xx}-\lambda S_{yy}+2\lambda S_{xy}^2/S_{xx}+o(\lambda)$ (Taylor, assuming $S_{xx}>0$). The numerator becomes $2\lambda S_{xy}^2/S_{xx}+o(\lambda)$; dividing by $2\lambda S_{xy}$ gives $S_{xy}/S_{xx}$.

(b) Divide numerator and denominator by $\lambda$ and let $\lambda\to\infty$: the numerator tends to $S_{yy}+\sqrt{S_{yy}^2}=2S_{yy}$ and the denominator to $2S_{xy}$, giving $S_{yy}/S_{xy}$.

(c) Differentiating the first-order identity in $\lambda$: with $D=(S_{xx}-\lambda S_{yy})^2+4\lambda S_{xy}^2$,
$$\frac{d\hat\beta}{d\lambda}\sqrt{D}=\hat\beta\,(S_{yy}-S_{xy}\hat\beta).$$
If $S_{xy}>0$ then $\hat\beta>0$ (numerator $-(S_{xx}-\lambda S_{yy})+\sqrt{D}>0$), and $\hat\beta\le S_{yy}/S_{xy}$: indeed $\hat\beta=2S_{xy}/(S_{xx}-\lambda S_{yy}+\sqrt{D})\le S_{yy}/S_{xy}$ is equivalent, after clearing denominators, to $(2S_{xy}^2-S_{yy}S_{xx}+\lambda S_{yy}^2)^2\le S_{yy}^2D$, whose difference from the right side is $4S_{xy}^2(S_{xx}S_{yy}-S_{xy}^2)\ge 0$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. Hence $d\hat\beta/d\lambda\ge 0$. If $S_{xy}<0$ then $\hat\beta<0$, and $\hat\beta\ge S_{yy}/S_{xy}$ follows from $\sqrt{D}\le S_{xx}+\lambda S_{yy}\iff 4\lambda S_{xy}^2\le 4\lambda S_{xx}S_{yy}$, again Cauchy–Schwarz; then $S_{xy}\hat\beta\le S_{yy}$ gives $S_{yy}-S_{xy}\hat\beta\ge 0$, so $d\hat\beta/d\lambda\le 0$.

(d) For $S_{xy}>0$, (a)–(c) give $S_{xy}/S_{xx}=\hat\beta(0+)\le\hat\beta(1)\le\hat\beta(\infty)=S_{yy}/S_{xy}$; for $S_{xy}<0$, $\hat\beta$ decreases from $S_{xy}/S_{xx}$ to $S_{yy}/S_{xy}$, so $\hat\beta(1)$ again lies between them.

(e) With $\lambda=1$ (orthogonal least squares): $S_{xx}=9.095$, $S_{yy}=1358.25$, $S_{xy}=96.15$. OLS of $y$ on $x$: $S_{xy}/S_{xx}=10.57$; reciprocal of OLS of $x$ on $y$: $S_{yy}/S_{xy}=14.13$; EIV MLE: $\hat\beta(1)=14.10$. Indeed $10.57<14.10<14.13$, the MLE lying between the two OLS slopes, close to the $x$-on-$y$ reciprocal because $S_{yy}\gg S_{xx}$.
