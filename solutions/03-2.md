**Central idea.** The worst case is the smallest unacceptable defect count ($D = 6$): the acceptance probability decreases as $D$ grows, so bound it at $D = 6$ with the hypergeometric pmf.

**Solution.** Given $D$ defectives, the number of defectives in the sample is hypergeometric$(100,D,K)$, and $P(\text{accept}\mid D)$ decreases in $D$ (adding a defective to the lot can only increase the sample's defective count). Hence it suffices to require $P(\text{accept}\mid D=6) < 0.10$.
(a) $P(\text{accept}\mid D=6) = \binom{94}{K}\big/\binom{100}{K}$. This equals $0.1006$ at $K=31$ and $0.0918$ at $K=32$, so $K = 32$.
(b) $P(\text{accept}\mid D=6) = \left[\binom{94}{K}+6\binom{94}{K-1}\right]\big/\binom{100}{K}$. This equals $0.1022$ at $K=50$ and $0.0933$ at $K=51$, so $K = 51$.
