**Central idea.** For $\mu=0,\sigma=1$ the tail integrals are arctans; Exercise 3.38 lifts the result to general $(\mu,\sigma)$.

**Solution.** For $\mu=0,\sigma=1$: $P(X\ge0)=\int_0^\infty\frac{dz}{\pi(1+z^2)}=\frac1\pi\cdot\frac\pi2=\frac12$, and by symmetry $P(X\le0)=\frac12$, proving (a). $P(X\ge1)=\int_1^\infty\frac{dz}{\pi(1+z^2)}=\frac1\pi\left(\frac\pi2-\frac\pi4\right)=\frac14$; by symmetry $P(X\le-1)=\frac14$, proving (b). For general $\mu,\sigma$, put $z_{1/2}=0$ and $z_{1/4}=1$ (upper-tail notation); then $x_{1/2}=\mu$, $x_{1/4}=\mu+\sigma$ (and by symmetry $x_{3/4}=\mu-\sigma$), so Exercise 3.38 gives $P(X\ge\mu)=P(X\le\mu)=1/2$ and $P(X\ge\mu+\sigma)=P(X\le\mu-\sigma)=1/4$.
