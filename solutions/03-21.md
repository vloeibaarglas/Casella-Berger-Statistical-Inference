**Central idea.** The mgf integrand is $e^{tx}/(1+x^2)$; for $t\ne0$ the exponential beats the polynomial denominator on one tail — no surprise, since the Cauchy has no mean.

**Solution.** $M_X(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{tx}\frac{dx}{\pi(1+x^2)}$. For $t>0$, $e^{tx}/(1+x^2)\to\infty$ as $x\to\infty$, so the integral diverges to $+\infty$; for $t<0$ it diverges as $x\to-\infty$. At $t=0$, $M_X(0)=1$. So the integral is finite only at $t=0$ — expected, since the Cauchy distribution has no mean, hence no mgf on any interval about 0.
