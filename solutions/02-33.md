**Central idea.** Sum/integrate $e^{tx}$ against the law (recognizing the exponential series in (a), a geometric series in (b), and completing the square in (c)), then read off $M'(0)$, $M''(0)$.

**Solution.** (a) $M(t)=\sum_{x\ge0}e^{tx}\frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x}{x!}=e^{-\lambda}\sum\frac{(\lambda e^t)^x}{x!}=e^{-\lambda}e^{\lambda e^t}=e^{\lambda(e^t-1)}$. $M'(t)=\lambda e^tM(t)$, $M'(0)=\lambda$; $M''(t)=(\lambda e^t+\lambda^2e^{2t})M(t)$, $M''(0)=\lambda+\lambda^2$; $\mathrm{Var}X=\lambda$.
(b) With $q=1-p$: $M(t)=p\sum_{x\ge0}(qe^t)^x=\frac{p}{1-qe^t}$ for $qe^t<1$. $M'(t)=\frac{pqe^t}{(1-qe^t)^2}$, $M'(0)=\frac{q}{p}$; $M''(t)=\frac{pqe^t}{(1-qe^t)^2}+\frac{2pq^2e^{2t}}{(1-qe^t)^3}$, $M''(0)=\frac{q}{p}+\frac{2q^2}{p^2}$; $\mathrm{Var}X=\frac{q}{p}+\frac{q^2}{p^2}=\frac{q}{p^2}=\frac{1-p}{p^2}$.
(c) $tx-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}=-\frac{(x-(\mu+\sigma^2t))^2}{2\sigma^2}+\mu t+\frac{\sigma^2t^2}{2}$, so $\int\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{tx}e^{-(x-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)}dx=e^{\mu t+\sigma^2t^2/2}$. $M'(0)=\mu$, $M''(0)=\sigma^2+\mu^2$, $\mathrm{Var}X=\sigma^2$.
