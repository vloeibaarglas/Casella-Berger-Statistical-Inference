**Central idea.** Multinomial probabilities are maximized by the most "spread out" allocation; Stirling turns $n!/n^n$ into closed form; missing $x_i$ in all $n$ draws is $((n-1)/n)^n$.

**Solution.** (a) An unordered outcome with composition $(k_1,\dots,k_n)$, $\sum k_i = n$, has probability $\frac{n!}{k_1!\cdots k_n!}/n^n$. Since $k_i! \geq 1$ with equality iff all $k_i = 1$, the maximum is attained uniquely (as a multiset) at $(1,\dots,1)$ — the sample $\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}$ with average $(\sum x_i)/n$ — with probability $n!/n^n$. (b) By Stirling, $n! \approx \sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^n$, so $n!/n^n \approx \sqrt{2\pi n}\,e^{-n}$. (c) Each draw misses $x_i$ with probability $(n-1)/n$, independently: $((n-1)/n)^n = (1-1/n)^n \to e^{-1}$.
