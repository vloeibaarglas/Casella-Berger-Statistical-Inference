**Central idea.** The Neyman–Pearson likelihood ratio is monotone increasing in $\sum X_i^2$ (because $\sigma_1 > \sigma_0$ flips the sign of the exponent), and $\sum X_i^2/\sigma_0^2 \sim \chi^2_n$ under $H_0$ pins down $c$.

**Solution.** The likelihood ratio is
$$\frac{L(\sigma_1 \mid \mathbf x)}{L(\sigma_0 \mid \mathbf x)} = \left(\frac{\sigma_0}{\sigma_1}\right)^n \exp\!\left[-\frac{\sum x_i^2}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_1^2}-\frac{1}{\sigma_0^2}\right)\right] = \left(\frac{\sigma_0}{\sigma_1}\right)^n \exp\!\left[\frac{\sum x_i^2}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_0^2}-\frac{1}{\sigma_1^2}\right)\right].$$
Since $\sigma_0 < \sigma_1$, $\frac{1}{\sigma_0^2}-\frac{1}{\sigma_1^2} > 0$, so the ratio is a strictly increasing function of $T(\mathbf x) = \sum x_i^2$. By Neyman–Pearson, the MP test rejects iff the ratio exceeds $k$, equivalently iff $\sum X_i^2 > c$. Under $H_0$, $\sum X_i^2/\sigma_0^2 \sim \chi^2_n$, so size $\alpha$ requires $P(\chi^2_n > c/\sigma_0^2) = \alpha$, i.e.
$$c = \sigma_0^2\,\chi^2_{n,\alpha},$$
the upper-$\alpha$ point of $\chi^2_n$.
