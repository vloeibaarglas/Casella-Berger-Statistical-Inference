**Central idea.** Tower laws give the moments; the joint is binomial$\times$uniform; integrating out $x$ is a beta integral that collapses to the discrete uniform $1/(n+1)$.

**Solution.** (a) $\mathrm{E}Y = \mathrm{E}[nX] = n/2$. $\mathrm{Var}\,Y = \mathrm{E}[nX(1-X)]+\mathrm{Var}(nX) = n(1/2-1/3)+n^2/12 = n(n+2)/12$.
(b) $f_{X,Y}(x,y) = \binom{n}{y}x^y(1-x)^{n-y}\cdot 1$, $0<x<1$, $y = 0,\dots,n$.
(c) $P(Y=y) = \binom{n}{y}\int_0^1 x^y(1-x)^{n-y}\,dx = \binom{n}{y}B(y+1,n-y+1) = \frac{1}{n+1}$: $Y$ is discrete uniform on $\{0,\dots,n\}$.
