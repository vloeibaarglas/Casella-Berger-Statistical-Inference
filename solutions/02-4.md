**Central idea.** This is the Laplace (double-exponential) law; symmetry about 0 halves every computation.

**Solution.** (a) $f\ge0$, and $\int_{-\infty}^0 \frac12\lambda e^{\lambda x}dx = \frac12 = \int_0^\infty \frac12\lambda e^{-\lambda x}dx$, so the total integral is 1.
(b) For $t\le0$: $P(X<t) = \int_{-\infty}^t \frac12\lambda e^{\lambda x}dx = \frac12e^{\lambda t}$. For $t>0$: $P(X<t) = \frac12 + \int_0^t\frac12\lambda e^{-\lambda x}dx = 1-\frac12e^{-\lambda t}$.
(c) For $t\le0$: $P(|X|<t)=0$. For $t>0$, by symmetry, $P(|X|<t) = 2\int_0^t\frac12\lambda e^{-\lambda x}dx = 1-e^{-\lambda t}$.
