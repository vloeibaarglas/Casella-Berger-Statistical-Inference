**Central idea.** Only the sums $\mu + \tau_i$ enter the means $EY_{ij}$; shifting $\mu$ up and every $\tau_i$ down by the same constant leaves the distribution untouched.

**Solution.** Under (11.2.2), $Y_{ij} \sim n(\mu + \tau_i, \sigma^2)$ independently (with the usual normal-error assumptions), so the distribution depends on $(\mu, \tau_1,\dots,\tau_k)$ only through the $k$ sums $\mu + \tau_i$. For any $c \neq 0$, $(\mu + c, \tau_1 - c, \dots, \tau_k - c)$ is a distinct parameter vector with $(\mu+c) + (\tau_i - c) = \mu + \tau_i$ for every $i$, hence the identical distribution for all $Y_{ij}$. E.g. $(\mu, \tau_1,\dots,\tau_k)$ and $(\mu+1, \tau_1-1,\dots,\tau_k-1)$.
