**Central idea.** Both statistics scale by $a$ and ignore $b$, so their ratio kills both the location and the scale parameters.

**Solution.** (a) Write $X_i = \sigma Z_i+\mu$ with $Z_i$ iid from the base distribution. Then $T_i(\mathbf{X}) = T_i(\sigma\mathbf{Z}+\mu) = \sigma T_i(\mathbf{Z})$ by the hypothesis, so $\frac{T_1(\mathbf{X})}{T_2(\mathbf{X})} = \frac{T_1(\mathbf{Z})}{T_2(\mathbf{Z})}$, free of $(\mu,\sigma)$: ancillary (where $T_2\ne 0$).
(b) $R(a\mathbf{x}+b) = (ax_{(n)}+b)-(ax_{(1)}+b) = aR(\mathbf{x})$, and $S(a\mathbf{x}+b) = aS(\mathbf{x})$ for $a > 0$ (translation cancels in deviations, scaling factors out). Hence $R/S$ is ancillary by (a).
