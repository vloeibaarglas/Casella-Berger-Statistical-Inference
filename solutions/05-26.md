**Central idea.** Order-statistic events are counting events: $\{X_{(i)} \leq u\} = \{U \geq i\}$. The mixed partial of the multinomial sum telescopes — each interior term's $u$-derivative cancels a neighbor's — leaving only the $(i, j)$-cell term.

**Solution.** (a) Each $X_\ell$ falls independently into $(-\infty, u]$, $(u, v]$, $(v, \infty)$ with probabilities $F_X(u)$, $F_X(v)-F_X(u)$, $1-F_X(v)$; counting the $n$ iid trials gives the multinomial pmf.
(b) For $u < v$: $\{X_{(i)} \leq u\} = \{U \geq i\}$ and $\{X_{(j)} \leq v\} = \{U+V \geq j\}$, so $F_{X_{(i)},X_{(j)}}(u,v) = P(U \geq i, U+V \geq j)$. Splitting on whether $U \geq j$ or $i \leq U \leq j-1$ (in which case $U+V \geq j$ needs $V \geq j-U$) and inserting the multinomial pmf from (a) gives the displayed sum.
(c) Differentiating termwise: $\frac{\partial^2}{\partial v\,\partial u}$ of the $(k,m)$ summand produces terms proportional to $f_X(u)f_X(v)$ times $F_X(u)^{k-1}(F_X(v)-F_X(u))^{m-1}(1-F_X(v))^{n-k-m}$ with multinomial-type coefficients. Using $k\cdot\frac{n!}{k!m!(n-k-m)!} = n\cdot\frac{(n-1)!}{(k-1)!m!(n-k-m)!}$ and the analogous $m$-identity (the "(5.4.5) relationships"), each differentiated term splits into pieces that cancel the corresponding pieces from the $(k\pm1, m\pm1)$ neighbors — a telescoping sum. All interior terms cancel; only the boundary term $(k,m) = (i, j-i)$ survives:
$$f_{X_{(i)},X_{(j)}}(u,v) = \frac{n!}{(i-1)!\,(j-i-1)!\,(n-j)!}\,[F_X(u)]^{i-1}[F_X(v)-F_X(u)]^{j-i-1}[1-F_X(v)]^{n-j}f_X(u)f_X(v),$$
for $u < v$ (and $0$ otherwise), the usual joint pdf of two order statistics. The $P(U \geq j)$ term contributes nothing to the mixed partial.
