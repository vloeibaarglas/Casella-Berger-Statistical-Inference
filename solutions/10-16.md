**Central idea.** With $g(p)=p(1-p)$, $g''(p)=-2$, the second-order delta method gives $\mathrm{Var}(g(\hat p))\approx 2\sigma^4/n^2 = 2[\mathrm{Var}(\hat p)]^2$; near $p=1/2$ the first-order term vanishes, which is exactly why the ordinary delta method collapses.

**Solution.** Theorem 5.5.26: $n(g(\hat p)-g(p))\xrightarrow{d}\frac{\sigma^2g''(p)}{2}\chi^2_1$ with $\sigma^2=p(1-p)$. Hence $\mathrm{Var}(g(\hat p))\approx \frac{1}{n^2}\left(\frac{\sigma^2g''(p)}{2}\right)^2\cdot 2$. With $g''(p)=-2$: $= \frac{2\sigma^4}{n^2} = 2[\mathrm{Var}(\hat p)]^2$.
(a) *As printed this is incorrect.* At $\hat p=11/24$, $n=24$: $2[\hat p(1-\hat p)/n]^2 = 2(0.0103449)^2 = 0.000214\approx .00021$, not $.00007$. (The printed $.00007$ is the *first-order* delta-method value $(1-2\hat p)^2\mathrm{Var}(\hat p) = 7.18\times 10^{-5}$.) This is a book erratum.
(b) *As printed this is incorrect.* The exact variance, from the binomial moments of $T\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(24,11/24)$ applied to $\hat p(1-\hat p)=T/n-T^2/n^2$, is $0.000271$ (confirmed by direct summation and simulation), not $.00529$. This is a book erratum.
(c) The (first-order) Delta Method is bad because $g'(p)=1-2p\approx 0$ near $p=1/2$ ($\hat p=11/24=0.458$): the linear term it relies on nearly vanishes, so it reports $.00007$ while missing the dominant quadratic fluctuation (true value $.00027$, a $4\times$ underestimate). The second-order Delta Method does better ($.00021$, within $\sim 20\%$) since it captures the quadratic term. For the nonparametric bootstrap of Bernoulli data, $n\hat p^*\mid\hat p\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,\hat p)$ exactly. Therefore its conditional variance is the exact binomial variance formula with $p$ replaced by $\hat p$; at $\hat p=11/24$ it is $0.0002710672072$. This establishes the numerical claim directly. At fixed $p\ne1/2$, $2[\operatorname{Var}(\hat p)]^2$ is the quadratic contribution alone, not the full second-order normal approximation, which also includes the linear term and equals $0.0002858458887$ here. At $p=1/2$ the quadratic term is the leading term. Do not infer moment convergence from convergence in distribution alone or claim automatic second-order bootstrap accuracy at a stationary point.
