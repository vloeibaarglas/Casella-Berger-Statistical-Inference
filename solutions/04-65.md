**Central idea.** The text proves $|\mathrm{Corr}|\le1$ from $0 \le \mathrm{Var}(X/\sigma_X \pm Y/\sigma_Y)$; the same idea with arbitrary coefficients — $0 \le \mathrm{Var}(tX+Y)$ as a quadratic in $t$ — gives $|\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)| \le \sigma_X\sigma_Y$ via the nonpositive discriminant.

**Solution.** For any real $t$, $0 \le \mathrm{Var}(tX+Y) = t^2\mathrm{Var}\,X+2t\,\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)+\mathrm{Var}\,Y$. A quadratic $at^2+bt+c \ge 0$ for all $t$ has discriminant $b^2-4ac \le 0$, so $4[\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)]^2-4\mathrm{Var}\,X\,\mathrm{Var}\,Y \le 0$, i.e. $|\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)| \le \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}\,X\,\mathrm{Var}\,Y} = \sigma_X\sigma_Y$.
