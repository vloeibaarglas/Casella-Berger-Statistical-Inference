**Central idea.** Condition on the reduced sample space $\{BB, BG, GB\}$ — the conditioning event, not the family, is what matters.

**Solution.** A priori $\{BB, BG, GB, GG\}$ are equally likely. Given "at least one boy", the sample space shrinks to $\{BB, BG, GB\}$, exactly one of which is $BB$. Hence $P(\text{both boys} \mid \text{at least one boy}) = 1/3$.
