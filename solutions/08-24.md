**Central idea.** With singletons the suprema vanish and the LRT is just the reciprocal likelihood ratio — the Neyman–Pearson test in disguise.

**Solution.** For $H_0:\theta=\theta_0$ vs.\ $H_1:\theta=\theta_1$,
$$\lambda(\mathbf x) = \frac{L(\theta_0\mid\mathbf x)}{L(\theta_1\mid\mathbf x)} = \left(\frac{L(\theta_1\mid\mathbf x)}{L(\theta_0\mid\mathbf x)}\right)^{-1}.$$
The LRT rejects for $\lambda(\mathbf x) \le c$, i.e. for $L(\theta_1)/L(\theta_0) \ge 1/c =: k$ — exactly the Neyman–Pearson form (with randomization on $\{L(\theta_1)=kL(\theta_0)\}$ if needed to hit the size). Yes, equivalent: the LRT of simple vs.\ simple *is* the NP most powerful test.
