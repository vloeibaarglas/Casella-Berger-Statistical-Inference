**Central idea.** $\bar X\perp S$ with known variances; the optimal convex combination is inverse-variance weighting; $\bar X-cS$ is a nonzero unbiased estimator of zero.
**Solution.** (a) $E_\theta[a\bar X+(1-a)cS]=a\theta+(1-a)\theta=\theta$.
(b) $\mathrm{Var}(cS)=c^2\mathrm{Var}(S)=c^2\theta^2(1-\tfrac1{c^2})=\theta^2(c^2-1)$ (since $E_\theta S=\theta/c$ and $E_\theta S^2=\theta^2$), and $\bar X\perp S$, so $\mathrm{Var}(a\bar X+(1-a)cS)=a^2\theta^2/n+(1-a)^2\theta^2(c^2-1)$. Minimizing in $a$: $\frac{a}{n}=(1-a)(c^2-1)$, i.e. $a^*=\dfrac{c^2-1}{1/n+c^2-1}=\dfrac{n(c^2-1)}{1+n(c^2-1)}$.
(c) $(\bar X,S^2)$ is sufficient (two-parameter exponential family). It is not complete: $\bar X-cS$ satisfies $E_\theta[\bar X-cS]=0$ for all $\theta>0$ but is not a.s. zero.
