**Central idea.** The logarithm is strictly increasing, so it preserves order — and hence maximizers.
**Solution.** Since $L(\theta\mid\mathbf{x})\ge 0$ and $\log$ is strictly increasing on $(0,\infty)$, for any $\theta_1,\theta_2$ with positive likelihood, $L(\theta_1\mid\mathbf{x})\le L(\theta_2\mid\mathbf{x})$ iff $\log L(\theta_1\mid\mathbf{x})\le\log L(\theta_2\mid\mathbf{x})$. Thus both functions attain their maximum at the same $\hat\theta$ (points with $L=0$ cannot be maximizers unless $L\equiv 0$).
