**Central idea.** The beta$(\theta,1)$ family has MLR in $X$ since $f(x\mid\theta_2)/f(x\mid\theta_1) = (\theta_2/\theta_1)x^{\theta_2-\theta_1}$ is increasing in $x$; Karlin–Rubin then hands over the UMP test directly.

**Solution.** $f(x\mid\theta) = \theta x^{\theta-1}$, $0<x<1$; $P_\theta(X>t) = 1-t^\theta$, increasing in $\theta$.
(a) Size $= \sup_{\theta\le1}P_\theta(X>1/2) = \sup_{\theta\le1}(1-(1/2)^\theta) = 1/2$ (attained at $\theta=1$). Power: $\gamma(\theta) = 1-(1/2)^\theta$ for $\theta>1$, increasing and concave, from $1/2$ at $\theta=1$ up to $1$ as $\theta\to\infty$ (and $\gamma(\theta)\to0$ as $\theta\to0^+$).
(b) $f(x\mid2)/f(x\mid1) = 2x$, strictly increasing in $x$; NP rejects iff $X>c$ with $P_1(X>c)=1-c=\alpha$, i.e. reject iff $X>1-\alpha$.
(c) Yes. For $\theta_2>\theta_1$, $f(x\mid\theta_2)/f(x\mid\theta_1)=(\theta_2/\theta_1)x^{\theta_2-\theta_1}$ is increasing in $x$: MLR in $X$. By Karlin–Rubin, $\{X>1-\alpha\}$ is UMP size $\alpha$ for $H_0:\theta\le1$ vs.\ $H_1:\theta>1$ (size is $\sup_{\theta\le1}(1-(1-\alpha)^\theta)= \alpha$ at $\theta=1$).
