**Central idea.** (5.6.5) gives the standard uniform-based generators: inverse-cdf for the exponential, $-2\log U \sim \chi^2_2$, and sums thereof; each is verified by cdf differentiation or mgf.

**Solution.** (5.6.5) collects the basic constructions from uniforms $U, U_1, U_2, \ldots \sim$ iid uniform$(0,1)$:
- $X = -\log U \sim \text{exponential}(1)$: $P(-\log U \leq x) = P(U \geq e^{-x}) = 1-e^{-x}$, $x > 0$.
- $Y = -2\log U \sim \chi^2_2$: $P(-2\log U \leq y) = P(U \geq e^{-y/2}) = 1-e^{-y/2}$, the exponential$(1/2)$ = $\chi^2_2$ cdf.
- $\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-\log U_i) \sim \text{Gamma}(n,1)$: sum of $n$ iid exponential$(1)$ variables (mgf $(1-t)^{-n}$).
- $-2\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log U_i \sim \chi^2_{2k}$: sum of $k$ iid $\chi^2_2$ variables (mgf $(1-2t)^{-k}$).
- $-\beta\sum_{j=1}^{a}\log U_j \sim \mathrm{gamma}(a,\beta)$ (scale $\beta$): $-\log U_j \sim$ exponential$(1)$, so $-\beta\log U_j \sim$ exponential$(\beta)$, and the sum of $a$ iid exponential$(\beta)$ variables is gamma$(a,\beta)$.
- $\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{a}\log U_j}{\sum_{j=1}^{a+b}\log U_j} \sim \mathrm{beta}(a,b)$: writing the sums as $-G_a$ and $-(G_a+G_b)$ with $G_a \sim$ gamma$(a,1) \perp G_b \sim$ gamma$(b,1)$, the ratio is $G_a/(G_a+G_b) \sim$ beta$(a,b)$.
These are the building blocks for generating gamma, chi-square, and beta variables (used in 5.48).
