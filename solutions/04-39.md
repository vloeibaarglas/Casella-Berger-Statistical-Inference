**Central idea.** Dividing the multinomial joint pmf by the binomial$(m,p_j)$ marginal of $X_j$ leaves a binomial pmf in $x_i$; the covariance follows from $\mathrm{E}[X_iX_j] = \mathrm{E}[X_j\mathrm{E}(X_i|X_j)]$.

**Solution.** $P(X_i=x_i,X_j=x_j) = \frac{m!}{x_i!x_j!(m-x_i-x_j)!}p_i^{x_i}p_j^{x_j}(1-p_i-p_j)^{m-x_i-x_j}$. Dividing by $P(X_j=x_j) = \binom{m}{x_j}p_j^{x_j}(1-p_j)^{m-x_j}$ gives $\binom{m-x_j}{x_i}\left(\frac{p_i}{1-p_j}\right)^{x_i}\left(\frac{1-p_i-p_j}{1-p_j}\right)^{m-x_j-x_i}$: binomial$(m-x_j,p_i/(1-p_j))$. $X_j \sim \text{binomial}(m,p_j)$ by the usual marginal argument. $\mathrm{E}[X_iX_j] = \mathrm{E}[X_j(m-X_j)p_i/(1-p_j)] = \frac{p_i}{1-p_j}\left(m^2p_j-mp_j(1-p_j)-m^2p_j^2\right) = m(m-1)p_ip_j$, so $\mathrm{Cov}(X_i,X_j) = m(m-1)p_ip_j-m^2p_ip_j = -mp_ip_j$.
