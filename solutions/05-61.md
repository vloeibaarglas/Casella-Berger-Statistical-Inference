**Central idea.** The proposal must dominate both endpoints. For gamma targets, increase the proposal scale as well as choosing a shape no larger than the target shape.

**Solution.** (a) Assume $a,b>1$ so $k=\lfloor a\rfloor$, $l=\lfloor b\rfloor$ are positive. The density ratio is $B(k,l)y^{a-k}(1-y)^{b-l}/B(a,b)$, which is bounded. If either shape is below one, the proposed zero-shape beta distribution does not exist; that is another missing restriction.
(b) With the *same* scale $b$, the ratio for $V\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(k,b)$ is proportional to $y^{a-k}$ and is unbounded. The authors' seventh-printing errata specify $V\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(\lfloor a\rfloor,b+1)$. More generally, for $V\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(k,c)$ with $0<k\le a$ and $c>b$,
$$\frac{f_Y(y)}{f_V(y)}=\frac{\Gamma(k)c^k}{\Gamma(a)b^a}y^{a-k}\exp\{-y(1/b-1/c)\}.$$
For $k<a$ it peaks at $y_*=(a-k)/(1/b-1/c)$ and has finite maximum
$$M(k,c)=\frac{\Gamma(k)c^k}{\Gamma(a)b^a}\left[\frac{a-k}{e(1/b-1/c)}\right]^{a-k}.$$
Thus the earlier claim that no single integer-shape gamma proposal works was false; it holds only when the scale is fixed at $b$.
(c) Shape $\lfloor a\rfloor+1>a$ makes the ratio diverge at zero for gamma proposals of any scale and for the indicated beta proposal.
(d) For beta integer shapes, minimize
$$M(k,l)=\frac{B(k,l)}{B(a,b)}\left(\frac{a-k}{a+b-k-l}\right)^{a-k}\left(\frac{b-l}{a+b-k-l}\right)^{b-l}$$
over $1\le k\le\lfloor a\rfloor$, $1\le l\le\lfloor b\rfloor$. Floors are not always optimal: at $(a,b)=(4.2,2.9)$, $M(4,2)=1.81954$ but $M(3,2)=1.21757$.
For a gamma proposal of fixed shape $k<a$, minimize over its scale: differentiating $\log M$ gives $c_*=ab/k$. The optimized envelope is
$$M_*(k)=\frac{\Gamma(k)a^a e^{-(a-k)}}{\Gamma(a)k^k}.$$
For integer $k$, $\log M_*(k+1)-\log M_*(k)=1-(k+1)\log(1+1/k)<0$. Consequently the optimal positive integer shape is $k=\lfloor a\rfloor$ with scale $c=ab/\lfloor a\rfloor$, assuming $a>1$. If the proposal scale is fixed specifically at $b+1$, minimize $M(k,b+1)$ over the finite admissible integer grid instead. If $0<a<1$, no positive integer shape can dominate the singularity at zero. In all admissible cases $E(N)=M$.
