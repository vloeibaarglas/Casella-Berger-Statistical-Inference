**Central idea.** Chernoff's trick: $P(X\ge a)=P(e^{tX}\ge e^{ta})\le e^{-ta}E[e^{tX}]$ for $t>0$ (Markov on $e^{tX}$); any $h$ dominating $\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}$ works the same way.

**Solution.** (a) For $0<t<h$: $\{X\ge a\}=\{e^{tX}\ge e^{ta}\}$, so $P(X\ge a)\le E[e^{tX}]/e^{ta}=e^{-at}M_X(t)$ by Markov's inequality. (b) For $-h<t<0$: $\{X\le a\}=\{e^{tX}\ge e^{ta}\}$ (multiplying by negative $t$ flips $\le$), so $P(X\le a)\le e^{-at}M_X(t)$. (c) $P(X\ge0)\le E[e^{tX}]$, $t\ge0$. In general, $P(X\ge0)\le Eh(t,X)$ whenever $h(t,x)\ge\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)$ for all $x$ (i.e. $h$ is nonnegative everywhere and at least 1 on $[0,\infty)$): then $P(X\ge0)=E[\mathbf{1}_{\{X\ge0\}}]\le E[h(t,X)\mathbf{1}_{\{X\ge0\}}]\le Eh(t,X)$.
