**Central idea.** $\mathrm{Cov}(\hat\varepsilon_i,\hat\alpha) = \sigma^2\sum_j e_jc_j$ and $\sum_j e_jc_j = c_i - \sum c_j^2 - x_i\sum c_jd_j = 0$ (and similarly for $\hat\beta$) by the four coefficient identities; normality upgrades uncorrelated to independent.

**Solution.** For fixed $i$, (11.3.30) gives $e_j = \delta_{ij} - c_j - d_jx_i$, so $\hat\varepsilon_i = \sum_j e_jY_j$. By Lemma 11.3.2, $\mathrm{Cov}(\hat\varepsilon_i,\hat\alpha) = \sigma^2\sum_j e_jc_j$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(\hat\varepsilon_i,\hat\beta) = \sigma^2\sum_j e_jd_j$. Now $\sum_j e_jc_j = c_i - \sum_jc_j^2 - x_i\sum_jc_jd_j = \big[\frac{1}{n}-\frac{\bar x(x_i-\bar x)}{S_{xx}}\big] - \big[\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\bar x^2}{S_{xx}}\big] - x_i\big[-\frac{\bar x}{S_{xx}}\big] = 0$, and $\sum_j e_jd_j = d_i - \sum_jc_jd_j - x_i\sum_jd_j^2 = \frac{x_i-\bar x}{S_{xx}} + \frac{\bar x}{S_{xx}} - \frac{x_i}{S_{xx}} = 0$, using $\sum c_j^2 = 1/n+\bar x^2/S_{xx}$, $\sum d_j^2 = 1/S_{xx}$, $\sum c_jd_j = -\bar x/S_{xx}$. So both covariances are 0; $(\hat\varepsilon_i,\hat\alpha,\hat\beta)$ are jointly normal (linear in the $Y_j$), hence independent.
