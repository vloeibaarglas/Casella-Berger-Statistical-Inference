**Central idea.** Geometric probability: scale every area by the wall area $A$; conditioning on hitting the board cancels the common factor $\pi r^2/A$.

**Solution.** Let the board have radius $r$ (area $\pi r^2$). Hitting any region has probability proportional to its area, and the wall is hit with probability 1, so $P(\text{hit board}) = \pi r^2/A$.
(a) For $i = 1,\dots,5$, with $p_i = \frac{(6-i)^2-(5-i)^2}{25}$ from Example 1.2.7,
$$P(\text{scoring } i) = P(\text{board hit})\,p_i = \frac{\pi r^2}{A}\cdot\frac{(6-i)^2-(5-i)^2}{25},$$
and $P(\text{scoring } 0) = 1 - \pi r^2/A$ (dart hits the wall but misses the board). These are nonnegative and sum to $\frac{\pi r^2}{A}\sum_{i=1}^5 p_i + 1 - \frac{\pi r^2}{A} = 1$.
(b) $P(\text{scoring } i \mid \text{board hit}) = \frac{(\pi r^2/A)\,p_i}{\pi r^2/A} = p_i$, exactly Example 1.2.7.
