**Central idea.** The weight $f/g$ converts a $g$-expectation into an $f$-expectation (change of measure); self-normalizing divides by weights summing to $\to 1$, and for constant $h$ the weights cancel exactly, giving zero variance.

**Solution.** (a) $E_g\left[\frac{f(Y)}{g(Y)}h(Y)\right] = \int \frac{f(y)}{g(y)}h(y)g(y)\,dy = \int f(y)h(y)\,dy = E_f[h(X)]$; average over $m$ iid copies.
(b) By the WLLN the average $\to_p E_g[(f(Y)/g(Y))h(Y)] = E_f[h(X)]$ (integrability: $E_g|(f/g)h| = E_f|h| \leq \sqrt{E_f[h^2]} < \infty$).
(c) Write the estimator as $\frac{\frac1m\sum w_ih(Y_i)}{\frac1m\sum w_j}$ with $w_i = f(Y_i)/g(Y_i)$. Numerator $\to_p E_f[h(X)]$ by (b); denominator $\to_p E_g[w] = \int f = 1$; Slutsky gives $\to_p E_f[h(X)]$. If $h \equiv c$, the estimator equals $c\,\frac{\sum w_i}{\sum w_j} = c$ exactly — zero variance — whereas (a)'s estimator is $c\cdot\frac1m\sum w_i$ with positive variance. Hence superior.
