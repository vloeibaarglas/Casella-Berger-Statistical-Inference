**Central idea.** The ratio is $\frac{c(\theta_2)}{c(\theta_1)}\exp([w(\theta_2)-w(\theta_1)]t)$ — an exponential in $t$ with positive slope exactly when $w$ increases.

**Solution.** For $\theta_2>\theta_1$,
$$\frac{g(t\mid\theta_2)}{g(t\mid\theta_1)} = \frac{c(\theta_2)}{c(\theta_1)}\exp\!\bigl([w(\theta_2)-w(\theta_1)]t\bigr),$$
and $w(\theta_2)-w(\theta_1)>0$ since $w$ is increasing; hence the ratio is strictly increasing in $t$: MLR in $T$. Examples: (i) $n(\theta,1)$: $w(\theta)=\theta$; (ii) Poisson$(\theta)$ (for $T=\sum X_i$): $w(\theta)=\log\theta$; (iii) binomial$(n,\theta)$: $w(\theta)=\log\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}$ (equivalently exponential$(\theta)$ with $T=\sum X_i$: $w(\theta)=-1/\theta$).
