**Central idea.** Location–scale: $\mu$ slides the graph, $\sigma$ stretches it horizontally by $\sigma$ and flattens it vertically by $1/\sigma$; the shape — a symmetric bump peaking at $\pm\sqrt3/2$ with height $1701/1024\approx1.66$ — is unchanged.

**Solution.** $f$ is a symmetric bump on $(-1,1)$ with $f'(x)=\frac{63}{2}x^5(3-4x^2)$, hence peaks at $x=\pm\sqrt3/2$ of height $f(\pm\sqrt3/2)=\frac{63}{4}\cdot\frac{27}{256}=\frac{1701}{1024}\approx1.661$, and $f(0)=f(\pm1)=0$. (a) is $f$ itself: support $(-1,1)$, peaks at $\pm0.866$ of height $1.661$. (b) shifts it rigidly: support $(2,4)$, peaks at $3\pm0.866$, same height $1.661$. (c) support $(1,5)$, peaks at $3\pm1.732$, height halved to $\approx0.831$. On the same axes: (a) a tall narrow bump at the origin; (b) the identical bump centered at 3; (c) a twice-as-wide, half-as-tall bump centered at 3.
