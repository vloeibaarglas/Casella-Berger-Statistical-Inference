**Central idea.** Simulate size and power ($n=15$, nominal $\alpha=0.1$, Huber $k=1.5$): the (10.3.6) plug-in and the bootstrap variance both match the book's $Z_{GW}$; the median test is competitive only for heavy tails.

**Solution.** *Note:* (10.3.8) as printed has denominator $[1-\frac1n\sum I(|x_i-\hat\theta_M|<k)]^2$, which estimates $[P(|X|\ge k)]^2$; to match (10.3.7) it should be $[\frac1n\sum I(|x_i-\hat\theta_M|\le k)]^2$ (used below). Rejection rates of $|Z|>z_{0.05}$ (600 simulations, $B=200$ bootstrap):

| | Normal | | Double exp. | | $t_5$ | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | $\theta_0$ (size) | $\theta_0+.5\sigma$ (power) | size | power | size | power |
| (10.3.6) plug-in | 0.123 | 0.653 | 0.127 | 0.712 | 0.098 | 0.690 |
| (10.3.8) corrected | 0.123 | 0.653 | 0.127 | 0.712 | 0.098 | 0.690 |
| bootstrap var | 0.122 | 0.672 | 0.122 | 0.708 | 0.097 | 0.678 |
| median (boot SE) | 0.108 | 0.480 | 0.082 | 0.655 | 0.085 | 0.557 |

(a) The (10.3.6) variance $\frac{\frac1n\sum\psi^2}{(\frac1n\sum\psi')^2}$ performs essentially identically to (the corrected) (10.3.8) — both are consistent plug-ins for (10.2.6); neither dominates, and (10.3.6) is the more direct plug-in. (b) The bootstrap variance is fully automatic (no analytic work) and matches the plug-ins on both size and power — desirable as a general-purpose alternative. (c) The median-based test has the most conservative size but notably less power for normal data (0.48 vs.\ 0.65); for double-exponential data it is nearly competitive (0.66 vs.\ 0.71), as expected since the median is the MLE there. All tests share the book's finding: $z$-cutoffs are slightly too small (sizes $0.10$–$0.13$ above nominal $0.10$).
