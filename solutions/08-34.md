**Central idea.** (a) $P_\theta(T > c) = 1 - F_0(c - \theta)$ shifts monotonically. (b) With $r(t) = g(t \mid \theta_2)/g(t \mid \theta_1)$ nondecreasing, compare $r(t)$ to $r(c)$ on $\{T > c\}$ and $\{T \le c\}$; one of the two comparisons always goes the right way.

**Solution.** (a) If $\theta$ is a location parameter, $T - \theta$ has a distribution $F_0$ free of $\theta$, so $P_\theta(T > c) = 1 - F_0(c - \theta)$, nondecreasing in $\theta$.
(b) Let $\theta_1 < \theta_2$ (equality is trivial) and $r(t) = g(t \mid \theta_2)/g(t \mid \theta_1)$, nondecreasing by MLR. Then $P_{\theta_2}(T > c) = \int_c^\infty r(t)g(t \mid \theta_1)\,dt \ge r(c)P_{\theta_1}(T > c)$ and $P_{\theta_2}(T \le c) = \int_{-\infty}^c r(t)g(t \mid \theta_1)\,dt \le r(c)P_{\theta_1}(T \le c)$. If $r(c) \ge 1$ the first inequality gives the result; if $r(c) < 1$ the second gives $P_{\theta_2}(T \le c) < P_{\theta_1}(T \le c)$, i.e. the same conclusion.
