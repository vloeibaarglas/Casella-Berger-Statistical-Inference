**Central idea.** The normal density is symmetric, so the likelihood depends on $x$ only through $x^2 = |x|^2$ — a direct factorization.

**Solution.** The pdf is $f(x\mid\sigma) = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\exp(-x^2/(2\sigma^2)) = g(|x|\mid\sigma)\cdot 1$ with $g(t\mid\sigma) = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\exp(-t^2/(2\sigma^2))$. By the Factorization Theorem, $|X|$ is sufficient for $\sigma$ (equivalently, $P(X = x \mid |X| = t)$ is $\pm t$ with probability $1/2$ each, free of $\sigma$). Yes.
