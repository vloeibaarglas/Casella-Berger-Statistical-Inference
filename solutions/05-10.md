**Central idea.** For a normal population the central moments are $\theta_2 = \sigma^2$, $\theta_3 = 0$, $\theta_4 = 3\sigma^4$; both routes must give $2\sigma^4/(n-1)$.

**Solution.** (a) $\theta_1 = \mu$, $\theta_2 = \sigma^2$, $\theta_3 = 0$ (symmetry), $\theta_4 = 3\sigma^4$ (standard normal fourth moment).
(b) From 5.8(b): $\mathrm{Var}\, S^2 = \frac{1}{n}\left(3\sigma^4 - \frac{n-3}{n-1}\sigma^4\right) = \frac{\sigma^4}{n}\cdot\frac{3(n-1)-(n-3)}{n-1} = \frac{\sigma^4}{n}\cdot\frac{2n}{n-1} = \frac{2\sigma^4}{\,n-1\,}.$
(c) $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_{n-1}$ has variance $2(n-1)$, so $\mathrm{Var}\, S^2 = \frac{\sigma^4}{(n-1)^2}\cdot 2(n-1) = \frac{2\sigma^4}{n-1}$, agreeing with (b). (Also 5.8(c) gives $\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{X}, S^2) = \theta_3/n = 0$ here — the familiar normal independence in covariance form.)
