**Central idea.** Survivors $\approx$ Binomial$(2000,0.01)$; with $n$ large and $p$ small, Poisson$(20)$ is the practical approximation — and 100 is $\approx 18$ standard deviations above the mean.

**Solution.** (a) $X \sim$ Binomial$(2000,0.01)$ (each insect survives independently with probability $0.01$), well approximated by Poisson$(\lambda = 20)$. (b) $P(X < 100) = \sum_{k=0}^{99}\binom{2000}{k}(0.01)^k(0.99)^{2000-k}$. (c) Poisson approximation: $\sum_{k=0}^{99}e^{-20}20^k/k! \approx 1$ to all practical precision (99 is about $17.7$ standard deviations above the mean 20). So it is essentially certain that fewer than 100 survive — as expected, since $EX = 20$.
