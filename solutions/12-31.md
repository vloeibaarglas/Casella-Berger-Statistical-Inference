**Central idea.** Nonparametric bootstrap: resample the (centered) fitted residuals with replacement instead of assuming a parametric error law; everything else mirrors Exercise 12.30.

**Solution.** (a) As in Exercise 12.30(a): LAD fit $\tilde\alpha=18.59$, $\tilde\beta=-0.89$ (resp. the M-estimate fit), with fitted residuals $\hat e_i=y_i-(\tilde\alpha+\tilde\beta x_i)$, $i=1,\dots,23$.
(b) Center the residuals, $\hat e^c_i=\hat e_i-\bar{\hat e}$ (they need not have mean zero for LAD/M fits). For $b=1,\dots,B$: draw $e^{*(b)}_1,\dots,e^{*(b)}_n$ with replacement from $\{\hat e^c_i\}$, form $y^{*(b)}_i=\tilde\alpha+\tilde\beta x_i+e^{*(b)}_i$, and refit by LAD (resp. M-estimation) to obtain $(\tilde\alpha^{*(b)},\tilde\beta^{*(b)})$.
(c) With $B$ large (e.g. $B=1000$), report $\mathrm{se}(\tilde\alpha)=\mathrm{sd}\{\tilde\alpha^{*(b)}\}$ and $\mathrm{se}(\tilde\beta)=\mathrm{sd}\{\tilde\beta^{*(b)}\}$. Unlike the parametric bootstrap, no error distribution is assumed — the empirical residual distribution drives the resampling — so the resulting standard errors are valid under whatever error law generated the data, at the cost of discreteness when $n$ is small.
