**Central idea.** Minimizing the full posterior LINEX loss, including its linear term, gives $-\frac1c\log E[e^{-c\theta}\mid X]$; under a normal posterior the mgf yields the closed form $\bar X-c\sigma^2/(2n)$.
**Solution.** (a) As a function of $d=a-\theta$, $L=e^{cd}-cd-1$: for $c=1$ it rises exponentially for $d>0$ (overestimation penalized heavily) and linearly for $d<0$; $c=0.5$ is milder; $c=0.2$ is nearly symmetric (approaching $\tfrac{c^2}{2}d^2$ as $c\to0$).
(b) $E[L(\theta,a)\mid X]=e^{ca}E[e^{-c\theta}\mid X]-ca+cE[\theta\mid X]-1$; $\frac{d}{da}=ce^{ca}E[e^{-c\theta}\mid X]-c=0$ gives $a=-\frac1c\log E[e^{-c\theta}\mid X]$ (a minimum by convexity in $a$).
(c) With flat prior, $\theta\mid\bar X\sim\mathrm{n}(\bar X,\sigma^2/n)$; $E[e^{-c\theta}\mid\bar X]=\exp(-c\bar X+c^2\sigma^2/(2n))$ (normal mgf), so $\delta^B(\bar X)=\bar X-c\sigma^2/(2n)$.
(d) Write $\delta^B-\theta=Z-c\sigma^2/(2n)$ with $Z:=\bar X-\theta\mid\bar X\sim\mathrm{n}(0,\sigma^2/n)$. Posterior LINEX loss of $\delta^B$: $E[e^{cZ-c^2\sigma^2/(2n)}]-cE[Z-c\sigma^2/(2n)]-1=1+\frac{c^2\sigma^2}{2n}-1=\frac{c^2\sigma^2}{2n}$. For $\bar X$: $E[e^{cZ}]-1=e^{c^2\sigma^2/(2n)}-1>\frac{c^2\sigma^2}{2n}$ (since $e^t-1>t$), confirming $\delta^B$ is better under LINEX.
(e) Under squared error: posterior expected loss of $\delta^B$ is $\frac{\sigma^2}{n}+\frac{c^2\sigma^4}{4n^2}$ (variance plus squared bias), versus $\frac{\sigma^2}{n}$ for $\bar X$ — under symmetric loss the unbiased $\bar X$ wins, as expected.
