**Central idea.** Go to polar coordinates: the angular integral contributes $\pi/2$, the $r$ in the area element cancels the $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ in the denominator, leaving $\int_0^\infty g = 1$.

**Solution.** $f \ge 0$ is clear. With $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$ ($r>0$, $0<\theta<\pi/2$), $dx\,dy = r\,dr\,d\theta$:
$$\iint_{x,y>0} f = \int_0^{\pi/2}\int_0^\infty \frac{2g(r)}{\pi r}\,r\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\int_0^\infty g(r)\,dr = 1.$$
