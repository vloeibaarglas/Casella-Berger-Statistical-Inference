**Central idea.** The binomial–beta identity turns Clopper–Pearson tail equations into beta quantiles, and the beta–$F$ relation turns those into $F$ cutoffs.

**Solution.** $L(x)$ solves $P_L(X \ge x) = \alpha/2$, i.e. $P(Y \le L) = \alpha/2$ for $Y \sim \text{beta}(x,n-x+1)$. Since $\frac{Y}{1-Y}\cdot\frac{n-x+1}{x} \sim F_{2x,2(n-x+1)}$, $\frac{L}{1-L}\cdot\frac{n-x+1}{x} = F_{2x,2(n-x+1),1-\alpha/2} = 1/F_{2(n-x+1),2x,\alpha/2}$, giving
$$L(x) = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{n-x+1}{x}F_{2(n-x+1),2x,\alpha/2}}.$$
Numerical check: for $n=10$, $x=3$, $\alpha=.05$, direct solution of $P_p(X\ge3)=.025$ gives $L=.066740$, matching the formula above (using $\frac{n-x}{x}$ in place of $\frac{n-x+1}{x}$ would give $.075553$ instead). The upper endpoint: $U(x)$ solves $P_U(X\le x)=\alpha/2$, i.e. $P(Z\ge U)=\alpha/2$ for $Z\sim\text{beta}(x+1,n-x)$, yielding the stated form (verified: $.652453$ both ways).
