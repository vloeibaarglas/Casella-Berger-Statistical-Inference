**Central idea.** (a) and (c) are the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus; (b) and (d) differentiate under the integral, justified by an integrable dominating derivative.

**Solution.** (a) $e^{-\lambda x}$, by FTC (integrand continuous).
(b) For $\lambda\ge\delta>0$, $\big|\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}e^{-\lambda t}\big|=te^{-\lambda t}\le te^{-\delta t}$, integrable on $[0,\infty)$; hence $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}dt=\int_0^\infty(-t)e^{-\lambda t}dt=-\frac{1}{\lambda^2}$ (also $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac1\lambda$).
(c) $\int_t^1x^{-2}dx=\frac1t-1$ ($t>0$), so the derivative is $-\frac{1}{t^2}$ (FTC directly).
(d) For $t<1$, substitute $u=x-t$: $\int_1^\infty\frac{dx}{(x-t)^2}=\int_{1-t}^\infty u^{-2}du=\frac{1}{1-t}$, so the derivative is $\frac{1}{(1-t)^2}$. Justification: for $t\le t_0<1$, $\big|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(x-t)^{-2}\big|=\frac{2}{(x-t)^3}\le\frac{2}{(x-t_0)^3}$, integrable on $[1,\infty)$; the integral diverges for $t\ge1$.
