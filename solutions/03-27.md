**Central idea.** Log-concavity (or the sign of $(\log f)'$) locates the mode; the beta with $\alpha,\beta<1$ is U-shaped — the counterexample.

**Solution.** (Unimodal = nondecreasing then nonincreasing.) (a) Uniform: constant on $(a,b)$, 0 outside — nondecreasing up to any $x^*\in[a,b]$, nonincreasing after: unimodal (flat top). (b) Gamma: $\frac{d}{dx}\log f=\frac{\alpha-1}{x}-\frac1\beta$; for $\alpha\ge1$ increases to the mode $(\alpha-1)\beta$ then decreases; for $\alpha<1$ strictly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$ with $\lim_{x\to0^+}f(x)=\infty$ (no finite mode): all unimodal. (c) Normal: strictly increasing to $\mu$, strictly decreasing after: unimodal. (d) No: for $\alpha<1,\beta<1$ the beta pdf is U-shaped with modes at both endpoints (e.g. $\alpha=\beta=1/2$), hence not unimodal. (For $\alpha,\beta\ge1$ it is unimodal with mode $(\alpha-1)/(\alpha+\beta-2)$, flat when $\alpha=\beta=1$.)
