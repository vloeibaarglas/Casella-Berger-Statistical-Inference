**Central idea.** Score $=l'(\theta_0)/\sqrt{I(\theta_0)}$: for $\mu$ this is the usual $z$-statistic; for $\sigma$ it is a centered, scaled sum of squared deviations.

**Solution.** (a) $l(\mu) = -\sum(X_i-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)+c$, $l'(\mu) = n(\bar X-\mu)/\sigma^2$, $I(\mu)=n/\sigma^2$. At $\mu_0$: $S = l'(\mu_0)/\sqrt{I(\mu_0)} = \frac{n(\bar X-\mu_0)/\sigma^2}{\sqrt n/\sigma} = \sqrt n(\bar X-\mu_0)/\sigma$.
(b) With $\mu$ known, $l(\sigma) = -n\log\sigma-\sum(X_i-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)$, $l'(\sigma) = -n/\sigma+\sum(X_i-\mu)^2/\sigma^3$, and $I(\sigma_0)=2n/\sigma_0^2$ (from 10.35). The score statistic is
$$S = \frac{l'(\sigma_0)}{\sqrt{I(\sigma_0)}} = \frac{-n/\sigma_0+\sum(X_i-\mu)^2/\sigma_0^3}{\sqrt{2n}/\sigma_0} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mu)^2-n\sigma_0^2}{\sigma_0^2\sqrt{2n}}\;\xrightarrow{d}\;n(0,1)\ \text{under }H_0.$$
