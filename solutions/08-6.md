**Central idea.** With the mean-parametrization, both MLEs are sample means; the likelihood ratio collapses to a function of the single fraction $T$, which under $H_0$ is the ratio of independent gammas with common scale — hence beta.

**Solution.** (Here exponential$(\theta)$ means mean $\theta$: $f(x\mid\theta) = \theta^{-1}e^{-x/\theta}$.) Let $S_X = \sum X_i$, $S_Y = \sum Y_i$. $L(\theta,\mu) = \theta^{-n}\mu^{-m}\exp(-S_X/\theta - S_Y/\mu)$.
(a) Unconstrained MLEs: $\hat\theta = S_X/n$, $\hat\mu = S_Y/m$. Under $H_0$ ($\theta=\mu$): $\hat\theta_0 = (S_X+S_Y)/(n+m)$. The exponential factors equal $e^{-(n+m)}$ in both, so
$$\lambda = \frac{\hat\theta_0^{-(n+m)}}{\hat\theta^{-n}\hat\mu^{-m}} = \frac{n^n m^m}{(n+m)^{n+m}}\, T^n(1-T)^m, \qquad T = \frac{S_X}{S_X+S_Y}.$$
(b) The bracketed constant is fixed, and $g(T) = T^n(1-T)^m$ on $(0,1)$ is maximized at $T = n/(n+m)$, strictly increasing before and strictly decreasing after. Hence $\{\lambda \le c\} = \{T \le c_1\} \cup \{T \ge c_2\}$: the LRT rejects for $T$ far from $n/(n+m)$ in either direction.
(c) Under $H_0$, $S_X \sim \text{Gamma}(n,\theta)$ and $S_Y \sim \text{Gamma}(m,\theta)$ independently (shape–scale form, common scale $\theta$). For independent gammas with common scale, $S_X/(S_X+S_Y) \sim \text{beta}(n,m)$, independent of $\theta$.
