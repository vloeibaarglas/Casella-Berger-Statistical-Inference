**Central idea.** Detailed balance: $f(y)q(z\mid y)\alpha(y,z) = f(z)q(y\mid z)\alpha(z,y)$ (both equal $\min(f(y)q(z\mid y),\,f(z)q(y\mid z))$); integrating out the current state leaves $f$ invariant.

**Solution.** Let the proposal density be $q(z\mid y)$ and $\alpha(y,z) = \min\!\left(1, \frac{f_Y(z)q(y\mid z)}{f_Y(y)q(z\mid y)}\right)$. The chain's kernel is $K(y,dz) = q(z\mid y)\alpha(y,z)\,dz + r(y)\delta_y(dz)$ with $r(y) = 1-\int q(z\mid y)\alpha(y,z)\,dz$. If $Z_i \sim f_Y$,
$$P(Z_{i+1} \in A) = \iint_A f_Y(y)q(z\mid y)\alpha(y,z)\,dz\,dy + \int_A f_Y(y)r(y)\,dy.$$
Detailed balance: $f_Y(y)q(z\mid y)\alpha(y,z) = \min\big(f_Y(y)q(z\mid y),\,f_Y(z)q(y\mid z)\big) = f_Y(z)q(y\mid z)\alpha(z,y)$, symmetric in $y,z$. Hence the first term equals $\int_A f_Y(z)\left[\int q(y\mid z)\alpha(z,y)\,dy\right]dz = \int_A f_Y(z)(1-r(z))\,dz$. Adding the rejection term $\int_A f_Y(z)r(z)\,dz$ gives $\int_A f_Y(z)\,dz = P(Z_i \in A)$. So $Z_{i+1} \sim f_Y$: the target is a stationary (stable) distribution of the Metropolis chain.
