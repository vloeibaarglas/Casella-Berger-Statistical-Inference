**Central idea.** Three standard errors for $r$: nonparametric bootstrap (model-free), parametric bootstrap (bivariate normal plug-in), and the delta method $(1-r^2)/\sqrt n$ — the first-order approximation is optimistic.

**Solution.** (a) The 2024 CRC reprint prints $(5.58,2.81)$ (likely a typo for the canonical $(558,2.81)$ in Efron 1982; see addendum). With the printed value, $r=0.4927656216$.
(b) Resampling the 15 pairs with replacement ($B=1000$): SE$^*(r)\approx 0.20$; the histogram is left-skewed with mass piling near 1 (correlation is bounded above).
![Bootstrap histogram of r]({{ '/plots/ex1017_boot_hist.png' | relative_url }})
(c) Bivariate normal MLEs: $\hat\mu_X=563.4$, $\hat\mu_Y=3.095$, $\hat\sigma_X=154.1$, $\hat\sigma_Y=0.235$, $\hat\rho=0.493$. Simulating 1000 samples of 15 pairs: SE$^*\approx 0.19$.
(d) SE$(r)\approx (1-r^2)/\sqrt{15} = (1-0.243)/3.873 = 0.196$. This is smaller than both bootstrap SEs (0.19–0.20): the first-order delta method is optimistic for $n=15$, missing the skewness the bootstrap captures. Approximate pdf: $r\;\dot\sim\;n(0.493, 0.196^2)$.
(e) $z=\tfrac12\log\frac{1+r}{1-r}\;\dot\sim\;n(\zeta,\frac{1}{n-3})$ with $\zeta=\tfrac12\log\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}$; here $z=0.540$, SE $=1/\sqrt{12}=0.289$. The approximate pdf of $r$ follows by transformation: $f_R(r) = \phi\big((z(r)-0.540)/0.289\big)\cdot\frac{1}{0.289(1-r^2)}$, $z(r)=\tfrac12\log\frac{1+r}{1-r}$ — shifted left relative to (d)'s symmetric normal, better reflecting the skewness.
