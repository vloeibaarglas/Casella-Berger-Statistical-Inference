**Central idea.** Under $(x_1,z) = (x_1,(x_2-1)/x_1)$ the Jacobian $|J| = x_1$ weights the conditional density toward large $x_1$, giving $X_1|Z=0 \sim \text{gamma}(2,1)$ versus $X_1|X_2=1 \sim \text{exponential}(1)$; the wedge $B_1$ widens with $x_1$ (informative) while the strip $B_2$ does not.

**Solution.** (a) With $x_2 = 1+zx_1$, $|J| = x_1$: $f_{X_1,Z}(x_1,z) = x_1e^{-x_1}e^{-(1+zx_1)}$ ($1+zx_1>0$). At $z=0$: $f_{X_1|Z=0}(x_1) \propto x_1e^{-x_1}$, i.e. $X_1|Z=0 \sim \text{gamma}(2,1)$ (mean $2$). But $X_1|X_2=1 \sim \text{exponential}(1)$ (mean $1$) by independence — different distributions from "the same" information.
(b) $B_1$ is a wedge $1-\epsilon x_1 < x_2 < 1+\epsilon x_1$ around the ray $x_2=1$, widening as $x_1$ grows; $B_2$ is the horizontal strip $1-\epsilon<x_2<1+\epsilon$. $P(B_1|X_1=x_1) = P(1-\epsilon x_1<X_2<1+\epsilon x_1) \approx 2\epsilon x_1e^{-1}$ grows with $x_1$ (informative: observing $B_1$ favors large $X_1$), while $P(B_2|X_1=x_1) = P(1-\epsilon<X_2<1+\epsilon)$ is constant in $x_1$ (uninformative, by independence).
(c) $P(X_1\le x|B_1) = \frac{\int_0^x (e^{-(1-\epsilon t)}-e^{-(1+\epsilon t)})e^{-t}dt}{\int_0^\infty (e^{-(1-\epsilon t)}-e^{-(1+\epsilon t)})e^{-t}dt} \to \frac{\int_0^x te^{-t}dt}{\int_0^\infty te^{-t}dt} = \int_0^x te^{-t}dt$ as $\epsilon\to0$ (using $e^{-(1-\epsilon t)}-e^{-(1+\epsilon t)} \sim 2\epsilon t e^{-1}$): the gamma$(2,1)$ cdf, agreeing with (a). $P(X_1\le x|B_2) = P(X_1\le x)$ by independence $\to 1-e^{-x}$, the exponential$(1)$ cdf, agreeing with $X_1|X_2=1$.
