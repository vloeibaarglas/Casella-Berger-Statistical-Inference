**Central idea.** Conditionally on $S=\sum X_i$, the sample is multinomial — giving $E[S^2\mid\bar X]=\bar X$ directly — and Lehmann–Scheffé makes this the UMVUE story in miniature.
**Solution.** (a) $\sum X_i$ is complete sufficient (Poisson exponential family) and $\bar X$ is unbiased for $\lambda$; by Lehmann–Scheffé $\bar X$ is the UMVUE.
(b) Given $\sum X_i=s$, $(X_1,\dots,X_n)\sim\mathrm{multinomial}(s;\frac1n,\dots,\frac1n)$, so $X_1\mid s\sim\mathrm{binomial}(s,1/n)$ and $E[\sum X_i^2\mid s]=n\big[\frac{s}{n}(1-\frac1n)+\frac{s^2}{n^2}\big]=s(1-\frac1n)+\frac{s^2}{n}$. Hence $E[S^2\mid\bar X]=\frac{1}{n-1}\big(E[\sum X_i^2\mid S]-\frac{S^2}{n}\big)=\frac{S}{n}=\bar X$. Then $\mathrm{Var}(S^2)=\mathrm{Var}(E[S^2\mid\bar X])+E[\mathrm{Var}(S^2\mid\bar X)]=\mathrm{Var}(\bar X)+E[\mathrm{Var}(S^2\mid\bar X)]>\mathrm{Var}(\bar X)$, since $S^2$ is not a.s. constant given $\bar X$.
(c) Yes: it is the Lehmann–Scheffé theorem in action. In general, if $T$ is complete sufficient and $W$ is unbiased for $\tau(\theta)$, then $E[W\mid T]$ is the (unique) best unbiased estimator; here $W=S^2$, $T=\bar X$, and the identity $E[S^2\mid\bar X]=\bar X$ explicitly exhibits the UMVUE $\bar X$ as the Rao–Blackwellization of $S^2$.
