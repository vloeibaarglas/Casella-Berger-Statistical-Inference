**Central idea.** $x^2$ is monotone on $(-1,0)$ and $(0,2)$ but the two images $(0,1)$ and $(0,4)$ differ, violating condition (iii); letting the pieces carry their own ranges repairs the formula.

**Solution.** (a) $F_X(x)=\frac19(x+1)^2$ on $[-1,2]$. For $0<y<1$, $F_Y(y)=P(-\sqrt y\le X\le\sqrt y)=F_X(\sqrt y)-F_X(-\sqrt y)=\frac{4\sqrt y}{9}$; for $1\le y<4$, $F_Y(y)=P(X\le\sqrt y)=F_X(\sqrt y)=\frac19(\sqrt y+1)^2$. Differentiating,
$$f_Y(y) = \begin{cases}\dfrac{2}{9\sqrt y}, & 0<y<1,\\[8pt]\dfrac{1}{9}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt y}\right), & 1<y<4,\end{cases}$$
(0 otherwise). Theorem 2.1.8 is not directly applicable because on $(-1,0)$ the image of $x^2$ is $(0,1)$ while on $(0,2)$ it is $(0,4)$ — not the same $\mathcal Y$ for each piece.
(b) If the $A_i$ partition a superset of $\mathcal X$, the proof of Theorem 2.1.8 goes through unchanged: $P(X\in A_i\setminus\mathcal X)=0$, and differentiating $F_Y(y)=\sum_i P(g_i(X)\le y,\,X\in A_i)$ gives $f_Y(y)=\sum_i f_X(g_i^{-1}(y))\,|dg_i^{-1}/dy|$, each summand restricted to its own range $\mathcal Y_i$. With $A_1=(-2,0)$, $A_2=(0,2)$: $g_1^{-1}(y)=-\sqrt y$, $g_2^{-1}(y)=\sqrt y$, each with derivative magnitude $1/(2\sqrt y)$. Since $f_X(-\sqrt y)=\frac29(1-\sqrt y)$ is nonzero only for $0<y<1$ while $f_X(\sqrt y)=\frac29(1+\sqrt y)$ is nonzero for $0<y<4$: for $0<y<1$, $f_Y(y)=\frac{1}{9\sqrt y}[(1-\sqrt y)+(1+\sqrt y)]=\frac{2}{9\sqrt y}$; for $1<y<4$, $f_Y(y)=\frac{1}{9\sqrt y}(1+\sqrt y)$ — agreeing with (a).
