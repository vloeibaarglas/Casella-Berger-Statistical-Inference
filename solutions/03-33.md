**Central idea.** Write each family in natural form $\eta(\theta)$; a one-dimensional $\theta$ tracing a genuine curve in a $k\ge2$-dimensional $\eta$-space is a curved exponential family.

**Solution.** (a) $f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta}}\exp(-\frac{x^2}{2\theta}+x-\frac\theta2)$: exponential family with natural parameters $\eta_1=-\frac{1}{2\theta}$ (statistic $x^2$), $\eta_2=1$ (statistic $x$). Curve: the horizontal ray $\{(\eta_1,1):\eta_1<0\}$ — degenerate (one natural coordinate constant) but 1-d in a 2-d space. (b) $f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi a}\,\theta}\exp(-\frac{x^2}{2a\theta^2}+\frac{x}{a\theta}-\frac{1}{2a})$: $\eta_1=\frac{1}{a\theta}$, $\eta_2=-\frac{1}{2a\theta^2}$. Eliminating $\theta$: $\eta_2=-\frac{a}{2}\eta_1^2$ with $\eta_1>0$, the right half of a downward parabola with vertex at the origin. (c) $f=\frac{\alpha^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\frac1x\exp(\alpha\log x-\alpha x)$: $\eta_1=\alpha$, $\eta_2=-\alpha$: the ray $\eta_2=-\eta_1$, $\eta_1>0$ (the mean-1 curved family). (d) $-(x-\theta)^4=-x^4+4\theta x^3-6\theta^2x^2+4\theta^3x-\theta^4$, so $f=Ce^{-\theta^4}e^{-x^4}\exp(4\theta x^3-6\theta^2x^2+4\theta^3x)$ is an exponential family with $\eta(\theta)=(4\theta,-6\theta^2,4\theta^3)$, $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$: the twisted cubic $\eta_2=-\frac38\eta_1^2$, $\eta_3=\frac1{16}\eta_1^3$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ — curved ($d=1<k=3$). (Sketches: (a) horizontal ray at $\eta_2=1$ in the left half-plane; (b) right half of $\eta_2=-\frac a2\eta_1^2$; (c) ray from the origin into the fourth quadrant along $\eta_1+\eta_2=0$; (d) a twisted-cubic space curve through the origin, odd in $\theta$.)
