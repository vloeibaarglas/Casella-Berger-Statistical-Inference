**Central idea.** The difference of the two sides is a sum of squares, hence $\geq 0$ (Cauchy–Schwarz); equality forces every $2\times 2$ minor $a_ib_j - a_jb_i$ to vanish, i.e., all centered points proportional — collinearity.

**Solution.** Both sides expand to $\sum_{i,j}(a_i^2b_j^2 - a_ib_ia_jb_j)$: indeed,
$$\sum_{i,j}\big(a_i^2b_j^2 - a_ib_ia_jb_j\big) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\big(a_i^2b_j^2 + a_j^2b_i^2 - 2a_ib_ia_jb_j\big) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}(a_ib_j - a_jb_i)^2 = \sum_{i<j}(a_ib_j - a_jb_i)^2,$$
since diagonal terms vanish and $(i,j)$, $(j,i)$ pairs coincide. (For $n = 2$ this is $(a_1^2+a_2^2)(b_1^2+b_2^2) - (a_1b_1+a_2b_2)^2 = (a_1b_2-a_2b_1)^2$, and the general case follows by induction on $n$.)
For the sample correlation, apply the identity to the centered values $a_i = x_i - \bar{x}$, $b_i = y_i - \bar{y}$: with $r = \sum a_ib_i/\sqrt{\sum a_i^2 \sum b_i^2}$,
$$1 - r^2 = \frac{\sum_{i<j}(a_ib_j - a_jb_i)^2}{\left(\sum a_i^2\right)\left(\sum b_i^2\right)} \geq 0.$$
Thus $|r| = 1$ iff $a_ib_j - a_jb_i = 0$ for all $i < j$, i.e., $(a_i, b_i)$ is proportional to $(a_j, b_j)$ for all $i,j$ — all points $(x_i, y_i)$ lie on the line $y - \bar{y} = c(x - \bar{x})$ for some $c$. (The hint's "$=1$" is $|r| = 1$ up to sign; $r = \pm 1$ both give straight lines.)
