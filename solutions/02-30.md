**Central idea.** Integrate (or sum) $e^{tx}$ against the density; (c) is a shifted Laplace, (d) a negative-binomial series.

**Solution.** (a) $M(t)=\frac1c\int_0^c e^{tx}dx=\frac{e^{ct}-1}{ct}$ for $t\ne0$ ($M(0)=1$).
(b) $M(t)=\frac{2}{c^2}\int_0^c xe^{tx}dx=\frac{2}{c^2}\cdot\frac{e^{ct}(ct-1)+1}{t^2}=\frac{2(e^{ct}(ct-1)+1)}{c^2t^2}$.
(c) Shifting by $\alpha$: $M(t)=e^{\alpha t}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{tx}\frac{1}{2\beta}e^{-|x|/\beta}dx=e^{\alpha t}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\beta^2t^2}$, $|t|<1/\beta$.
(d) $M(t)=p^r\sum_{x\ge0}\binom{r+x-1}{x}[(1-p)e^t]^x=p^r(1-(1-p)e^t)^{-r}=\left(\frac{p}{1-(1-p)e^t}\right)^r$, for $(1-p)e^t<1$ (i.e. $t<-\log(1-p)$).
