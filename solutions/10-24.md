**Central idea.** It is Slutsky's theorem: a standardized sum converging in distribution, divided by an average converging in probability.

**Solution.** From the Taylor expansion, $\sqrt n(\hat\theta_M-\theta_0) = \dfrac{-\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi(X_i-\theta_0)}{\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi'(X_i-\theta_0)}$. By (10.2.4) the numerator $\xrightarrow{d} n(0,E_{\theta_0}[\psi(X-\theta_0)]^2)$ (CLT, using $E_{\theta_0}\psi(X-\theta_0)=0$), and by (10.2.5) the denominator $\xrightarrow{p} E_{\theta_0}\psi'(X-\theta_0)$ (WLLN). Slutsky's theorem gives
$$\sqrt n(\hat\theta_M-\theta_0)\xrightarrow{d} n\!\left(0,\frac{E_{\theta_0}[\psi(X-\theta_0)]^2}{[E_{\theta_0}\psi'(X-\theta_0)]^2}\right),$$
which is (10.2.6).
