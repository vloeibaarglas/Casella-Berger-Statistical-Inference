**Central idea.** With $\alpha=\beta=c$, the MSE numerator is $c^2+(n-4c^2)p(1-p)$; choosing $4c^2=n$ kills the $p$-dependence.
**Solution.** From Example 7.3.5, with $Y\sim\mathrm{binomial}(n,p)$ and $\hat p_B=(Y+\alpha)/(n+\alpha+\beta)$,
$$\mathrm{MSE}(\hat p_B)=\frac{np(1-p)+(\alpha-(\alpha+\beta)p)^2}{(n+\alpha+\beta)^2}.$$
With $\alpha=\beta=c$: numerator $=np(1-p)+c^2(1-2p)^2=c^2+(n-4c^2)p(1-p)$. Taking $c=\sqrt{n/4}=\sqrt{n}/2$ (so $4c^2=n$) makes the numerator identically $n/4$, hence $\mathrm{MSE}(\hat p_B)=\frac{n/4}{(n+\sqrt{n})^2}$, constant in $p$.
