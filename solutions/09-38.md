**Central idea.** For fixed length, sliding the interval trades density at the endpoints; unimodality makes the total probability single-peaked in the slide position, maximized at equal endpoint densities.

**Solution.** (a) FTC: $\frac{d}{da}[F(a+c)-F(a)] = f(a+c)-f(a)$.
(b) Let $m$ be the mode. For $a+c < m$, $f$ increasing gives $f(a+c)-f(a) > 0$; for $a > m$, $<0$. On $(m-c,m)$, $h(a) = f(a+c)-f(a)$ has $h'(a) = f'(a+c)-f'(a) < 0$ (since $a+c>m>a$), with $h(m-c) > 0 > h(m)$: exactly one root, the unique maximizer.
(c) If $[u,v]$, $v-u < c^*$, had $\int_u^v f = 1-\alpha$, then $\int_u^{u+c^*}f \ge 1-\alpha$, but (b) gives $\int_a^{a+c^*}f \le \int_{a^*}^{a^*+c^*}f = 1-\alpha$ with equality only at $a^*$; hence $\int_u^{u+c^*}f = 1-\alpha$, $u = a^*$, and $\int_u^v f < 1-\alpha$, contradiction. So $[a^*,a^*+c^*]$ is shortest.
