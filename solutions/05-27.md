**Central idea.** (a) The joint-to-marginal ratio collapses to the pdf of the $i$th order statistic of a *truncated* sample: given $X_{(j)} = y$, the $j-1$ smaller observations are iid from $F_X(\cdot)/F_X(y)$. (b) For a uniform sample, range $R = X_{(n)}-X_{(1)}$ and midrange $V = (X_{(1)}+X_{(n)})/2$ have constant joint density, so $V \mid R = r$ is uniform.

**Solution.** (a) For $i < j$ and $x < y$, dividing the joint pdf (5.26) by the marginal pdf of $X_{(j)}$,
$$f_{X_{(i)}\mid X_{(j)}}(x \mid y) = \frac{(j-1)!}{(i-1)!\,(j-i-1)!}\left[\frac{F_X(x)}{F_X(y)}\right]^{i-1}\left[\frac{F_X(y)-F_X(x)}{F_X(y)}\right]^{j-i-1}\frac{f_X(x)}{F_X(y)},$$
$0$ for $x \geq y$. This is exactly the pdf of the $i$th order statistic from a sample of size $j-1$ drawn from the truncated distribution with cdf $F_X(\cdot)/F_X(y)$ on $(-\infty, y)$.
(b) (Example 5.4.7: for a uniform$(0,1)$ sample, $R = X_{(n)} - X_{(1)}$ is the range and $V = \tfrac{1}{2}(X_{(1)}+X_{(n)})$ the midrange.) From $f_{X_{(1)},X_{(n)}}(u,v) = n(n-1)(v-u)^{n-2}$, the transform $(r,m) = (v-u, (u+v)/2)$ (Jacobian 1) gives $f_{R,V}(r,m) = n(n-1)r^{n-2}$ on $\tfrac{r}{2} < m < 1-\tfrac{r}{2}$, $0 < r < 1$. Hence $f_R(r) = n(n-1)r^{n-2}(1-r)$ and
$$f_{V\mid R}(m \mid r) = \frac{1}{1-r}, \qquad \frac{r}{2} < m < 1-\frac{r}{2}:$$
given the range, the midrange is uniform over its feasible interval.
