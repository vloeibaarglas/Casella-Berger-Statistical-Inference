**Central idea.** $\sum_{x\ge1}(1-p)^x/x = -\log p$ (Mercator series) makes the terms sum to 1; moments come from geometric-series sums $\sum xq^x = q/(1-q)^2$.

**Solution.** (a) Terms are positive since $\log p < 0$; $\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-p)^x}{x} = -\log(1-(1-p)) = -\log p$, so $\sum_{x\ge1}P(X=x) = \frac{-\log p}{-\log p} = 1$. (b) $EX = \frac{-1}{\log p}\sum_{x\ge1}(1-p)^x = \frac{-(1-p)}{p\log p}$; $E[X^2] = \frac{-1}{\log p}\sum_{x\ge1}x(1-p)^x = \frac{-(1-p)}{p^2\log p}$. Hence
$$\text{Var}\,X = \frac{-(1-p)}{p^2\log p}-\frac{(1-p)^2}{p^2(\log p)^2} = \frac{-(1-p)}{p^2\log p}\left(1+\frac{1-p}{\log p}\right) > 0,$$
positive because $\log p < p-1$ for $0<p<1$.
