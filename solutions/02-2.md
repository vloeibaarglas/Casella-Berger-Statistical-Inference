**Central idea.** Same monotone change-of-variables formula; track how the support transforms ($x\in(0,1)$ sends $-\log x$ to $(0,\infty)$, while $e^x$ of a positive variable lands in $(1,\infty)$).

**Solution.** (a) $x=\sqrt y$, $0<y<1$: $f_Y(y) = 1\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt y} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt y}$.
(b) $x = e^{-y}$, $|dx/dy| = e^{-y}$, $y>0$:
$$f_Y(y) = \frac{(n+m+1)!}{n!m!}e^{-ny}(1-e^{-y})^m e^{-y} = \frac{(n+m+1)!}{n!m!}e^{-(n+1)y}(1-e^{-y})^m,\quad y>0.$$
(c) $x = \log y$, $dx/dy = 1/y$, $y>1$:
$$f_Y(y) = \frac{1}{\sigma^2}(\log y)e^{-(\log y/\sigma)^2/2}\cdot\frac1y,\quad y>1.$$
