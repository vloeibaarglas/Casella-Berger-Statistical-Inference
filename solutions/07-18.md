**Central idea.** Factor the bivariate normal into marginal$\times$conditional: the marginal pieces give $(\bar x,\frac1n\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2)$ and $(\bar y,\frac1n\sum(y_i-\bar y)^2)$, and the remaining profile likelihood in $\rho$ is maximized at the sample correlation.
**Solution.** (a) Equating population and sample moments through second order gives directly the stated estimators (using $E(X-\mu_X)(Y-\mu_Y)=\rho\sigma_X\sigma_Y$).
(b) From $f(x,y)=f(y\mid x)f(x)$ with $X\sim\mathrm{n}(\mu_X,\sigma_X^2)$, the factor $f(x\mid\mu_X,\sigma_X^2)$ alone determines $\hat\mu_X=\bar x$, $\hat\sigma_X^2=\frac1n\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2$; symmetrically $\hat\mu_Y=\bar y$, $\hat\sigma_Y^2=\frac1n\sum(y_i-\bar y)^2$. Conditionally, $Y\mid x\sim\mathrm{n}(\mu_Y+\rho\frac{\sigma_Y}{\sigma_X}(x-\mu_X),\sigma_Y^2(1-\rho^2))$; substituting the four MLEs, the profile log-likelihood in $\rho$ is $-\frac n2\log(1-\rho^2)-\frac{1}{2(1-\rho^2)}\sum\big(\frac{y_i-\bar y}{\hat\sigma_Y}-\rho\frac{x_i-\bar x}{\hat\sigma_X}\big)^2$. Differentiating (or writing it as a regression through the standardized variables) gives $\hat\rho=\sum(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)/\sqrt{\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2\sum(y_i-\bar y)^2}$, the sample correlation — identical to the MOM estimators.
