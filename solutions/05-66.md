**Central idea.** Medians are equivariant under increasing affine maps, so standardize to $n(0,1)$; the asymptotic variance $1/(4f(0)^2) = \pi/2$ can be checked directly against simulation.

**Solution.** (a) $x \mapsto (x-\mu)/\sigma$ is strictly increasing, hence preserves order: the median (any usual definition, e.g., $X_{((n+1)/2)}$ for odd $n$) transforms by the same map. So it suffices to study $n(0,1)$.
(b) Simulating 60,000 samples of size 15 from $n(0,1)$ and recording the sample median (8th order statistic): the medians are centered near 0 with sample sd $\approx 0.318$; the histogram is bell-shaped, slightly heavier-tailed than normal.
(c) The asymptotic law gives $\mathrm{sd}(m) \approx \sqrt{\frac{1}{4f(0)^2n}} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi/2}{15}} \approx 0.324$ (since $f(0) = 1/\sqrt{2\pi}$). The simulated $0.318$ is remarkably close — yes, $n = 15$ is already large enough for the asymptotics to be quite accurate here (the normal population is the friendliest case for the median's CLT).
