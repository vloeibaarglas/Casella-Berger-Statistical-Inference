**Central idea.** $\sum X_i \sim \text{Poisson}(n\lambda)$; a zero expectation for all $\lambda > 0$ is a power series in $\lambda$ identically zero, forcing every coefficient to vanish.

**Solution.** $T = \sum X_i \sim \text{Poisson}(n\lambda)$. If $\mathrm{E}_\lambda[g(T)] = \sum_{k=0}^\infty g(k)e^{-n\lambda}(n\lambda)^k/k! = 0$ for all $\lambda > 0$, then $\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{g(k)n^k}{k!}\lambda^k = 0$ for all $\lambda > 0$. A power series vanishing on $(0,\infty)$ has all coefficients zero, so $g(k) = 0$ for every $k$: $T$ is complete.
