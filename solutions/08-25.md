**Central idea.** In each case the log likelihood ratio is affine in the natural sufficient statistic with slope of the sign of $\theta_2-\theta_1$.

**Solution.** It suffices to check the joint (or sufficient-statistic) ratio for $\theta_2>\theta_1$.
(a) For a sample, with $S=\sum X_i$: $\frac{L(\theta_2)}{L(\theta_1)} = \exp\!\left[\frac{\theta_2-\theta_1}{\sigma^2}S - \frac{n(\theta_2^2-\theta_1^2)}{2\sigma^2}\right]$, increasing in $S$ (hence in $\bar X$).
(b) With $S=\sum X_i$: $\frac{L(\theta_2)}{L(\theta_1)} = \left(\frac{\theta_2}{\theta_1}\right)^S e^{-n(\theta_2-\theta_1)}$, increasing in $S$ since $\theta_2/\theta_1>1$.
(c) $\frac{f(x\mid\theta_2)}{f(x\mid\theta_1)} = \left(\frac{\theta_2}{\theta_1}\right)^x\left(\frac{1-\theta_2}{1-\theta_1}\right)^{n-x}$; the log-ratio is $x\log\frac{\theta_2(1-\theta_1)}{\theta_1(1-\theta_2)}+\text{const}$, increasing in $x$ because $\theta_2>\theta_1$ implies $\frac{\theta_2}{1-\theta_2}>\frac{\theta_1}{1-\theta_1}$.
