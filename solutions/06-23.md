**Central idea.** The support $(\theta,2\theta)$ brackets $\theta$ between $X_{(n)}/2$ and $X_{(1)}$; the ratio criterion shows both extremes are needed, and a linear combination has constant (zero) mean.

**Solution.** The joint pdf is $\theta^{-n}I(x_{(n)}/2 < \theta < x_{(1)})$. For sample points $\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}$ the ratio $f(\mathbf{x}\mid\theta)/f(\mathbf{y}\mid\theta)$ equals $1$ on $\theta > \max(x_{(n)},y_{(n)})/2$ intersected with the respective upper bounds; it is $\theta$-free (where defined) iff $x_{(1)} = y_{(1)}$ and $x_{(n)} = y_{(n)}$. By Theorem 6.2.13, $(X_{(1)},X_{(n)})$ is minimal sufficient. It is not complete: $\mathrm{E}_\theta X_{(1)} = \theta\frac{n+2}{n+1}$ and $\mathrm{E}_\theta X_{(n)} = \theta\frac{2n+1}{n+1}$, so $g = (2n+1)X_{(1)}-(n+2)X_{(n)}$ is nonzero with $\mathrm{E}_\theta g \equiv 0$. (For $n = 1$, $X_{(1)} = X_{(n)}$ a.s. and the witness vanishes; the argument needs $n \ge 2$.)
