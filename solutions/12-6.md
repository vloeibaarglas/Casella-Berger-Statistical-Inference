**Central idea.** $(X_i,Y_i)$ is an affine (linear-plus-constant) transformation of the independent normals $(\xi_i,\delta_i,\epsilon_i)$, hence jointly normal; only the means, variances, and covariance need computing.

**Solution.** Write $X_i=\xi_i+\delta_i$, $Y_i=\alpha+\beta\xi_i+\epsilon_i$ with $\xi_i\sim n(\xi,\sigma^2_\xi)$, $\delta_i\sim n(0,\sigma^2_\delta)$, $\epsilon_i\sim n(0,\sigma^2_\epsilon)$ independent. As a linear function of independent normals, $(X_i,Y_i)$ is bivariate normal with $EX_i=\xi$, $EY_i=\alpha+\beta\xi$, $\mathrm{Var}\,X_i=\sigma^2_\delta+\sigma^2_\xi$, $\mathrm{Var}\,Y_i=\sigma^2_\epsilon+\beta^2\sigma^2_\xi$, and $\mathrm{Cov}(X_i,Y_i)=\mathrm{Cov}(\xi_i+\delta_i,\alpha+\beta\xi_i+\epsilon_i)=\beta\sigma^2_\xi$, which is (12.2.19).
