**Central idea.** The observations in Example 5.5.16 are negative binomial, counting failures before $r$ successes. Thus $V_n\sim\mathrm{NB}(nr,p)=\mathrm{NB}(20,.7)$, not binomial. Approximate its cdf by a normal cdf and difference consecutive values, treating the lower support boundary explicitly.

**Solution.** The exact pmf is $P(V_n=v)=\binom{v+19}{19}(.7)^{20}(.3)^v$. The approximating normal has
$$\mu=\frac{20(.3)}{.7}=8.5714285714,\qquad \sigma=\sqrt{\frac{20(.3)}{.7^2}}=3.4992710611.$$
For $v\ge1$, the uncorrected approximation is $\Phi((v-\mu)/\sigma)-\Phi((v-1-\mu)/\sigma)$; the continuity-corrected version is $\Phi((v+1/2-\mu)/\sigma)-\Phi((v-1/2-\mu)/\sigma)$. At $v=0$, use $P(V_n=0)=P(V_n\le0)$: the corresponding cdf approximations are $\Phi(-\mu/\sigma)$ and $\Phi((1/2-\mu)/\sigma)$.

Another convention approximates the zero cell by a finite normal interval too: $(-1,0]$ without correction and $(-1/2,1/2]$ with correction. Those give $0.0040$ and $0.0058$, respectively, and explain the zero row in the newly supplied draft. The table above instead uses the support-aware cdf convention. Neither normal approximation is exact at this boundary. The continuity correction improves many interior entries (at $v=10$, $0.1097$ becomes $0.1046$, compared with exact $0.0944$), but does not improve every entry (for example $v=2$).
