**Central idea.** Regression through the origin: all information about $\beta$ is in $T = \sum x_iY_i$; $\mathrm{Var}\,\hat\beta = \sigma^2/\sum x_i^2$ is minimized by pushing every $x_i$ to the endpoint of $[A,B]$ with the largest $|x|$; the MPE is the MLE by symmetry and unimodality.

**Solution.** (a) $\ell(\beta) = -\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_i(y_i-\beta x_i)^2$ plus a constant; setting $\ell'(\beta) = \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_ix_i(y_i-\beta x_i) = 0$ gives $\hat\beta = \dfrac{\sum_i x_iy_i}{\sum_i x_i^2}$. $E\hat\beta = \beta\sum_ix_i^2/\sum_ix_i^2 = \beta$; $\mathrm{Var}\,\hat\beta = \sigma^2\sum_ix_i^2/(\sum_ix_i^2)^2 = \sigma^2/\sum_ix_i^2$.
(b) $\ell''(\beta) = -\sum_ix_i^2/\sigma^2$, so $I(\beta) = E[-\ell''(\beta)] = \sum_ix_i^2/\sigma^2$, and the Cramér–Rao Lower Bound is $\sigma^2/\sum_ix_i^2$.
(c) $\hat\beta$ is unbiased and attains the CRLB, hence is the best (uniformly minimum variance) unbiased estimator.
(d) $\mathrm{Var}\,\hat\beta = \sigma^2/\sum_ix_i^2$ is minimized by maximizing $\sum_ix_i^2$ over $[A,B]^n$. Since $x_i^2 \le \max(A^2,B^2)$ for each $i$, place every $x_i$ at the endpoint with the larger absolute value — all at $A$ if $|A| > |B|$, all at $B$ if $|B| > |A|$ (if $|A| = |B|$, any allocation attains $\sum_ix_i^2 = n\max(A^2,B^2)$).
(e) $f(\mathbf{y}\mid\beta) \propto \exp\big(-\frac{\sum_ix_i^2}{2\sigma^2}(\beta-\hat\beta)^2\big)$: as a function of $\beta$ it is symmetric and unimodal about the MLE $\hat\beta$. For a window of fixed length $2r$, $\int_{D-r}^{D+r}g(\beta)\,d\beta$ with $g$ symmetric and unimodal about $\hat\beta$ is maximized by centering the window on the mode, $D = \hat\beta$. Hence the maximum probability estimator is $\hat\beta = \sum_ix_iY_i/\sum_ix_i^2$, the MLE.
