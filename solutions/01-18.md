**Central idea.** Exactly one empty cell forces exactly one doubled cell; count: choose the empty cell, choose the doubled cell, choose its 2 balls, permute the rest.

**Solution.** Total outcomes: $n^n$. For exactly one empty cell, some other cell holds exactly 2 balls and the rest hold 1 each. Choose the empty cell ($n$ ways), the doubled cell ($n-1$ ways), the 2 balls in it ($\binom{n}{2}$ ways), and arrange the remaining $n-2$ balls in the remaining $n-2$ cells ($(n-2)!$ ways). Favorable: $n(n-1)\binom{n}{2}(n-2)! = \binom{n}{2}n!$. Hence $P = \binom{n}{2}n!/n^n$.
