**Central idea.** The median is the LAD location estimator; Taylor-expand the expected objective, with the empirical fluctuation handled by the convex expansion in 12.27.

**Solution.** Let $m$ be the population median, assume $f$ is continuous and positive at $m$, and write $\epsilon_i=Y_i-m$. In the corrected 12.27, take $x_i=1$ and $q_n=1$. The true-parameter sign sum $Z_n=n^{-1/2}\sum_i\operatorname{sign}(\epsilon_i)$ converges to $N(0,1)$.
The exact absolute-loss identity there gives
$$\sum_i(|\epsilon_i-h/\sqrt n|-|\epsilon_i|)=-hZ_n+f(m)h^2+o_p(1),$$
uniformly on compact $h$ sets. Its minimizing argument therefore satisfies $\sqrt n(M_n-m)=Z_n/[2f(m)]+o_p(1)$, for any chosen sample median, and
$$\sqrt n(M_n-m)\Rightarrow N\left(0,\frac1{4f(m)^2}\right).$$
The expected sign score has derivative $-2f(m)$; the empirical sign score has jumps and derivative zero between them. Substituting the latter into an ordinary differentiable Taylor formula is invalid.
