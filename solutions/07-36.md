**Central idea.** The substitution $u=ct$ pulls out $c^r$; each family then reduces to gamma-type integrals (or a truncation at $1/X_{(n)}$).
**Solution.** (a) With $u=ct$: numerator $=c^{-(n+r)}\int u^{n+r-1}\prod f(ux_i)\,du$, denominator $=c^{-(n+2r)}\int u^{n+2r-1}\prod f(ux_i)\,du$; the ratio scales by $c^r$.
(b) With $f=\phi$: $\prod\phi(tx_i)\propto e^{-t^2\sum x_i^2/2}$. For $r=2$, $\int_0^\infty t^{n+1}e^{-at^2/2}dt=2^{n/2}\Gamma(\frac n2+1)/a^{(n+2)/2}$ ($a=\sum x_i^2$) and similarly for $t^{n+3}$; the ratio is $a/(n+2)$. Pitman estimator of $\sigma^2$: $\sum X_i^2/(n+2)$.
(c) $f(x)=e^{-x}\mathbf{1}_{(x>0)}$: $\prod f(tx_i)=e^{-t\sum x_i}$ ($t>0$). For $r=1$: numerator $=n!/(\sum x_i)^{n+1}$, denominator $=(n+1)!/(\sum x_i)^{n+2}$; ratio $=(\sum X_i)/(n+1)$.
(d) $f=\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}$: $\prod f(tx_i)=\mathbf{1}_{[0,1/x_{(n)}]}(t)$. For $r=1$: numerator $=(1/x_{(n)})^{n+1}/(n+1)$, denominator $=(1/x_{(n)})^{n+2}/(n+2)$; Pitman estimator of $\theta$: $\frac{n+2}{n+1}X_{(n)}$.
