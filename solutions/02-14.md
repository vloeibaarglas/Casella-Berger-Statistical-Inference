**Central idea.** Write the survival function as an integral (sum) of the density (mass), then swap the order: the mean is the total mass "above" each level.

**Solution.** (a) $1-F_X(x)=\int_x^\infty f_X(t)\,dt$, so by Tonelli on $\{0\le x\le t<\infty\}$,
$$\int_0^\infty[1-F_X(x)]dx=\int_0^\infty\int_x^\infty f_X(t)\,dt\,dx=\int_0^\infty\int_0^t dx\,f_X(t)\,dt=\int_0^\infty t f_X(t)\,dt=EX.$$
(b) $1-F_X(k)=P(X>k)=\sum_{j>k}f_X(j)$, so
$$\sum_{k\ge0}(1-F_X(k))=\sum_{k\ge0}\sum_{j\ge k+1}f_X(j)=\sum_{j\ge1}j\,f_X(j)=EX,$$
each $j\ge1$ being counted exactly $j$ times (for $k=0,\dots,j-1$). Part (b) is the discrete analogue of (a): the integral of the survival function becomes a sum of survival probabilities, with $F_X(k)=P(X\le k)$ in the role of $F_X(x)$.
