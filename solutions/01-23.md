**Central idea.** Sum over $k$ of $P(\text{both get } k)$ and recognize Vandermonde's identity $\sum_k \binom{n}{k}^2 = \binom{2n}{n}$.

**Solution.** $P(\text{both score } k \text{ heads}) = \big[\binom{n}{k}(1/2)^n\big]^2$. Summing over $k$,
$$P(\text{tie}) = \frac{1}{4^n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^2 = \frac{1}{4^n}\binom{2n}{n},$$
since $\sum_k \binom{n}{k}\binom{n}{n-k} = \binom{2n}{n}$ (choose $n$ from $2n$ by splitting into two groups of $n$).
