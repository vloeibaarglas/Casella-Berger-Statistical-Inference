**Central idea.** The mean–variance pair $(\theta,a\theta^2)$ traces a one-dimensional parabola in $\Re^2$; sufficiency comes from the exponential-family form, while incompleteness comes from an unbiased estimator of zero built from $\mathrm{E}\bar{X}^2$ and $\mathrm{E}S^2$.

**Solution.** (a) The parameter space $\{(\theta,a\theta^2):\theta > 0\}$ is the parabola $\sigma^2 = a\mu^2$ ($\mu > 0$), a one-dimensional curve in $\Re^2$; it contains no nonempty open subset of $\Re^2$.
(b) The joint pdf is $(2\pi a\theta^2)^{-n/2}\exp\big(-\sum(x_i-\theta)^2/(2a\theta^2)\big) = (2\pi a\theta^2)^{-n/2}\exp\big(-\sum x_i^2/(2a\theta^2) + \sum x_i/(a\theta) - n/(2a)\big)$, i.e. an exponential family in $(\sum X_i^2,\sum X_i)$, equivalently in $(\bar{X},S^2)$ since $\sum X_i^2 = (n-1)S^2 + n\bar{X}^2$. By Theorem 6.2.10, $T = (\bar{X},S^2)$ is sufficient. It is not complete: $\mathrm{E}_\theta S^2 = a\theta^2$ and $\mathrm{E}_\theta\bar{X}^2 = \theta^2 + a\theta^2/n = \theta^2(n+a)/n$, so $g(\bar{X},S^2) = a\bar{X}^2 - \frac{n+a}{n}S^2$ is nonzero with $\mathrm{E}_\theta g = a\theta^2\frac{n+a}{n} - \frac{n+a}{n}a\theta^2 = 0$ for all $\theta > 0$.
