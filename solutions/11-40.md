**Central idea.** The identity $\frac{a^2}{c}+\frac{b^2}{d}-\frac{(a+bt)^2}{c+dt^2} = \frac{(adt-bc)^2}{cd\,(c+dt^2)} \ge 0$ proves (a); applied with $c = S^2/n$ it gives the (11.3.44) maximum $\big[n(\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y})^2 + S_{xx}(\hat\beta-\beta)^2\big]/S^2$, and with $c = S^2(1+1/n)$ it gives the prediction-band maximum — the $n/(n+1)$ factor comes from the extra $+1$ in the prediction variance.

**Solution.** (a) For $c,d > 0$,
$$\frac{a^2}{c} + \frac{b^2}{d} - \frac{(a+bt)^2}{c+dt^2} = \frac{(a^2d+b^2c)(c+dt^2) - cd(a+bt)^2}{cd\,(c+dt^2)} = \frac{(adt-bc)^2}{cd\,(c+dt^2)} \ge 0,$$
with equality at $t = bc/(ad)$ (if $a = 0$ the ratio $\uparrow b^2/d$ as $|t|\to\infty$; if $b = 0$ the maximum $a^2/c$ is attained at $t = 0$). Hence the supremum over $t$ is $a^2/c + b^2/d$. (This is the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in disguise.)
(b) Write $(\hat\alpha+\hat\beta x_0)-(\alpha+\beta x_0) = (\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y}) + (\hat\beta-\beta)(x_0-\bar x)$, using $\hat\alpha = \bar Y-\hat\beta\bar x$ and $\mu_{\bar Y} = \alpha+\beta\bar x$. Apply (a) with $t = x_0-\bar x$, $a = \bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y}$, $b = \hat\beta-\beta$, $c = S^2/n$, $d = S^2/S_{xx}$:
$$\sup_{x_0}\frac{\big((\hat\alpha+\hat\beta x_0)-(\alpha+\beta x_0)\big)^2}{S^2\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{(x_0-\bar x)^2}{S_{xx}}\right)} = \frac{(\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y})^2}{S^2/n} + \frac{(\hat\beta-\beta)^2}{S^2/S_{xx}} = \frac{n(\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y})^2 + S_{xx}(\hat\beta-\beta)^2}{S^2},$$
which is (11.3.44) — the gap in the proof of Theorem 11.3.6.
(c) For a new observation the prediction variance carries an extra $+1$: apply (a) with the same $a,b,t$ but $c = S^2\big(1+\frac{1}{n}\big) = S^2\frac{n+1}{n}$, $d = S^2/S_{xx}$:
$$\sup_t\frac{\big((\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y})+(\hat\beta-\beta)t\big)^2}{S^2\left[1+\frac{1}{n}+\frac{t^2}{S_{xx}}\right]} = \frac{(\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y})^2}{S^2(n+1)/n} + \frac{(\hat\beta-\beta)^2}{S^2/S_{xx}} = \frac{\frac{n}{n+1}(\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y})^2 + S_{xx}(\hat\beta-\beta)^2}{S^2}.$$
*Note:* this is where the $n/(n+1)$ factor genuinely belongs — it comes from the $1+1/n$ in the prediction-interval denominator, not from (11.3.44). 
(d) Write $U = n(\bar Y-\mu_{\bar Y})^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_1$, $V = S_{xx}(\hat\beta-\beta)^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_1$, $W = \mathrm{SSE}/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_{n-2}$, mutually independent. The statistic in (c) is $M = \dfrac{U/(n+1) + V}{W/(n-2)}$. Moment-match the numerator $N = U/(n+1)+V$: $E[N] = 1 + \frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{n+2}{n+1}$, $\mathrm{Var}(N) = 2 + \frac{2}{(n+1)^2}$. Approximating $N \;\dot\sim\; g\chi^2_h$ as in Example 7.2.3 (Patnaik), with $g = \mathrm{Var}(N)/(2E[N])$ and $h = 2E[N]^2/\mathrm{Var}(N)$:
$$g = \frac{(n+1)^2+1}{(n+1)(n+2)}, \qquad h = \frac{(n+2)^2}{(n+1)^2+1},$$
so $M \;\dot\sim\; gh\cdot F_{h,n-2}$ — a constant times an $F$ variable, whose $1-\alpha$ quantile calibrates the displayed mean-error band. Interpreting it as a simultaneous prediction band requires defining and including the future error process.
