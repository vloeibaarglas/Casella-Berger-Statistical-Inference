**Central idea.** $Y_1$ is the absolute value of a $n(0, 1/n)$ variable; $Y_2$ averages iid $|X_i|$. The triangle inequality $|\sum X_i| \leq \sum |X_i|$ gives $Y_1 \leq Y_2$ pointwise.

**Solution.** $\bar{X} \sim n(0, 1/n)$, so $Y_1 = |\bar{X}| \stackrel{d}{=} |Z|/\sqrt{n}$ with $Z \sim n(0,1)$. Since $E|Z| = \sqrt{2/\pi}$,
$$EY_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi n}}.$$
By linearity, $EY_2 = E|X_1| = \sqrt{2/\pi}$. By the triangle inequality, $Y_1 = \frac{1}{n}|\sum X_i| \leq \frac{1}{n}\sum|X_i| = Y_2$ a.s., hence
$$EY_1 = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi n}} \leq \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}} = EY_2,$$
strict for $n > 1$. (Averaging before taking absolute values cancels signs and shrinks the mean.)
