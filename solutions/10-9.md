**Central idea.** $T=\sum X_i$ is complete sufficient; Rao–Blackwellize the naive unbiased indicators $I(X_1=0)$, $I(X_1=1)$ onto $T$. Both UMVUEs turn out asymptotically efficient (ARE $=1$).

**Solution.** $T=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\sim$ Poisson($n\lambda$) is complete sufficient.
(a) $E[I(X_1=0)] = e^{-\lambda}$. The UMVUE is $E[I(X_1=0)\mid T=t] = P(X_1=0\mid T=t) = \frac{e^{-\lambda}e^{-(n-1)\lambda}((n-1)\lambda)^t/t!}{e^{-n\lambda}(n\lambda)^t/t!} = \left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^t$. So $\delta_1(T) = \left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^T$.
(b) $E[I(X_1=1)] = \lambda e^{-\lambda}$. Similarly $P(X_1=1\mid T=t) = \frac{\lambda e^{-\lambda}e^{-(n-1)\lambda}((n-1)\lambda)^{t-1}/(t-1)!}{e^{-n\lambda}(n\lambda)^t/t!} = \frac{t}{n}\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{t-1}$ (and $0$ for $t=0$). So $\delta_2(T) = \frac{T}{n}\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{T-1}$.
(c) The MLEs are $e^{-\bar X}$ and $\bar X e^{-\bar X}$. By the delta method their asymptotic variances are the Cramér–Rao bounds: for $\tau_1(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda}$, $[\tau_1']^2/(nI(\lambda)) = e^{-2\lambda}/(n/\lambda) = \lambda e^{-2\lambda}/n$; for $\tau_2(\lambda)=\lambda e^{-\lambda}$, $\lambda e^{-2\lambda}(1-\lambda)^2/n$. Now $\delta_1(T) = \exp(T\log(1-1/n)) = e^{-\bar X}(1+o_p(1))$ and $\delta_2(T) = \bar X e^{-\bar X}(1+o_p(1))$, so both UMVUEs have the same asymptotic variances as the MLEs: $\mathrm{ARE}=1$ in both cases (with ARE as in Definition 10.1.16). Preference: the UMVUEs — exactly unbiased, asymptotically efficient, and simple closed forms; the MLE is asymptotically equivalent but biased in finite samples.
(d) $n=15$, $T=104$, $\bar x = 6.933$. For $e^{-\lambda}$: UMVUE $(14/15)^{104} = 0.000765$; MLE $e^{-6.933} = 0.000975$. For $\lambda e^{-\lambda}$: UMVUE $(104/15)(14/15)^{103} = 0.005685$; MLE $6.933\,e^{-6.933} = 0.006758$.
