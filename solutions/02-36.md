**Central idea.** An mgf must converge in a two-sided neighborhood of 0; for $t>0$ the factor $e^{tx}$ eventually dominates the Gaussian-in-$\log x$ decay.

**Solution.** With $x=e^y$ ($dx/x=dy$), $M_X(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{te^y}e^{-y^2/2}dy$. For fixed $t>0$, $te^y-y^2/2\to+\infty$ as $y\to\infty$ (the exponential dominates the quadratic), so the integrand is eventually $\ge1$ and the integral diverges. Hence $E[e^{tX}]=\infty$ for every $t>0$; since an mgf must be finite on some interval $(-h,h)$ with $h>0$, the lognormal has no mgf — despite having all moments finite.
