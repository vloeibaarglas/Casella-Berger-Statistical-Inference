**Central idea.** Linear combinations of independent normals are jointly (bivariate) normal, and for jointly normal variables zero covariance means independence; here $\mathrm{Cov}(U,V) = \mathrm{Var}X-\mathrm{Var}Y = 0$.

**Solution.** $(U,V)$ is a linear transformation of independent normals, hence bivariate normal. $\mathrm{Cov}(U,V) = \mathrm{Var}X-\mathrm{Var}Y = \sigma^2-\sigma^2 = 0$, so $U,V$ are independent. $U \sim n(\mu+\gamma, 2\sigma^2)$ and $V \sim n(\mu-\gamma, 2\sigma^2)$.
