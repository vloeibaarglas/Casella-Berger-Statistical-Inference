**Central idea.** The tangent line lies strictly below $g$ except at the point of tangency, so $\mathrm{E}[g(X)-(a+bX)] > 0$ whenever $X$ takes other values with positive probability.

**Solution.** Since $a+bx$ is tangent at $\mathrm{E}X$, $a+b\mathrm{E}X = g(\mathrm{E}X)$. Then $\mathrm{E}g(X)-g(\mathrm{E}X) = \mathrm{E}[g(X)-a-bX]$. The integrand is $\ge0$ everywhere and $>0$ when $X\ne\mathrm{E}X$; if $P(X\ne\mathrm{E}X)>0$ its expectation is $>0$, giving $\mathrm{E}g(X) > g(\mathrm{E}X)$. Equality holds only if $P(X=\mathrm{E}X)=1$.
