**Central idea.** The mgf of a randomly stopped sum is $M_H(t) = \exp(\lambda(M_X(t)-1))$; the logarithmic mgf $M_X(t) = \log(1-(1-p)e^t)/\log p$ turns this into $[p/(1-(1-p)e^t)]^r$ using $e^{-\lambda} = p^r$.

**Solution.** $M_{X_i}(t) = \frac{-1}{\log p}\sum_{t\ge1}\frac{((1-p)e^t)^t}{t} = \frac{\log(1-(1-p)e^t)}{\log p}$. By Theorems 4.4.3/4.6.7, $M_H(t) = \mathrm{E}[(M_X(t))^N] = \exp(\lambda(M_X(t)-1)) = e^{-\lambda}(1-(1-p)e^t)^{\lambda/\log p}$. With $r = -\lambda/\log p > 0$ and $e^{-\lambda} = p^r$ (since $p^r = e^{r\log p} = e^{-\lambda}$), $M_H(t) = \left(\frac{p}{1-(1-p)e^t}\right)^r$, the mgf of the negative binomial$(r,p)$ (failures before $r$ successes). Hence $H \sim \text{NB}(r,p)$.
