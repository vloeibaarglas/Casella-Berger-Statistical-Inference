**Central idea.** Split $|a_nW_n+b_n-\theta|\le|a_n||W_n-\theta|+|a_n-1||\theta|+|b_n|$ and let the deterministic terms vanish, leaving the consistency of $W_n$.
**Solution.** Theorem 10.1.5: if $W_n$ is consistent for $\theta$, $a_n\to1$, $b_n\to0$, then $U_n=a_nW_n+b_n$ is consistent for $\theta$. For $\varepsilon>0$,
$$|U_n-\theta|\le|a_n||W_n-\theta|+|a_n-1||\theta|+|b_n|.$$
Choose $N$ so that for $n\ge N$: $|a_n|\le2$, $|a_n-1||\theta|<\varepsilon/3$, $|b_n|<\varepsilon/3$. Then $P_\theta(|U_n-\theta|\ge\varepsilon)\le P_\theta(2|W_n-\theta|\ge\varepsilon/3)=P_\theta(|W_n-\theta|\ge\varepsilon/6)\to0$ by consistency of $W_n$. Hence $U_n$ is consistent for $\theta$.
