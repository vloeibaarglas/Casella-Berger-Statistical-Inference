**Central idea.** Integrate by parts to peel off one term of the Poisson sum at a time; probabilistically it is Poisson-process duality: the $\alpha$th arrival comes after $x$ iff fewer than $\alpha$ arrivals occurred by time $x$.

**Solution.** Let $I_\alpha(x) = \int_x^\infty \frac{z^{\alpha-1}e^{-z}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}dz$. Parts ($u=z^{\alpha-1}/\Gamma(\alpha)$, $dv=e^{-z}dz$): $I_\alpha(x) = \frac{x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}}{(\alpha-1)!}+I_{\alpha-1}(x)$; $I_1(x)=e^{-x}$. Induction gives the sum. Probabilistic: if $Z\sim\text{gamma}(\alpha,1)$ is the waiting time for the $\alpha$th event in a unit-rate Poisson process and $Y\sim\text{Poisson}(x)$ counts events by time $x$, both sides equal $P(Z>x)=P(Y\le\alpha-1)$.
