**Central idea.** For n$(\mu,\sigma^2)$ the natural parameters are $(\mu/\sigma^2,-1/(2\sigma^2))$; each approximation constrains $(\mu,\sigma^2)$ to a one-dimensional curve, hence a curve in $\eta$-space.

**Solution.** (a) $\eta_1=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda/n}=n$ (constant!), $\eta_2=-\frac{n}{2\lambda}$: the vertical ray $\{(\eta_1,\eta_2):\eta_1=n,\ \eta_2<0\}$. (b) $\eta_1=\frac{n}{1-p}$, $\eta_2=-\frac{n}{2p(1-p)}$: eliminating $p$, $\eta_2=-\frac{\eta_1^2}{2(\eta_1-n)}$ for $\eta_1>n$ — a branch in the lower half-plane running from $(n^+,-\infty)$ (as $p\to0^+$) toward the asymptote $\eta_2=-(\eta_1+n)/2$ (as $p\to1^-$). (c) $\eta_1=\frac{r(1-p)/p}{r(1-p)/(np^2)}=np$ and $\eta_2=-\frac{np^2}{2r(1-p)}$, but $p$ is the varying parameter, so $\eta_1$ is not constant. Eliminating $p = \eta_1/n$: $\eta_2=-\frac{\eta_1^2}{2r(n-\eta_1)}$ for $0 < \eta_1 < n$ — a curve in the lower half-plane from $(0^+,0^-)$ (as $p\to0^+$) descending toward the asymptote $\eta_1 = n$ (as $p\to1^-$).
