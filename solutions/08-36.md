**Central idea.** Choose a two-tail test matching both endpoint rejection probabilities; convexity proves that it uniformly improves the original test.

**Solution.** (a) Define
$$\psi(x)=1\{x<0.05\text{ or }x>0.55\}.$$
Under $\theta=1$, its rejection probability is $0.05+0.45=0.5$. Under $\theta=2$, it is $1-0.95^2+0.45^2=0.3$. More generally $E_\theta\psi=1-0.95^\theta+0.45^\theta$.
To prove dominance, put $u=1-x$, $h(u)=\psi(1-u)-\phi(1-u)$. The supplied endpoint probabilities imply $\int_0^1h(u)\,du=\int_0^1uh(u)\,du=0$. Moreover $h\ge0$ outside $(0.45,0.95)$ and $h\le0$ inside it, since $0\le\phi\le1$.
Let $L_\theta(u)$ be the affine line joining $u^{\theta-1}$ at $u=0.45$ and $u=0.95$. For $\theta<1$ or $\theta>2$, $u^{\theta-1}$ is convex, so its difference from this chord is nonnegative outside the two cutpoints and nonpositive between them. Hence
$$E_\theta(\psi-\phi)=\theta\int_0^1h(u)[u^{\theta-1}-L_\theta(u)]\,du\ge0.$$
For $1<\theta<2$, the function is concave, so the inequality reverses: $E_\theta\psi\le E_\theta\phi$. At both endpoints equality holds. Thus the new test has no greater rejection probability at any null parameter and no smaller power at any alternative parameter; it uniformly improves risk under $0$–$1$ testing loss. If $\phi$ differs from $\psi$ on a set of positive measure, the inequalities are strict away from the endpoints. Both supplied numbers are essential. This resolves the earlier open doubt without imposing unbiasedness or a minimax interpretation.

(b) Since $f(x \mid 1) \equiv 1$ on $(0,1)$, $\gamma(\theta) = \int_0^1 \phi(x)\theta(1-x)^{\theta-1}dx$, and differentiating under the integral gives $\gamma'(1) = \int_0^1 \phi(x)[1+\ln(1-x)]dx$. We need $\int_0^1 \phi = .1$ and $\int_0^1 \phi(x)\ln(1-x)dx = -.1$. Take a two-sided nonrandomized test $\phi = \mathbf 1_{[0,u]} + \mathbf 1_{[1-s,1]}$ with $u + s = .1$. Then $\int \phi\ln(1-x)dx = -(1-u)\ln(1-u) - u + s\ln s - s$; with $u = .1 - s$ this equals $-.1$ iff $s\ln s = (.9+s)\ln(.9+s)$, solved by $s \approx .0196$, $u \approx .0804$. So $\phi(x) = \mathbf 1_{[0,.0804]}(x) + \mathbf 1_{[.9804,1]}(x)$ is two-sided, satisfies $E_1\phi = .1$, and $\gamma'(1) = .1 - .1 = 0$.
