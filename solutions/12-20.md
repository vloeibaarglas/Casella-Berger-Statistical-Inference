**Central idea.** With $\beta=0$ the model collapses to iid Bernoulli trials; the boundary cases $0$ or $n$ successes push the logit to $\pm\infty$ but the likelihood ratio only needs likelihood values, which stay finite.

**Solution.** (a) With $\beta=0$, $\pi(x)\equiv\pi=e^\alpha/(1+e^\alpha)$ and $L(\pi)=\pi^{\sum y_i}(1-\pi)^{n-\sum y_i}$, the ordinary Bernoulli likelihood, maximized at $\hat\pi=\sum y_i/n$ for $0<\sum y_i<n$.

(b) Inverting the logit, $\hat\alpha_0=\log(\hat\pi/(1-\hat\pi))=\log\big((\sum y_i)/(n-\sum y_i)\big)$.

(c) If $\sum y_i=0$, $\hat\pi=0$ and $\log(\hat\pi/(1-\hat\pi))=-\infty$; if $\sum y_i=n$, it is $+\infty$ — no finite $\hat\alpha_0$ exists. But the LRT statistic $-2\log\lambda=2[\log L(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta)-\log L(\hat\alpha_0,0)]$ needs only $\log L(\hat\alpha_0,0)=\sum y_i\log\hat\pi+(n-\sum y_i)\log(1-\hat\pi)=0$ (with $0\log 0=0$), which is well defined (equal to $0$), so the test statistic is finite and well defined.
