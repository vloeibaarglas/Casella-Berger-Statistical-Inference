**Central idea.** (a) An increasing likelihood ratio tilts odds $P_\theta(X>t)/P_\theta(X\le t)$ upward. (b) Stochastic order only constrains cdfs, so a likelihood ratio can wiggle non-monotonically while cdfs stay ordered — e.g. ratios $(.5,2,1)$ on $\{1,2,3\}$.

**Solution.** (a) Let $\theta_1<\theta_2$ and $r(x)=f(x\mid\theta_2)/f(x\mid\theta_1)$, increasing. For any $t$ (ignoring null-set technicalities),
$$P_{\theta_2}(X\le t) = E_{\theta_1}[r(X)\mathbf 1_{X\le t}] \le r(t)P_{\theta_1}(X\le t),$$
$$P_{\theta_2}(X>t) = E_{\theta_1}[r(X)\mathbf 1_{X>t}] \ge r(t)P_{\theta_1}(X>t),$$
since $r(X)\le r(t)$ on $\{X\le t\}$ and $\ge r(t)$ on $\{X>t\}$. Dividing: $\frac{P_{\theta_2}(X>t)}{P_{\theta_2}(X\le t)} \ge \frac{P_{\theta_1}(X>t)}{P_{\theta_1}(X\le t)}$, so $P_{\theta_2}(X>t)\ge P_{\theta_1}(X>t)$: $F_{\theta_2}(t)\le F_{\theta_1}(t)$ for all $t$, i.e. stochastically increasing.
(b) On $\{1,2,3\}$ let $f(\cdot\mid\theta_1)=(.5,.25,.25)$ and $f(\cdot\mid\theta_2)=(.25,.5,.25)$. Then $F_{\theta_1}=(.5,.75,1)$, $F_{\theta_2}=(.25,.75,1)$: $F_{\theta_2}\le F_{\theta_1}$ everywhere, so the family is stochastically increasing. But $f(x\mid\theta_2)/f(x\mid\theta_1) = (.5,2,1)$ is not monotone in $x$: no MLR.
