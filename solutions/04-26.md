**Central idea.** Competing exponentials (scale convention, as in the text): the minimum is exponential with scale $\lambda\mu/(\lambda+\mu)$, the winner is $X$ with probability $\mu/(\lambda+\mu)$, and — by memorylessness — the minimum's distribution doesn't depend on who won.

**Solution.** (a) With $X \sim$ exponential$(\lambda)$, $Y \sim$ exponential$(\mu)$ in the text's scale convention ($f_X(x) = \frac1\lambda e^{-x/\lambda}$), for $z > 0$: $P(Z > z, W = 1) = P(X > z, X < Y) = \int_z^\infty \frac1\lambda e^{-x/\lambda}e^{-x/\mu}\,dx = \frac{\mu}{\lambda+\mu}e^{-z(\lambda+\mu)/(\lambda\mu)}$. Hence $P(Z \le z, W=1) = \frac{\mu}{\lambda+\mu}(1-e^{-z(\lambda+\mu)/(\lambda\mu)})$ and similarly $P(Z \le z, W=0) = \frac{\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}(1-e^{-z(\lambda+\mu)/(\lambda\mu)})$. The joint density is $f(z,1) = \frac1\lambda e^{-z(\lambda+\mu)/(\lambda\mu)}$, $f(z,0) = \frac1\mu e^{-z(\lambda+\mu)/(\lambda\mu)}$, $z>0$.
(b) $P(W=1) = \lambda/(\lambda+\mu)$, $P(W=0) = \mu/(\lambda+\mu)$, and $P(Z\le z|W=i) = 1-e^{-(\lambda+\mu)z} = P(Z\le z)$ for $i=0,1$. Thus $Z \sim \text{exponential}(\lambda+\mu)$ independent of $W$.
